# Commission & Covenant Continuity Investigation

This is the full working-out of the Israel/Gentile commission question first raised in [[00b-biblical-epistemology]]'s worked example. That file has the compressed version and the methodology (7-step test). This file has the fuller case, run in the order recommended: (0) what the Tanakh anticipated before Yeshua's ministry even began, (1) Yeshua's earthly commission, (2) covenant continuity in the Hebrew Scriptures, (3) whether the commission applies to every individual believer today. A note on where this returns to preterism sits at the end — deliberately still deferred, per the stated sequencing.

Directly answers the original objection: was the Great Commission simply "a command to every Christian," detached from who Yeshua actually addressed and when? The answer that survives the full text, below, is neither "Yeshua came only for Israel" nor "the Great Commission proves universal individual-evangelist duty" — both fail the 7-step test standing alone.

> **Prose** (captured per ADR-0004 — this is the connective reasoning that should survive into the eventual book, not just the citations): context does not weaken Scripture; context protects Scripture from being made to say more than it says. The fact that Yeshua initially sent the Twelve specifically to Israel does not erase the later mission to the nations. Likewise, the later mission to the nations does not erase the Israel-centered context in which Yeshua's ministry began. Biblical interpretation becomes distorted when one stage of the story is used to cancel another. The task is not to choose whichever verse supports a conclusion already reached, but to follow the development of the whole narrative.

## 0. Before Yeshua's Ministry — What the Tanakh Anticipated

Important groundwork: without this, Paul's Gentile mission can look like something introduced from outside the story rather than a continuation of it.

**Genesis 12:1–3** — Abraham's call already contains the nations. ESV: "I will make of you a great nation, and I will bless you and make your name great, so that you will be a blessing... and in you all the families of the earth shall be blessed." CJB: "you are to be a blessing... by you all the families of the earth will be blessed." RVR1960: "serás bendición... serán benditas en ti todas las familias de la tierra." Israel's election and the blessing of the nations are not competing ideas from the very first covenant promise — they're the same promise.

**Isaiah 42:6** — the servant given "as a covenant for the people, a light for the nations" (ESV) / "a light for the Goyim" (CJB) / "por luz de las naciones" (RVR1960).

**Isaiah 49:6** — stronger still: restoring Israel alone is called "too light a thing" (ESV: "It is too light a thing that you should be my servant to raise up the tribes of Jacob and to bring back the preserved of Israel; I will make you as a light for the nations, that my salvation may reach to the end of the earth." / CJB: "It is not enough that you are merely my servant..." / RVR1960: "Poco es para mí que tú seas mi siervo... también te di por luz de las naciones, para que seas mi salvación hasta lo postrero de la tierra.") **This is the exact verse Paul and Barnabas quote in Acts 13:47** ("I have made you a light for the Gentiles, that you may bring salvation to the ends of the earth," already in this file below) — not a loose echo, a direct citation. The Gentile mission isn't Paul finding new justification after the fact; he's naming his source.

## 1. Yeshua's Earthly Commission — Who Was He Sent To?

### John the Baptist's ministry — inside Israel, calling Israel to repentance
John's ministry (Matthew 3:1–12) prepares Israel for the Messiah — he isn't calling Gentiles to become Israelites, he's warning Israelites that ethnic descent doesn't excuse unrepentance. To the Pharisees and Sadducees:
**Matthew 3:7–10** — ESV: "You brood of vipers!... do not presume to say to yourselves, 'We have Abraham as our father,' for I tell you, God is able from these stones to raise up children for Abraham." CJB: "don't suppose you can comfort yourselves by saying, 'Avraham is our father'! For I tell you that God can raise up for Avraham sons from these stones!" RVR1960: "no penséis decir dentro de vosotros mismos: A Abraham tenemos por padre; porque yo os digo que Dios puede levantar hijos a Abraham aun de estas piedras." Relevant later for covenant-identity: physical descent was never sufficient on its own, even said to Israel about Israel, before the Gentile question is raised at all.

### The Israel-focused starting point
**Matthew 10:5–6** (already in [[01-convictions]] A.9) and **Matthew 15:24** (already cited) establish this plainly: the Twelve were sent to "the lost sheep of the house of Israel," and Yeshua states the same about His own mission.

### The Canaanite woman — the exception that proves the rule, and previews the expansion
**Matthew 15:22–28** — the fullest version of this exchange. Yeshua's answer to the Twelve's request to send her away is a direct restatement of Matthew 15:24's boundary: "I was sent only to the lost sheep of the house of Israel." The woman persists; Yeshua commends her faith and heals her daughter — without revoking the stated boundary. The healing happens *despite* the stated scope, not because the scope was quietly universal all along.
ESV: "'O woman, great is your faith! Let it be done for you as you desire.' And her daughter was healed instantly."
CJB: "'Lady, you are a person of great trust. Let your desire be granted.' And her daughter was healed at that very moment."
RVR1960: "Oh mujer, grande es tu fe; hágase contigo como quieres. Y su hija fue sanada desde aquella hora."

**Mark 7:24–30** is the same episode (Mark calls her "a Gentile, a Syrophoenician by birth" / CJB: "a Greek, by birth a Syro-phoenician" — explicit Gentile identification Matthew doesn't state as plainly). Same structure: the "children's bread... dogs" boundary language, her reply, the healing granted "for this statement" (Mark) — again, an exception inside a stated boundary, not evidence the boundary didn't exist.

**Reading discipline:** these two passages are the single strongest textual check against overstating the Israel-only reading — a real Gentile receives real faith-commendation and real healing from Yeshua Himself, during the explicitly Israel-focused phase. Equally, they're not evidence the boundary was fictional — Yeshua states it as the reason He initially declines, twice, in His own words. Both things are true in the text at the same time.

### The Roman centurion — the nations pictured entering Israel's story, not replacing it
Matthew 8:5–13 (already in [[05-scripture-index]]) has the sharpest version of this: "with no one in Israel have I found such faith" (ESV), immediately followed by **Matthew 8:11**: "many will come from east and west and recline at table with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven." Notice what this does *not* say — it doesn't say the nations replace Israel's covenant story or start a separate one. They're pictured entering the existing one, reclining with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob specifically. Directly relevant when this investigation eventually reaches Romans 11's olive tree (already cited, [[01-convictions]] A.16) — same shape, stated earlier and more concretely than the metaphor.

### The post-resurrection turn
**Luke 24:44–45** — before the more familiar "go to all nations" language (24:46–49, already in [[05-scripture-index]]), Yeshua reframes the whole Hebrew Scriptures as pointing to Himself and opens the disciples' minds to understand them. This precedes and grounds the nations-language that follows in the same passage — the expansion is presented as the *fulfillment* of "the Law of Moses and the Prophets and the Psalms," not a departure from them.

**Matthew 28:18–20** (already in [[05-scripture-index]]) — the Greek behind "all nations" is **πάντα τὰ ἔθνη** (*panta ta ethnē*). *Ethnē* can mean nations, Gentiles, or peoples depending on context — worth naming explicitly rather than quietly importing a modern "countries" reading (per the translation policy, ADR-0003: original language first when a question turns on exact wording). Not resolved here which sense is primary — flagged so the term itself doesn't get treated as self-evidently settling the question.

**Mark 16:17–20** — the commissioning language (already have 16:15–16) continues with confirming signs and closes: "the Lord worked with them and confirmed the message by accompanying signs." Textual note already on record in [[05-scripture-index]]: Mark 16:9–20 is absent from some earliest manuscripts — worth holding that caveat on this extended section too.

**Acts 1:6–7** — immediately before the "ends of the earth" commission (1:8, already cited), the disciples ask if this is when the kingdom is restored *to Israel*. Yeshua doesn't correct the Israel-frame — He says the timing isn't theirs to know. The Israel-restoration expectation is still live in the disciples' minds at the moment of commissioning; the "ends of the earth" language in the very next verse doesn't erase it, it sits right next to it. **Acts 1:8's own sequence** — "Jerusalem and... all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth" — maps onto the literary structure of Acts itself (Jerusalem-focused chapters, then Judea/Samaria, then the wider Gentile mission) — probably not accidental structure, the book's own shape narrates the expansion rather than just asserting it.

### Where the boundary is explicitly and formally lifted
**Acts 10** (Cornelius) is the turning point, not a single "nations" proof-text. Peter needed a vision first — worth noting plainly: if Matthew 28 already meant, to the apostles, "go indiscriminately evangelize Gentiles everywhere," a vision wouldn't have been necessary. That the expansion required divine correction to Peter specifically indicates it wasn't obvious or already-understood, even to an apostle who'd heard the Great Commission directly. Peter's own account of the social boundary being crossed:
**Acts 10:28** — ESV: "You yourselves know how unlawful it is for a Jew to associate with or to visit anyone of another nation, but God has shown me that I should not call any person common or unclean." / CJB: "for a man who is a Jew to have close association with someone who belongs to another people... is something that just isn't done. But God has shown me not to call any person common or unclean." / RVR1960: "cuán abominable es para un varón judío juntarse o acercarse a un extranjero; pero a mí me ha mostrado Dios que a ningún hombre llame común o inmundo."

Then Peter's stated realization:
Acts 10:34–35 — ESV: "Truly I understand that God shows no partiality, but in every nation anyone who fears him and does what is right is acceptable to him." / CJB: "God does not play favorites, but... whoever fears him and does what is right is acceptable to him, no matter what people he belongs to." / RVR1960: "Dios no hace acepción de personas, sino que en toda nación se agrada del que le teme y hace justicia."

Then the confirming sign, matching Acts 2's Pentecost pattern:
Acts 10:44–48 — ESV: "the Holy Spirit fell on all who heard the word... the gift of the Holy Spirit was poured out **even** on the Gentiles... Can anyone withhold water for baptizing these people, who have received the Holy Spirit just as we have?" / CJB: "the Ruach HaKodesh fell on all who were hearing the message... the gift of the Ruach HaKodesh was **also** being poured out on the Goyim." / RVR1960: "el Espíritu Santo cayó sobre todos los que oían el discurso... también sobre los gentiles se derramase el don del Espíritu Santo." That word "even"/"also" carries real narrative weight — the Jewish believers with Peter are described as astonished. This wasn't instantaneously obvious to everyone the moment Yeshua ascended; the text itself records genuine surprise.

**Acts 13:46–47** — Paul and Barnabas state the turn explicitly, as a decision made in response to rejection, and ground it in Isaiah: "we are turning to the Gentiles... 'I have made you a light for the Gentiles, that you may bring salvation to the ends of the earth.'" (ESV) The Gentile mission is framed as prophetic fulfillment (Isaiah), not innovation.

**Acts 15** (the Jerusalem council) is where the church formally settles the question rather than leaving it to individual apostolic precedent. Peter's speech recalls Cornelius directly:
Acts 15:7–11 — ESV: "God, who knows the heart, bore witness to them, by giving them the Holy Spirit just as he did to us, and he made no distinction between us and them, having cleansed their hearts by faith." / CJB: "he made no distinction between us and them, but cleansed their heart by trust." / RVR1960: "ninguna diferencia hizo entre nosotros y ellos, purificando por la fe sus corazones."

James's ruling, the actual formal decision:
Acts 15:19–20 — ESV: "we should not trouble those of the Gentiles who turn to God, but should write to them to abstain from the things polluted by idols, and from sexual immorality, and from what has been strangled, and from blood." / CJB and RVR1960 track closely — see [[05-scripture-index]] for full text.

### Paul's explicit self-description
Already established in [[01-convictions]] A.16 (Acts 9:15, Galatians 2:7–9, Romans 11:13, Romans 15:15–16). Adding here:
**Romans 1:16** — ESV: "the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes, to the Jew first and also to the Greek." / CJB: "to the Jew especially, but equally to the Gentile." / RVR1960: "al judío primeramente, y también al griego."

This verse is the clearest single statement of the *order without exclusion* pattern that runs through the whole investigation: Jew first, then Greek/Gentile — not Jew-only, and not a flat "no distinction" that erases the historical sequence either.

## 2. Covenant Continuity — Not a Pauline Invention

The stronger claim (already partly established in [[01-convictions]] A.12, A.16, and [[00b-biblical-epistemology]]'s worked example) restated with the full source list assembled: **the inclusion of the nations in the Messiah is not something Paul introduced — he's developing a thread already present in the Hebrew Scriptures.**

Sources already fully cited (CJB+ESV, and now RVR1960 pending in the backfill pass):
- Ruth 1:16, Ruth 4:13–17 — a Moabite grafted into Israel's line, into David's own genealogy
- Genesis 41:45, 50–52 — Joseph marries Asenath, an Egyptian
- Exodus 2:16–22 — Moses marries Zipporah, a Midianite
- Ezekiel 37:22 — restoration into one nation, one king
- Romans 11:17, Ephesians 2:11–22, Galatians 3:28–29, John 10:16 — the New Testament's own explicit framing of this as continuity, not invention

**What Acts 15 adds to this list specifically:** the Jerusalem council's ruling wasn't "Gentiles must become ethnically/ritually Israel first" — it was a minimal set of requirements (15:19–20), explicitly reasoned as *not* placing on Gentile believers "a yoke... neither our fathers nor we have been able to bear" (15:10, Peter's speech). That's a real, textual data point for how the earliest apostolic leadership itself drew the Israel/nations line — worth weighing directly rather than assuming either "Gentiles become Jews" or "Gentiles are unrelated to Israel" without checking the actual ruling.

## Acts 15 as a Discernment Methodology Case Study, Not Just a Passage About Gentiles

Worth flagging on its own: the Jerusalem council doesn't just settle a policy question — it's a live example of [[00b-biblical-epistemology]]'s Model B cycle operating inside Scripture itself, not just as a method this vault applies *to* Scripture. The council had: **Scripture** (the prophets, cited explicitly — Amos, per Acts 15:16–18, not yet quoted here), **testimony** (Peter and then Paul/Barnabas reporting what they'd witnessed), **observed action of the Spirit** (the Cornelius precedent, Gentiles visibly receiving the Ruach before any ritual requirement was imposed), and **apostolic deliberation** ("after there had been much debate," 15:7, already cited above). They encountered something unexpected (Gentiles receiving the Spirit), examined testimony, compared it with Scripture, deliberated together, discerned what the Spirit was doing, and reached a conclusion without claiming to have understood everything perfectly from the start. That's Encounter → Classify → Seek → Examine → Compare → Test → Discern → Hold (provisionally, since Acts 15's ruling is itself later discussed further, e.g. in Galatians) — the same shape, demonstrated inside the text rather than just applied to it.

## 3. Does the Commission Apply to Every Individual Believer? Distinguishing the Categories

The question this whole investigation was originally trying to answer, addressed last and deliberately — not first, per the method above. Neither shortcut survives: "Matthew 28 says go, therefore every Christian must personally proselytize" has to be demonstrated, not assumed from the verse alone; "that commission belonged only to the apostles, with no continuing application" also has to be demonstrated, not assumed as the safe default. What the text actually gives, once categories stop getting collapsed into each other:

**Ephesians 4:11–12** — distinct roles named, not identical assignments: ESV: "he gave the apostles, the prophets, the evangelists, the shepherds and teachers, to equip the saints for the work of ministry." / CJB: "he gave some people as emissaries, some as prophets, some as proclaimers of the Good News, and some as shepherds and teachers." / RVR1960: "a unos, apóstoles; a otros, profetas; a otros, evangelistas; a otros, pastores y maestros."

**1 Corinthians 12:4–7, 27–30** — the same principle stated explicitly as rhetorical questions: ESV: "Are all apostles? Are all prophets? Are all teachers?... to each is given the manifestation of the Spirit for the common good." / CJB: "Not all are emissaries, are they? Not all are prophets, are they?" / RVR1960: "¿Son todos apóstoles?, ¿son todos profetas?" — different gifts, different functions, explicitly not identical across all believers.

**2 Timothy 4:5** — but Timothy specifically, individually, is told: ESV: "do the work of an evangelist, fulfill your ministry." / CJB: "do the work that a proclaimer of the Good News should." / RVR1960: "haz obra de evangelista, cumple tu ministerio." A specific charge to a specific person, not stated as generic to all believers in the letter's own terms.

**1 Peter 3:15** — a different, broader category — not evangelism as a role, but readiness to answer: ESV: "always being prepared to make a defense to anyone who asks you for a reason for the hope that is in you." / CJB: "remaining always ready to give a reasoned answer to anyone who asks you to explain the hope you have in you." / RVR1960: "estad siempre preparados para presentar defensa... ante todo el que os demande razón de la esperanza."

**Matthew 5:14–16** — conduct as witness, not proclamation: ESV: "let your light shine before others, so that they may see your good works and give glory to your Father." / CJB: "let your light shine before people, so that they may see the good things you do and praise your Father." / RVR1960: "alumbre vuestra luz delante de los hombres, para que vean vuestras buenas obras."

**Colossians 4:5–6** — wise conduct and gracious speech specifically toward outsiders: ESV: "Walk in wisdom toward outsiders... Let your speech always be gracious, seasoned with salt, so that you may know how you ought to answer each person." / CJB: "Behave wisely toward outsiders... let your conversation always be gracious." / RVR1960: "Andad sabiamente para con los de afuera... Sea vuestra palabra siempre con gracia."

**What this establishes:** apostolic mission (Matthew 28, the Twelve, Paul's specific commission) ≠ the spiritual gift of evangelism (Ephesians 4, not given to all per 1 Corinthians 12) ≠ a specific individual charge (2 Timothy 4:5, addressed to Timothy) ≠ readiness to answer when asked (1 Peter 3:15) ≠ righteous, visible conduct (Matthew 5:14–16, Colossians 4:5–6). These may overlap in a given believer's life — but they are not automatically the same biblical category, and "Matthew 28 was addressed to the apostles" doesn't collapse the other four into non-existence, any more than it expands automatically into "therefore every believer has an identical evangelistic mandate." Which specific claim is actually warranted for which category remains genuinely open — this section distinguishes the categories being conflated, it doesn't yet fully resolve what obligation (if any) attaches to each for a believer today.

## What This Investigation Establishes (and Doesn't)

Passes the 7-step test ([[00b-biblical-epistemology]]):
- The nations were never outside Yahweh's stated intention — Abraham's own call already includes "all the families of the earth" (Genesis 12:3), and Isaiah 49:6 (quoted directly by Paul and Barnabas in Acts 13:47) names Israel-restoration-alone as "too light a thing." Israel's election and the blessing of the nations are the same promise from the start, not competing claims reconciled later.
- Yeshua's earthly ministry and the Twelve's initial sending were genuinely, explicitly Israel-focused — not a technicality to explain away. John the Baptist's ministry was likewise internal to Israel, calling Israel to repentance.
- Gentile faith and inclusion were anticipated and enacted *within* that same ministry (the Canaanite/Syrophoenician woman, the Roman centurion), not absent until Paul — and pictured as entering Israel's existing story (reclining with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob), not replacing it.
- The formal turn to the Gentiles happens at a specific, narratable point (Cornelius, Acts 10 — which required a vision to overcome a real, stated social boundary, and produced genuine astonishment) and gets institutionally ratified (Acts 15, itself a live demonstration of the epistemological cycle operating inside Scripture).
- Paul's Gentile apostleship is explicit self-testimony, repeated across multiple letters, not a single proof-text.
- The whole sequence sits inside covenant continuity running from Genesis through Ruth, Joseph, Moses, the prophets, into Ephesians and Galatians — "grafted in" (Romans 11:17) is the New Testament's own metaphor for this, not an outside imposition.
- The apostolic-mission-to-individual-application question is now properly categorized (Section 3) — apostolic mission, spiritual gift of evangelism, individual charge, readiness to answer, and visible conduct are distinguished as five different biblical categories, not collapsed into one.

Does **not** yet fully establish:
- Which specific obligation, if any, attaches to which of Section 3's five categories for a believer today — the categories are distinguished, the application question within each isn't fully resolved.
- How this affects the preterist material specifically — deliberately deferred; this investigation was done *before* returning to preterism, per the stated sequencing.
