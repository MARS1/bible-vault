# "Coming on the Clouds" — What Did the Prophets Mean?

**The pressure point.** To a modern reader, "coming on the clouds" sounds immediately like a description of Yeshua physically traveling downward through the atmosphere. Before assigning that meaning, the method requires the prior question:

> **What did "coming with/on the clouds" already mean inside the Scriptures Yeshua and His audience knew?**

## 1. Daniel 7 — and the Direction of Travel

Yeshua is clearly drawing on **Daniel 7:13–14** (already in [[05-scripture-index]] and [[06-revelation-19-22-timeline]]): "one like a son of man, **coming with the clouds of heaven**."

**But notice where he is going.** The Son of Man is not described descending from heaven to earth. He comes **to the Ancient of Days** and is presented before Him — then receives dominion, glory, a kingdom, and the service of peoples, nations, and languages.

The movement in Daniel 7 is:
**Son of Man → clouds → Ancient of Days → enthronement/kingdom**
not:
**Son of Man → clouds → physical descent to earth.**

**Biblical observation:** Daniel's Son of Man comes with clouds *into the presence of the Ancient of Days* and receives dominion.
**Interpretive implication:** when Yeshua uses Danielic cloud language about Himself, bodily descent cannot be *assumed* as the meaning. The passage He is quoting is fundamentally an **enthronement and vindication scene**.

This does not settle every New Testament "coming" passage. It settles what the source passage is doing.

**Daniel 7:22** — and the wider vision's sequence matters: ESV: "until the Ancient of Days came, and **judgment was given for the saints of the Most High**, and the time came when the saints possessed the kingdom." CJB: "the time came for the holy ones to take over the kingdom." RVR1960: "los santos recibieron el reino."

Daniel's order: beastly kingdoms → heavenly court sits → judgment → Son of Man vindicated → saints vindicated → kingdom. **Daniel 7 is one of Revelation's primary scriptural reservoirs** — this sequence will matter there.

## 2. Clouds Already Carry Meaning — the Tanakh Vocabulary

**Presence and glory:**
**Exodus 13:21** — Yahweh leads Israel by day in a pillar of cloud.
**Exodus 19:9** — ESV: "Behold, **I am coming to you in a thick cloud**, that the people may hear when I speak with you." CJB: "I am coming to you in a thick cloud." RVR1960: "yo vengo a ti en una nube espesa."
**Exodus 24:15–18** — the cloud covers Sinai; Yahweh's glory rests on the mountain.
**Exodus 40:34–35** — ESV: "the cloud covered the tent of meeting, and **the glory of the Lord filled the tabernacle**. And Moses was not able to enter." RVR1960: "una nube cubrió el tabernáculo de reunión, y la gloria de Jehová llenó el tabernáculo."

**Divine intervention and deliverance:**
**Psalm 18:9–12** — ESV: "He **bowed the heavens and came down**; thick darkness was under his feet. He rode on a cherub and flew; he came swiftly on the wings of the wind." CJB: "He lowered heaven and came down with thick darkness under his feet." RVR1960: "Inclinó los cielos, y descendió."
Psalm 18 is David's poetic description of Yahweh's **historical** deliverance from his enemies — heavens bowing, darkness, thunder, cosmic disturbance, describing real rescue in real history.

**Judgment — and this is the load-bearing control:**
**Isaiah 19:1** — ESV: "An oracle concerning Egypt. Behold, **the Lord is riding on a swift cloud and comes to Egypt**; and the idols of Egypt will tremble at his presence." CJB: "Look! Adonai is riding a swift cloud, on his way to Egypt." RVR1960: "Jehová monta sobre una ligera nube, y entrará en Egipto."

Ask the plain historical question: did Egyptians observe Yahweh physically riding through the atmosphere on a cloud? The prophecy describes **Yahweh coming in judgment against Egypt**, historically realized through events on earth.

**Nahum 1:3** — ESV: "The Lord is slow to anger and great in power... **His way is in whirlwind and storm, and the clouds are the dust of his feet.**"

**The assembled vocabulary:**
| Text | Cloud imagery signifies |
|---|---|
| Exodus 13, 19, 24, 40 | presence, glory, revelation |
| Psalm 18 | divine intervention, deliverance |
| Isaiah 19 | **judgment against a nation** |
| Nahum 1:3 | judgment, majesty |
| Daniel 7 | Son of Man's enthronement and vindication |

> **Prose:** clouds in Scripture are not meteorological decoration. They are theological vocabulary — and the vocabulary was fully available to a first-century Jewish audience long before Yeshua opened His mouth on the Mount of Olives. **What this establishes is not that preterism is correct. It establishes that "cloud coming can only mean bodily descent" is a claim the Tanakh does not permit.** Isaiah 19 alone forecloses it: Scripture itself can say Yahweh comes on a cloud when the fulfillment is judgment worked out through historical events.

## 3. Matthew 24:29–30, Read Against That Vocabulary

**Matthew 24:29–30** — ESV: "the sun will be darkened, and the moon will not give its light, and the stars will fall from heaven, and the powers of the heavens will be shaken. Then will appear in heaven the sign of the Son of Man, and then **all the tribes of the earth** will mourn, and they will see the Son of Man **coming on the clouds of heaven** with power and great glory."

**A translation datapoint worth flagging.** ESV and RVR1960 render *gē* as "the earth" / "la tierra." **CJB renders it "all the tribes of the Land"** — capital-L, i.e. the land of Israel. That is a translation tradition making an explicit interpretive judgment about scope, in the same direction the *oikoumenē* evidence in [[00g-matthew-24-and-the-oikoumene]] pointed. Not proof; a witness worth recording, and consistent with ADR-0003's lexical-precision extension (*gē* = earth/land/region by context).

An audience saturated in the Tanakh could hear Daniel 7 (vindication and enthronement), Isaiah 19 (judgment), and Exodus (presence and glory) simultaneously. **The modern reader's instinct toward a single visual meaning is the assumption; the Tanakh vocabulary is the evidence.**

## 4. The Trial — Yeshua's Own Use of Daniel

This may matter more than Matthew 24 itself.

**Matthew 26:64** — ESV: "You have said so. But I tell you, **from now on you will see** the Son of Man seated at the right hand of Power and coming on the clouds of heaven." RVR1960: "**desde ahora veréis** al Hijo del Hombre sentado a la diestra del poder de Dios, y viniendo en las nubes del cielo." *(Note: CJB renders it "one day you will see," a different reading of the temporal phrase — worth recording, since the translations diverge exactly where the timing question lives.)*

**Mark 14:62** — ESV: "'I am, and **you will see** the Son of Man seated at the right hand of Power, and coming with the clouds of heaven.'"

**Yeshua is combining two texts:** **Psalm 110:1** ("The Lord says to my Lord: 'Sit at my right hand, until I make your enemies your footstool'") with **Daniel 7:13**.

**And note the audience:** the high priest and the men judging Him. He is telling His own judges that *they* will see this.

> **Prose:** the irony is almost unbearable. Yeshua stands in their court, bound and apparently powerless, and tells the men condemning Him that the real court is elsewhere — that He will be seated at the right hand of Power and vindicated in Danielic terms. The accused is telling the judges they are about to discover He is the King. Whatever else "coming on the clouds" means in that sentence, its immediate rhetorical force is **vindication and enthronement**, spoken to a specific first-century audience about themselves.

### On "you will see" — handled honestly, not by redefinition
Someone can fairly object: *He said they would **see** Him.* That word should not be erased.

Biblical "seeing" can describe **recognizing or experiencing a manifestation** rather than optical observation of the event itself — one can "see" salvation or "see" judgment in the sense of witnessing its historical outworking. A preterist reading generally takes Yeshua's opponents as seeing His vindication through the resurrection, the establishment of His assembly, and judgment on the covenant order that rejected Him. A futurist reading takes it as literal future visual manifestation.

**Both must be tested from context. Do not settle it by redefining "see."** That would be exactly the move this framework exists to catch, performed in our own favor.

### The Matthew 24 ↔ Matthew 26 comparison
Same Gospel, same author, same speaker, same Danielic language — but in 26:64 addressed to His judges as people who will see it. That poses a real question for any reading that pushes *all* cloud-coming language exclusively thousands of years past those listeners:

> **Why does Yeshua address His own judges as those who will see this vindication?**

Not proof. Significant evidence.

## 5. Revelation 1:7 and Its Tanakh Sources

**Revelation 1:7** — ESV: "Behold, **he is coming with the clouds**, and every eye will see him, **even those who pierced him**, and all tribes of the earth will wail on account of him." **CJB: "all the tribes of the Land will mourn him"** — again "the Land." RVR1960: "todos los linajes de la tierra harán lamentación por él."

"Those who pierced him" raises the same temporal question: how do those responsible for the piercing participate in seeing this? Live options include literal resurrection for a future visible coming; representative/corporate language; first-century covenantal judgment; other readings. **Not decided here.** But the recurring association between *those who rejected/pierced Messiah* and *cloud-coming vindication* is now on record across Matthew 26, Mark 14, and Revelation 1.

**Underneath it: Zechariah 12:10** — ESV: "when they look on me, **on him whom they have pierced**, they shall mourn for him, as one mourns for an only child." CJB: "they will look to me, whom they pierced." RVR1960: "mirarán a mí, a quien traspasaron."

John is weaving Daniel and Zechariah together. Revelation's imagery did not appear from nowhere — it is saturated in the Tanakh.

> **Methodological rule for the Revelation study (new, 2026-08-10):** **Before assigning a modern literal meaning to an image in Revelation, first identify where John obtained the image from the Hebrew Scriptures and how it functioned there.** This is not a preterist rule — it is a reading rule any interpretation should survive. Recorded here and applied prospectively to [[06-revelation-19-22-timeline]].

> ### 📖 Exegetical Sidebar — "Literal" Does Not Mean "Physicalistic"
> *(Layer: Exegetical Sidebar, per ADR-0006.)*
>
> Calling prophetic language "symbolic" does not mean saying the events it describes are unreal. Isaiah's picture of Yahweh riding a cloud into Egypt is symbolic imagery describing **real judgment**. Psalm 18 uses cosmic imagery to describe **real deliverance**. Daniel's beasts symbolically represent **real kingdoms**. Biblical symbolism frequently *intensifies* historical reality rather than replacing it.
>
> **What this establishes:** the interpretive alternatives are not "literal means real" versus "symbolic means imaginary." A symbol can describe something entirely real while communicating theological meaning beyond a photographic description of the event.
>
> **What it does not establish:** that every image in prophecy is symbolic, or that symbolic readings are automatically preferable. Each image still requires its own case.
>
> Essential to settle before Revelation, in both directions.

## 6. What This Establishes — and What It Doesn't

**Strong biblical observations:**
- Cloud imagery repeatedly signifies Yahweh's presence, glory, authority, and judgment.
- Daniel 7 depicts the Son of Man coming with clouds **to the Ancient of Days**, receiving kingdom authority.
- Yeshua applies Danielic cloud language to Himself, combined with Psalm 110:1.
- Yeshua tells His first-century judges they will see it (Matthew 26:64, Mark 14:62).
- Matthew 24:30 uses the same Son-of-Man/cloud imagery.

**Strong interpretive conclusion:**
> **"Coming on the clouds" cannot automatically be equated with physical descent through the sky.** Scripture uses the imagery more broadly than that. This holds regardless of whether one ultimately accepts preterism.

**Reasonable preterist hypothesis:** Matthew 24:30 may describe Yeshua's vindication, enthronement, and covenantal judgment, historically manifested in the events surrounding Jerusalem's judgment.

**Remaining open:** does Matthew 24:30 mean *only* that? Or does its language also anticipate some further manifestation? **Not established. Keep open.**

## 7. A Guardrail for Hypothesis B2

[[00g-matthew-24-and-the-oikoumene]] logged Hypothesis B2 — that part of Matthew 24 might reach the postmillennial "little season." This section tightens the guardrail:

If Matthew 24's cloud language already has a plausible first-century, Tanakh-grounded reading, there is **less** reason to push it into Revelation 20's little season merely because both concern judgment. **Compatibility is not identity** — the same principle from [[00b-biblical-epistemology]]'s Worked Example 2.

The clean order: understand Matthew 24 on its own terms → understand Revelation 20 on its own terms → *then* ask whether they overlap. Not: notice both involve judgment → assume connection.

## Manuscript Voice

> **Prose:** I had unconsciously inherited an assumption that "coming on the clouds" had only one possible meaning: Yeshua visibly descending through the sky. Yet when I followed the phrase backward through Scripture, the assumption became harder to maintain. Yahweh rides a cloud into Egypt in Isaiah, makes clouds the dust of His feet in Nahum, descends in cloud at Sinai, and appears amid cosmic imagery in the Psalms. Daniel then sees the Son of Man coming with clouds — not downward to earth, but toward the Ancient of Days to receive dominion. The question therefore changed. Instead of asking, "How could Matthew 24 have happened if Yeshua did not visibly descend from the sky?" I first had to ask, "Why did I assume that was what the biblical phrase meant?"
>
> This is one of the purposes of exegesis: not merely discovering answers, but discovering assumptions hidden inside the questions themselves.
