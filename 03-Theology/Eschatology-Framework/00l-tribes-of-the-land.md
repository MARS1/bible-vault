# "All the Tribes of the Earth" — or "of the Land"?

**Matthew 24:30** — ESV: "then **all the tribes of the earth** will mourn, and they will see the Son of Man coming on the clouds of heaven." **CJB: "all the tribes of the Land will mourn."** RVR1960: "lamentarán todas las tribus de la tierra."

Sounds all-encompassing in English. The method asks two questions first: **what word is being translated, and what passage is Yeshua drawing from?**

**γῆ (*gē*)** — earth, land, ground, country, region, by context (ADR-0003's lexical extension). So "all the tribes of the **land**" is a legitimate rendering, not a special-pleading one — and CJB already prints it that way.

**φυλαί (*phylai*)** — tribes. Usable broadly, but carrying natural Israelite covenantal resonance.

Two terms, both pointing the same direction. Not proof. Contextually significant.

## 1. The Phrase Reaches Back to Zechariah 12

**Zechariah 12:10** (already in [[05-scripture-index]]) — "they will look on me, on him whom they have **pierced**, and they shall **mourn** for him."

**Zechariah 12:11–12** — ESV: "On that day the mourning in Jerusalem will be as great as the mourning for Hadad-rimmon in the plain of Megiddo. **The land shall mourn, each family by itself.**" CJB: "Then **the land will mourn, each family by itself**." RVR1960: "Y **la tierra lamentará, cada linaje aparte**."

And Zechariah names the families specifically — the house of David, the house of Nathan, the house of Levi, the family of the Shimeites, and all the families that remain.

| Zechariah 12 | Matthew 24:30 |
|---|---|
| the pierced one | the Son of Man |
| **the land** mourns | tribes of the **gē** mourn |
| **each family by itself** | **all the tribes** |

Yeshua's phrase is not floating free. It appears to draw on **Zechariah's mourning of the families of the land in connection with the pierced one** — which is considerably more specific than every ethnic group on the earth mourning simultaneously.

## 2. Revelation 1:7 Makes the Combination Explicit

**Revelation 1:7** (already in [[05-scripture-index]]) — "he is coming with the clouds, and every eye will see him, **even those who pierced him**, and all tribes of the earth will wail." **CJB: "all the tribes of the Land will mourn him."**

John is deliberately fusing two sources:
- **Daniel 7** → coming with clouds
- **Zechariah 12** → the pierced one + mourning tribes/families

**Revelation is telling us how to read its own imagery.** That is the Intertextual Priority rule ([[00b-biblical-epistemology]]) operating with the text's own cooperation rather than against its grain.

## 3. Who Are "Those Who Pierced Him"?

Handled carefully, because the simple answer is wrong in both directions.

Yeshua was crucified under Roman authority — so "only Jews pierced Him" is historically false. But the New Testament also assigns covenant responsibility to specific first-century groups within Israel while naming Roman participation:

**Acts 2:23** — ESV: "this Jesus, delivered up according to the definite plan and foreknowledge of God, **you crucified and killed by the hands of lawless men**." CJB: "through the agency of persons not bound by the Torah, **you** nailed him up on a stake." RVR1960: "prendisteis y matasteis por manos de inicuos." *(Peter addressing an Israelite audience: "you" — with Gentile hands as the instrument.)*

**Acts 4:27** — ESV: "in this city there were gathered together against your holy servant Jesus... **both Herod and Pontius Pilate, along with the Gentiles and the peoples of Israel**." CJB: "Herod and Pontius Pilate, with Goyim and the peoples of Isra'el." RVR1960: "Herodes y Poncio Pilato, con los gentiles y el pueblo de Israel."

**So "those who pierced Him" has genuinely representative scope** — both Israelite leadership and Gentile authority. Yet Zechariah's own frame (land + families + mourning) keeps covenantal Israel central to the *mourning* imagery specifically.

## 4. A Discipline That Must Be Kept: *gē* Does Not Always Mean Israel

**No new rigid dictionary.** The framework has refused this twice already (stars, clouds) and must refuse it here.

**Matthew 5:18** — ESV: "until **heaven and earth** pass away, not an iota, not a dot, will pass from the Law." CJB: "until heaven and earth pass away, not so much as a *yud* or a stroke will pass from the Torah." RVR1960: "hasta que pasen el cielo y la tierra."

Here *gē* is plainly broader than Judea. So the question is never *"can gē mean land?"* — it can. The question is:

> **What does it most likely mean in this particular passage, given its immediate context and its Zechariah background?**

And because Matthew 24 sits inside a Jerusalem/Temple judgment discourse ([[00g-matthew-24-and-the-oikoumene]]) and echoes Zechariah's mourning of the land, **"land" deserves serious consideration** — not automatic adoption.

*(Matthew 5:18's "heaven and earth pass away" is itself the next investigation — see below.)*

## 5. "Every Eye Will See Him" — Three Readings, None Chosen

Revelation 1:7's "every eye" should not be minimized. But note the qualifier attached in the same breath: "**even those who pierced him**."

- **Futurist:** every human literally sees Yeshua at a future bodily return, including resurrected persons connected to the crucifixion.
- **Preterist:** "every eye" is prophetic universal language for the public manifestation of Messiah's vindication and judgment, especially on the covenant generation that rejected Him.
- **Layered:** a first-century manifestation anticipating a larger final fulfillment.

**Not settled by assertion.** The comparison required: Daniel 7 · Zechariah 12 · Matthew 24 · Matthew 26 · Revelation 1.

**And Matthew 26:64 keeps pressing** ([[00h-coming-on-the-clouds]]): Yeshua tells the high priest **"you will see"** — addressing His own judges. That doesn't require the high priest to physically witness every aspect. It does establish that Messiah's vindication is directed toward, and relevant to, **His first-century judges**. Revelation then says "even those who pierced Him." That pattern recurs across two books and should be weighed rather than explained away.

## 6. Is the Mourning Grief or Terror?

Zechariah's mourning is not purely hostile — there is sorrow, recognition, lament. Zechariah 12:10 pairs it with "a spirit of grace and pleas for mercy."

So a real question the text doesn't settle: **is the mourning in Matthew 24:30 the grief of repentance, the terror of judgment, or both depending on who is mourning?** Some may mourn because they recognize the Messiah they rejected; others because judgment has arrived.

**That ambiguity is worth preserving rather than resolving toward whichever reading is more convenient.**

## 7. Four Buckets

**Biblical observation:** Matthew 24:30 uses *phylai* + *gē*. Zechariah 12 has families/tribes mourning in the land over the pierced one. Revelation 1:7 fuses cloud-coming with the pierced-one motif. Matthew 26:64 addresses Yeshua's first-century judges with Danielic cloud language.
**Historical observation:** Jerusalem and the Temple were destroyed within that generation. Yeshua's execution involved both Jewish leadership and Roman authority.
**Interpretation:** Matthew 24:30 may describe covenantal mourning and judgment centered on the land of Israel in connection with Messiah's vindication.
**Open question:** does the imagery stop there, or also anticipate a later universal manifestation? **Kept open.**

## Manuscript Voice

> **Prose:** The phrase "all the tribes of the earth" initially sounded universal in the modern geographical sense. Yet the Greek word *gē* can mean either earth or land, and the expression points back to Zechariah 12, where the land mourns family by family over the one who was pierced. Revelation then combines the same pierced-one prophecy with Daniel's cloud-coming imagery. Once those texts were placed beside one another, the question became less simple. Was Yeshua describing every nation on earth mourning simultaneously, or the tribes of the covenant land confronting the vindication of the Messiah they had rejected? The English word "earth" had concealed an exegetical question that the Greek and the prophets reopened.
>
> Again, the discovery did not prove a preterist reading by itself. It removed another assumption. **Translation can settle a question for the reader before the reader realizes there was a question to begin with.** Returning to the underlying language does not automatically produce the correct interpretation, but it exposes interpretive decisions that translation can hide.

That last point is a major epistemological claim and applies well beyond this verse — it is the strongest single argument for ADR-0003's original-language priority, arrived at from the inside rather than asserted at the start.

**Matthew 24:32–35** — the fig tree, "when you see all these things," "this generation will not pass away," and "**heaven and earth will pass away, but my words will not pass away**."

The hinge question: **does "heaven and earth passing away" necessarily mean the destruction of physical creation, or does prophetic/covenantal Scripture use heaven-and-earth language for an established order?**

Requires **Deuteronomy 32, Isaiah 51, Isaiah 65–66, Jeremiah, and Hebrews** — and it may be the single biggest hinge between partial and full preterist readings.
