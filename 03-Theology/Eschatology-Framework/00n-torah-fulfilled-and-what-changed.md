# Stage 14 — "Until Heaven and Earth Pass Away"

> *Sequence note.* This stage resumes the thread the narrative had earned at the end of [[00m-fig-tree-generation-heaven-and-earth]]. The Daniel 2 / Rupes Nigra material raised in the same session is **preserved as forward bookmarks** in [[04-open-questions]] and did **not** displace this stage. Branches get kept; they do not get to reorder the spine (ADR-0007).

## 1. The Trigger — an Intratextual Alarm

Stage 13 ended on:

> **Matthew 24:35** — ESV: "Heaven and earth will pass away, but my words will not pass away."

And an alarm should go off immediately, because **Yeshua has already used almost exactly that expression earlier in the same Gospel.**

### Matthew 5:17–19

**ESV:** "Do not think that I have come to abolish the Law or the Prophets; I have not come to abolish them but to fulfill them. For truly, I say to you, **until heaven and earth pass away**, not an iota, not a dot, will pass from the Law **until all is accomplished**. Therefore whoever relaxes one of the least of these commandments and teaches others to do the same will be called least in the kingdom of heaven, but whoever does them and teaches them will be called great in the kingdom of heaven."

**CJB:** "Don't think that I have come to abolish the Torah or the Prophets. I have come not to abolish but to **complete**. Yes indeed! I tell you that **until heaven and earth pass away**, not so much as a *yud* or a stroke will pass from the Torah — **not until everything that must happen has happened**. So whoever disobeys the least of these *mitzvot* and teaches others to do so will be called the least in the Kingdom of Heaven. But whoever obeys them and so teaches will be called great in the Kingdom of Heaven."

**RVR1960:** "No penséis que he venido para **abrogar** la ley o los profetas; no he venido para abrogar, sino para **cumplir**. Porque de cierto os digo que **hasta que pasen el cielo y la tierra**, ni una jota ni una tilde pasará de la ley, **hasta que todo se haya cumplido**."

Note CJB translates *plēroō* as "**complete**," not "fulfill" — a translation choice that already leans against "fulfill = terminate." Recorded as a translation datum, not as proof.

The right first question is not "does this prove Torah is still binding?" nor "does this prove Torah passed away?" Both are downstream theological questions. First: **what did Yeshua actually say?**

And He said **two "untils."**

---

## 2. The Two Untils — the Governing Structure

**A.** ἕως ἂν παρέλθῃ ὁ οὐρανὸς καὶ ἡ γῆ — *heōs an parelthē ho ouranos kai hē gē* — "until heaven and earth pass away"

**B.** ἕως ἂν πάντα γένηται — *heōs an panta genētai* — "until all things happen / come to pass / are accomplished"

**Methodological control, established here and binding on everything downstream:** any interpretation of this verse must account for **both** temporal markers simultaneously. Isolating A while ignoring B — or B while ignoring A — is not reading the sentence.

**The relationship between A and B is itself undetermined by the text.** At least four possibilities exist, and the verse does not choose between them:

| Reading | A and B relate how? |
|---|---|
| **Coterminous** | Both clauses mark the same moment, stated twice |
| **B defines A** | "Until heaven and earth pass away" is *explained* by "until all is accomplished" |
| **Independent** | Two separate conditions, both of which must hold |
| **Merism / emphatic** | A rhetorical way of saying "never, for as long as anything stands" |

Recording this as **undetermined** is not evasion. Choosing one silently and building on it is how eisegesis gets in.

---

## 3. "I Did Not Come to Abolish" — *katalyō*

**καταλύω — *katalyō*** — to destroy, demolish, overthrow, abolish, dismantle. Used of buildings being torn down (Matthew 24:2 uses the cognate for the Temple's stones).

Yeshua **explicitly denies** this is His mission toward — and note the object carefully —

> **"the Law *or the Prophets*."**

Not "the Law" alone. **"The Law and the Prophets"** is a conventional way of referring broadly to Israel's Scriptures.

**Consequence, and it is a real one:** reducing Matthew 5:17 to "which Mosaic regulations do Christians have to obey?" may already have narrowed the question below what Yeshua actually addressed. He is stating His relationship to **the whole scriptural story that preceded Him**, not issuing a compliance checklist.

---

## 4. "Fulfill" — *plēroō*, and Matthew's Own Usage

**πληρόω — *plēroō*** — fill, make full, fulfill, complete, bring to realization.

Matthew has already trained his reader in this verb before Yeshua ever uses it here. This is **authorial usage** — the same discipline applied to *genea* in Stage 13.

| Reference | ESV | CJB | RVR1960 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Matthew 1:22** | "All this took place **to fulfill** what the Lord had spoken by the prophet" | "All this happened in order to **fulfill** what *Adonai* had said through the prophet" | "Todo esto aconteció para que **se cumpliese** lo dicho por el Señor por medio del profeta" |
| **Matthew 2:15** | "This was **to fulfill** what the Lord had spoken by the prophet, 'Out of Egypt I called my son.'" | "This happened in order to **fulfill** what *Adonai* had said through the prophet, 'Out of Egypt I called my son.'" | "para que **se cumpliese** lo que dijo el Señor por medio del profeta... De Egipto llamé a mi Hijo." |
| **Matthew 2:17** | "Then was **fulfilled** what was spoken by the prophet Jeremiah" | "In this way were **fulfilled** the words spoken through the prophet *Yirmeyahu*" | "Entonces **se cumplió** lo que fue dicho por el profeta Jeremías" |
| **Matthew 4:14** | "so that what was spoken by the prophet Isaiah might be **fulfilled**" | "This happened in order to **fulfill** what *Yesha'yahu* the prophet had said" | "para que **se cumpliese** lo dicho por el profeta Isaías" |

So when Matthew's Yeshua says "I came not to abolish but **to fulfill**," Matthew's reader has already been taught to hear *plēroō* as **Scripture reaching its intended realization**.

**What this does NOT establish — both directions blocked:**

- ❌ *plēroō* = terminate. The verb does not carry that, and Yeshua has just **explicitly negated** the verb that does (*katalyō*). Making "fulfill" a synonym for the word He denied is not exegesis.
- ❌ *plēroō* = "absolutely nothing about covenantal administration can ever change." That is not in the verb either.

**Safest formulation the text actually supports:**

> **Yeshua presents Himself as the one in whom the Law and the Prophets reach their intended fulfillment.**

What that *entails* is now the investigation — not the assumption.

---

## 5. Two Opposite Shortcuts, Both Blocked

**Shortcut 1 — "Yeshua fulfilled the Law, therefore He abolished it."**
Blocked by the verse itself: *"I have not come to abolish."* This is not an inference from context; it is the sentence.

**Shortcut 2 — "Not abolished means nothing about Torah's covenantal administration can change."**
Blocked by the New Testament's own reporting, which describes changes involving priesthood, sacrifice, Temple, circumcision, covenant administration, access to the Holy Place, and Gentile inclusion. And by Yeshua Himself:

> **Matthew 12:6** — ESV: "I tell you, **something greater than the temple is here**." CJB: "there is in this place something greater than the Temple!" RVR1960: "uno mayor que el templo está aquí."

So something more sophisticated is happening than *Law deleted* versus *everything stays administratively identical forever*.

**This is the [[00b-biblical-epistemology]] Anti-Replacement Principle operating again** — the fifth occasion. Escaping a rigid reading does not license installing the opposite rigid reading.

## 6. Melchizedek Earns Its Place

In Stage 8 ([[00i-stars-the-dragon-and-the-cross]]) the king-priest question was raised and deliberately **not** allowed to derail Matthew 24. It has now earned entry, because the argument being made here is exactly the one Hebrews makes.

> **Psalm 110:4** — "You are a priest forever after the order of Melchizedek." *(already indexed — [[05-scripture-index]])*

### Hebrews 7:11–12

**ESV:** "Now if perfection had been attainable through the Levitical priesthood (for under it the people received the law), what further need would there have been for another priest to arise after the order of Melchizedek, rather than one named after the order of Aaron? **For when there is a change in the priesthood, there is necessarily a change in the law as well.**"

**CJB:** "Therefore, if it had been possible to reach the goal through the system of *cohanim* derived from Levi (since in connection with it, the people were given the *Torah*), what need would there have been for another, different kind of *cohen*, the one spoken of as to be compared with *Malki-Tzedek* and not to be compared with *Aharon*? **For if the system of *cohanim* is transformed, there must of necessity occur a transformation of *Torah*.**"

**RVR1960:** "Porque **cambiado el sacerdocio, necesario es que haya también cambio de ley**."

### Lexical note — and a brake

The Greek noun is **μετάθεσις — *metathesis*** (verb **μετατίθημι — *metatithēmi***): transposition, transfer, change, removal.

The word's own range does **not** settle the question:
- **Hebrews 11:5** uses *metatithēmi* of **Enoch being taken up** — transferred, not destroyed.
- **Hebrews 12:27** uses *metathesis* of the **removal** of what is shaken.

So the same author uses the word family both ways. **CJB reads "transformation"; ESV and RVR1960 read "change."** None of them reads "abolition" — but neither does the vocabulary alone prove continuity. **The word opens the range; context has to decide.** Recorded as *insufficient by itself*, per Intertextual Priority's limiting clause.

### The methodologically decisive point

Hebrews does **not** argue that Yahweh's revelation was defective and got discarded. It argues **from the Tanakh itself** — from Psalm 110 — that another priesthood **had already been anticipated**.

> The later writer is not inventing an escape hatch from Torah. He is arguing that **Torah and the Prophets themselves pointed beyond the existing Levitical arrangement.**

## 7. Jeremiah Does the Same Thing with Covenant

### Jeremiah 31:31–33

**ESV:** "Behold, the days are coming, declares the LORD, when I will make **a new covenant with the house of Israel and the house of Judah**, **not like the covenant that I made with their fathers** on the day when I took them by the hand to bring them out of the land of Egypt, my covenant that they broke, though I was their husband, declares the LORD. For this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, declares the LORD: **I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts.** And I will be their God, and they shall be my people."

**CJB:** "It will not be like the covenant I made with their fathers on the day I took them by their hand and brought them out of the land of Egypt; because they, for their part, violated my covenant... 'For this is the covenant I will make with the house of Isra'el after those days,' says *Adonai*: '**I will put my *Torah* within them and write it on their hearts**; I will be their God, and they will be my people.'"

**RVR1960:** "He aquí que vienen días, dice Jehová, en los cuales haré **nuevo pacto con la casa de Israel y con la casa de Judá**. **No como el pacto que hice con sus padres**... **Daré mi ley en su mente, y la escribiré en su corazón**; y yo seré a ellos por Dios, y ellos me serán por pueblo."

> **Versification note.** CJB follows Hebrew versification here, where ESV's 31:31 corresponds to CJB's 31:30. The CJB text above is the same passage under different numbering, not a different passage. Same category of discrepancy already recorded for Zechariah 2:6 in [[00k-the-gathering-of-the-elect]].

**Three observations, in order:**

1. **The new covenant is made *with Israel and Judah*.** Not: "I am abandoning Israel and founding an unrelated Gentile religion." This connects directly to the covenant-continuity thread established across [[00c-commission-covenant-investigation]] and [[00e-olive-tree-and-covenant-identity]].

2. **"Not like the covenant I made with their fathers"** is an *explicit* distinction. Something genuinely new is announced. This forbids flattening the covenants into one undifferentiated arrangement.

3. **And where does Torah go?** *Into them.* CJB renders it "**my Torah**" outright. The promised new covenant is simultaneously **not like the former covenant** and **Yahweh's Torah written internally**.

**Therefore Jeremiah himself blocks both reductions:**
- new covenant ≠ Torah erased
- new covenant ≠ the Mosaic covenant continuing entirely unchanged

## 8. Hebrews 8 Cashes the Bookmark — and Raises a Dating Question

Stage 13 bookmarked **Hebrews 8:13** deliberately. It is now in range, because Hebrews quotes Jeremiah 31 at length and then concludes:

> **Hebrews 8:13** — ESV: "In speaking of a new covenant, he **makes the first one obsolete**. And what is **becoming obsolete and growing old** is **ready to vanish away**." CJB: "on its way to vanishing altogether." RVR1960: "está **próximo a desaparecer**."

**Note the aspect:** *becoming* obsolete. *Growing* old. *Ready to* vanish. That is a **transition in progress**, not a completed demolition — as of the moment of writing.

And the same letter describes an operating system in the present tense:

> **Hebrews 8:4** — ESV: "Now if he were on earth, he would not be a priest at all, **since there are priests who offer gifts according to the law**." CJB: "since there **already are** *cohanim* offering the gifts required by the *Torah*." RVR1960: "**habiendo aún sacerdotes** que presentan las ofrendas según la ley."

> **Hebrews 10:1–2** — ESV: "...it can never, by the same sacrifices that **are continually offered** every year, make perfect those who draw near. Otherwise, **would they not have ceased to be offered**...?" CJB: "by means of the same sacrifices **repeated endlessly year after year**... **wouldn't the offering of those sacrifices have ceased?**"

### The historical question this raises

> **Was Hebrews written during a transitional period in which the New Covenant had already been inaugurated through Messiah, while the old Temple administration had not yet physically disappeared?**

If so, AD 70 becomes relevant again — **not** because AD 70 is being forced into everything, but because an actual institution that Hebrews describes as *aging and near disappearance* **did in fact disappear**.

### Brake — and this one is substantial

**The present-tense argument for a pre-AD-70 date is an inference, not a statement.** Hebrews never dates itself. Three genuine counter-considerations, recorded because they cut against the convenient reading:

1. **Greek present tense can describe a system generically or customarily**, not only as currently operating — the way one might describe rules of a practice without asserting it is happening at this moment.
2. **Other writers use present-tense description of Temple service after AD 70** (this is the standard scholarly objection). **Flagged for verification** — the specific citations have not been checked in primary sources by this project and must not be asserted until they are.
3. **Hebrews 8:4/10:1 describe Levitical service as defined by Torah**, which is arguably true as a description of the *law's* provision regardless of whether the altar is standing.

**Status: the pre-70 dating is a supporting historical hypothesis at "permits," not "establishes."** Building a chronology on it would be exactly the kind of move this framework has been refusing since Stage 1.

**Observation first. Interpretation second.**

## 9. Back to Matthew 5 — the Intratextual Link

Now the vocabulary lines up, and the alignment is checkable rather than impressionistic.

| Passage | Greek | English |
|---|---|---|
| **Matthew 5:18** | ἕως ἂν **πάντα γένηται** | "until **all** is accomplished" |
| **Matthew 24:34** | ἕως ἂν **πάντα ταῦτα γένηται** | "until **all these things** take place" |

**The only difference is ταῦτα — "these things."** Same conjunction, same particle, same verb, same mood. Add to that:

- **Matthew 5:18** — "until heaven and earth **pass away**" (*parelthē*)
- **Matthew 24:34** — "this generation will not **pass away**" (*parelthē*)
- **Matthew 24:35** — "heaven and earth will **pass away**... my words will not **pass away**" (*pareleusontai / parelthōsin*)

**What this establishes:** Matthew uses a tight cluster of shared vocabulary — *passing away* + *all things happening* — in both places. Ignoring that would be poor method.

**What this does NOT establish:** that Matthew 5:18 and Matthew 24:35 describe the *same event*. Shared vocabulary is a reason to ask the question, not an answer to it. A writer can reuse a phrase for a different referent.

**Verification flag:** the Greek of the Lukan parallel (Luke 21:32) has **not** been checked by this project. Do not cite it as corroboration until it has been.

## 10. New Glossary Entry — Intratextuality

**Intertextuality** — relationships **between** texts. Example: Matthew 24 ↔ Daniel 7.

**Intratextuality** — relationships **within** the same text or the same author's work. Example: Matthew 5:18 ↔ Matthew 24:35.

So the question posed in §9 is properly named:

> **Does Matthew intend his reader to hear Yeshua's earlier "until heaven and earth pass away" when Yeshua later announces "heaven and earth will pass away"?**

That is an **intratextual** question. Added to the [[00b-biblical-epistemology]] glossary.

**Why the distinction matters practically:** intratextual evidence is often *stronger* than intertextual, because it does not require assuming the later writer knew and intended the earlier source — the same author wrote both. But it is also *narrower*: it tells you about Matthew's usage, not about what Yeshua's Aramaic-speaking hearers necessarily understood.

## 11. Matthew's Narrative Sequence — Where He Placed the Phrase

This is placement evidence, not proof. Stated as such.

| Ref | Text |
|---|---|
| **Matthew 23:35** | "all the righteous blood shed on **earth/land** (*gē*)..." — from Abel onward |
| **Matthew 23:36** | "Truly, I say to you, **all these things will come upon this generation**." |
| **Matthew 23:37** | "O Jerusalem, Jerusalem..." |
| **Matthew 23:38** | "See, **your house is left to you desolate**." |
| *(narrative)* | He leaves the Temple. Disciples point out its buildings. |
| **Matthew 24:2** | "There will not be left here **one stone upon another**." |
| **Matthew 24:34** | "**this generation**..." |
| **Matthew 24:35** | "**heaven and earth will pass away**..." |

**Read the sequence:** Jerusalem → house desolate → Temple → stones thrown down → this generation → heaven and earth passing.

**At minimum:** Matthew has placed heaven-and-earth language **inside a narrative about the judgment of Jerusalem and its Temple.**

**Explicitly does NOT prove:** heaven and earth = Temple/covenant system. Adjacency is not identity — the same discipline applied to the Ezekiel 11:23 / Mount of Olives hypothesis in [[00j-cosmic-signs-and-intertextual-priority]], which was held at "supports," not "establishes."

**What it does do:** makes the question **considerably less arbitrary** than it looks when Matthew 24:35 is quoted alone. Status: **permits, strengthened by placement.** Unchanged from Stage 13's ruling.

## 12. The Six-Way Distinction — Possibly the Most Important Structural Finding So Far

Inherited as one package, but not one thing:

| # | Element | Is it identical to the others? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Torah** (revelatory instruction) | — |
| 2 | **Mosaic / Sinai covenant** (a specific covenant administration) | overlaps, not identical |
| 3 | **Levitical priesthood** | overlaps, not identical |
| 4 | **Temple** (a building and its location) | overlaps, not identical |
| 5 | **Sacrificial administration** | overlaps, not identical |
| 6 | **Yahweh's moral commandments** | overlaps, not identical |

They overlap enormously. **They are not interchangeable terms.**

**This may explain why arguments about "the Law" become irresolvable.** Someone asks:

> "Is the Law abolished?"

But **which question is actually being asked?**

- Did murder become righteous?
- Does the Levitical priesthood remain operative?
- Do animal sacrifices remain required?
- Does circumcision define covenant membership?
- Does Sabbath remain?
- Do dietary distinctions remain?
- Does the Sinai covenant remain administratively operative?
- Does Torah remain revelatory instruction?

**Those are not one question.** And answering all eight with a single slogan —

> "We're under grace, not law"

or:

> "The Torah never changes"

— **is not exegesis. It is a way of avoiding exegesis while sounding decisive.**

> **This is now a formal method rule.** See [[00b-biblical-epistemology]]: **the Category Collapse Test.**

## 13. Why Acts First Was the Right Order

This distinction was **not** constructed theoretically. It was **watched being wrestled with** in [[00c-commission-covenant-investigation]].

Gentiles receive the Ruach. Then the question arises: must they be circumcised and keep the Law of Moses to be saved?

**Acts 15 answers it. And note what the apostles do *not* say:**

- ❌ "Moses was wrong."
- ❌ "Yahweh's commandments are meaningless."
- ❌ "Gentiles must simply become ethnically Jewish."

They are navigating **covenant continuity during transformation** — precisely what Jeremiah 31 and Hebrews 7–8 would lead one to expect.

> **The earlier Acts investigation has now become evidence for reading Matthew, rather than a self-contained side study.** This is the dual-architecture (ADR-0007) paying its first real dividend: the *order* of the investigation produced an interpretive resource that a topical arrangement alone would not have generated.

## 14. Circumcision — Tracing the Expression Back Into Torah

The personal question that was deliberately deferred earlier — *if I truly love Yahweh and Yeshua, must I be physically circumcised to demonstrate obedience?* — now belongs here rather than as a tangent.

It is exactly the question produced when someone recognizes Torah's authority but **has not yet distinguished**:

- **covenant faithfulness**, from
- **the historical covenant sign administered to Abraham's physical descendants and subsequently within Israel.**

**But do not jump straight to "circumcision of the heart replaces circumcision."** Trace the expression back first — because **Paul did not invent it.**

> **Deuteronomy 10:16** — ESV: "**Circumcise therefore the foreskin of your heart**, and be no longer stubborn." CJB: "Therefore, circumcise the foreskin of your heart; and don't be stiffnecked any longer!" RVR1960: "**Circuncidad, pues, el prepucio de vuestro corazón**, y no endurezcáis más vuestra cerviz."

> **Deuteronomy 30:6** — ESV: "And the LORD your God **will circumcise your heart and the heart of your offspring**, so that you will love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul, that you may live." CJB: "Then *Adonai* your God will circumcise your hearts and the hearts of your children..." RVR1960: "Y **circuncidará Jehová tu Dios tu corazón**, y el corazón de tu descendencia..."

> **Jeremiah 4:4** — ESV: "**Circumcise yourselves to the LORD; remove the foreskin of your hearts**, O men of Judah and inhabitants of Jerusalem; lest my wrath go forth like fire..." CJB: "circumcise yourselves for *Adonai*, remove the foreskins of your heart!" RVR1960: "**Circuncidaos a Jehová, y quitad el prepucio de vuestro corazón**..."

**Note also** — Deuteronomy 30:6 sits in the *same chapter* as Deuteronomy 30:4's "uttermost parts of heaven," already load-bearing in [[00k-the-gathering-of-the-elect]]. The gathering of the scattered and the circumcision of the heart are announced in one breath.

**The Torah itself already said it.** Heart-circumcision language is **not** an anti-Torah innovation imported later. It is Torah's own vocabulary, picked up by Jeremiah, and only then developed by Paul.

**Brake:** this establishes the *source* of the language. It does **not** by itself settle whether physical circumcision was thereby set aside, retained, or re-scoped for Gentiles specifically. That is a row in the table below, not a conclusion here.

## Manuscript Voice

> **Prose:** A pattern had been repeating for long enough that it deserved to be named rather than noticed again. Again and again, ideas I had first encountered as supposedly *Christian* corrections to the Hebrew Scriptures turned out, on closer examination, to have originated **inside the Hebrew Scriptures themselves**. Gentile inclusion was already anticipated by the prophets. A new covenant was promised by Jeremiah. Circumcision of the heart appeared already in Deuteronomy, centuries before Paul was accused of inventing it. A priest beyond Levi was anticipated by Psalm 110, in a psalm attributed to David and quoted by Yeshua against His own interrogators.
>
> The apostolic writers were not, on this evidence, assembling a replacement religion in the aftermath of Yeshua's death. They were repeatedly arguing that what was happening through Messiah had **already been embedded within Israel's own Scriptures** — and arguing it to audiences who could check.
>
> This is why the phrase *one covenant story from Genesis to Revelation* keeps proving useful. But it is only useful under one condition: that the covenants themselves are allowed to retain their actual distinctions. A story is not the same thing as a flat surface. Jeremiah announced a covenant explicitly *"not like"* the one made at Sinai, and then said Yahweh's Torah would be written on hearts — in the same sentence. Whatever that is, it is neither erasure nor stasis. And I had spent years being told it had to be one or the other.

## 15. The Unresolved Problem — Stated, Not Solved

If Matthew 5:18 says not one *yud* or stroke passes from Torah **until all is accomplished** — and if someone argues that "all was accomplished" by AD 70 — **then what happens to Torah afterward?**

That is not a small question, and it must not be answered with a slogan. Because Yeshua's very next sentence blocks the casual exit:

> **Matthew 5:19** — ESV: "Therefore **whoever relaxes one of the least of these commandments and teaches others to do the same will be called least in the kingdom of heaven**, but whoever does them and teaches them will be called great in the kingdom of heaven." CJB: "So whoever **disobeys the least of these *mitzvot*** and teaches others to do so will be called the least in the Kingdom of Heaven."

**That verse prevents saying "Fulfilled. Done. Irrelevant now."**

But simultaneously:
- Paul tells Gentile believers physical circumcision is not their covenantal requirement.
- Hebrews says the priesthood changed.
- Sacrifice changes, because Messiah's sacrifice is presented as sufficient.
- Temple access changes.

**Something changed. Something did not. What?**

**That is the question this stage has earned. It is deliberately left open.**

> **Honest note on the shape of the difficulty:** this is the first point in the framework where a preterist reading creates a problem *for the reader*, rather than solving one. If "all is accomplished" landed in the first century, Matthew 5:19 has to mean something afterward — and the easy answers ("it was only for the transitional period") are exactly the kind of protective exception this project has been refusing to grant its opponents. It does not get to grant one to itself. Recorded as an unresolved cost of the position, not a footnote.

## 16. Next Investigation — the What-Changed Table

**Not** "Did Christianity replace Judaism?" **Not** "Are Christians under the Law?" Both questions arrive pre-loaded with later theological baggage.

**The cleaner question:**

> **According to Torah, the Prophets, Yeshua, Acts, and the apostolic writings themselves — what does Scripture explicitly say changed, and what does it explicitly preserve, when the New Covenant was inaugurated?**

**Method: build the table. Populate each row from Scripture. No denomination answers a row for us. Only after the table is populated do we ask what theological label fits the resulting picture.**
