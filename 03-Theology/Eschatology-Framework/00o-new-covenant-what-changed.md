# Stage 15 — What Actually Changes in the New Covenant?

> **Deliberate deceleration.** This stage does not race back to Matthew 24:36. Stage 14 uncovered a structural question that governs everything downstream, and answering it later would mean building on an unexamined assumption.

**Governing question, deliberately narrow:**

> **When Scripture speaks about the New Covenant, what does it explicitly say changes, what continues, and what remains unresolved?**

**Starting point discipline:** not "Christianity says…", not "Judaism says…", and **not Paul**. The phrase *new covenant* originates in the prophets. That is where the investigation starts. Paul enters in §16, as a witness to be interpreted rather than as the lens through which everything prior gets read.

---

## 1. Jeremiah 31:31 — the Recipients Do Not Change

> **Jeremiah 31:31** — ESV: "Behold, the days are coming, declares the LORD, when I will make **a new covenant with the house of Israel and the house of Judah**." RVR1960: "haré **nuevo pacto con la casa de Israel y con la casa de Judá**." *(CJB text and versification note at §4.)*

**First observation, and it is load-bearing:**

Jeremiah does **not** say Israel failed, therefore Yahweh abandons Israel and founds a new religion among the Gentiles. He says: **house of Israel + house of Judah → New Covenant.**

This converges with what was already established independently in [[00e-olive-tree-and-covenant-identity]] (Romans 11's olive tree) and [[00c-commission-covenant-investigation]] (Ephesians 2's "brought near"). Gentiles are not presented as establishing an unrelated covenantal tree. **That is continuity — and it was reached from a different direction, which is worth something.**

Then Jeremiah immediately supplies discontinuity.

---

## 2. "Not Like the Covenant I Made with Their Fathers" — and a Refinement

> **Jeremiah 31:32** — ESV: "**not like the covenant that I made with their fathers** on the day when I took them by the hand to bring them out of the land of Egypt, my covenant that they broke, though I was their husband."

**Explicit.** Therefore the following is ruled out by the text itself:

> ❌ New Covenant = Mosaic Covenant under a new name.

And Yahweh names *which* covenant: **the Exodus covenant.** Not covenant-in-general.

### The refinement this forces

The covenant with **Abraham** precedes Sinai. **Sinai** comes later. The **Davidic** covenant adds another dimension. The prophets promise a **New** covenant. These belong to one story. **They are not interchangeable.**

So the working phrase used earlier in this project — *"one covenant story from Genesis to Revelation"* — is **not precise enough**, and gets superseded:

> ### ✅ **One covenantal story containing multiple covenants, culminating in Messiah.**

**Why the imprecision mattered:** "one covenant" invites exactly the flattening this framework has been refusing everywhere else. It makes Sinai and the New Covenant sound like one thing described twice, which is the error Jeremiah 31:32 explicitly forbids. **Recorded as a self-correction** in [[00a-narrative-spine]]'s corrections table — the original phrasing is preserved there rather than silently overwritten (ADR-0007).

---

## 3. Why Did the Mosaic Covenant Need Transformation? Jeremiah Answers

Yahweh states the defect, and it is **not** located where later polemic often puts it:

> **Jeremiah 31:32** — "**my covenant that they broke**." RVR1960: "porque ellos **invalidaron mi pacto**."

**Nothing is wrong with Yahweh.** Jeremiah does not say the instruction was defective or wicked. **The covenant was broken by the people.**

Hebrews develops precisely this argument, and its wording is careful:

> **Hebrews 8:7–8** — ESV: "For if that first covenant had been faultless, there would have been no occasion to look for a second. For **he finds fault with them** when he says: 'Behold, the days are coming...'" CJB: "For God does find fault **with the people** when he says..." RVR1960: "Porque **reprendiéndolos** dice..."

**Note what all three translations do:** the fault-finding lands on **them / the people**, not on the covenant's content. ESV's "*if that first covenant had been faultless*" is the closest thing to a statement about the covenant itself — and even there, the follow-on clause redirects to *them*.

**Brake:** this observation should not be pressed into "the Sinai covenant had no deficiency whatsoever," because Hebrews' own argument elsewhere (7:11's "if perfection had been attainable through the Levitical priesthood") clearly attributes *some* insufficiency to the arrangement. **What the text supports:** the fault is not moral defect in Yahweh's instruction. **What it does not support:** that the arrangement was in every sense final and complete.

And Yahweh's remedy is not another external contract. **He changes something inside the people.**

## 4. "I Will Put My Torah Within Them"

> **Jeremiah 31:33** — ESV: "**I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts.** And I will be their God, and they shall be my people." CJB: "**I will put my Torah within them and write it on their hearts**; I will be their God, and they will be my people." RVR1960: "**Daré mi ley en su mente, y la escribiré en su corazón.**"

**Hebrew:**

**נָתַתִּי אֶת־תּוֹרָתִי בְּקִרְבָּם** — *natatti et-torati beqirbam* — "I will put my Torah **within them**"

**וְעַל־לִבָּם אֶכְתֲּבֶנָּה** — *ve'al-libam ekhtavennah* — "and **upon their heart** I will write it"

**What is *not* happening:** Torah → discarded. **What is happening:** Torah → **internalized**. The *locus* changes: **external/stone → internal/heart**.

Note **CJB reads "my Torah" outright**, and **RVR1960 splits the location across two faculties** — *en su mente* (in their mind) *y en su corazón* (and on their heart) — where ESV and CJB both read "within them / on their hearts."

### The brake, applied immediately

Jeremiah does **not** here give a verse-by-verse account of how each Mosaic command functions under the New Covenant. Therefore:

> **"Torah written on the heart" must not become a slogan that pre-answers Sabbath, circumcision, sacrifice, dietary law, and festivals without investigating each one.**

That would be a **Category Collapse** ([[00b-biblical-epistemology]]) wearing a Jeremiah quotation as cover. Each question gets investigated individually.

### The fourth verse — and a confirmed versification finding

> **Jeremiah 31:34** — ESV: "And **no longer shall each one teach his neighbor** and each his brother, saying, 'Know the LORD,' for **they shall all know me, from the least of them to the greatest**, declares the LORD." RVR1960: "**todos me conocerán, desde el más pequeño de ellos hasta el más grande.**"

> **📖 Versification — now confirmed, not assumed.** A direct CJB lookup of "Jeremiah 31:33" returns the text ESV numbers as **31:34** ("No longer will any of them teach his fellow community member... for all will know me, from the least of them to the greatest"). **The offset is exactly one verse**, confirming the note recorded provisionally in [[00n-torah-fulfilled-and-what-changed]] §7. A CJB citation of Jeremiah 31 in this range is one verse behind ESV/RVR1960 — check the wording, never the number alone. *(A first attempt to fetch "Jeremiah 31:34" in CJB returned the sun/moon/stars passage — i.e. ESV's 31:35 — which is what surfaced the offset in the first place. Recorded so the error is reproducible rather than mysterious.)*

> **⚠️ An honest tension, recorded not resolved.** "They shall **all** know me, from the least to the greatest" and "**no longer shall each one teach** his neighbor" are quoted approvingly by Hebrews 8 as describing the covenant being inaugurated. **Whatever else is claimed about the New Covenant's inauguration, this clause is not obviously descriptive of any observable community, then or now** — teaching plainly continued in the apostolic congregations, and Hebrews itself is a teaching document. Possible readings: an eschatological not-yet; a claim about covenant membership (all *within* it know Him, unlike Israel where membership was ethnic); hyperbolic covenant language. **Not adjudicated here.** Filed to [[04-open-questions]] — it is a genuine cost for any reading that treats the New Covenant as fully consummated.

## 5. Ezekiel 36 — the Ruach Side of the Same Transformation

> **Ezekiel 36:26–27** — ESV: "And I will give you **a new heart**, and **a new spirit** I will put within you. And I will remove the heart of stone from your flesh and give you a heart of flesh. And **I will put my Spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes and be careful to obey my rules**." CJB: "I will put my Spirit inside you and **cause you to live by my laws, respect my rulings and obey them**." RVR1960: "pondré dentro de vosotros mi Espíritu, y **haré que andéis en mis estatutos, y guardéis mis preceptos**."

**Read the sequence:** new heart → new spirit → Yahweh's Spirit within → **obedience**.

**This complicates any model in which the Spirit *replaces* obedience.** Ezekiel presents the Ruach as the **power by which covenant faithfulness happens** — all three translations agree on the causal construction ("*cause you to* walk / *haré que* andéis").

**Consequence for this vault's own epistemology.** [[00d-grace-and-stewardship]] and [[00b-biblical-epistemology]] have described the Ruach as illuminating and guiding. **Ezekiel adds a third function: producing covenant faithfulness.** That is an addition to the framework's own model of the Spirit, arrived at from the prophets rather than from the epistemology section's needs.

## 6. Ezekiel 37 — Resurrection Language Enters the Semantic Family

The new-heart/new-Spirit promise is followed immediately by the valley of dry bones. Yahweh asks whether the bones can live; they assemble; breath enters; they live. Then Yahweh interprets His own vision:

> **Ezekiel 37:11** — ESV: "Son of man, **these bones are the whole house of Israel**. Behold, they say, 'Our bones are dried up, and our hope is lost; we are indeed cut off.'" CJB: "**These bones are the whole house of Isra'el**." RVR1960: "**todos estos huesos son la casa de Israel**."

**Resurrection imagery is applied by the text itself to the restoration of Israel.** This is not an interpreter's allegory — Yahweh supplies the referent, exactly as Revelation 1:20 supplies its own key (see [[00i-stars-the-dragon-and-the-cross]]).

### Methodological entry — and the brake in the same breath

This joins a growing family of **established semantic possibilities**:

| Image | Also functions as | Established at |
|---|---|---|
| cosmic collapse | historical judgment | [[00j-cosmic-signs-and-intertextual-priority]] |
| cloud-coming | divine judgment / enthronement | [[00h-coming-on-the-clouds]] |
| heaven and earth | possibly more than physical cosmology | [[00m-fig-tree-generation-heaven-and-earth]] |
| **death → resurrection** | **covenantal / national restoration** | **here** |

> **❗ Anti-Replacement Principle, stated before anyone gets ideas:** this does **not** establish that resurrection language *always*, or even usually, means national restoration. It establishes that **the possibility exists in Scripture's own usage** — nothing more. Any specific passage (1 Corinthians 15, Daniel 12, John 5, Revelation 20) must still be argued on its own contextual evidence. **The precedent is recorded; the range is not thereby collapsed in the opposite direction.**

This brake matters more than usual, because this particular semantic possibility is the one most useful to a preterist reading — which is exactly why it must not be granted more than it has earned.

## 7. Ezekiel 37:15–28 — Two Sticks, One King, and a Complication

> **Ezekiel 37:19** — ESV: "Behold, I am about to take **the stick of Joseph** (that is in the hand of **Ephraim**) and the tribes of Israel associated with him. And I will join with it **the stick of Judah**, and make them **one stick, that they may be one in my hand**."

> **Ezekiel 37:22** — ESV: "And I will make them **one nation** in the land, on the mountains of Israel. And **one king shall be king over them all**, and they shall be **no longer two nations**, and no longer divided into two kingdoms."

> **Ezekiel 37:24** — ESV: "**My servant David shall be king over them**, and they shall all have one shepherd. **They shall walk in my rules and be careful to obey my statutes.**" CJB: "**they will live by my rulings and keep and observe my regulations**." RVR1960: "**y andarán en mis preceptos**."

> **Ezekiel 37:26** — ESV: "**I will make a covenant of peace with them. It shall be an everlasting covenant with them.** And I will set them in their land and multiply them, and **will set my sanctuary in their midst forevermore**." RVR1960: "**haré con ellos pacto de paz, pacto perpetuo será con ellos**... y **pondré mi santuario entre ellos para siempre**."

> **Ezekiel 37:27–28** — ESV: "**My dwelling place shall be with them**, and I will be their God, and they shall be my people. Then the nations will know that I am the LORD who sanctifies Israel, when **my sanctuary is in their midst forevermore**."

### Two findings and one genuine problem

**Finding 1 — David.** David had been dead for centuries when Ezekiel wrote. So either a literal resurrected David, or **the Davidic Messiah**. The broader canonical narrative pushes strongly toward the latter, and this project reads it that way — **while noting that "the larger narrative pushes toward it" is an interpretive judgment (Tier B), not a statement Ezekiel makes.**

**Finding 2 — and this one cuts against a simple "Torah discarded" reading.** Ezekiel 37:24 places **"they shall walk in my rules and be careful to obey my statutes"** *inside* the everlasting-covenant promise, alongside the one king. **Torah-keeping is not presented as the thing being left behind; it is presented as a feature of the restored state.** This is direct prophetic evidence for the "Torah internalized, not erased" side of Jeremiah 31:33.

> ### ⚠️ The problem — recorded because it is a real cost, not a footnote
>
> **Ezekiel 37:26–28 promises a sanctuary in their midst "forevermore," twice.** RVR1960: *"pondré mi santuario entre ellos para siempre."*
>
> This sits inside the same everlasting-covenant passage that supplies the one-king and Torah-keeping material this stage is using. **A framework that reads Temple language as passing away in AD 70 owes an account of this promise** — and so does any reading that treats the New Covenant as having fully arrived without a sanctuary.
>
> **Live options, none selected:** (a) fulfilled in Messiah/the Spirit-indwelt people as the sanctuary (2 Corinthians 6:16 quotes Ezekiel 37:27 — *that citation is flagged and not yet verified by this project*); (b) fulfilled in the Second Temple period partially and conditionally; (c) still future; (d) Ezekiel 40–48's temple vision governs it and needs its own investigation.
>
> **Filed to [[04-open-questions]]. Ezekiel 40–48 is bookmarked, deliberately not opened here** — it is a large investigation and cashing it mid-stage would repeat exactly the error Stage 13 avoided with Daniel 2.

So Jeremiah does not stand alone: **Jeremiah 31** (New Covenant) + **Ezekiel 36** (new heart, Spirit) + **Ezekiel 37** (restored Israel, one king, everlasting covenant, Torah-keeping, sanctuary) belong together. **And together they are harder to systematize than any one of them alone.** That is the honest report.

## 8. Yeshua Enters — the Chronological Anchor

Only now does the New Testament enter.

> **Luke 22:20** — ESV: "This cup that is poured out for you is **the new covenant in my blood**." CJB: "This cup is **the New Covenant, ratified by my blood**, which is being poured out for you." RVR1960: "Esta copa es **el nuevo pacto en mi sangre**, que por vosotros se derrama."

> **1 Corinthians 11:25** — ESV: "This cup is **the new covenant in my blood**. Do this, as often as you drink it, in remembrance of me." CJB: "This cup is **the New Covenant effected by my blood**."

Jeremiah: *"I will make a New Covenant."* Yeshua: ***"This is the New Covenant in my blood."*** He identifies His approaching death with the inauguration of Jeremiah's promised covenant.

**Note CJB's interpretive verbs** — "ratified by" (Luke), "effected by" (1 Cor) — where ESV/RVR1960 read the plainer "in my blood." CJB is making the inaugural sense explicit. Recorded as a translation datum: the reading is reasonable, but **"in" is what the Greek preposition gives, and the inaugural force is inferred from context, not from the preposition.**

> **Chronological anchor established:** whatever eschatological system eventually results, **the New Covenant is textually tied to Yeshua's sacrificial death** — not to AD 70, not to Pentecost, not to a future event. Paul independently preserves the same tradition, which strengthens it.

**This anchor has teeth.** It means the New Covenant's *inauguration* and the old administration's *disappearance* are, on this evidence, **two datable events roughly forty years apart** — which is precisely the "becoming obsolete... ready to vanish" transitional window Hebrews 8:13 describes ([[00n-torah-fulfilled-and-what-changed]] §8). **That convergence is noted as suggestive, and explicitly not treated as proof** — Hebrews' dating remains at "permits," per Stage 14's brake.

## 9. The Torn Veil — Observation, Then Apostolic Interpretation

Raised earlier in connection with heavenly access ([[00i-stars-the-dragon-and-the-cross]]); it now has a natural home. **The three-layer discipline applies** (ADR-0006) — observation and interpretation kept visibly separate.

**Observation:**

> **Matthew 27:51** — ESV: "And behold, **the curtain of the temple was torn in two, from top to bottom**. And the earth shook, and the rocks were split." CJB: "At that moment the ***parokhet*** in the Temple was ripped in two from top to bottom." RVR1960: "el **velo del templo se rasgó en dos, de arriba abajo**."

**Apostolic interpretation** — this project is not inventing symbolism around Matthew's veil; Hebrews itself develops the relationship:

> **Hebrews 10:19–22** *(already indexed)* — confidence to enter the holy places by the blood of Yeshua, by **"the new and living way"** opened through the curtain.

**What "from top to bottom" does and does not establish.** It is a detail Matthew chose to include, and the direction is not accidental — it reads as an act from above rather than below. **But Matthew does not interpret it.** The theological weight comes from Hebrews, not from Matthew's sentence. Keeping that seam visible is the point.

## 10. The Priesthood Question, Therefore, Changes

Under the Levitical arrangement: **priests → Holy Place**; **High Priest → Holy of Holies, under prescribed conditions**. Hebrews presents Yeshua differently:

> **Hebrews 4:14** — ESV: "Since then we have **a great high priest who has passed through the heavens**, Jesus, the Son of God, let us hold fast our confession." CJB: "since we have a great ***cohen gadol*** who has passed through to the highest heaven, Yeshua, the Son of God." RVR1960: "teniendo un **gran sumo sacerdote que traspasó los cielos**, Jesús el Hijo de Dios."

> **Hebrews 4:15** — ESV: "For we do not have a high priest who is **unable to sympathize with our weaknesses**, but one who **in every respect has been tempted as we are, yet without sin**."

Developed via **Psalm 110:4** — "a priest forever, after the order of Melchizedek" ([[00n-torah-fulfilled-and-what-changed]] §6).

### The structural point

Israel's ordinary arrangement keeps the offices **separate**: **Davidic kingship → Judah**; **Levitical/Aaronic priesthood → Levi**. Different tribes; the separation is structural, not incidental.

**Messiah is presented as uniting kingship and everlasting priesthood in a higher order.** This is not a minor administrative adjustment — it is a change that the Levitical system, by its own tribal logic, could not produce from within itself. Which is exactly Hebrews 7:11–12's argument.

## 11. Zechariah 6:12–13 — Anticipated, With Contrary Evidence Recorded

> **Zechariah 6:12** — ESV: "Behold, **the man whose name is the Branch**: for he shall branch out from his place, and **he shall build the temple of the LORD**." CJB: "There is coming a man whose name is ***Tzemach* [Sprout]**. He will sprout up from his place and rebuild the temple of *Adonai*." RVR1960: "el varón cuyo nombre es **el Renuevo**... y **edificará el templo de Jehová**."

> **Zechariah 6:13** — ESV: "It is he who shall build the temple of the LORD and **shall bear royal honor, and shall sit and rule on his throne. And there shall be a priest on his throne**, and the counsel of peace shall be between them both." CJB: "he will take up royal splendor, sitting and ruling from his throne. **There will be a *cohen* before his throne**; and they will accept each other's advice in complete harmony." RVR1960: "él llevará gloria, y se sentará y dominará en su trono, y **habrá sacerdote a su lado**; y consejo de paz habrá entre ambos."

The passage strikingly brings **royal and priestly imagery together around the Branch**, and Hebrews' priest-king is therefore not invented from nothing — the prophets were already working this ground.

> ### ⚠️ Contrary evidence — the translations diverge on the decisive clause
>
> | Version | Rendering | Implies |
> |---|---|---|
> | **ESV** | "there shall be **a priest on his throne**" | plausibly the *same* figure |
> | **CJB** | "There will be a *cohen* **before his throne**" | a **separate** figure |
> | **RVR1960** | "habrá sacerdote **a su lado**" ("at his side") | a **separate** figure |
>
> **Two of the three standard translations read the priest as a distinct figure standing before or beside the throne — not as the enthroned one himself.** And all three end with "the counsel of peace shall be **between them both**," which reads more naturally of **two** parties than one.
>
> **Therefore: Zechariah 6:13 is weaker support for "Messiah unites both offices" than it first appears, and this stage does not lean on it.** The priest-king case rests on **Psalm 110:4 + Hebrews 7**, which are explicit. Zechariah is recorded as **suggestive, not load-bearing.**
>
> *This is the same discipline applied to CJB's "this people" at Matthew 24:34 ([[00m-fig-tree-generation-heaven-and-earth]]) — when translations split on a clause this project would like to use, the split gets printed, not buried.*

## 13. Torah Is Not One Undifferentiated Pile — but Watch the Taxonomy

Later Christian theology commonly divides Torah into **moral / civil / ceremonial** law. The framework has real analytical use.

> ### ❗ But Torah itself does not present three formally labeled codes called "moral," "civil," and "ceremonial."
>
> **These are later theological classifications.** That does not make them illegitimate — it makes them **theological taxonomy, not biblical terminology**, and they must be labeled as such wherever this project uses them.

**Why this matters concretely:** the tripartite division does real work in arguments — "the ceremonial law passed, the moral law remains" — and if the division is itself an interpretive overlay, then an argument that *depends* on it is resting on a premise Scripture did not supply. **That is not a refutation of the division. It is a requirement to argue for it rather than assume it.**

> **This is a direct application of the Category Collapse Test** ([[00b-biblical-epistemology]]) — but running in the opposite direction. Category Collapse warns against packaging several things as one. This warns against **splitting one thing into categories the source text never named**, and then reasoning from the split as though it were given. **Both are failures to let the text define its own units.** Recorded in 00b as the rule's second face.

So rather than starting from those categories, **let the apostolic writings show what they actually do with particular commandments.**

## 14. Circumcision — the First Test Case

Chosen because it was already met **historically** in Acts ([[00c-commission-covenant-investigation]]), so the narrative is already in hand.

**It predates Moses.** That alone complicates the standard categories:

> **Genesis 17:10–11** — ESV: "This is **my covenant**, which you shall keep, between me and you and your offspring after you: Every male among you shall be circumcised. You shall be circumcised in the flesh of your foreskins, and it shall be **a sign of the covenant** between me and you." CJB: "this will be **the sign of the covenant** between me and you." RVR1960: "**será por señal del pacto** entre mí y vosotros."

**So circumcision is not merely "Levitical law."** It is an **Abrahamic covenant sign**, given centuries before Sinai. Any tripartite taxonomy has to decide where it goes — and the difficulty of that placement is itself evidence about the taxonomy (§13).

Then **Torah itself** speaks of heart-circumcision (Deuteronomy 10:16; 30:6), and **Jeremiah** does too (4:4) — all three established in [[00n-torah-fulfilled-and-what-changed]] §14.

Then Paul:

> **Romans 2:28–29** *(already indexed)* — covenant identity is not reducible to outward circumcision; circumcision "**a matter of the heart, by the Spirit**."

> **1 Corinthians 7:18** — ESV: "Was anyone at the time of his call already circumcised? Let him not seek to remove the marks of circumcision. **Was anyone at the time of his call uncircumcised? Let him not seek circumcision.**" CJB: "He shouldn't undergo ***b'rit-milah***."

> **1 Corinthians 7:19** — ESV: "**For neither circumcision counts for anything nor uncircumcision, but keeping the commandments of God.**" CJB: "Being circumcised means nothing, and being uncircumcised means nothing; **what does mean something is keeping God's commandments**." RVR1960: "La circuncisión nada es, y la incircuncisión nada es, **sino el guardar los mandamientos de Dios**."

### Stop there. That sentence destroys the easy binary.

Paul does **not** say circumcision doesn't matter *because* commandments don't matter. He says circumcision does not determine covenant standing — **and that keeping God's commandments does mean something.** All three translations agree on this; there is no translational escape hatch.

> ### The exegetical problem, stated rather than hidden
>
> **How can circumcision be commanded within Torah, while Paul distinguishes physical circumcision from "keeping the commandments of God"?**
>
> This is not a contradiction to conceal. It is an **investigation to run.** And it strongly suggests Paul holds some worked-out conception of **how commandments function through Messiah under the New Covenant** — a conception this project has not yet recovered from the text.

## 15. Which Is Exactly Why Food Comes Later, Not Now

Recall the Acts 10 **Exegetical Sidebar** ([[00c-commission-covenant-investigation]]). Peter states the vision's meaning himself:

> **Acts 10:28** *(already indexed)* — "**God has shown me that I should not call any person common or unclean.**"

**The revealed interpretation concerns people.** Therefore Acts 10 cannot simply be deployed as "God told Peter pork is clean now."

> **But — and this is the whole discipline — that does not itself prove dietary distinctions remain binding on New Covenant Gentiles.** That is a *different* question requiring its own evidence.
>
> **Disproving an argument does not prove its opposite. Anti-Replacement Principle.**

Food gets investigated separately, on its own evidence. Same for **Sabbath**, **festivals**, **circumcision**, **sacrifices**, **purity regulations**, and **Temple commandments**.

## 16. The Eleven-Question Protocol

The framework's epistemology, applied specifically to any Torah command. **We do not ask "which side am I on?"** We ask:

1. Where does the command **originate**? *(Abraham? Sinai? Creation?)*
2. **To whom** was it given?
3. What **purpose** does Scripture assign it?
4. Do the **Prophets** anticipate its transformation?
5. What does **Yeshua** say about it?
6. How does **Acts** portray it in practice?
7. How do the **apostolic writings** interpret it?
8. Is it **explicitly retained**?
9. Explicitly **transformed**?
10. Explicitly **discontinued**?
11. Or are we **inferring beyond what Scripture directly states**?

**Question 11 is the one that keeps the other ten honest.** Added to [[00b-biblical-epistemology]] as a specialized instrument alongside the general 7-step passage test — this one is for commands, where the general test is for passages.

## 17. The Companion Question — Purpose

Something deeper surfaced. The original question —

> "What changed in the New Covenant?"

— needs a companion:

> ### **"What was the purpose of the thing that changed?"**

**Without purpose, transformation is unintelligible.** Why priesthood? Why sacrifice? Why Temple? Why circumcision? Why clean/unclean? Why Sabbath? Why festivals?

If Yeshua **fulfills** Torah, then identifying Torah's **purpose** is necessary for knowing what fulfillment means. Otherwise "fulfill" stays a dictionary argument — which is precisely where Stage 14 left it.

Paul supplies one provocative statement:

> **Galatians 3:24** — ESV: "So then, **the law was our guardian until Christ came**, in order that we might be justified by faith." CJB: "the ***Torah* functioned as a custodian** until the Messiah came." RVR1960: "la ley ha sido **nuestro ayo**, para llevarnos a Cristo."

> **Galatians 3:25** — ESV: "But now that faith has come, **we are no longer under a guardian**." CJB: "**we are no longer under a custodian**." RVR1960: "**ya no estamos bajo ayo**."

**παιδαγωγός — *paidagōgos*.** Older English renders it "schoolmaster," which misleads. A *paidagōgos* was not primarily a classroom teacher but a **guardian/custodian** — typically a household slave — responsible for supervising and escorting a child. **All three modern translations here avoid "schoolmaster":** ESV "guardian," CJB "custodian," RVR1960 "ayo" (tutor/guardian). The translations have already moved; the popular English memory has not.

**What Galatians 3:25 does not say:** that Yahweh's wisdom became false. **What it says:** something about Torah's **covenantal function relative to Messiah** has changed.

And it must be reconciled with the same author:

> **Romans 7:12** — ESV: "So **the law is holy, and the commandment is holy and righteous and good**." CJB: "So **the Torah is holy**; that is, the commandment is holy, just and good." RVR1960: "la ley a la verdad es **santa**, y el mandamiento **santo, justo y bueno**."

> **Paul says both.** Therefore **any interpretation of Paul requiring one statement to erase the other is too crude to be correct** — regardless of which direction the erasure runs. This cuts equally against "Paul abolished Torah" and against "Paul meant nothing changed."

## Manuscript Voice — The Question Changed

> **Prose:** I had originally approached the question as though only two answers were available: either the Torah remained, or the Torah had been abolished. But Scripture itself began dismantling the question before I could answer it. Jeremiah promised a covenant that would be genuinely new and would nevertheless place Yahweh's Torah inside His people. Ezekiel promised the Spirit not as an escape from obedience but as the very power by which Yahweh's people would walk faithfully — and then, in the same everlasting covenant, said they would keep His statutes. Deuteronomy had spoken of circumcision of the heart long before Paul was accused of inventing it. Psalm 110 had anticipated a priesthood beyond Levi before Hebrews ever explained one.
>
> The question therefore could no longer simply be, *did the Law remain or disappear?* The better question became: what was each part doing, what did Messiah fulfill, what did Scripture itself say changed, and what did Yahweh carry forward in transformed form?
>
> That distinction mattered because fulfillment could no longer be treated as either destruction or stasis. Something genuinely changed. Something genuinely continued. And Scripture — not an inherited theological system, and not my preference for a tidy answer — would have to tell me which was which.
>
> What I did not expect was that the honest version of the answer would include a column marked *unresolved*, and that two of the first twelve rows would land in it.

## 18. Next — Sacrifice and Priesthood, Not Sabbath or Food

**The reason is methodological, not preferential.** Scripture gives its clearest and most fully-argued answer here. If one example can be established where:

> **Torah remains true → its purpose is fulfilled in Messiah → its covenantal administration genuinely changes**

then there is a **concrete biblical model** of what fulfillment looks like, before touching the far more disputed questions. Building the model on the clearest case and then testing the hard cases against it is the correct order. Doing it the other way round means arguing about Sabbath with no agreed sense of what "fulfilled" even means.

**The chain:** **Leviticus (Day of Atonement) → Psalm 110 → Yeshua → Hebrews 7–10.**

**The claim to be tested — stated as a hypothesis, not a conclusion:**

> **Yeshua does not merely cancel the sacrificial pattern. He is presented as the reality to which the pattern was pointing.**

If that holds under examination, this project will finally have a **worked biblical example of what Matthew's *plēroō* actually looks like** — rather than continuing to argue over its dictionary definition, which is exactly where Stage 14 had to stop.

**Held open at Leviticus:** the Day of Atonement chapter has not been read in this project yet. Its details — two goats, the scapegoat sent away, the annual repetition, the high priest's own sin offering — must be read **before** Hebrews' interpretation of them, not after. Reading Hebrews first and Leviticus second would guarantee finding exactly what Hebrews said was there.
