# Stage 18 — Clean and Unclean: From Noah to Messiah

**We do not begin with "can believers eat pork?"** That question is loaded, narrow, and presumes the categories. **Stage 17's corrected model requires tracing the institution from its earliest appearance.**

> **The question:** what purpose do clean/unclean distinctions serve in Torah, what precedes Sinai, what does Yeshua actually say about food and defilement, what does Peter's vision actually mean, and what do the apostles subsequently require — or not require — of Gentiles?

> ### ⚠️ **NO VERDICT IS REACHED IN THIS STAGE.** It ends with an evidence-standing table (§27), several items still marked unresolved, and the next investigation named. That is the honest state of the material, not an evasion.

---

## 1. The Distinction Predates Israel

> **Genesis 7:2** — ESV: "Take with you seven pairs of **all clean animals**, the male and his mate, and a pair of **the animals that are not clean**." CJB: "Of every clean animal you are to take seven couples, and of the animals that are not clean, one couple."

**Hebrew: טָהוֹר — *tahor*** (clean/pure) and **לֹא טְהֹרָה — *lo tehorah*** (not clean).

**Before Abraham. Before Israel. Before Moses. Before Sinai. Before Leviticus.**

> ### **OBSERVATION (Tier A): the biblical distinction between clean and unclean animals does not originate in the Mosaic covenant.**

**And the brake, immediately.** Genesis 7 does **not** say *"Noah therefore observed Leviticus 11 dietary law."* **That would import later legislation backward into Genesis** — the reverse-direction error that Intertextual Priority exists to prevent. So: **what does Genesis itself show Noah doing with the distinction?**

---

## 2. The First Explicit Function Is Sacrificial

> **Genesis 8:20** — ESV: "Then Noah built an altar to the LORD and took **some of every clean animal and some of every clean bird and offered burnt offerings** on the altar."

**Three-part precision, and it is what prevents over-reading:**

| | Status |
|---|---|
| clean/unclean distinction | **explicit** |
| connection between clean animals and **sacrifice** | **explicit** |
| complete dietary prohibition against **eating** unclean animals | **not yet explicit** |

---

## 3. Genesis 9:3 Is a Real Difficulty — Put It on the Table

> **Genesis 9:3** — ESV: "**Every moving thing that lives shall be food for you.** And as I gave you the green plants, **I give you everything**." CJB: "Every moving thing that lives will be food for you; just as I gave you green plants before, **so now I give you everything**."

> ### ⚠️ **This cuts against a universal-pre-Sinai-dietary reading, and it is not explained away.**
>
> Someone arguing the clean/unclean distinction was universally *dietary* before Sinai must answer: **how does that square with "every moving thing that lives"?** **That is a strong objection and it stands.**

**Then the immediate restriction:**

> **Genesis 9:4** — ESV: "But **you shall not eat flesh with its life, that is, its blood**." CJB: "Only flesh with its life, which is its blood, you are not to eat."

**So the explicit post-Flood dietary structure is:** animals granted as food, **with an explicit prohibition concerning blood/life.**

> **🔖 Flag forward: the blood prohibition becomes load-bearing at Acts 15** (§22). Note it is given to **Noah** — i.e. to humanity broadly, not to Israel specifically. That is a **Covenantal Jurisdiction** datum.

---

## 4. The First Genuine Open Question

| Layer | Content |
|---|---|
| **ESTABLISHED** | Clean/unclean categories predate Sinai (Gen 7:2) |
| **ESTABLISHED** | Genesis explicitly associates clean animals with **sacrifice** (Gen 8:20) |
| **ESTABLISHED** | Genesis 9 broadly grants animals as food **while explicitly prohibiting blood** |
| **⚠️ OPEN** | Did "every moving thing" include animals already classified unclean for sacrificial purposes — or would Noah naturally have understood the existing distinction to constrain what counted as food? |

> **Neither "Noah ate unclean animals" nor "Noah followed Israel's later dietary restrictions" is explicitly stated. Both are inferences. Filed to [[04-open-questions]].**

---

## 5. Before Leviticus 11 — the Covenantal Frame

> **Exodus 19:5–6** — ESV: "if you will indeed obey my voice and keep my covenant, **you shall be my treasured possession among all peoples**… and you shall be to me **a kingdom of priests and a holy nation**." CJB: "you will be **a kingdom of *cohanim*** for me, **a nation set apart**."

**Hebrew: קָדוֹשׁ — *qadosh*, holy / set apart.** **CJB renders it "set apart" outright**, which makes the sense visible in English.

**Israel is being constituted as a people set apart for Yahweh.** Leviticus's distinctions are legislated *inside* that frame — which the next sections show the text itself saying.

---

## 6. Leviticus 11 — the Explicit Legislation

> **Leviticus 11:3** — ESV: "Whatever **parts the hoof and is cloven-footed and chews the cud**, among the animals, you may eat."

> **Leviticus 11:7–8** — ESV: "And **the pig**, because it parts the hoof and is cloven-footed **but does not chew the cud, is unclean to you**. **You shall not eat any of their flesh**, and you shall not touch their carcasses; they are unclean to you." CJB: "the pig is unclean for you… **You are not to eat meat from these** or touch their carcasses."

> ### **This is the explicit proposition. Not inference, not tradition, not later rabbinic interpretation: Torah explicitly prohibits Israel from eating pig.**

Aquatic creatures require fins and scales; further categories follow.

---

## 7. "Unclean **to you**" — a Covenantal Jurisdiction Question

**Leviticus repeatedly says variations of "unclean *to you*" / "detestable *to you*."** CJB reads "unclean **for** you."

> **The jurisdiction question:** **is Yahweh identifying an ontological property of these creatures for all humanity, or establishing a holiness boundary particularly for Israel — or some combination?**

**Both constraints stand simultaneously and neither may be dropped:**
- **Genesis prevents saying the category itself began with Israel.**
- **Leviticus clearly applies the dietary legislation specifically to Israel.**

**Filed as unresolved, not adjudicated.**

---

## 8. Leviticus Tells Us Why — and It Is Theological

> **Leviticus 11:44** — ESV: "For I am the LORD your God. **Consecrate yourselves therefore, and be holy, for I am holy.**"

> **Leviticus 11:45** — ESV: "For I am the LORD who brought you up out of the land of Egypt to be your God. **You shall therefore be holy, for I am holy.**"

> **Leviticus 11:46–47** — ESV: "This is the law about beast and bird… **to make a distinction between the unclean and the clean, and between the living creature that may be eaten and the living creature that may not be eaten**." CJB: "**Its purpose is to distinguish** between the unclean and the clean."

**The stated purpose chain: holiness → separation → distinction/discernment → Israel's relationship with Yahweh.**

> ### **Whatever else may be true about health, *Leviticus itself supplies a theological reason.* This is not presented as ancient nutrition advice, and any reading that reduces it to that is contradicting the text's own stated purpose.**

---

## 9. Deuteronomy Confirms the Chain

> **Deuteronomy 14:2** — ESV: "For **you are a people holy to the LORD your God**, and the LORD has chosen you to be a people for **his treasured possession**." CJB: "you are a people **set apart as holy** for *Adonai* your God."

> **Deuteronomy 14:3** — ESV: "**You shall not eat any abomination.**" CJB: "You are not to eat anything disgusting."

**Same order, independently: identity → holiness → dietary distinction.**

> **Establishable:** **within the Sinai covenant, clean/unclean dietary practice functions at least partly as an expression of Israel's holy/set-apart covenant identity.**
>
> **NOT establishable from this:** "therefore the animals only *became* unclean at Sinai." **Genesis 7:2 prevents that simplification.**

---

## 10. The Prophets Complicate "It Doesn't Matter Anymore"

> **Isaiah 65:2–4** — ESV: "I spread out my hands all the day to **a rebellious people**… sacrificing in gardens and making offerings on bricks, **who eat pig's flesh**, and broth of tainted meat is in their vessels." CJB: "**they eat pig meat** and their pots hold soup made from disgusting things."

> **Isaiah 66:17** — ESV: "**eating pig's flesh and the abomination and mice, shall come to an end together**, declares the LORD." CJB: "**eating pig meat, reptiles and mice, will all be destroyed together**."

**Isaiah places consumption of unclean creatures inside prophetic language about rebellion and judgment.** That is not a cooking manual.

> ### ⚖️ **But context cuts both ways here, and both cuts are recorded:**
>
> **The passage also involves idolatrous/pagan ritual behavior** — "sacrificing in gardens," "making offerings on bricks," those who "sanctify themselves" for such practice. **So we cannot extract:** ❌ *"Isaiah 66 says anyone eating bacon at any time is damned."* **That is not what the passage explicitly says.**
>
> **But neither can we honestly say:** ❌ *"The Prophets treat dietary distinctions as insignificant."* **They plainly do not.**

---

## 11. Mark 7 — Identify the Actual Dispute First

> **Mark 7:2** — ESV: "they saw that some of his disciples ate with **hands that were defiled, that is, unwashed**." CJB: "ate with ritually unclean hands, that is, **without doing *n'tilat-yadayim***."

> **Mark 7:5** — ESV: "**Why do your disciples not walk according to the tradition of the elders, but eat with defiled hands?**" CJB: "Why don't your *talmidim* live in accordance with **the Tradition of the Elders**?"

> ### **The immediate dispute is NOT "why are your disciples eating pigs?" It is eating with ceremonially unwashed hands, under the *tradition of the elders*.**

**CJB names the specific practice — *n'tilat-yadayim*, the ritual hand-washing — which makes the subject unmistakable in English.**

---

## 12. Yeshua's Own Control Verse

> **Mark 7:8** — ESV: "**You leave the commandment of God and hold to the tradition of men.**" CJB: "**You depart from God's command and hold onto human tradition.**"

> ### ❗ **An extraordinarily important control.** Whatever Yeshua is about to teach, **Mark has just portrayed Him criticizing people for setting aside Yahweh's commandments in favor of human tradition.** Any reading of what follows has to survive standing next to verse 8.

> **Mark 7:15** — ESV: "**There is nothing outside a person that by going into him can defile him**, but the things that come out of a person are what defile him."

> **Mark 7:18–19** — ESV: "…since it enters not his heart but his stomach, and is expelled? (**Thus he declared all foods clean.**)"

---

## 13. 🔴 Mark 7:19 — the Translations Split, and RVR1960 Is the Outlier That Matters

**Greek: καθαρίζων πάντα τὰ βρώματα — *katharizōn panta ta brōmata*** — literally something like **"cleansing all the foods."** ***Katharizōn* is a participle. The Greek does not contain an explicit "he declared."**

| Version | Rendering | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **ESV** | "(Thus **he declared** all foods clean.)" | **supplies a subject and a declarative verb** |
| **NIV** | "(In saying this, **Jesus declared** all foods clean.)" | **supplies the subject by name** |
| **CJB** | "(Thus he declared all foods **ritually** clean.)" | supplies subject **and narrows the scope with an added adverb** |
| **RVR1960** | "Esto decía, **haciendo limpios** todos los alimentos." | **keeps the participle** — "making clean," no subject supplied |

> ### **Two separate interpretive moves are visible, and each is an addition to the Greek:**
>
> **1. ESV/NIV/CJB supply "he declared."** RVR1960 preserves the participial form. **The historical alternative reading — that the digestive process itself is what "cleanses" the food — is grammatically available precisely because the participle has no stated subject.** *(This project does not adjudicate the grammar here; it records that the ambiguity is real and that one witness preserves it.)*
>
> **2. CJB alone inserts "ritually."** That narrows the claim substantially — from *all foods are clean* to *all foods are ritually clean*, i.e. bearing on the hand-washing dispute rather than on species.
>
> ### ⚖️ **Honesty about interest, in both directions.** CJB is a Messianic Jewish translation with a stake in the narrower reading. **ESV and NIV are traditional-Christian translations with a stake in the broader one.** **Neither observation settles the grammar** — it means the parenthesis in every English Bible here is an interpretive act, and the reader is generally not told so. *(Same category of finding as CJB's "this people" at Matthew 24:34 — see [[00m-fig-tree-generation-heaven-and-earth]].)*

### And a second question survives even if the declarative reading is right

> **What does Mark mean by "foods" — βρώματα *brōmata*?**
>
> - **every biological creature is now food**, or
> - **all things already classified as food cannot morally defile someone merely through ritual hand contamination**?
>
> **Those are not identical claims.** Settling the grammar would not by itself settle the referent. **Both remain open.**

---

## 14. Matthew's Parallel — Parallel-Text Control

> **Matthew 15:20** — ESV: "These are what defile a person. **But to eat with unwashed hands does not defile anyone.**" CJB: "eating without doing *n'tilat-yadayim* does not make a person unclean." RVR1960: "**el comer con las manos sin lavar no contamina al hombre**."

> ### **Matthew's summary is much narrower than "every animal previously prohibited by Torah may now be eaten."** Matthew has no parenthesis at all.

**This does not by itself prove dietary distinctions continue.** **It tells us what Matthew understood the controversy to concern — eating with unwashed hands — and that has to constrain the reading of Mark.**

> **🆕 Named tool: PARALLEL-TEXT CONTROL** — when two Gospels record the same episode, the narrower summary constrains the broader one's ambiguous clause, because both authors are reporting the same event. Added to [[00b-biblical-epistemology]].

## 15. Peter as a Chronological Witness

> **Acts 10:11–13** — ESV: "something like a great sheet descending… In it were **all kinds of animals and reptiles and birds of the air**… '**Rise, Peter; kill and eat.**'" CJB: "'**Get up, Kefa, slaughter and eat!**'"

> **Acts 10:14** *(indexed)* — "**By no means, Lord; for I have never eaten anything that is common or unclean.**"

> ### **Peter personally heard the Mark 7 / Matthew 15 teaching. Years later his immediate, reflexive response is that he has never eaten anything common or unclean.**
>
> ### **Therefore, at minimum: Peter did not understand Yeshua's earlier teaching as an obvious instruction to begin eating previously unclean animals.**

**That is not speculation — Peter states his own practice.** It does not yet settle what Yahweh is about to teach him. **But it strongly constrains simplistic claims about what Peter thought Yeshua had already taught.**

---

## 16. The Voice Answers

> **Acts 10:15** — ESV: "**What God has made clean, do not call common.**" CJB: "**Stop treating as unclean what God has made clean.**"

**Three times.** **If we stopped here, "Yahweh has declared previously unclean animals edible" would be an understandable conclusion.** **Luke does not stop here, and neither may we.**

> **🔍 Note CJB collapses the *koinos* distinction here** — ESV keeps "**common**" (*koinos*), CJB reads "**unclean**." That matters because Peter's own v.14 distinguishes "common **or** unclean." See §24.

---

## 17. Peter Does Not Initially Know What It Means

> **Acts 10:17** — ESV: "Now while Peter was **inwardly perplexed as to what the vision that he had seen might mean**…" CJB: "Kefa was **still puzzling over the meaning of the vision**…"

> ### **If the meaning were simply "you may now eat pork," Luke presents Peter as not arriving at that conclusion.**

**And while he is puzzling — Cornelius's men arrive.** **The narrative sequencing is deliberate: the vision and the Gentile visitors are joined by Luke, not by us.**

---

## 18. Peter Supplies His Own Interpretation

> **Acts 10:28** *(indexed)* — "**God has shown me that I should not call any PERSON common or unclean.**"

> **Acts 10:34–35** — ESV: "**Truly I understand that God shows no partiality**, but **in every nation anyone who fears him and does what is right is acceptable to him**." CJB: "God does not play favorites… **no matter what people he belongs to**."

> ### **Peter does not say "God showed me that pigs are clean." He says he must not call any *human being* common or unclean.**
>
> ### **ESTABLISHED (very high confidence): the primary narrative purpose of Peter's sheet vision is Gentile inclusion, not dietary permission.**

**This is not a preferred interpretation imposed from outside — Luke places Peter's interpretation inside the narrative.**

---

## 19. ⚖️ The Brake, Applied in the Opposite Direction Too

> **Do NOT move from** *"the vision's primary meaning concerns people"* **to** *"therefore the vision has no implications whatsoever for food."*
>
> ### **That is the Anti-Replacement Principle. Showing interpretation A is insufficient does not establish B.**

**The vision deliberately uses food categories.** The command really is *kill and eat*. Yahweh really does say *what God cleansed, don't call common*.

> **So a legitimate question remains:** **does Yahweh use dietary imagery merely as an analogy Peter would grasp, or does the vision simultaneously reflect some transformed status of food distinctions?**
>
> **Acts 10 makes the human/Gentile meaning explicit. Any dietary implication would require additional evidence. That is the precise position — neither more nor less.**

---

## 20. Acts 11 Confirms the Trajectory — and Contains a Gap Worth Naming

> **Acts 11:2–3** — ESV: "the circumcision party criticized him, saying, '**You went to uncircumcised men and ate with them.**'" CJB: "'You went into the homes of uncircumcised men and **even ate with them**!'"

> **Acts 11:18** — ESV: "**Then to the Gentiles also God has granted repentance that leads to life.**" CJB: "**This means that God has enabled the *Goyim* as well to do *t'shuvah* and have life!**"

> ### 🔬 **A worked observation/inference demonstration — and the gap is the point**
>
> The objection in Acts 11:3 is explicitly about **table fellowship with uncircumcised Gentiles**. **But Luke never tells us what was on the table.**
>
> | Layer | Content |
> |---|---|
> | **OBSERVATION** | Peter ate with Gentiles |
> | **OBSERVATION** | **Scripture does not tell us what they ate** |
> | **OBSERVATION** | Peter explicitly interprets the vision as concerning **persons** |
> | **OBSERVATION** | The Ruach falls on the Gentiles |
> | **OBSERVATION** | Jerusalem concludes Yahweh granted **Gentiles repentance leading to life** |
> | **INFERENCE** | *Peter ate Levitically unclean animals at Cornelius's house* |
> | **STATUS** | **UNSUPPORTED BY THE NARRATIVE** |
>
> **And inserting an unclean meal would be especially poor method here**, because Acts 10:14 has just gone out of its way to record Peter saying he has *never* eaten anything common or unclean. **Had Luke wanted the double meaning, this is exactly where he would have supplied it. He does not.**

### The conclusion Acts 10–11 *does* decisively establish

> **Yahweh's saving work through Messiah and the gift of the Ruach are not restricted to circumcised Israelites; Gentiles are being received by God without first becoming circumcised Jewish proselytes.**

**Note the precision:** connecting this specifically to *the Abrahamic promise* is an **intertextual/theological conclusion** — strongly supported by Genesis 12:3, Galatians 3, Romans 11, and Ephesians 2, **but Luke does not use "the promise" in Acts 11:18.** The narrower statement is what Acts itself carries.

## 21. Acts 15 Reconnects to Noah

> **Acts 15:20** *(indexed)* — abstain from things polluted by idols, sexual immorality, **what has been strangled**, and **blood**.

> **Acts 15:29** — ESV: "that you abstain from **what has been sacrificed to idols, and from blood, and from what has been strangled, and from sexual immorality**."

> **Why *these* particular requirements?** Two proposals commonly discussed, **and they are not mutually exclusive:**
> - a connection with **Leviticus 17–18**, where laws concerning blood, sacrifice, and sexual practice apply not only to native Israelites but to the **ger**, the foreign resident among them;
> - a connection with the **Noahic blood prohibition** of Genesis 9:4 (§3).

> ### 🚧 **But we do not announce "Acts 15 is the Noahide laws." The text does not say that.** The relationship is investigated, not asserted. **Filed to [[04-open-questions]].**

**The Genesis 9:4 link is genuinely striking**, though — a prohibition given to **Noah**, i.e. to humanity rather than to Israel specifically, reappearing in the one list the apostles hand to Gentiles.

## 23. Romans 14 — and the Vegetarian Problem

> **Romans 14:2** — ESV: "One person believes he may eat anything, while **the weak person eats only vegetables**." CJB: "another whose trust is weak **eats only vegetables**."

**Greek: λάχανα — *lachana*, vegetables/plants/herbs.**

> ### ❗ **This immediately blocks the standard reading.**
>
> The passage is often read as *Torah-observant Jew refusing pork vs. Christian who understands pork is now permitted.* **But Torah never required Jews to eat only vegetables.** **So whatever is happening in Rome, at least one group's practice extends well beyond avoiding Levitically unclean animals.**

> **Romans 14:3** — ESV: "**Let not the one who eats despise the one who abstains, and let not the one who abstains pass judgment on the one who eats**, for God has welcomed him."

**Note Paul's actual instruction is symmetrical — neither party is told to change their practice.** That is itself a datum about what kind of dispute this is.

## 24. Romans 14:14 — and the *Koinos* / *Akathartos* Distinction

> **Romans 14:14** — ESV: "I know and am persuaded in the Lord Jesus that **nothing is unclean in itself**, but it is unclean for anyone who thinks it unclean." CJB: "**nothing is unclean in itself.** But if a person considers something unclean, then for him it is unclean."

> **Romans 14:20** — ESV: "**Everything is indeed clean**, but it is wrong for anyone to make another stumble by what he eats."

**That sounds decisive. Then check the Greek.**

> ### 🔴 **Paul uses κοινός — *koinos* — here.**
>
> ***Koinos*** = **common / profane / defiled-by-association**.
> ***Ἀκάθαρτος — akathartos*** = the standard Septuagint term for animals categorized as **inherently unclean**.
>
> **And Peter's Acts 10:14 distinguishes them explicitly: "common OR unclean" — *koinon ē akatharton*.**
>
> ### **English flattens both into "unclean." Greek does not. That lexical distinction could matter enormously, and it must be investigated rather than collapsed.**

**Recorded as a live lexical question, not as a conclusion in either direction** — *koinos* can also be used broadly, and one word choice does not by itself establish that Paul excludes Levitical categories. **[[04-open-questions]].**

## 25. Romans 14 May Not Be About Levitical Categories At All

**The chapter concerns disputes involving eating vs. abstaining *and* esteeming particular days. One party eats only vegetables.** Candidate explanations, none selected:

- meat associated with **idolatry / pagan sacrifice**
- **unknown preparation or market provenance**
- **ritual contamination**
- **ascetic practice** (voluntary abstention as spiritual discipline)
- disputes between communities with **different scruples**

> ### **Historical context is required before Romans 14 can be used as a universal repeal of Leviticus 11 — or as evidence that it isn't one.**
>
> **Investigate these; do not choose whichever explanation helps the current hypothesis.**

## 26. 🆕 Two New Instruments This Stage Produced

### A. The Contextual Question Principle

> ### **Before using a passage as evidence for a theological proposition, first identify the question, controversy, or situation the author was actually addressing. A passage may have legitimate implications beyond its immediate context, but those implications must be demonstrated rather than assumed.**

**Applied three times in this stage already:**

| Passage | The actual dispute |
|---|---|
| **Mark 7** | **unwashed hands** |
| **Acts 10–11** | **Gentile inclusion** — do not call a *person* common or unclean |
| **Romans 14** | **still under investigation** |

**Implications may grow *out of* the original context. They may not replace it. Exegesis before systematic theology.** Added to [[00b-biblical-epistemology]] and [[00-methodology-current]].

### B. Species Classification vs. Preparation/Provenance — Two Different Axes

**"Clean food" can silently mean five different things. Separate the axes:**

| **Axis 1 — species classification** | **Axis 2 — preparation / provenance / condition** |
|---|---|
| Is this creature in a Levitically permitted category? | **how was it killed?** · **was blood properly handled?** · **was it sacrificed to an idol?** · **is it diseased or contaminated?** · **does eating it violate someone's conscience?** |

> **A Torah-clean animal — a cow — can still raise every Axis-2 question.** **And an animal raised under immaculate conditions can still belong to a species Leviticus classifies unclean.** **The axes are independent.**
>
> **✍️ Author's Reflection:** the modern concerns that press hardest in practice — not knowing how an animal was killed, how it was processed, what it was given, where it came from — sit almost entirely on **Axis 2**. **That is a real concern and a biblically-recognized category** (Genesis 9:4's blood; Acts 15's strangled things; 1 Corinthians 8–10's idol-meat), **but it is not the same question as whether a species is clean.** Keeping them apart prevents a genuine conviction on one axis from silently deciding the other.

## Manuscript Voice — Peter Wouldn't Let Me Use the Easy Answer

> **Prose:** The clean-food question exposed how easily a familiar theological conclusion can be read backward into a passage. I had repeatedly heard Peter's sheet summarized as though God lowered unclean animals from heaven simply to announce that the dietary laws were over. **But Peter himself told me what Yahweh showed him: "I should not call any person common or unclean."** Luke then confirmed the trajectory when the believers concluded that Yahweh had granted repentance to the Gentiles.
>
> That did not prove dietary distinctions continued unchanged. **But it did remove Acts 10 as an effortless proof that they had been abolished.** And Peter's own reaction made the problem sharper. **Years after hearing Yeshua's teaching about what enters the mouth, Peter could still say truthfully, "I have never eaten anything common or unclean."** Whatever Peter thought Yeshua meant, he apparently had not understood Him to mean *start eating everything*.
>
> **The method therefore required restraint in both directions.** I could not use Peter's vision to repeal Leviticus without addressing Peter's own interpretation. **But neither could I use Peter's interpretation to prove the vision had no secondary implications for food.** Primary meaning had to be established before secondary implication could even be considered.

> **Prose — the parenthesis nobody mentions:** What unsettled me most was smaller and more technical. Every English Bible I had ever read put a parenthesis at the end of Mark 7:19 — *thus he declared all foods clean* — and I had absorbed it as though it were Mark's plain sentence. **It is a participle in Greek, with no stated subject.** One of my three witnesses, the Spanish, simply keeps the participle and lets the ambiguity stand. Another adds a single word, *ritually*, that narrows the claim considerably. **Three translations, three different degrees of interpretation, presented to the reader as translation.**
>
> I do not know yet how that grammar resolves. **What I know is that I had been quoting a conclusion while believing I was quoting a verse.**

## 28. Next — What Was Actually Happening at the Roman Table

**Before 1 Corinthians 8–10, a short historical/exegetical investigation is warranted:** **what does "the weak eats only vegetables" most plausibly refer to?**

> **If that can be identified, Romans 14 becomes much harder to weaponize in either direction** — which is exactly why it should be done before moving on rather than after.

**Then the passages that will genuinely pressure-test a continuing-dietary-distinction reading:**

**1 Corinthians 8–10 · Colossians 2:16–23 · 1 Timothy 4:1–5**

### The 1 Timothy 4 problem, stated in advance

> **1 Timothy 4:4** — ESV: "**For everything created by God is good, and nothing is to be rejected if it is received with thanksgiving.**"

**Isolated, that sounds devastating to a clean-food position.** **But read the verse before it:**

> **1 Timothy 4:3** — ESV: "who forbid marriage and **require abstinence from foods that God created to be received with thanksgiving** by those who believe and know the truth."

> ### **The question becomes: does "everything created by God" mean every created organism is therefore biblically classified as *food* — or is Paul speaking of things already understood as *brōmata*, foods?**
>
> **And note the target of Paul's rebuke:** those who **forbid marriage** *and* require abstinence — **which looks like the ascetic profile, not the Levitical one.** *(Recorded as an observation about the passage's opponents, not yet as an argument.)*
>
> **This cannot be answered by preference. We have to follow Paul's argument.**
