# Stage 18.7 — Acts 15: Were the Four Requirements the Whole Torah for Gentiles?

> **📐 Why its own file.** This is Stage 18 continued, but Acts 15 is **where both investigations converged independently** — circumcision arrived here in Stage 17, food arrived here in Stage 18.6. It also produces two new instruments. Splitting it keeps [[00s-clean-and-unclean-from-noah-to-messiah]] readable; **the spine holds the sequence.**

**The question is not what the four requirements *were*. It is what the apostles understood themselves to be *doing* by issuing them.**

---

## 1. Reconstruct the Problem Before Interpreting the Answer

> **Acts 15:1** *(indexed)* — "Unless you are circumcised according to the custom of Moses, **you cannot be saved**."

**The proposition is not** *"should Gentiles learn Torah?"* **It is: must Gentile believers be circumcised in order to be saved?**

And the strongest Torah-observant position gets stated explicitly — **by believers:**

> **Acts 15:5** — ESV: "But **some believers who belonged to the party of the Pharisees** rose up and said, '**It is necessary to circumcise them and to order them to keep the law of Moses**.'" CJB: "some of those who had come to trust were from the party of the *P'rushim*… 'It is necessary to circumcise them and **direct them to observe the *Torah* of *Moshe***.'"

> ### **Luke calls them *believers*. The dispute is happening INSIDE the believing community — not between Christianity and Judaism.**

**Their proposition has two elements: circumcise the Gentiles, AND command them to keep the Torah of Moses.** Whatever the council decides, it decides against *that* proposition — which is narrower than "against Torah."

---

## 2. Peter Appeals to Cornelius — Acts 10 Becomes Evidence

> **Acts 15:7–8** *(indexed)* — Yahweh chose that "the Gentiles should hear the word of the gospel and believe," and **"gave them the Holy Spirit just as he did to us."**

**Peter's argument: Yahweh already rendered a verdict — and not by circumcising Cornelius first.** The Ruach fell **while Peter was still speaking.**

> **Acts 15:9** *(indexed)* — Yahweh "made no distinction between us and them, **having cleansed their hearts by faith**."

> ### **Purity language again — but what is being cleansed is *hearts*.**
>
> **After the circumcision stage, four threads are converging:** **circumcision of heart** (Deut 10:16, 30:6; Jer 4:4) · **cleansing of heart** (Acts 15:9) · **gift of the Ruach** (Ezek 36:27; Acts 10:44) · **faith**. *(Recorded as convergence, not as a claim that Acts 15:9 quotes Deuteronomy — see [[00s-clean-and-unclean-from-noah-to-messiah]] §9's classification.)*

---

## 3. ⚠️ "The Yoke" — What Exactly Is Peter Calling Unbearable?

> **Acts 15:10** *(indexed)* — "Why are you putting God to the test by **placing a yoke on the neck of the disciples that neither our fathers nor we have been able to bear**?"

**This verse is frequently read as: *the Torah itself was an unbearable burden*. Careful — Scripture elsewhere describes Yahweh's commandments very differently:**

> **Deuteronomy 30:11** — ESV: "For this commandment that I command you today is **not too hard for you**, neither is it far off." CJB: "this *mitzvah*… **is not too hard for you, it is not beyond your reach**."

> **Psalm 19:7** — ESV: "**The law of the LORD is perfect, reviving the soul**; the testimony of the LORD is sure, making wise the simple." CJB *(19:8)*: "**The *Torah* of *Adonai* is perfect, restoring the inner person.**"

> **1 John 5:3** — ESV: "For this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments. **And his commandments are not burdensome.**" CJB: "**his commands are not burdensome**."

> **📌 Versification confirmed:** **CJB numbers Psalm 19:7 as 19:8** — the Psalms superscription is counted as verse 1. *(Surfaced the same way the Jeremiah 31 offset was: a direct fetch of "CJB Psalm 19:7" returned the sun's-circuit verse. Third confirmed offset in this project, after Jeremiah 31 and Zechariah 2:6.)*

### The exegetical question, held open

> **What precisely is Peter calling the unbearable yoke?**
> - the Torah as Yahweh gave it?
> - the entire covenantal administration?
> - **the demand that Gentiles become circumcised proselytes and assume the full Mosaic legal identity?**
> - the accumulated *manner* in which Torah had come to be administered?

**Not settled by slogan.** **But context strongly ties Peter's "yoke" to the proposition actually under debate at Acts 15:5** — Gentile circumcision plus commanded Torah-keeping *as a condition of inclusion*. **That is the narrowest reading the context supports, and the narrowest reading is the one this project takes when the text does not specify.**

> **Acts 15:11** *(indexed)* — "**we will be saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus, just as they will**." **Salvation is the issue.** *(Direction of comparison already noted at [[00q-circumcision-covenant-sign-and-heart]] §10.)*

---

## 4. James Appeals to the Prophets

> **Acts 15:14** — ESV: "**Simeon has related how God first visited the Gentiles, to take from them a people for his name.**" CJB: "taking from among the *Goyim* **a people to bear his name**."

**Then James quotes Amos 9:11–12** — David's fallen tent restored, the nations seeking Yahweh.

> ### **Gentile inclusion is interpreted through the prophetic story, not as the abandonment of Israel's story.** Squarely inside the *one covenantal story containing multiple covenants* refinement ([[00r-what-is-a-covenant]]).

---

## 5. The Judgment — and the *gar*

> **Acts 15:19–20** *(indexed)* — "**we should not trouble those of the Gentiles who turn to God**," but should write to them to abstain from **things polluted by idols · sexual immorality · what has been strangled · blood**.

> **Acts 15:21** *(indexed)* — "**For** from ancient generations Moses has had in every city those who proclaim him, for he is read every Sabbath in the synagogues."

> ### **That "for" matters. Greek: γάρ — *gar*, which normally introduces a reason or explanation.**
>
> **James does not randomly change subject. There is a logical relationship between v.20 and v.21. The question is *what* relationship.**

---

## 6. Reading A — Fellowship Requirements

**Jewish believers lived throughout the Greco-Roman world. Since Moses was read everywhere, Jewish sensitivities concerning idolatry, blood, sexual immorality, and strangled meat were universally known.** Therefore Gentile believers should abstain from these **so that Jewish and Gentile followers of Yeshua could share table fellowship.**

**On this reading, v.21 explains *why these particular issues were especially urgent*.**

**Plausible** — and it fits the enormous Jewish/Gentile fellowship problem visible elsewhere in the New Testament (Galatians 2; Acts 11:3).

---

## 7. Reading B — Entry Requirements, Not the Entire Curriculum

**James could be saying:** *don't overwhelm Gentile converts immediately; give them these essentials, **because** Moses is read every Sabbath in every city* — i.e. **they will continue hearing Scripture and learning.**

**On this reading, Acts 15 is not saying** *"Gentile believers have exactly four moral commandments forever."* **It is saying something closer to:** *don't make complete Torah mastery — or circumcision — the admission requirement. Begin here.*

**Grammatically and narratively possible, and v.21 would make considerable sense under it.**

> ### 🚧 **BUT THE BRAKE.** Does Acts 15:21 explicitly say *"the Gentiles will attend synagogue every Sabbath and gradually learn the rest of Torah"*?
>
> ### **No. It does not.**
>
> ### **Reading B is an INFERENCE — potentially a strong one, worth investigating — but it cannot be upgraded to textual statement because it fits a hypothesis.**

---

## 8. A Third Possibility — Refusing the False Binary

**A and B are not necessarily mutually exclusive.** Moses being proclaimed throughout the cities could explain **both** why Jewish/Gentile fellowship required immediate attention to these practices **and** the scriptural environment in which Gentile converts would keep encountering Israel's Scriptures.

> **And a fourth possibility that must stay open: James may not be constructing our later theological categories at all.** The question *"is this an entry requirement or a permanent list?"* may be one the text was never answering.

---

## 9. ❗ The Reductio — "Only Four Commandments" Cannot Be Right

**Suppose someone says Acts 15 proves Gentile believers had only four rules. Then ask a simple textual question. Were Gentile believers permitted to:**

**murder? · steal? · bear false witness? · dishonor parents?**

**Obviously not. None of those is among the four. Yeshua and the apostles condemn all of them elsewhere.**

> ### **Therefore the four requirements CANNOT constitute an exhaustive list of moral obligations for Gentile believers.**
>
> ### **Whatever Acts 15 is doing, it is not giving the entire ethical content of discipleship in four bullet points.**

**This is one of the strongest arguments in the stage, and it requires no lexical or historical work — only reading the list and asking what it omits.**

---

## 10. So Why *These* Four? — Leviticus 17–18

**Several commands in Leviticus 17–18 are addressed not only to native Israelites:**

> **Leviticus 17:10** — ESV: "If any one of the house of Israel **or of the strangers who sojourn among them** eats any blood, I will set my face against that person." CJB: "or **one of the foreigners living with you**."

> **Leviticus 17:12** — ESV: "No person among you shall eat blood, **neither shall any stranger who sojourns among you** eat blood."

> **Leviticus 18:26** — ESV: "But you shall keep my statutes and my rules and do none of these abominations, **either the native or the stranger who sojourns among you**." CJB: "**neither the citizen nor the foreigner living with you**."

**Leviticus 17 also addresses improperly slaughtered animals and sacrifice; Leviticus 18 addresses prohibited sexual relations.**

**Compare the Acts 15 list:**

| Acts 15 | Leviticus 17–18 |
|---|---|
| idolatrous pollution / sacrifice | Lev 17 — sacrifice regulations |
| sexual immorality | Lev 18 — prohibited relations |
| strangled animals | Lev 17 — improper slaughter |
| blood | Lev 17:10, 12 — **explicitly including the sojourner** |

> ### **The overlap is striking — and it is with precisely the laws Torah itself applied to BOTH Israelites and resident foreigners.**
>
> **A serious intertextual hypothesis. Not announced as established** — the same restraint applied at [[00s-clean-and-unclean-from-noah-to-messiah]] §21.

---

## 11. Glossary — *Ger*

**Hebrew: גֵּר — *gēr*** — **sojourner / resident alien / foreigner living among Israel.**

**The *ger* is not identical to a native-born Israelite. But neither is he simply an unrelated foreigner living far away.** He is a **non-native dwelling among Israel and therefore participating to some degree in Israel's communal life.** Torah repeatedly gives him **both protections and obligations.**

> **Numbers 15:15–16** — ESV: "For the assembly, there shall be **one statute for you and for the stranger who sojourns with you**, a statute forever throughout your generations. **One law and one rule shall be for you and for the stranger who sojourns with you.**" CJB: "**The same *Torah* and standard of judgment will apply to both you and the foreigner living with you.**"

> ### **The Tanakh already contains categories for foreigners attaching themselves to Yahweh's people. Gentile inclusion does not appear from nowhere in Acts.**
>
> *(Converges with Ezekiel 47:22–23 and Exodus 12:48, both already indexed — and with the Ruth precedent at [[00q-circumcision-covenant-sign-and-heart]] §29.)*

### 🚧 But do not flatten *ger* into "Gentile Christian"

**Not automatically identical categories.** Ancient Israel under the Sinai covenant and a first-century Gentile follower of Yeshua are **historically and covenantally different situations.** *"Levitical* ger *= Gentile Christian"* is too crude.

> **The legitimate question:** **did James deliberately draw upon Torah's established requirements for foreigners dwelling among Israel when determining requirements for Gentiles entering the Messiah community?**
>
> **The verbal and conceptual overlap makes it worth serious investigation. It does not settle it.**

---

## 12. 🔴 The Textual Asymmetry — and It Cuts Against Continuity

**Notice what is NOT in Acts 15.**

| Explicitly commanded | Explicitly absent |
|---|---|
| abstain from **blood** | ❌ **unclean animal species** |
| abstain from **strangled meat** | |
| abstain from **idol pollution** | |
| abstain from **sexual immorality** | |

> ### **If James is drawing from Leviticus 17–18, it is notable that Leviticus 11's animal classification is NOT reproduced.**
>
> ### **That is real evidence AGAINST assuming the council imposed the entire Levitical dietary code on Gentile believers. We let it stand.**

> ### ⚖️ **But Acts 15:21 prevents the opposite conclusion — "therefore Moses no longer matters" — because James invokes Moses being proclaimed every Sabbath in the very next sentence.**
>
> ### **Both simplistic answers hit a wall. That is the finding.**

---

## 13. Back to Peter's Sheet — Sharper Now

**Acts 10:** Peter says *I have never eaten common or unclean food* → vision → Peter interprets it *don't call any **person** common or unclean* → Gentiles receive the Ruach.

**Acts 15:** Peter uses that very event to argue **Yahweh cleansed their hearts by faith.**

> ### **Notice what Peter does NOT say at the council:** ❌ *"Remember my vision? Yahweh abolished the food laws."*
>
> **He uses Cornelius to establish Gentile acceptance without circumcision — in the one setting where a dietary argument would have been maximally useful to him, in front of the people who most needed convincing.**

**Does not prove dietary law continues.** **Does make the primary meaning of Acts 10 increasingly difficult to dispute** — this is now the third independent line pointing the same way (Peter's own interpretation at 10:28; Luke's conclusion at 11:18; Peter's *use* of the episode here).

---

## 14. 🆕 Formal Rule — Covenantal Transmission

**Stage 16 gave us *truth vs. administration* as different axes. Acts 15 requires a third instrument.**

> ### **COVENANTAL TRANSMISSION**
> **When people from outside Israel are incorporated into Messiah's people, ask of each prior covenantal command: is it explicitly REAFFIRMED, TRANSFORMED, UNIVERSALIZED, SPIRITUALLY DEEPENED, LEFT UNSTATED, or ATTACHED TO A PRIOR ADMINISTRATION?**

**Far better than *"is Torah abolished: yes or no?"* — because Scripture itself is not behaving like that binary.**

| Element | What the texts so far actually do |
|---|---|
| **Circumcision** | transformed around heart/Ruach; **not imposed physically on Gentile converts as a salvation requirement** |
| **Blood** | **explicitly retained for Gentiles** (Acts 15:20, 29; 21:25) |
| **Sexual holiness** | **explicitly retained** |
| **Idolatry** | **explicitly retained** |
| **Strangled meat** | **explicitly retained** |
| **Sacrificial system** | fulfilled/reconfigured around Messiah's once-for-all priesthood and sacrifice |
| **Clean animal species** | ⚠️ **still under investigation — and conspicuously absent from the decree** |
| **Sabbath** | ⚠️ **separate investigation required** |

---

## 15. ❗ This Blocks a Very Common Mistake — in Both Directions

| ❌ Mistake | ❌ Refuted by |
|---|---|
| *If one Mosaic command changes administratively, all must change identically* | **circumcision** (Stage 17) **and Acts 15's explicit retentions** |
| *If one Torah command continues, all must continue identically* | **Messiah's priesthood** (Stage 16) |

> ### **The unit of analysis must be granular:**
> ### **What does Scripture do with THIS particular command or institution across the covenant story?**

**Which will be required for: Sabbath · feasts · tithing · priesthood · sacrifice · circumcision · food — and more.**

---

## Manuscript Voice — Acts 15 Refused Both Systems

> **Prose:** I expected Acts 15 eventually to force the investigation into one of two familiar systems: either Torah remained intact for everyone, or Messiah had rendered it irrelevant for Gentiles. **The council did neither.** Physical circumcision was not imposed upon Gentile converts, yet blood remained prohibited. Sexual holiness remained. Idolatrous contamination remained. The apostles did not hand Gentiles the entire Mosaic code — **but neither did James speak as though Moses had become obsolete.** Immediately after giving the decree, he observed that Moses had been proclaimed from ancient generations and was read every Sabbath.
>
> That forced another correction in the method. **"Does Torah continue?" was too crude a question.** Scripture required each command to be traced through the covenant story individually. Some realities were fulfilled in Messiah, some transformed in administration, some explicitly reaffirmed, some applied to the nations, and some remained genuinely disputed. **The task was no longer to defend a system. It was to discover what Scripture itself did with each command.**
>
> **And this was precisely where intellectual honesty became uncomfortable.** Acts 15 explicitly retained the prohibition of blood for Gentiles, but it did not explicitly prohibit unclean animal species. **I could not insert Leviticus 11 into James's decree merely because I wanted continuity. Yet neither could I remove Moses from the conversation when James himself mentioned Moses in the very next sentence.** Both observations had to remain standing until further evidence resolved their relationship.
>
> There was also a smaller thing I noticed and did not want to notice. At the council, in front of the people who most needed persuading, **Peter had the perfect opportunity to say that his vision had abolished the food laws — and he said something else instead.** He said Yahweh had cleansed their *hearts*. A man defending a position tends to use his strongest available argument. Peter didn't use that one, and the most economical explanation is that he did not think he had it.

## 16. Next — Acts 21:17–26, the Control Case We Cannot Skip

**Years after the council, Paul returns to Jerusalem.**

> **Acts 21:20** — ESV: "**how many thousands there are among the Jews of those who have believed. They are all zealous for the law.**" CJB: "**how many tens of thousands of believers there are among the Judeans, and they are all zealots for the *Torah***."

> **Acts 21:21** — ESV: "**they have been told about you that you teach all the Jews who are among the Gentiles to forsake Moses**, telling them not to circumcise their children or walk according to our customs." CJB: "**to apostatize from *Moshe***, telling them not to have a *b'rit-milah* for their sons."

**James proposes that Paul publicly demonstrate the accusation is false —**

> **Acts 21:26** — ESV: "Then Paul took the men, and the next day **he purified himself along with them and went into the temple**, giving notice when the days of purification would be fulfilled **and the offering presented** for each one of them."

**— and, crucially, explicitly recalls the *separate* decision concerning Gentile believers:**

> **Acts 21:25** — ESV: "**But as for the Gentiles who have believed, we have sent a letter with our judgment** that they should abstain from what has been sacrificed to idols, and from blood, and from what has been strangled, and from sexual immorality."

> ### **That verse may be the single most clarifying sentence available for what Acts 15 did and did not mean** — because James, years later, **distinguishes** what was decided about Gentiles from what is expected of Jewish believers, in the same breath.
>
> **Three things it puts in play, none of them resolved here:** (a) "**many thousands**" of **Torah-zealous Jewish believers** existing as a normal state of affairs decades in; (b) the charge that Paul taught **Jews** to forsake Moses treated as a **false rumor** requiring public refutation; (c) **Paul entering the Temple with an offering** — after Hebrews' "once for all."

**Before leaving Acts and returning to the remaining food passages, Acts 21 is the next control case.**
