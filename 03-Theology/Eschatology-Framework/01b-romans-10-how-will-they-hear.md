# Stage 23 — Romans 10: "How Will They Hear Without Someone Preaching?"

> ### **THIS IS THE STAGE WE SAID WE WOULD RUN AT RATHER THAN AVOID.**
>
> Romans 10:14–15 is the single strongest text against the project's original position. It was approached deliberately, with the outcome unprotected. **What it did to the position is recorded below without softening, in both directions — including one verified finding that WEAKENS a bridge the preterist hypothesis would have wanted.**

**Method carried in from Stage 21½:** preterism is downstream of the evidence, never its lens. Romans 10 is not read backward through Romans 11.

---

## 1. The Subject Has Not Changed — It Is Still Israel

> **Romans 10:1** — ESV: "Brothers, my heart's desire and prayer to God for them is that they may be saved."

"Them" has no new antecedent. It is the same Israelites from Romans 9. Stage 22 established that Paul's whole argument is generated by anguish over biological Israel; Romans 10:1 continues that same sentence-level concern.

> **Romans 10:2** — ESV: "they have a **zeal for God**, but **not according to knowledge**."

> ### **Romans 10 is not a disconnected missionary handbook. Its immediate subject is Israel's response to Messiah — and that governs how the preaching chain in 10:14–15 may be read.**

**Tier A (Text).** The antecedent is textual, not inferred.

---

## 2. Their Problem Is Not Absence of Zeal

Paul concedes the zeal. The defect is its basis.

> **Romans 10:3** — ESV: "being **ignorant of the righteousness of God**, and seeking to establish **their own**, they did not submit to God's righteousness."

This is a claim about **how righteousness is received**, not about religious indifference. Worth holding onto: Paul is not describing people who ignored the message. He is describing people who were zealous and still missed it — which is a different diagnosis, and it matters when we reach verse 18.

---

## 3. Deuteronomy 30 Is Doing the Work — Again

Paul contrasts the righteousness Moses describes (10:5) with "the righteousness based on faith" (10:6) — and then makes the second case **by quoting Moses**.

> **Deuteronomy 30:11–14** — ESV, **VERIFIED BY RETRIEVAL**: "this commandment that I command you today is **not too hard for you, neither is it far off**. It is **not in heaven**, that you should say, '**Who will ascend to heaven** for us…?' Neither is it **beyond the sea**… But **the word is very near you. It is in your mouth and in your heart**, so that you can do it."

> **Romans 10:6–8** — ESV: "Do not say in your heart, '**Who will ascend into heaven?**'… or '**Who will descend into the abyss?**'… But what does it say? '**The word is near you, in your mouth and in your heart**' (that is, the word of faith that we proclaim)."

### ❗ Paul modified the quotation, and the modification is not cosmetic

| Deuteronomy 30 (ESV, verified) | Romans 10 (ESV, verified) |
|---|---|
| "Who will **ascend to heaven**" | "Who will **ascend into heaven**" — matches |
| "Neither is it **beyond the sea**… who will **go over the sea**" | "Who will **descend into the abyss**" — **CHANGED** |

**Paul replaced "beyond the sea" with "into the abyss," which lets him gloss it as "to bring Christ up from the dead."** The sea→abyss substitution is what makes the christological reading possible.

**Tier A** that the change exists. **Tier B** what it implies about Paul's hermeneutic. **Open question q62** below.

> ### **The structural point stands regardless: Paul's argument about faith-righteousness is built FROM Torah, not against it. This is the same pattern found at Stages 14, 17, and 18.10 — Paul's critique of Torah-misuse is consistently made from inside Torah.**

**Note also:** Deuteronomy 30 is the chapter that promises **circumcision of the heart** (Stage 17). Paul reaching for *this* chapter, specifically, is not arbitrary.

---

## 4. ⚠️ Romans 10:4 — TRANSLATION DIVERGENCE #6

**The lexical brake fires immediately.**

> **Romans 10:4**
> - **ESV:** "For Christ is **the end of the law** for righteousness to everyone who believes."
> - **CJB:** "For **the goal at which the Torah aims** is the Messiah, who offers righteousness to everyone who trusts."

**These are not stylistic variants. They are different propositions.** ESV permits "Messiah terminates Torah." CJB asserts "Messiah is what Torah was driving toward."

### Greek — VERIFIED BY INTERLINEAR RETRIEVAL

**τέλος — *telos*, Strong's 5056.** Noun, nominative neuter singular. Sentence-initial in the Greek: *τέλος γὰρ νόμου Χριστὸς…*

*Telos* legitimately carries: **end · goal · culmination · outcome · completion · purpose.** The termination sense and the goal sense are both real; the word does not decide between them.

> ### ❗ **Romans 10:4 CANNOT be settled from the English word "end." Anyone quoting ESV's "end of the law" as though the verse said "Torah is abolished" has substituted one sense of an ambiguous noun for the verse itself.**

**This is now the SIXTH occurrence of the same failure mode** (after Mark 7:19, Colossians 2:14, Hebrews 4:9, Ephesians 2:15, Romans 9:22). See §17.

**Classification:** what *telos* means **here** is **NOT ESTABLISHED**. Recorded as **open question q63**, not resolved in either direction. The surrounding context (10:5–8 arguing *from* Moses) is a **contextual** datum that leans toward the goal/culmination sense — **recorded as contextual, not lexical.**

## 5. Then Access Universalizes

> **Romans 10:11–13** — ESV, verified: "**Everyone** who believes in him will not be put to shame… there is **no distinction between Jew and Greek**… **everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved**."

10:13 quotes Joel.

> **Joel 2:32** — ESV, **VERIFIED BY RETRIEVAL**: "everyone who calls on the name of the Lord shall be saved. For **in Mount Zion and in Jerusalem** there shall be those who escape… and among the survivors shall be those whom the Lord calls."

**Worth noting and NOT flattening:** Joel's "everyone who calls" sits inside an explicitly **Zion/Jerusalem-located** promise. Paul universalizes the first clause. Whether Joel's own locative frame carries over is **not established here** — flagged, not resolved.

> ### **Expansion without erasure — the same pattern as Stages 21 and 22. Israel remains the immediate problem; salvation in Messiah is not ethnically restricted.**

---

## 6. The Chain (10:14–15)

> **Romans 10:14–15** — ESV, verified: "How then will they **call** on him in whom they have not **believed**? And how are they to believe in him of whom they have never **heard**? And how are they to hear without someone **preaching**? And how are they to preach unless they are **sent**?"

Formally:

**call ← believe ← hear ← proclaimer ← sending**

**This is a genuine and strong establishment of the necessity of proclamation.** The project does not soften it.

---

## 7. What the Chain Establishes — and What It Does Not

The chain is a **logical dependency structure**. Read for what it actually asserts:

| Proposition | Status |
|---|---|
| Faith ordinarily requires proclamation | **ESTABLISHED** |
| Proclaimers are **sent** | **ESTABLISHED** — it is the last link Paul himself adds |
| Every believer personally occupies the sent-herald role | **NOT ESTABLISHED by this text** |
| Every believer is commanded here to preach to every region | **NOT ESTABLISHED by this text** |

> ### ❗ **The final link cuts against the universal-obligation reading rather than for it. Paul does not end at "someone must preach." He adds "unless they are SENT" — introducing commission, which is a restriction on the set of heralds, not an expansion of it.**

**This is the pivot of the whole stage.** The text most likely to overturn the original position contains, in its own last clause, the distinction the original position was trying to draw.

### Greek

- **κηρύσσω — *kēryssō*, Strong's 2784** — to herald / proclaim publicly. Carries public-announcement force, not "mention in conversation."
- **ἀποστέλλω — *apostellō*** — to send; the root of *apostolos*, "sent one."

**Brake:** *apostellō* here does **not** restrict the sending to the Twelve. The word-family link is real; a restriction to a closed office is **not** established by the vocabulary alone. **Do not collapse "the logic involves commission" into "only apostles may proclaim."** Both overreadings fail.

## 8. Isaiah 52:7 — The Herald Is a Covenantal Figure

> **Isaiah 52:7** — ESV, **VERIFIED BY RETRIEVAL**: "How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him who **brings good news**, who **publishes peace**… who **publishes salvation**, who says **to Zion**, '**Your God reigns**.'"

The herald Paul invokes announces **peace, salvation, and the reign of God — to Zion**. This is royal/covenantal proclamation with a named addressee, not generic motivational speech. And Isaiah 52 runs directly into the suffering servant of Isaiah 53 — which Paul quotes two verses later.

**Paul's preaching chain is rooted in Israel's prophetic restoration language.** Tier A/B.

---

## 9. ⚠️ Romans 10:15 — TRANSLATION DIVERGENCE #7

> **Romans 10:15**
> - **ESV:** "And how are they to preach **unless they are sent**?" — agent-neutral passive
> - **CJB:** "And how can people proclaim him **unless God sends them**?" — **agent supplied**

CJB names God as the sender. ESV leaves the passive's agent unstated.

> ### ❗ **This is the SAME failure mode as Romans 9:22 (Stage 22), where ESV read agent-neutral and CJB supplied the agent. Twice now, in adjacent chapters, CJB has resolved an unstated agent that the Greek leaves open.**

**This is a pattern about the witness, not about Paul.** Recorded as such. Supplying God as sender is theologically unsurprising and may well be right — but **it is the translator's resolution, not the text's**, and the project may not cite CJB here as evidence of what Paul stated. **Greek verification of 10:15's agent structure: open question q64.**

## 10. "Obeying the Gospel" — Faith and Obedience Again

> **Romans 10:16** — ESV: "But they have not all **obeyed** the gospel." CJB: "they haven't all **paid attention to** the Good News **and obeyed it**."

Paul can say **obey** the good news, then quote Isaiah 53:1 — "who has **believed** what he has heard from us?" — treating the two as the same failure.

> ### **unbelief ↔ disobedience.** Same equivalence found in Hebrews (Stages 19–20). The gospel is not "hear facts → assent." It calls for faithful response.

**Tier A.** The equivalence is on the surface of the text.

---

## 11. 10:17 — Read In Context, Not As A Slogan

> **Romans 10:17** — ESV: "So faith comes from hearing, and hearing through the word of Christ."

Routinely quoted free-floating. In context it is a **premise Paul is about to use against an excuse** — he is building toward "so they cannot claim they never heard." Verse 18 is the payoff, and it changes what 10:17 is doing.

---

## 12. 10:18 — "Have They Not Heard? **Indeed They Have.**"

> **Romans 10:18** — ESV, verified: "But I ask, **have they not heard? Indeed they have**, for 'Their voice has gone out to all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world.'"

**Paul's answer to "have they heard?" is YES.** Not "not yet."

This is the load-bearing sentence for the project's original question. Israel's stumbling is **not** attributed to a proclamation deficit. That reframes the entire chain above: 10:14–15 is deployed to **remove an excuse**, not primarily to issue a commission.

### Greek — VERIFIED BY INTERLINEAR RETRIEVAL

| English | Greek | Strong's |
|---|---|---|
| "all the **earth**" | πᾶσαν τὴν **γῆν** (*pasan tēn gēn*) | 1093 |
| "the ends of the **world**" | τὰ πέρατα τῆς **οἰκουμένης** (*tēs oikoumenēs*) | **3625** |

**οἰκουμένη is present in Romans 10:18.** The tranche framing — "*oikoumenē* appears in the Psalm quotation tradition depending textual form/context" — was **hedged more than necessary. Retrieval confirms it outright.** Promoted from conditional to verified.

### And Matthew 24:14 — VERIFIED BY INTERLINEAR RETRIEVAL

> *καὶ **κηρυχθήσεται** τοῦτο τὸ **εὐαγγέλιον** τῆς βασιλείας ἐν ὅλῃ τῇ **οἰκουμένῃ**… καὶ τότε ἥξει τὸ **τέλος**.*

| Element | Greek | Strong's |
|---|---|---|
| "will be proclaimed" | **κηρυχθήσεται** (*kērychthēsetai*) | **2784** — same lemma as Romans 10:14–15's *kēryssō* |
| "world" | **οἰκουμένῃ** | **3625** — same lemma as Romans 10:18 |
| "the end" | **τέλος** | **5056** — same lemma as Romans 10:4 |

**Three lemma overlaps between Matthew 24:14 and Romans 10, verified.** This looks like a powerful bridge.

**It is weaker than it looks. §13 is why.**

## 13. 🛑 THE BRAKE — Paul Did Not Choose the Word *Oikoumenē*

Romans 10:18 is a **quotation of Psalm 19:4** (MT 19:5 / LXX 18:5). So the question that decides the bridge is: **is οἰκουμένης Paul's word, or the LXX's?**

### LXX — VERIFIED BY RETRIEVAL (rung 4 of the source ladder; see §19)

> **LXX Psalm 18:5:** *εἰς πᾶσαν τὴν **γῆν** ἐξῆλθεν ὁ **φθόγγος** αὐτῶν καὶ εἰς τὰ πέρατα τῆς **οἰκουμένης** τὰ ῥήματα αὐτῶν*

Set beside Romans 10:18's *πᾶσαν τὴν γῆν … τὰ πέρατα τῆς οἰκουμένης*:

> ### ❗❗ **PAUL IS QUOTING THE LXX VERBATIM. Both γῆν and οἰκουμένης are inherited from the Psalm. Paul did not select *oikoumenē* as his own description of gospel reach.**

**Consequence, stated plainly and against the project's own hypothesis:**

An argument of the form *"Paul says the gospel reached the **oikoumenē**, the same word as Matthew 24:14, therefore Matthew 24:14's condition was met in the first century"* **rests on a word Paul borrowed from a Psalm about the heavens declaring God's glory.** The lexical overlap is real; it is **not independent attestation** of Paul's geographic assessment.

> ### **This is the "never collapse evidence types" rule (ADR-0010) operating on a case where the project would have preferred the stronger reading. The overlap is INTERTEXTUAL, not INDEPENDENT-LEXICAL. It may not be reported as the latter.**

### The MT/LXX divergence underneath it

> **Psalm 19:5 (Hebrew) — VERIFIED via Sefaria:** בְּכׇל־הָאָ֨רֶץ יָ֘צָ֤א **קַוָּ֗ם** וּבִקְצֵ֣ה **תֵ֭בֵל** מִלֵּיהֶ֑ם
> Revised JPS: "Their **voice** carries throughout the earth, their words to the end of the world."

| | Hebrew (MT) | LXX / Paul |
|---|---|---|
| the thing that goes out | **קַוָּם — *qavvam*, "their LINE"** (measuring-line/cord) | **φθόγγος — "their SOUND/VOICE"** |
| "earth" | הָאָרֶץ — *ha'aretz* | γῆν |
| "world" | **תֵבֵל — *tevel*** (inhabited world) | **οἰκουμένης** |

**Paul follows the LXX against the Hebrew consonantal reading.** *Qav* ("line") and *phthongos* ("voice") are not the same word. **His "have they heard? — indeed they have" argument depends on the LXX's "voice."**

**Tier A** (the divergence is verified text). **Not resolved:** whether the LXX reflects a different Vorlage or an interpretive rendering — **open question q65**.

**And note *tevel*:** the Hebrew already had an "inhabited world" term standing where the LXX put *oikoumenē*. The scope question is therefore older than the Greek.

## 14. ⚠️ Romans 10:18 — TRANSLATION DIVERGENCE #8, AND THIS ONE INVERTS THE ARGUMENT

> **Romans 10:18**
> - **ESV:** "Their voice has gone out to all the **earth**, and their words to the ends of the **world**."
> - **CJB:** "Their voice has gone out throughout the whole **world** and their words to the ends of the **earth**."

**CJB reverses the two English terms relative to the Greek word order.**

| Greek position | Greek word | ESV renders | CJB renders |
|---|---|---|---|
| first | **γῆν** (*gē*, earth) | "earth" ✓ | "**world**" ✗ |
| second | **οἰκουμένης** (*oikoumenē*) | "world" ✓ | "**earth**" ✗ |

> ### ❗❗ **PRACTICAL CONSEQUENCE: anyone building the Matthew 24:14 ↔ Romans 10:18 bridge while reading CJB in English will attach *oikoumenē* to the WRONG HALF OF THE VERSE.** Matthew 24:14's *oikoumenē* would get matched to CJB's "earth" clause instead of its true counterpart.

**This is the strongest practical argument yet for ADR-0008's source-language-first policy.** Two responsible English translations, neither wrong as English, produce **opposite mappings** of the exact term the eschatological argument depends on. No amount of care at the English level detects this. **Only the Greek does.**

Recorded not as a criticism of CJB — as a demonstration that the project's method is load-bearing rather than ceremonial.

Paul closes with Moses and Isaiah:

- **Isaiah 65:1** (10:20) — "I have been found by those who did not seek me"

**Gentile inclusion + Israel's disobedience — precisely where Romans 11 opens.** The jealousy motif (Deut 32:21) is planted here and detonates in Romans 11.

## 16. Role and Act — The Distinction Becomes Textual

The original position ("not everyone is an evangelist") was previously **personal intuition**. It now has textual support that does not depend on Romans 10 alone:

| Text | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Romans 10:15** | Proclaimers are **sent** — commission is part of the logic |
| **Ephesians 4:11** | Messiah gives *apostles, prophets, **εὐαγγελιστής — euangelistēs**, shepherds, teachers* — **evangelist is a distinct listed gift** |
| **1 Corinthians 12:29** | "**Are all apostles? Are all prophets? Are all teachers?**" — rhetorical, implied answer **no** |

> ### **The body of Messiah contains DIFFERENTIATED callings. Scripture does not portray every member as occupying every ministry office.**

### The mandatory counterweight — stated with equal force

- **1 Peter 3:15** — believers are to be **ready to give an answer** for the hope in them
- **Acts** repeatedly shows ordinary believers speaking about Messiah

> ### **RESULTING FORMULATION (holds both):** *Not everyone is an evangelist — and that does not relieve believers of responsibility to speak truthfully about Messiah when faithfulness, opportunity, or direct questioning calls for it.*

**Romans 10 does not overturn this. It sharpens it** — proclamation's necessity is raised, and the sending/role distinction is preserved in the same breath.

⚠️ **Ephesians 4:11 and 1 Corinthians 12:29 were cited from the tranche and are NOT YET VERIFIED BY RETRIEVAL in this session.** Listed as **open question q66** — the argument above is provisional at those two points until verified. *(Romans 10:15, and the Romans 10 text throughout, ARE verified.)*

## 18. What Stage 23 Established — Classified

### Established
- Proclamation is **necessary** for faith (10:14–17)
- Proclaimers are **sent** — commission is in Paul's own chain
- No Jew/Greek distinction in access (10:12)
- Paul grounds proclamation in **Israel's prophetic restoration** language (Isa 52:7)
- Paul answers "**have they heard?**" with "**indeed they have**" (10:18)
- **Faith and obedience are treated as the same response** (10:16)
- **Romans 10:18 contains οἰκουμένης and γῆν** — verified
- **Matthew 24:14 contains οἰκουμένη, kēryssō, and telos** — verified
- **Paul quotes LXX Ps 18:5 verbatim** — verified
- **Paul follows LXX (*phthongos*) against MT (*qav*)** — verified
- **Colossians 1:23 uses *ktisis*, NOT *oikoumenē*** — verified; but its verb **κηρυχθέντος** shares Matthew 24:14's lemma

### NOT established
- That every believer individually holds the herald role
- That Romans 10 commands every believer to preach to every region
- That *telos* in 10:4 means termination (**or** that it means goal — neither)
- **That the Matthew 24:14 ↔ Romans 10:18 overlap is independent attestation** — it is inherited from the LXX (§13)
- That "they have heard" means all later evangelistic work is finished — **that overreads in the opposite direction**

> ### **Both extremes fail. Again. This is now the characteristic shape of nearly every stage in this investigation, and that consistency is itself worth noting.**

## 19. 🔬 Researcher's Commentary — How This Stage Was Actually Worked

**The source ladder was exercised, and it mattered.** The LXX verification in §13 — the single most consequential finding here — **failed on three consecutive sources** before succeeding:

| Rung | Source | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | biblehub `/septuagint/` | HTTP 404 |
| 2 | biblehub `/sep/` | HTTP 200 — **Brenton's ENGLISH only, no Greek** |
| 3 | katabiblon | CAPTCHA wall |
| 4 | Elpenor, chapter page **19** | HTTP 200 — **wrong psalm** (their numbering) |
| **5** | **Elpenor, chapter page 18** (same edition, corrected chapter) | ✅ **full Greek** |

**Rungs 2 and 4 are the instructive ones: both returned HTTP 200 with real content that was not the content needed.** Had this stage stopped at "the request succeeded," it would have recorded a false verification. Had it stopped at rung 1's 404, it would have recorded a false *failure* — and the §13 brake, which is the most important finding in the stage, would never have been discovered. **The bridge would have been published in its stronger, unearned form.**

This is the rule from the 1 Enoch incident (Stage 17) paying for itself two stages later.

**On the finding itself:** §13 weakens a bridge this project's hypothesis wanted. It was pursued anyway, and recorded at full strength. The alternative — verifying only until the answer is favorable — is the exact failure the method exists to prevent.

**Failed line of reasoning, preserved:** an early read treated the three shared lemmas between Matthew 24:14 and Romans 10 (*oikoumenē*, *kēryssō*, *telos*) as three independent corroborations. They are not. *Oikoumenē* is inherited from the LXX (§13); *telos* in Romans 10:4 is a different syntactic and semantic use than Matthew 24:14's "the end will come"; only *kēryssō* is Paul's own verb in his own sentence. **Three overlaps collapsed to roughly one on inspection.** Recorded because the error is attractive and would recur.

## 20. ✍️ Manuscript Voice — Romans 10 Did Not Destroy the Original Question

> **Romans 10 initially looked like the passage most capable of overturning my original position. Paul's logic seemed unmistakable: people cannot call upon Messiah without believing, cannot believe without hearing, cannot hear without a preacher, and cannot preach without being sent. If any text required universal evangelism, surely this was it.**
>
> **But the context changed the question. Paul was still explaining Israel's present unbelief. The proclaimers in his chain were sent heralds, and Paul immediately asked whether Israel had heard. His answer was not "not yet." It was "indeed they have." The preaching chain therefore established the necessity of proclamation without establishing that every believer personally occupies the role of sent herald, or that the proclamation Paul described remained universally unfinished.**
>
> **The passage did not make preaching less important. It made the category more precise. Proclamation was necessary; proclaimers were sent; different members of Messiah's body possessed different ministries; and yet ordinary believers could still bear witness when called upon. The distinction I had been trying to articulate from the beginning — between witness and the particular ministry of an evangelist — was becoming increasingly textual rather than merely personal.**

And the eschatological turn:

> **More unexpectedly, Paul's answer "indeed they have heard" reopened Matthew 24. If Yeshua had predicted that the good news would be proclaimed throughout the *oikoumenē* before the end, and Paul could already speak in universal scriptural language about the message having gone out, then the question was no longer merely whether proclamation mattered. It was whether the New Testament itself believed the predicted proclamation had already reached its appointed first-century scope.**

✍️ **And then the correction, which belongs in the record just as much:**

> **The bridge I had just built turned out to be partly borrowed. Paul's universal language in Romans 10:18 was not Paul surveying the Roman world and reporting his findings — it was Paul quoting a Psalm about the heavens, word for word, in the Greek the Psalm already used. The overlap with Matthew 24:14 was real, but it was the overlap of a shared Scripture, not of two independent witnesses agreeing. I had wanted it to be the second. Discovering it was the first is not a setback to the investigation; it is the investigation working. I would rather reach the end of this with fewer arguments that hold than more that do not.**

## 21. Two New Methodological Rules

Both added to `00-methodology-current.md` and `00b-biblical-epistemology.md`.

### Rule — Role–Act Distinction

> **Do not equate performing an act with holding the formal ministry/office associated with that act.** A believer may teach, encourage, bear witness, or proclaim in a given circumstance without thereby being *a teacher, an evangelist, a prophet, or an apostle* in the specialized ministry sense. **Role language must be established independently of act language.**

### Rule — Universal-Language Scope Test

> **When an ancient text uses "all the earth," "the whole world," "every nation," or "all creation," do not assume either modern planetary exhaustiveness or merely local scope.** Determine, before assigning geographic scope: (a) the **semantic range** of the original term; (b) the **rhetorical genre**; (c) **whether the phrase is quoted from an earlier source, and if so whose word it is**; (d) the **historical audience**; (e) **comparable contemporary usage**.
>
> **Clause (c) is not optional and was added because of this stage.** Romans 10:18's *oikoumenē* is inherited from LXX Psalm 18:5 — a scope term in a quotation is evidence about the source text first, and about the quoting author only secondarily.

> ### **The scope test is going to be decisive at Matthew 24:14. It was written here, before we get there, deliberately — so it cannot be tuned to produce a preferred result once the answer starts to matter.**

## 23. Where This Leaves the Original Question

**The project's original question** — *is the prophetic worldwide proclamation associated with "the end" an unfinished commission imposed on every believer now?* — is now split cleanly into two by this stage:

1. **Is every believer the herald?** Romans 10 gives a real answer: **the chain runs through sending, and Scripture differentiates ministries.** Not established that every believer holds the office; not established that ordinary witness is excused.
2. **Was the predicted proclamation completed in the first century?** Romans 10:18 says Israel **heard**. But §13 shows Paul's universal phrasing is **quoted**, not surveyed. **q68 now carries this question**, and it must be answered from authors' own scope words.

> ### **Romans 10 was approached as the strongest text against the position. It did not overturn it — and it did not vindicate it either. It made both halves of the question answerable, which is more useful than a verdict.**
