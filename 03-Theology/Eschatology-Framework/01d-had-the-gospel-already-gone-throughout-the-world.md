# Stage 25 — Had the Gospel Already Gone "Throughout the World" Before AD 70?

> ### **This is q68, and it is the stage the whole preterist branch has been walking toward. Back to the main question.**

**The governing question:**

> **When Yeshua says the gospel will be proclaimed throughout the whole *oikoumenē* before "the end" (Matthew 24:14), do the New Testament writers later describe that proclamation as already having occurred within their own first-century horizon?**

**Neither shortcut is permitted.** Not *"Paul said whole world, therefore everyone everywhere heard."* Not *"ancient universal language is always exaggeration."* **Each text is tested.**

---

## 1. Method Carried In — Two Hazards Already Demonstrated

**Stage 23** proved a scope term can be **inherited** rather than chosen (Romans 10:18's *oikoumenē* is verbatim LXX). **Stage 24** proved **quotation behavior varies per quotation** (Romans 11:26 matches no witness). **Both hazards are live here**, and the Universal-Language Scope Test's clause (c) — *whose word is it?* — runs first on every text below.

---

## 2. 📛 Terminology Policy — Standing, Permanent

> ### **This study writes *earth · land · world · inhabited world · creation* according to what the underlying biblical term actually says. It does NOT use "globe," "globular," "planetary," or "planet Earth."**

**Not a cosmological position — exegetical hygiene.** Those words import a frame *gē*, *kosmos*, *oikoumenē*, and *ktisis* do not carry, and importing it decides the scope question before the investigation runs.

**Where "every geographical location on the earth" is meant, that is what gets written.**

*(Extends the existing "never write planet Earth" rule in ADR-0003 / [[00-methodology-current]]. **Applied retroactively 2026-08-16: twelve occurrences across nine files corrected**, including three instances of the phrase "modern planetary exhaustiveness" inside this project's own Universal-Language Scope Test — the rule's own statement violated the rule. Recorded rather than silently fixed.)*

## 3. Matthew 24:14 — Establishing the Term

> **Matthew 24:14** — *καὶ **κηρυχθήσεται** τοῦτο τὸ εὐαγγέλιον τῆς βασιλείας ἐν ὅλῃ τῇ **οἰκουμένῃ**… καὶ τότε ἥξει τὸ **τέλος**.*

**Verified at Stage 23:** *kēryssō* (2784) · ***oikoumenē* (3625)** · *telos* (5056).

***Oikoumenē*** = **inhabited world / inhabited earth**, and in first-century Roman contexts frequently the imperial world.

> ### **BRAKE, applied immediately and against the convenient reading: *oikoumenē* does NOT always equal "the Roman Empire." Context determines scope. The useful question is not what the word can mean but HOW NEW TESTAMENT AUTHORS THEMSELVES USE IT.**

---

## 4. 🔑 THE SCOPE-TERM TABLE — All Six Verified by Retrieval

**This table is the stage's central result.**

| Text | Greek scope term | Strong's | Whose word? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Matthew 24:14** | ἐν ὅλῃ τῇ **οἰκουμένῃ** | **3625** | **Matthew's / Yeshua's** |
| **Luke 2:1** | πᾶσαν τὴν **οἰκουμένην** | **3625** | **Luke's** |
| **Acts 11:28** | ἐφ' ὅλην τὴν **οἰκουμένην** | 3650 + **3625** | **Luke's** |
| **Romans 10:18** | πᾶσαν τὴν **γῆν** … τὰ πέρατα τῆς **οἰκουμένης** | 1093 + **3625** | ❗ **QUOTED verbatim from LXX Ps 18:5 — NOT Paul's own** |
| **Colossians 1:6** | ἐν παντὶ τῷ **κόσμῳ** | **2889** | **Paul's — but a DIFFERENT WORD** |
| **Colossians 1:23** | ἐν πάσῃ **κτίσει** τῇ ὑπὸ τὸν οὐρανόν | **2937** | **Paul's — a THIRD DIFFERENT WORD** |

### ❗❗ The result that was not expected

> ### **PAUL NEVER CHOOSES *OIKOUMENĒ* IN ANY PROCLAMATION TEXT.**
>
> **The one place it appears in Paul is Romans 10:18 — where Stage 23 verified he is quoting the Septuagint verbatim.** Where Paul writes in his own words he uses ***kosmos*** (Col 1:6) and ***ktisis*** (Col 1:23). **Two different words, neither of them Matthew's.**

**Consequence, stated against the project's own hypothesis:**

**The popular preterist move — *"Paul says the gospel reached the whole world, which is Matthew's oikoumenē, therefore Matthew 24:14 was fulfilled"* — collapses under retrieval.** It relies on **English** supplying a single word "world" for **three distinct Greek terms**, only one of which Matthew used, and that one Paul did not choose.

> ### **The bridge is not where it was thought to be. Luke, not Paul, is the real lexical control on Matthew's word.**

## 5. Luke's Two Controls — And They Are Strong

**Luke uses Matthew's exact term twice, in contexts where exhaustive reach is manifestly not meant.**

### Luke 2:1 — VERIFIED

> ESV: "a decree went out from Caesar Augustus that **all the world** should be registered." Greek: **πᾶσαν τὴν οἰκουμένην.**

**Augustus was not registering every geographical location on the earth.** The census operated within the **Roman imperial horizon**.

### Acts 11:28 — VERIFIED

> ESV: Agabus "foretold by the Spirit that there would be a great famine **over all the world**" — in the days of Claudius. Greek: **ἐφ' ὅλην τὴν οἰκουμένην.**

Whatever the precise breadth of the Claudian famines, **Luke is plainly using *oikoumenē* in an imperial / inhabited-world sense.**

> ### ❗ **THIS IS NEW TESTAMENT USAGE, NOT THEOLOGICAL CONVENIENCE. "Whole *oikoumenē*" demonstrably need not mean every geographical location on the earth — Luke uses it twice where it cannot.**

**Brake, and it is a real one:** ***Matthew is not Luke.*** This is **comparative New Testament usage**, which constrains the *possible* range of Matthew's term. **It does not define Matthew's term.** *(This is Intertextual Priority operating within the NT: earlier/parallel usage constrains possible meaning; immediate context still governs application.)*

---

## 6. Romans 15 — "Fulfilled," and the Verb Is Significant

> **Romans 15:19** — ESV: "from Jerusalem and all the way around to Illyricum **I have fulfilled the ministry of the gospel of Christ**."
> **Romans 15:23** — ESV: "**I no longer have any room for work in these regions.**"

**Millions in those regions had not personally heard Paul.** So what can the claim mean?

### Greek — VERIFIED BY INTERLINEAR RETRIEVAL

> **πεπληρωκέναι — *peplērōkenai*.** Lemma **πληρόω *plēroō*, Strong's 4137. PERFECT INFINITIVE ACTIVE.**

> ### ❗❗ **THIS IS THE SAME LEMMA AS MATTHEW 5:17'S "FULFILL" — the word this project studied at Stage 14.**

**And the perfect tense matters:** a completed action with a continuing resultant state. **Paul regards the gospel as *standing fulfilled* from Jerusalem around to Illyricum.**

> ### **WORKING DEFINITION THIS YIELDS: apostolic mission completion does NOT require every individual person to have been personally evangelized. A region can be regarded as evangelistically fulfilled in a FOUNDATIONAL sense while ordinary proclamation continues through established communities.**

**⚠️ TWO BRAKES, both necessary:**
1. **Shared lemma ≠ shared meaning.** *Plēroō* in Matthew 5:17 (Messiah and Torah) and in Romans 15:19 (Paul and a mission territory) are the same word doing **different work**. **Recorded as a lexical link, NOT as an argument that the two fulfilments are the same kind of event.** *(Stage 23's collapse of three "shared lemmas" to one is the precedent — this is exactly the error that pattern produces.)*
2. **Paul is describing HIS OWN pioneering assignment**, not certifying a prophecy.

**Open question q76.**

## 7. Colossians — Universal Language, Different Words

> **Colossians 1:6** — the gospel "**in the whole world** it is bearing fruit and increasing." **ἐν παντὶ τῷ κόσμῳ — *kosmos*, 2889. VERIFIED.**

> **Colossians 1:23** — the gospel "**has been proclaimed in all creation under heaven**." **ἐν πάσῃ κτίσει τῇ ὑπὸ τὸν οὐρανόν — *ktisis*, 2937. VERIFIED** (Stage 23).

**And Colossians 1:23's verb is κηρυχθέντος — aorist passive participle of *kēryssō* (2784), the same lemma as Matthew 24:14's future passive κηρυχθήσεται.**

> ### **THE VERB MATCHES; THE SCOPE NOUN DOES NOT. Matthew: future — "will be proclaimed." Colossians: aorist — "having been proclaimed." That tense pairing is genuinely striking and is the strongest single datum for a first-century fulfilment reading.**
>
> ### **But it must be stated at exactly that strength and no more: Paul does not write that Matthew 24:14 has been fulfilled.**

**ESTABLISHED:** Paul could regard the gospel's first-century expansion as legitimately describable in **comprehensively universal terms**.
**NOT ESTABLISHED:** that every individual had personally received an evangelistic presentation — **almost certainly too strong**, and Paul's own Romans 15 shows he did not mean it.

## 8. Representative Reach vs. Exhaustive Individual Hearing

**A distinction that may become central.** When biblical authors say **all nations**, they need not mean every individual of every nation has personally heard. They can mean **the message has penetrated the nations / reached the relevant world / been publicly proclaimed among the peoples.**

**Acts 2** dramatizes it: Parthians · Medes · Elamites · Mesopotamia · Judea · Cappadocia · Pontus · Asia · Phrygia · Pamphylia · Egypt · Libya · Rome · Crete · Arabia — and Luke summarizes as **"every nation under heaven."**

**Representative universal language.** The nations of the known world gathered at Jerusalem — and then dispersing.

⚠️ **Acts 2:5's Greek NOT retrieved this pass — q78.**

## 10. Acts 1:8 — The Geographic Program

> **Acts 1:8** — "you will be my witnesses in **Jerusalem** and in all **Judea** and **Samaria**, and to **the end of the earth**."

**That sequence is close to the structural outline of Acts itself**, which ends in **Rome** — the centre of the imperial *oikoumenē*.

**Jerusalem → Judea → Samaria → the nations.** Luke's narrative arc: **Jerusalem receives → diaspora carries → Samaria receives → Gentiles receive → Paul reaches imperial centres → Rome hears apostolic proclamation.**

> ### **Within Luke's narrative world, Jerusalem → Rome is a massive geographic completion. It does not mean everyone in the empire heard. It means the message reached its strategic and public horizon.**

**And this bears on the role question (Stage 23):** Acts 1:8 is spoken **to the apostolic group present**. That does not mean no later believer bears witness — **but Acts depicts the mission expanding through particular sent figures.** *(Role–Act Distinction applies.)*

## 11. 🔑 "The End" — and Why It Cannot Be Settled in English

> **Matthew 24:3** — the disciples ask about **συντέλεια τοῦ αἰῶνος — *synteleia tou aiōnos*, the consummation of the AGE.**

*(Previously verified in [[00g-matthew-24-and-the-oikoumene]] §35. Noted there: **CJB renders it "that the *olam hazeh* is ending"** — this present age.)*

### Three words that are not synonyms

| Greek | Strong's | Range |
|---|---|---|
| **αἰών** *aiōn* | 165 | **age / epoch** — a period |
| **κόσμος** *kosmos* | 2889 | world / order / world-system |
| **οἰκουμένη** *oikoumenē* | 3625 | inhabited world / inhabited land |

> ### ❗ **Older English renderings of Matthew 24:3 as "the end of the world" import a cosmological assumption the Greek does not carry. The Greek says CONSUMMATION OF THE AGE.**
>
> ### **That does not tell us WHICH age. It does establish that the actual question is: what age did the disciples understand to be ending? — which is central to preterism and is NOT answered by this stage.**

**And Matthew 24:14's "the end" is *telos* (5056)** — which this project has already had to brake on at Romans 10:4 (q63). **Destruction of creation? End of the Mosaic covenantal order? Destruction of Jerusalem? End of that eschatological crisis? Final judgment? Not answerable from English "end." Open question q77.**

## 12. The Methodological Consistency Point — Which Cuts Both Ways

**If someone insists Matthew 24:14 cannot have been fulfilled because the gospel had not reached every geographical location on the earth and every individual, Paul's statements create a problem** — *whole kosmos*, *all ktisis under heaven*.

One may answer: *Paul is using rhetorical universal language.* **Fair.**

> ### ❗ **But then the same rhetorical possibility must at least be CONSIDERED for Matthew's universal proclamation language. Demanding hyper-literal exhaustiveness from Matthew while allowing first-century rhetorical universality in Paul requires an explanation of the difference. That is a consistency requirement, not an argument for preterism.**

### ⚖️ And the brake against ourselves, at equal force

**"Paul says whole world, therefore Matthew 24:14 was fulfilled" is NOT ENOUGH.** Because: **different author · different vocabulary (verified — §4) · different rhetorical setting · different proposition.**

> ### **PAUL NEVER WRITES "MATTHEW 24:14 HAS NOW BEEN FULFILLED."**

### Honest epistemic status

| | Status |
|---|---|
| **Direct fulfilment claim** | ❌ **NOT PRESENT** |
| **Strong comparative evidence** | ✅ **PRESENT** |
| **Historical plausibility support** | ✅ **STRONG** |
| **Final identification** | ⏳ **Still requires Matthew's own chronology, and possibly external first-century evidence** |

## 13. Matthew 24's Internal Chronology — Serious, Not Conclusive

**Matthew 23:** judgment on **this generation** · the house left desolate. **Matthew 24:** no stone left on another → signs → **gospel proclaimed throughout the whole *oikoumenē*** → **the end** → later, **"this generation will not pass away until all these things take place"** (24:34).

**If 24:34 governs the preceding sequence, 24:14's proclamation belongs inside the same generational horizon.**

> ### **That is a serious argument. It is not conclusive by itself — and the apostolic universal-language claims make it historically more plausible without making it demonstrated.**

**Both halves of that sentence are load-bearing.**

## 14. Mission Completion ≠ Prohibition of Later Proclamation

**Directly relevant to the project's original question.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Apostolic / eschatological mission** — proclaim Messiah throughout the *oikoumenē* before the age's consummation | **can be completed** |
| **Ongoing kingdom witness** — teaching, evangelising, answering, discipling, serving, bearing witness | **continues** |

> ### **FULFILLED PROPHETIC PROCLAMATION ≠ NOBODY EVER PROCLAIMS AGAIN.**

**Structurally identical to distinctions already established: *fulfilment ≠ abolition* (Stage 14) and *removal ≠ replacement*.** **A crucial safeguard — and it protects against the preterist overreach, not the futurist one.**

## 16. But the Great Commission Remains — and Is Not Dodged

> **Matthew 28:18–20** — "**Go therefore and make disciples of all nations**… and behold, I am with you always, **to the end of the age**."

**The strongest counter-text after Romans 10.** **Nothing about universal evangelistic obligation may be concluded before it is examined:**

- exactly **who receives** Matthew 28
- what **"all nations"** (*panta ta ethnē*) means there
- what **"make disciples"** (*mathēteusate*) means
- whether the commission is **apostolic, ecclesial, or universally individual**
- what **"I am with you always, to the end of the age"** means
- **🔑 whether that "end of the age" (*synteleia tou aiōnos*) is the SAME phrase as Matthew 24:3 — and if so, what follows**

> ### **That last connection is potentially decisive and is the reason Stage 26 must be Matthew 28.**

## 17. ✍️ Manuscript Voice — The Apostles Spoke Like the World Had Been Reached

> **The proclamation question became much more difficult once I stopped assuming that "whole world" meant what the phrase means to a modern reader. Luke could describe Caesar's jurisdiction as the whole *oikoumenē*. Acts could describe Pentecost as gathering people from "every nation under heaven." Paul could say the gospel was bearing fruit in the whole world and had been proclaimed in all creation under heaven. He could even say that there was no longer room for his pioneering ministry across enormous regions while countless individuals still lived there.**
>
> **None of those statements proved that every human being had heard the gospel. But together they revealed something important about apostolic language: the mission could be regarded as reaching the nations without requiring an individual census of every person alive. Public establishment, representative reach and apostolic penetration of the inhabited world could satisfy language that sounded geographically exhaustive when translated into modern English.**
>
> **That did not prove Matthew 24:14 had been fulfilled. But it removed one of the strongest objections to the possibility. If Yeshua's *oikoumenē* belonged to the same first-century historical horizon in which Luke could describe Caesar's world and Paul could describe the gospel's reach, then the predicted proclamation before "the end" no longer required a modern communications network or knowledge of every land. The text itself had given me a different scale.**

And the restraint:

> **I also had to preserve the opposite discipline. Paul never wrote, "Matthew 24:14 is fulfilled." Comparative language could strengthen a fulfilment hypothesis without becoming an explicit fulfilment statement. Similarity was evidence, not identity.**

## 18. 🔬 Researcher's Commentary — The Bridge Moved

**This stage was expected to confirm a bridge. It relocated it instead.**

The anticipated result was that Paul's universal-language texts would corroborate Matthew's *oikoumenē*. **Retrieval showed Paul never chooses that word.** Colossians 1:6 is *kosmos*; Colossians 1:23 is *ktisis*; Romans 10:18's *oikoumenē* is quoted from the Septuagint. **The corroboration that actually exists comes from LUKE — Caesar's census and the Claudian famine — not from Paul.**

**This would have been invisible in English.** All three Greek words are routinely rendered "world," and the argument as commonly stated depends entirely on that English collapse. **The Equivalent-Scope Burden (§19) exists because of this stage.**

**Two findings were deliberately reported at less than their apparent strength:**
1. **Romans 15:19's *peplērōkenai* shares Matthew 5:17's lemma.** Attractive, and reported as a lexical link only — **because Stage 23 already demonstrated what happens when shared lemmas get counted as independent corroborations** (three collapsed to one).
2. **Colossians 1:23's aorist *kērychthentos* against Matthew 24:14's future *kērychthēsetai*.** The strongest single datum here — **and still not a fulfilment claim, because Paul does not make one.**

**On the terminology correction:** the project's own Universal-Language Scope Test contained the phrase *"modern planetary exhaustiveness"* — **the rule violated the rule it was stating.** Corrected in three files. **Recorded rather than silently fixed**, because a rule that can be broken by its own wording is worth remembering.

## 19. New Methodological Rule — Equivalent-Scope Burden

> **When two passages use different universal expressions, DO NOT assume identical scope merely because English translates both with "world," "all," or "earth."**
>
> **Compare — before treating them as equivalent:** original vocabulary · genre · rhetorical purpose · historical horizon · **and the author's own usage elsewhere**.

> ### **Keeps *oikoumenē* · *kosmos* · *gē* · *ktisis* · *aiōn* DISTINCT until context earns the synthesis.**

**Worked instance: this stage.** Six retrievals showed a scope argument that appeared to rest on one word actually resting on **four different words across three authors**, only one of whom used Matthew's term by choice.

**Companion to the Universal-Language Scope Test (Stage 23):** that test asks *what does this term mean and whose word is it?* — **this one asks whether two terms may be equated at all.**

## 20. What Stage 25 Established

### ✅ Established
- ***Oikoumenē* demonstrably need not mean every geographical location on the earth** — Luke 2:1 and Acts 11:28, both verified
- **Paul never chooses *oikoumenē* in a proclamation text** — verified across the corpus
- **Paul uses *kosmos* (Col 1:6) and *ktisis* (Col 1:23) — different words**
- **Romans 15:19's *peplērōkenai* is perfect infinitive active of *plēroō*** — Paul regarded a vast region as standing fulfilled **without every individual having heard him**
- **Colossians 1:23 shares Matthew 24:14's verb lemma**, aorist against future
- **Matthew 24:3 is *synteleia tou aiōnos* — consummation of the AGE, not of the earth**
- **Apostolic mission completion ≠ exhaustive individual evangelisation**

### ❌ Not established
- That Matthew 24:14 **was** fulfilled in the first century — **no NT author says so**
- Which **age** ends at Matthew 24:3 — **q77**
- What *telos* denotes at Matthew 24:14 — **q77**
- That *kosmos*, *ktisis*, and *oikoumenē* denote the same scope — **the Equivalent-Scope Burden now forbids assuming it**
- Proposition **D** — every later believer under the same universal apostolic commission

> ### **NET EFFECT: a major objection to the preterist reading was REMOVED (the scale objection), and a favoured argument for it was WEAKENED (the "Paul says whole world" bridge). The hypothesis is more plausible and less well-evidenced than when the stage began. That is what an honest stage looks like.**
