# Historical Observations

Facts about the world that prompted the questions below — not conclusions, not yet claims about *why*. In principle, a skeptic could go verify each of these independently. Explanations for *why* they're true belong in [[03-hypotheses]], not here.

1. Monumental stone architecture exists on every inhabited continent — not confined to Europe or the Middle East.
2. Many cities worldwide — including across the Americas, not just Old World cities — contain older buried architectural layers beneath present-day street level (basement windows at grade that make no sense relative to current street height; excavation work regularly exposing older structures underneath).
3. Ancient structures often appear more sophisticated than the construction methods commonly attributed to their period would explain.
4. Ziggurat-style and large ceremonial structures exist in multiple, geographically disconnected regions, including the Americas.
5. A recognizably similar architectural style (neoclassical / heavy-pillar civic architecture) appears across cities on multiple continents within a compressed historical window — noted specifically in Montevideo as a local example, alongside similar building stock elsewhere in the world.
6. Political borders, civilizations, and place names have changed repeatedly and substantially throughout recorded history.

These observations are what motivated the question of whether the conventional historical timeline is complete — see [[03-hypotheses]] for where that question leads.
