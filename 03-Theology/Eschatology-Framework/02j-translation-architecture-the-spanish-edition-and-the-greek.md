# Side Branch 45¾ — Translation Architecture: the Spanish Edition and the Greek

> ### **THE AUTHOR RAISED THIS IMMEDIATELY AFTER THE GREEK AUDIT, AND IT IS AN ARCHITECTURAL PROBLEM, NOT A DETAIL:**
>
> ### *"That 'might' is in English. I don't know what it would be in the Reina-Valera version… I wouldn't want it to just translate. Oh, I came across the English word 'might' and then in Spanish you just translate that word — when maybe that word isn't actually inside the Reina-Valera version."*
>
> ### **HE IS RIGHT. AND THE CHECK PRODUCED SOMETHING BETTER *AND* WORSE THAN HE EXPECTED.**

---

## 1. ✅ The "might" problem does NOT exist in Spanish

**RVR1960, Apocalipsis 20:3 — VERIFIED:** ✅

> **"y lo arrojó al abismo, y lo encerró, y puso su sello sobre él, **para que no engañase más a las naciones**, hasta que fuesen cumplidos mil años; y después de esto **debe ser desatado** por un poco de tiempo."**

### Line it up against the Greek

| | |
|---|---|
| **Greek** ✅ | **ἵνα μὴ πλανήσῃ ἔτι τὰ ἔθνη** — *hina* + *mē* + **AORIST SUBJUNCTIVE** |
| **Spanish** ✅ | **para que no engañase más a las naciones** — *para que* + no + **IMPERFECT SUBJUNCTIVE** |
| **English** | "so that he **might** not deceive the nations any longer" — purpose conjunction + **A SUPPLIED MODAL AUXILIARY** |

> ### ❗❗ **SPANISH DOES *EXACTLY* WHAT GREEK DOES: PURPOSE CONJUNCTION + SUBJUNCTIVE MOOD. IT NEEDS NO MODAL AUXILIARY, BECAUSE SPANISH RETAINS A PRODUCTIVE SUBJUNCTIVE THAT ENGLISH LARGELY LOST.**
>
> ### **THEREFORE THERE IS NO SPANISH WORD CORRESPONDING TO "MIGHT" AT ALL — AND AT THIS VERSE, *SPANISH IS STRUCTURALLY CLOSER TO THE GREEK THAN ENGLISH IS.***

**And the pattern holds through the verse: "hasta que **fuesen** cumplidos" (imperfect subjunctive) renders ἄχρι **τελεσθῇ** (aorist subjunctive) with the same mood; "más" renders **ἔτι**, "any longer"; "**debe** ser desatado" renders **δεῖ** λυθῆναι.**

> ### 📌 **SO THE AUTHOR'S INSTINCT WAS EXACTLY RIGHT AND THE ANSWER IS CLEAN: THE "MIGHT" DISCUSSION IS A PURELY ENGLISH ARTEFACT. TRANSLATED INTO SPANISH IT WOULD DISCUSS A WORD THAT IS NOT THERE — AND WOULD LOOK LIKE NONSENSE TO A SPANISH READER HOLDING A REINA-VALERA.**

---

## 2. 🛑🛑 BUT THE REAL DAMAGE IS SOMEWHERE ELSE — AND IT HITS THE LOAD-BEARING ARGUMENT

**The single strongest finding in Stage 45 was that ἔζησαν (20:4) and οὐκ ἔζησαν (20:5) are THE SAME VERB IN ADJACENT VERSES.**

**Now compare the Spanish — BOTH VERIFIED:** ✅

| | **20:4** | **20:5** | Parallel? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SBLGNT** ✅ | **καὶ ἔζησαν** | **οὐκ ἔζησαν** | ✅ **IDENTICAL VERB** |
| **ESV** | "they **came to life**" | "did not **come to life**" | ✅ preserved |
| **RVA-2015** ✅ | "Ellos **volvieron a vivir**" | "los demás muertos **no volvieron a vivir**" | ✅ **PRESERVED** |
| **RVR1960** ✅ | "y **vivieron**" | "los otros muertos **no volvieron a vivir**" | ❌ **BROKEN** |

> ### ❗❗❗ **RVR1960 RENDERS THE TWO OCCURRENCES *DIFFERENTLY*: "vivieron" in verse 4, but "no volvieron a vivir" in verse 5.**
>
> ### **A SPANISH READER USING RVR1960 SEES "THEY LIVED" AND "THEY DID NOT RETURN TO LIFE" — AND MAY NEVER NOTICE THE PARALLEL THAT CARRIES THE ENTIRE ARGUMENT.**
>
> ### **THE STRONGEST TEXTUAL PRESSURE THIS PROJECT HAS DOCUMENTED IS *PARTIALLY INVISIBLE* IN THE MOST WIDELY USED SPANISH BIBLE.**

**⚠️ And note what this is NOT: it is not an error by RVR1960. "Volver a vivir" is a natural Spanish rendering of coming back to life, and using it only where the sense is clearest is a defensible translator's choice. THE PROBLEM IS NOT INACCURACY — IT IS THAT AN ARGUMENT BUILT ON *VERBAL IDENTITY* CANNOT SURVIVE A TRANSLATION THAT VARIES ITS WORDING FOR STYLE.**

> ### 🔑 **THIS IS EXACTLY THE AUTHOR'S WORRY REALIZED — AND IN THE OPPOSITE DIRECTION FROM WHERE HE LOOKED FOR IT. He worried the English "might" would be mistranslated into Spanish. "Might" turned out fine. THE RESURRECTION VERB — THE ONE THE WHOLE MILLENNIAL QUESTION HANGS ON — IS THE ONE THAT DEGRADES (q215).**

## 3. 🔑 And the direction of advantage is NOT constant — Spanish is sometimes BETTER

**Revelation 20:6 — the second death has no *what* over them?**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **SBLGNT** ✅ | **οὐκ ἔχει ἐξουσίαν** | *exousia* — **AUTHORITY / RIGHT / JURISDICTION** |
| **ESV** | "has no **power**" | ⚠️ **loses the authority sense** |
| **RVR1960** ✅ | "no tiene **potestad**" | ✅ **POTESTAD = authority, jurisdiction, legal power** |
| **RVA-2015** ✅ | "no tiene ningún **poder**" | ⚠️ loses it, like ESV |

> ### ❗❗ **RVR1960's *POTESTAD* RENDERS ἐξουσία MORE ACCURATELY THAN THE ESV'S "POWER" DOES — AND MORE ACCURATELY THAN RVA-2015 DOES.**
>
> ### **SO THE ADVANTAGE SWITCHES *WITHIN THE SAME PASSAGE*: RVA-2015 preserves the resurrection parallel that RVR1960 breaks, while RVR1960 preserves the authority sense that RVA-2015 and the ESV both lose.**
>
> ### **NO SINGLE TRANSLATION IN EITHER LANGUAGE IS RELIABLE ACROSS THE WHOLE PASSAGE. THAT IS THE FINDING.**

**📌 And a smaller one, same direction: RVR1960's 20:4 "los que recibieron **facultad** de juzgar" carries the authority sense of κρίμα ἐδόθη αὐτοῖς well.**

## 4. ⚠️ A retrieval uncertainty, recorded rather than smoothed

**The RVA-2015 retrieval of 20:3 ENDED at *"hasta que se cumpliesen los mil años"* — WITHOUT the *"y después de esto debe ser desatado por un poco de tiempo"* clause that RVR1960 has and that SBLGNT's μετὰ ταῦτα δεῖ λυθῆναι αὐτὸν μικρὸν χρόνον requires.**

> ### 🛑 **THIS IS ALMOST CERTAINLY A RETRIEVAL TRUNCATION, NOT A TEXTUAL OMISSION BY RVA-2015. IT IS RECORDED AS A *RETRIEVAL UNCERTAINTY*, NOT AS A CLAIM ABOUT THE EDITION.**
>
> ### **Asserting "RVA-2015 omits the release clause" on this evidence would be exactly the error the Citation-Numbering Integrity rule was written to prevent (q193, q207). RE-RETRIEVE BEFORE ANY USE (q215).**

## 5. 🆕 THE ARCHITECTURAL CONSEQUENCE

**The author proposed the solution himself: *"the way I would prefer it is that we automatically include the Greek even in the Spanish version, because that's going to be necessary for a factual attribution to what it actually says."***

> ### **THAT IS CORRECT, AND THE EVIDENCE ABOVE MAKES IT NECESSARY RATHER THAN MERELY PREFERABLE.**

### 🆕 NEW RULE — Translation-Independent Source Anchoring

> **Every source-language claim appears in EVERY language edition with the Hebrew/Aramaic/Greek PRESENT — because the source text is the only element common to all editions.**
>
> ### **THE GREEK IS NOT A SCHOLARLY ORNAMENT IN THE SPANISH EDITION. IT IS THE ONLY THING THAT MAKES THE TWO EDITIONS THE SAME BOOK.**

**Worked example of what the Spanish note should look like — a Spanish explanation OF THE GREEK, not a translation of the English paragraph:**

> **El griego no contiene una palabra independiente equivalente al inglés *might*. La construcción **ἵνα μὴ + subjuntivo** expresa finalidad: "para que no engañase más a las naciones."**
>
> **✅ Y la Reina-Valera 1960 conserva aquí con bastante claridad la fuerza final de la construcción griega — usa el subjuntivo, igual que el griego.**

**⚠️ Note the verified detail: RVR1960 reads *engañase* (IMPERFECT subjunctive), not *engañe* (present subjunctive). The difference is small but it is the kind of thing that must be quoted, not approximated (S15).**

### 🆕 NEW RULE — Translation-Divergence Notes Are Written, Not Translated

> **Any commentary about a TRANSLATION ARTEFACT must be composed SEPARATELY FOR EACH LANGUAGE EDITION, against THAT edition's actual base translation. It may NEVER be translated across.**

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ **FORBIDDEN** | translating the English "might" discussion into Spanish — **it would analyse a word that does not exist in Reina-Valera** |
| ✅ **REQUIRED** | the Spanish edition instead notes that **RVR1960 renders 20:4 and 20:5 with different verbs where the Greek uses one**, which is the divergence a Spanish reader actually needs |

> ### ❗ **THE TWO EDITIONS WILL THEREFORE CONTAIN *DIFFERENT TRANSLATION NOTES AT DIFFERENT VERSES* — AND THAT IS CORRECT, NOT A DEFECT. Each edition must warn its own reader about its own base text.**

### 🆕 NEW RULE — Source-Language-First Translation

> **The Spanish edition must NOT be produced by translating the finished English prose.** For every passage where the argument depends on **wording · grammar · modality · tense/aspect · lexical range · or a translation divergence**, RE-CHECK, in this order:

> **1. the Greek/Hebrew/Aramaic source text · 2. the English translation(s) used in the English edition · 3. the selected Spanish translation(s), especially Reina-Valera · 4. WHETHER THE EXPLANATORY NOTE IS ACTUALLY NECESSARY IN SPANISH AT ALL.**

> ### **IF AN AMBIGUITY EXISTS ONLY IN ENGLISH, DO NOT MECHANICALLY TRANSLATE IT INTO SPANISH.**
> ### **IF THE SPANISH TRANSLATION INTRODUCES A *DIFFERENT* AMBIGUITY, ADDRESS THAT SPANISH-SPECIFIC ISSUE INDEPENDENTLY.**
> ### **IN BOTH EDITIONS, PRESERVE THE ORIGINAL-LANGUAGE EXPRESSION WHENEVER IT MATERIALLY CARRIES THE ARGUMENT.**

### 🏷️ NEW RULE — Tag every note by scope

> **Do not let commentary caused solely by ONE target language become universal manuscript commentary. Tag it:**

| Tag | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **`SOURCE-LANGUAGE`** | belongs in **every** edition | ἵνα μὴ πλανήσῃ ἔτι τὰ ἔθνη is a purpose clause; ἔτι implies prior deception |
| **`ENGLISH-SPECIFIC`** | English edition only | *"The English auxiliary* might *can sound more tentative than the underlying Greek purpose clause actually is."* |
| **`SPANISH-SPECIFIC`** | Spanish edition only | *"La RVR1960 traduce ἔζησαν y οὐκ ἔζησαν con formas distintas —* vivieron *y* no volvieron a vivir *— donde el griego usa el mismo verbo."* |

> ### **THAT TAGGING SAVES AN ENORMOUS CLEANUP LATER, AND IT IS CHEAP NOW AND EXPENSIVE RETROACTIVELY.**

### 📖 GLOSSARY — Translation Artifact

> **A wording, ambiguity, emphasis, or apparent theological problem CREATED OR AMPLIFIED BY A TRANSLATION CHOICE that is not present in the same form in the source-language text.**
>
> ### 🛑 **TRANSLATION ARTIFACTS MUST NOT BE TREATED AS SOURCE-TEXT EVIDENCE.**

**Standing example: Revelation 20:3's English *"might."***

**📌 And a vocabulary correction recorded because the author asked for the right word: he reached for *"terse,"* which means concise or compressed — not the phenomenon he was describing. The term he wanted is **TRANSLATION ARTIFACT** (or *target-language artifact* / *modal ambiguity*). Recorded so the manuscript uses the accurate term.**

### 🧭 The resulting production architecture

> ### ❌ **NOT: Greek → English book → translated Spanish book.**
>
> ### ✅ **BUT: GREEK/HEBREW RESEARCH SPINE → ENGLISH BRANCH · AND · GREEK/HEBREW RESEARCH SPINE → SPANISH BRANCH.**

| Layer | Contents |
|---|---|
| **RESEARCH SPINE** | Greek/Hebrew/Aramaic · textual variants · lexical and grammatical analysis · the findings themselves |
| **ENGLISH EDITION** | the same conclusions expressed through English translations, with **`ENGLISH-SPECIFIC`** artifacts explained |
| **SPANISH EDITION** | the same conclusions expressed **independently** through Spanish translations, with **`SPANISH-SPECIFIC`** artifacts explained |

> ### **THE TWO BRANCHES SHARE FINDINGS AND SOURCE TEXT. THEY DO NOT SHARE TRANSLATION COMMENTARY.**

### 🆕 NEW RULE — Name the Base Translation

> **Every edition must state WHICH translation it is built on (ESV; RVR1960 or RVA-2015 or another), because the divergence notes are only valid against a named base.** **An unnamed base makes every translation note unverifiable.**

## 7. 🔬 Researcher's Commentary

**The author asked whether an English translation artefact would survive translation into Spanish. The honest answer turned out to be layered.**

**The artefact he named does not survive — because it never existed in Spanish.** Spanish kept the subjunctive mood that English lost, so *"para que no engañase"* does natively what Greek's *ἵνα μὴ πλανήσῃ* does. **At that verse, Spanish is closer to the source than English is.** A translated commentary would have solemnly analysed a word no Spanish reader can find.

**But the check found something worse in the other direction, and it hits the argument that matters most.** RVR1960 renders ἔζησαν as *"vivieron"* and οὐκ ἔζησαν as *"no volvieron a vivir."* **The verbal identity that carries the entire first-resurrection pressure is broken in the most widely used Spanish Bible** — not by error, but by ordinary stylistic variation. **An argument built on one verb appearing twice cannot survive a translator who reasonably varies the wording.**

**And then the advantage switched back inside the same chapter: RVR1960's *potestad* renders ἐξουσία better than the ESV's "power," and better than RVA-2015's *poder*. So neither language holds a consistent edge, and no single translation in either language is reliable across the passage.**

**That is why the author's proposed solution is not a preference but a requirement. The Greek is the only element the two editions share. Everything else — including which verses need a warning — differs by language.**

**One thing was deliberately not asserted: the RVA-2015 retrieval of 20:3 came back short of the release clause. That is almost certainly truncation, and it is recorded as a retrieval uncertainty rather than a claim about the edition — because "this Bible omits a clause" is exactly the kind of statement that must never rest on one imperfect fetch.**

## 📌 BUT THREE THINGS GENUINELY EXCEED ADR-0008, AND THEY NEED THE AUTHOR'S DECISION

### ⚠️ 1. The RVR1960 designation is now CHALLENGED BY EVIDENCE

**ADR-0008 §5 designates RVR1960 as the Spanish Bible edition. That designation predates this finding.**

> ### ❗ **RVR1960 BREAKS THE ἔζησαν / οὐκ ἔζησαν PARALLEL THAT CARRIES THE ENTIRE FIRST-RESURRECTION ARGUMENT — AND RVA-2015 PRESERVES IT.**
>
> ### **THAT IS NOT A REASON TO SWITCH. RVR1960 also renders ἐξουσία BETTER (*potestad*), and it carries far more familiarity and liturgical weight. IT IS A REASON TO *DECIDE DELIBERATELY* RATHER THAN BY INHERITANCE.**
>
> ### **THE THIRD OPTION MAY BE BEST: KEEP RVR1960 AS THE BASE *AND* CARRY RVA-2015 AS A SECOND SPANISH WITNESS AT TRANSLATION-SENSITIVE VERSES — exactly as ADR-0003 already carries ESV *and* CJB in English.**

### 🆕 2. ADR-0008 permits translating explanatory prose. THIS BRANCH NARROWS THAT.

**ADR-0008 says *"explanatory prose may be translated and adapted."* Generally true — but this branch found a class where it is FORBIDDEN:**

> ### **COMMENTARY ABOUT A *TRANSLATION ARTIFACT* MAY NEVER BE TRANSLATED ACROSS EDITIONS, BECAUSE IT ANALYSES A WORD THAT MAY NOT EXIST IN THE TARGET BIBLE.**
>
> ### **The English "might" discussion is the standing proof: rendered into Spanish it would examine a word no Reina-Valera reader can find. A NARROW BUT REAL EXCEPTION TO ADR-0008's PROSE ALLOWANCE.**

### 🆕 3. The scope-tagging system did not previously exist

**`SOURCE-LANGUAGE` / `ENGLISH-SPECIFIC` / `SPANISH-SPECIFIC` is new operational machinery that makes ADR-0008's rule *enforceable* rather than merely stated.**
