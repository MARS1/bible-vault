# Historical & Theological Hypotheses

Ideas explored, not established facts. Held loosely, deliberately kept out of [[01-convictions]] regardless of how strongly felt — that's the point of this box.

1. Some monumental architecture (see [[02-historical-observations]]) may have originated during or around the millennial reign described in Revelation 20.
2. Advanced technology may have existed in earlier civilizations and later been lost or deliberately suppressed (the "Tartaria" theory — includes claims of buildings functioning as free-energy/wireless-power towers, evidenced by period photographs predating any plausible photo-doctoring capability).
   **Sourced (researched 2026-08-08):** Traced to Anatoly Fomenko (Russian mathematician, Moscow State University), whose "New Chronology" theory from the 1970s proposed a global "Great Tartaria" empire erased from history. Popularized in esoteric circles by Nikolai Levashov (1961–2012) through the 70s–80s. Exploded as an internet phenomenon from 2016 onward — r/Tartaria (45,000+ members), r/tartarianarchitecture, a 129,000+ member Facebook group, and dedicated YouTube/TikTok content (2.5M+ views). Mainstream historians treat "Tartary" as a vague 18th–19th-century European cartographic label for Central Asian/Siberian regions, not a unified advanced civilization — the Russian Geographical Society has explicitly called the modern theory an "extremist fantasy." Sources: Wikipedia "Anatoly Fomenko," "New Chronology (Fomenko)," "Nikolai Levashov"; All That's Interesting; Factually.co fact-checks.
3. So-called "mud floods" buried earlier-era architecture; possibly connected to divine judgment against that earlier civilization — this specific causal claim is unprovable and held as a hunch, not a position.
   **Sourced (researched 2026-08-08):** No single documented originator for the "mud flood" framing specifically — it coalesced out of the same late-20th-century Russian pseudohistory (Fomenko's New Chronology, see item 2) and became a visual, image-driven theory on Reddit/YouTube/Facebook/TikTok post-2016, tightly linked to the Tartaria community as its explanation for Tartaria's disappearance. Mainstream architectural historians and geologists attribute the visual evidence (buried/bricked windows at grade) to ordinary ground-level rise from urban waste accumulation (see [[02-historical-observations]] item 2) and to intentional 19th-century semi-basement design for servant quarters — not a catastrophic flood event, for which there is no geological record. Sources: SciCentric; Factually.co.
4. The conventional historical chronology may contain significant gaps or fabrications. Supporting pattern noticed: many Wikipedia entries for older monumental buildings say "founded" rather than "constructed," list unusually young architects with thin biographical records who often died young, and attribute implausibly fast, implausibly expensive construction to them by today's cost/knowledge standards.
5. Some ancient civilizations may fit into the biblical timeline differently than conventionally assumed.
6. The relationship between biblical chronology and secular world history deserves much deeper study — current secular chronology is not necessarily accepted as-is.
7. A geocentric / enclosed-earth cosmological model may be the correct reading of the biblical cosmology (firmament — Genesis 1; "four corners of the earth" — Isaiah 11:12, Revelation 7:1; a circular ice-wall boundary associated with Antarctica). This is a personal reading, not the historic consensus reading among Christians of those same passages — held here as hypothesis, not settled conviction, pending further study of how the four-corners/circle language has historically been interpreted.
   **Sourced (researched 2026-08-08):** The modern biblical-literalist enclosed-earth movement (distinct from historic pre-Copernican geocentrism) traces to Dr. Gerardus D. Bouw, who reorganized the Association for Biblical Astronomy in 1991 (from the earlier Tychonian Society) and authored "The Biblical Firmament." Robert Sungenis is the movement's most visible contemporary proponent — co-authored *Galileo Was Wrong* (2007) and co-produced the documentary *The Principle* (2014, narrated by Kate Mulgrew, who later said she was misled about its actual subject). Mainstream science rejects geocentrism on direct observational grounds — stellar parallax (Bessel, 1838) and Foucault's pendulum (1851) both independently confirm Earth's motion, and GPS/spacecraft navigation depend on heliocentric calculations working correctly. Notably, Answers in Genesis (a leading young-earth creationist organization, not a mainstream-science source) explicitly lists geocentrism as "an argument to avoid" — i.e. even biblical-literalist creationists don't consider this reading necessary for or supported by their own hermeneutic. Sources: Wikipedia "Gerardus D. Bouw," "Robert Sungenis," "The Principle (documentary)"; Answers in Genesis, "Geocentrism and Creation."
8. Saints from the millennial reign are already secured and cannot die again; some associate their location with the center of the earth, near a place called Rupes Nigra.
9. After the millennium and during the "little season," people who lived in an earlier era may be given a second chance to "respawn" on earth — proposed explanation for cases of people who seem to carry knowledge or recollections that don't fit their apparent lifetime. **RESOLVED (researched 2026-08-08): no biblical support found.** Revelation 20:7–15 (the actual "little season" passage) describes only Satan's release/final deception/destruction (vv.7–10) and the great white throne judgment of the dead (vv.11–15) — nothing describing people returning or reappearing on earth. High confidence (per researching agent) this has no textual basis anywhere in Scripture. Downgraded from "unconfirmed hypothesis" to "extrabiblical inference with no scriptural grounding" — keep for personal record, but don't present this as something Scripture teaches even loosely.
