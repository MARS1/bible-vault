## 10. ✍️ Manuscript Voice — The Same Words, The Wrong Pair

> **The point of this stage was to attack our own assumption. We had been reading chapter nineteen, then chapter twenty, and quietly treating that as one thing following another. So I tried to break it.**
>
> **The narrative broke it the way I expected. Chapter sixteen has all three adversaries alive and working — dragon, beast, false prophet, each with spirits coming out of his mouth. Chapter twenty has two of them already in the lake and only the dragon left to release. You cannot have a figure both operating and permanently disposed of in the same scene. So the sequence held.**

> **Then I laid the actual sentences side by side, and they said something else. The gathering clause in sixteen and the gathering clause in twenty are the same words in the same order. The one in nineteen is built differently — different verb form, different preposition. So the words tie sixteen to twenty, which is exactly the pair the actors say must be apart.**
>
> **I do not have a way to make those agree, and I am not going to pick the one I prefer. Both are real. The best I can offer is that a shared formula might mark a shared kind of event rather than a shared occurrence — and that is a thing I can test rather than assert, which is at least an honest place to leave it.**

> **The one clean thing today was fire. Ezekiel gave John the names. But the fire that comes down and devours is not Ezekiel's sentence — it is Elijah's, five words of it, verb and all.**
