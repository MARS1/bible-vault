# Stage 64 — Who Are “the Saints” in Revelation?

Good. We'll make **64–66 the closing sequence**:

**64 — Identify the saints from Revelation itself**
**65 — Final Eschatological Evidence Ledger**
**66 — The Position We Can Actually Defend**

Then the investigation closes with unresolved matters explicitly preserved as open questions rather than endlessly investigated.

The narrow question for Stage 64 is:

> **When Satan gathers Gog and Magog against “the camp of the saints” after the millennium, who has Revelation already taught its reader to recognize as “the saints”?**

We don't need to guess. John uses the terminology repeatedly.

---

## 64.1 — The word itself: ἅγιοι (*hagioi*)

Revelation 20:9 describes:

> **τὴν παρεμβολὴν τῶν ἁγίων**
> *tēn parembolēn tōn hagiōn*

“The camp of **the holy ones / saints**.”

**ἅγιος (*hagios*)** means holy, consecrated, set apart. The plural substantive **οἱ ἅγιοι** means “the holy ones,” conventionally translated “the saints.”

The crucial point is that Revelation 20 doesn't suddenly introduce an unidentified group.

John has already been talking about **the saints** throughout Revelation.

So Revelation itself should define them.

---

# 64.2 — Revelation 12 gives perhaps the clearest identification

After the dragon is cast down, Revelation 12:17 says he goes to make war against the rest of the woman's offspring:

> those **keeping the commandments of God**
> and **holding the testimony of Yeshua**.

Greek:

> **τῶν τηρούντων τὰς ἐντολὰς τοῦ θεοῦ
> καὶ ἐχόντων τὴν μαρτυρίαν Ἰησοῦ**

That gives us two characteristics:

**faithfulness/obedience toward Yah**

*

**testimony of Yeshua.**

That's enormously important for our project.

Revelation doesn't present the faithful merely as:

> “people who identify culturally as Christians.”

Nor merely:

> “ethnic Israelites.”

The dragon's target is identified through covenant faithfulness **and Yeshua**.

---

# 64.3 — Revelation 14 repeats essentially the same fingerprint

Immediately after the beast/mark complex, Revelation 14:12 says:

> “Here is the endurance of the saints…”

And then identifies them:

> **οἱ τηροῦντες τὰς ἐντολὰς τοῦ θεοῦ
> καὶ τὴν πίστιν Ἰησοῦ**

Those keeping:

> **the commandments of God**

and:

> **the faith/faithfulness of Yeshua.**

The precise grammatical nuance of **πίστιν Ἰησοῦ** deserves care—it can be discussed as faith in Yeshua / faithfulness associated with Yeshua—but we don't need to resolve that question to see the larger identification.

Again:

**Yah's commandments**

*

**Yeshua**

*

**endurance under pressure.**

And John explicitly calls these people:

> **the saints.**

That's about as strong an internal definition as we could ask for.

---

# 64.4 — Revelation 13 shows what “war against the saints” can mean

This helps with your earlier question about whether Revelation 20 requires modern conventional armies.

Revelation 13:7 says the beast is permitted:

> **ποιῆσαι πόλεμον μετὰ τῶν ἁγίων**

“to make **war — polemon — against the saints**.”

And to overcome them.

But the surrounding beast system includes:

**worship pressure**

**authority**

**economic exclusion**

**marking/allegiance**

**death for refusal**

and persecution.

Therefore Revelation itself demonstrates that:

> **“war against the saints” doesn't necessarily mean the saints themselves form an army and fight tanks with rifles.**

*Polemos* can describe an organized assault upon Yah's people manifested through political, religious, economic and lethal coercion.

That's already established from Revelation's own usage.

---

# 64.5 — But that does NOT turn Revelation 20 into the mark of the beast

This distinction you raised is exactly right.

Revelation 13 specifically gives:

> no buying or selling without the mark.

That belongs to the **beast complex**.

And according to Revelation's sequence we've established:

**beast + false prophet judged**

↓

**Satan bound**

↓

**millennium**

↓

**Satan released**

↓

**Gog/Magog.**

Therefore we cannot simply take:

> “can't buy or sell”

and transplant it across the millennium into Revelation 20.

And certainly Scripture doesn't say:

> **microchip.**

That's a modern interpretive overlay.

Whatever the mark ultimately signifies historically, Revelation itself never says “microchip.”

So our post-millennial diagnostic remains:

> **deception → nations → gathering → war → saints/camp/beloved city.**

Not:

> “new payment technology appears → therefore Gog/Magog.”

That eliminates an enormous amount of modern prophetic sensationalism immediately.

---

# 64.6 — Revelation's saints include martyrs—but are not reducible to martyrs

This is another important distinction.

Revelation repeatedly associates saints with persecution and blood.

Revelation 6 gives the slain beneath the altar.

Revelation 12 describes those who overcome through the Lamb and their testimony, who do not love their lives unto death.

Revelation 13 depicts war against saints.

Revelation 17–18 associates Babylon with the blood of saints.

Revelation 20 explicitly highlights those beheaded because of Yeshua's testimony and God's word.

So martyrdom is enormously important.

But:

> **saint ≠ definitionally “someone who has already been martyred.”**

Revelation 14:12 addresses living faithful people enduring.

Therefore the category is broader:

> **Yah's faithful covenant people associated with Yeshua, whether ultimately martyred or not.**

That fits our earlier Pauline distinction as well.

---

# 64.7 — What about Israel?

We shouldn't erase Israel from this.

Revelation is saturated with Israelite imagery:

**twelve tribes**

**144,000**

**Moses**

**temple**

**Jerusalem**

**Zion**

**covenant language**

**twelve apostles**

**New Jerusalem's twelve tribal gates.**

So this isn't Christianity detached from Israel.

But neither can “saints” simply mean:

> **ethnic descendants of Jacob irrespective of Yeshua.**

Revelation's own repeated fingerprint includes:

> **the testimony/faith of Yeshua.**

That's decisive.

This fits the larger covenant chain we've already developed:

**Israel**

↓

**Messiah**

↓

**faithful remnant**

↓

**Gentiles incorporated/grafted into covenant people**

rather than:

> Israel discarded → unrelated Gentile religion replaces Israel.

So for our historical diagnostic, neither:

> “attack on modern ethnic Jews = automatically Gog/Magog”

nor:

> “attack on institutional Christianity = automatically Gog/Magog”

is sufficient.

We need Revelation's actual category.

---

# 64.8 — What about “the camp”?

This becomes clearer now.

Revelation 20 doesn't merely say:

> “they persecuted some saints.”

It says Gog/Magog:

> went up over the breadth of **γῆ (*gē*)**

and:

> **surrounded the camp of the saints and the beloved city.**

As we previously found, **παρεμβολή (*parembolē*)** means camp/encampment and carries strong Israel/covenant-community associations.

That suggests something more concentrated or corporately identifiable than:

> isolated believers randomly scattered everywhere with no meaningful communal relationship.

I would therefore preserve your instinct:

**“surrounded” should actually mean something.**

We shouldn't metaphorize it away simply because we can't presently identify the event.

But neither can we demand a perfect circular military formation around one geographic coordinate.

The imagery can communicate:

> **Yah's covenant community brought under comprehensive hostile encirclement.**

That could have geographical, political, military and covenant dimensions simultaneously.

---

# 64.9 — And what about the “beloved city”?

This remains intentionally open.

Jerusalem/Zion is the obvious biblical resonance.

But Revelation culminates with:

> **New Jerusalem**

and Hebrews/Paul give us heavenly/covenantal Jerusalem language.

So we cannot yet prove:

> Revelation 20:9 = armies physically surrounding present-day geographic Jerusalem.

Nor can we prove:

> beloved city has absolutely no geographic dimension.

Our strongest present formulation remains:

> **“camp of the saints + beloved city” represents Yah's covenant people centered in Zion/Jerusalem imagery, with the exact geographical dimension unresolved.**

That is enough for the book.

We don't need to locate it on a speculative map.

---

# 64.10 — And that addresses Rupes Nigra too

Your Rupes Nigra possibility can remain an **open speculative geographical hypothesis**, but nothing in Revelation 20 presently establishes it.

Most importantly, Revelation doesn't require us to find:

> a physically hidden settlement at a proposed geographic center.

We already have an internally coherent biblical meaning for:

> camp + beloved city

without requiring that hypothesis.

So it belongs, if anywhere, in an **Open Questions appendix**, not the evidentiary chain.

That is exactly the kind of distinction Stage 66 needs to preserve.

---

# 64.11 — Can we identify the saints historically today?

Here's where we stop.

We can define the **biblical fingerprint**.

We cannot responsibly point at one modern organization and announce:

> **“There they are.”**

A denomination calling itself Christian doesn't automatically qualify.

A Torah-observant movement doesn't automatically qualify.

A Messianic group doesn't automatically qualify.

Ethnic Israel doesn't automatically exhaust the category.

Someone saying “Jesus” doesn't automatically establish covenant faithfulness.

Revelation's fingerprint concerns people characterized by:

> **faithfulness to Yah + Yeshua + endurance against pressure to abandon that allegiance.**

Yah ultimately knows His own.

That is enough for our eschatological investigation.

---

# 64.12 — So could Gog/Magog already be developing without the final encirclement having happened?

Textually, **yes, as a hypothesis**.

Remember Ezekiel's progression that we already studied:

**preparation**

↓

**gathering/mustering**

↓

**movement**

↓

**attack**

↓

**Yah's intervention.**

Revelation likewise gives:

**release**

↓

**deception**

↓

**gathering**

↓

**advance**

↓

**surrounding**

↓

**fire.**

Therefore:

> **Satan's release need not mean the camp is surrounded the same afternoon.**

There can reasonably be development between release and culmination.

That preserves the possibility of an **early little season**.

But here's our stopping boundary:

> We presently lack a sufficiently distinctive historical marker to establish that this process has begun.

That's where conviction must stop.

---

# Stage 64 — Finding

We can now define Revelation's saints with fairly high confidence:

> **The saints are Yah's covenant-faithful people identified with Yeshua HaMashiach, characterized in Revelation by keeping Yah's commandments, holding the testimony/faith of Yeshua, and enduring opposition rather than surrendering their allegiance. Martyrdom is prominent among them but does not exhaust the category.**

### Historical diagnostic

Therefore a credible post-millennial Gog/Magog identification would need something substantially resembling:

**deceived nations**

↓

**broad mobilization**

↓

**convergent hostility**

↓

**against an identifiable covenant-faithful people belonging to Yeshua**

↓

**encirclement/overwhelming assault**

↓

**direct termination by Yah.**

We have **not established that sequence in present history.**

That is a very important negative finding.
