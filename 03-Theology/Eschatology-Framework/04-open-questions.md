## Researched, Still Open

1. **The "respawn" verse — RESOLVED (researched 2026-08-08), answer: no.** Checked Revelation 20:7–15 directly and searched for any supporting text. Verses 7–10 describe Satan's release, final deception, and destruction; verses 11–15 describe the great white throne judgment of "the dead." Nothing in the passage or elsewhere describes people returning/reappearing on earth. High confidence this has no biblical support — treat as an extrabiblical inference, not a citable conviction or even a well-grounded hypothesis. [[03-hypotheses]] item 9 (the vault's item numbering there, unrelated to this file's own numbering) should be updated to reflect this negative finding rather than left as "not yet backed by a verse." Source: direct text check via biblegateway.com, ESV.
2. **Where were believers during the millennial reign, on the preterist reading? — RESOLVED (researched 2026-08-08), with a real answer to weigh.** Preterist commentators (Preterist Archives, Revelation Revolution's Revelation 20 commentary) place believers/saints **in heaven** during the millennium, reigning spiritually rather than being physically present on earth. On this reading, the "thousand years" spans the Christian era from Christ's first coming through history; the "first resurrection" (Revelation 20:5) is read as spiritual new birth in Christ, not a physical bodily resurrection; the "thrones" of Revelation 20:4 are heavenly, consistent with other throne imagery in Revelation. Confidence in this being the standard preterist position: moderately high (85–90%, per the researching agent) — but note this is what *preterist commentators* say, not yet a position confirmed against the primary text independently here. Whether to adopt this into [[01-convictions]] section B (present understanding) is a decision to make deliberately, not an automatic promotion — it resolves the open question of "what do preterists say" but is a separate step from "do I agree." Sources: preteristarchives.org/millennial-reign-of-christ/, revelationrevolution.org/revelation-20-a-preterist-commentary/.
3. **Historical identity of Gog and Magog.** Who/what does this refer to under a preterist, already-fulfilled reading of Revelation 20?
4. **Relationship, if any, between the unexplained-architecture observations and biblical chronology.** Is there an actual textual link, or is this purely a historical-hypothesis-side question that shouldn't be forced into the biblical timeline?
5. **Historical and theological value of Enoch, Jubilees, and Jasher.** How much weight should they carry — illuminating background material vs. something closer to authoritative? (Explicitly excludes Thomas and Judas, already judged inauthentic — not an open question.) **Progress (2026-08-08), see [[00b-biblical-epistemology]] Worked Example 2:** Jude 14–15 closely corresponds to 1 Enoch 1:9, and Enoch fragments were independently found among the Dead Sea Scrolls — real manuscript evidence, not just a modern claim. But Jude quoting Enoch doesn't by itself establish canonical/inspired status for 1 Enoch (same pattern as Paul quoting Greek poets in Acts 17:28 without canonizing them) — the observation is real, the further leap is a separate, still-open claim. Also tested: Deuteronomy 4:2, 12:32, and Proverbs 30:5–6 establish a real canonical pattern against adding/removing from Yahweh's words, alongside Revelation 22:18–19 — but that pattern **supports** general caution about tampering with revelation, it does **not require** the specific historical claim that Enoch/Jubilees/Jasher were deliberately removed in violation of that pattern. Still open: which communities regarded these books as authoritative, when, and what manuscript evidence exists for each beyond Enoch's DSS fragments. **Control case added:** Paul quotes non-biblical sources repeatedly (Aratus and Epimenides in Acts 17:28, Epimenides again in Titus 1:12–13, Menander in 1 Corinthians 15:33) without canonizing any of them — this rules out "Jude quotes Enoch, therefore all of 1 Enoch is Scripture." But it doesn't fully dissolve the question either: Jude names Enoch specifically and calls the material "prophecy," which is a stronger textual claim than Paul's use of a poet's turn of phrase. Full reasoning in [[00b-biblical-epistemology]] Worked Example 2.
6. **Reconciling biblical chronology with secular chronology.** Where do they actually conflict, and which discrepancies matter for this framework vs. which are noise? Includes calendar-transition questions specifically (Julian → Gregorian shift, older calendar systems) and how they affect any date comparison between biblical and secular timelines.
7. **Which historical traditions preserve genuine information vs. which don't.** No general method yet for sorting these — case by case so far.
8. **Great Commission: fulfilled or ongoing?** Colossians 1:6, 1:23, Romans 10:18, and Romans 16:25–26 read as if the gospel had already reached global scope within the apostolic generation — the preterist reading leans on this. But Matthew 28:20 explicitly extends the command "to the very end of the age," which reads as ongoing rather than closed. Genuinely unresolved here, not just a matter of picking the preterist-friendly verses and ignoring the rest.
    - **Substantial progress 2026-08-10, see [[00g-matthew-24-and-the-oikoumene]]:** Matthew 24:14's "whole world" is **οἰκουμένη** (*oikoumenē*, inhabited world) — the same word Luke uses for Caesar's census (Luke 2:1, which CJB renders "throughout the Empire") and a first-century famine (Acts 11:28, RVR1960: "toda la tierra habitada"). Additionally **Romans 15:19, 23** show Paul describing his own regional mission as "fulfilled" with "no room for work in these regions" — while millions there had obviously never heard him personally. **What this establishes:** "the gospel hasn't reached the whole world yet" cannot be read *into* Matthew 24:14 as though the Greek said "the modern globe." **What it does not establish:** that the verse *was* fulfilled in the first century. An assumption has been removed, not a conclusion proven. The question is now better-formed but still open.
10. **Worship directed to the Father vs. received by the Son — researched 2026-08-08.** *Terminology note: this is a Christology question (who the Messiah is, his relationship to the Father) not an eschatology question (last things) — different field of study, correctly kept separate from the millennium material. Not itself something to resolve as an open question — "Christology" is just the category label.* Your stated position: **the Messiah always points to the Father** — Section A below is your working reading. This stays in Open Questions rather than moving to Convictions because Section B is real, sourced counter-evidence you haven't yet worked through verse-by-verse, not because your position is unclear to you. It belongs here rather than folded into a conviction, because the textual evidence genuinely pulls two directions — this is not a case where research resolved a memory gap (like items 1, 2, 11), it's a real tension in the text that your stated reading has to account for.
    - **Toward "worship the Father, Yeshua redirects":** Mark 10:18/Luke 18:19 (Yeshua declines "good," pointing to God alone), John 17:3 ("you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent"), the Transfiguration voice ("this is my Son... listen to him" — Matthew 17:5 and parallels), John 14:9–11 ("whoever has seen me has seen the Father"), John 20:17 ("my God and your God"), the Lord's Prayer addressed to the Father.
    - **Counter-evidence — Yeshua/the Lamb receiving worship or honor equal to the Father:** John 5:23 ("that all may honor the Son, just as they honor the Father — whoever does not honor the Son does not honor the Father"), Hebrews 1:6 ("let all God's angels worship him"), Philippians 2:9–11 (every knee bowing, every tongue confessing Yeshua as Lord), Revelation 5:12–14 (the Lamb receiving the same praise as the one on the throne, worshipped by the elders), John 20:28 (Thomas: "My Lord and my God!" — not corrected by Yeshua), Matthew 28:9/17 and 14:33 (disciples worshipping the risen/miracle-working Yeshua without rebuke).
    - Full text for all of these (CJB + ESV) is in [[05-scripture-index]] under "Worship — Directed to the Father / Received by the Son."
    - This is a real, centuries-old theological fault line (unitarian/subordinationist readings vs. trinitarian readings) — not something this research pass resolves or should resolve. Keep testing it (1 Thessalonians 5:21) rather than picking a side to make the tension go away.
12. **Why does Revelation 22 still mention people "outside" the New Jerusalem, after Revelation 21 has introduced the eternal state? (raised 2026-08-09)** Revelation 22:14–15 — ESV: "Blessed are those who wash their robes... Outside are the dogs and sorcerers and the sexually immoral and murderers and idolaters, and everyone who loves and practices falsehood." CJB and RVR1960 track closely for v.14; v.15's exclusion list is consistent across translations. If Revelation 21–22 describes the final, fully perfected state, why does the text still speak of the wicked as an "outside" category at all? This is a real exegetical tension in the text itself — it does not by itself support the "respawn" theory already resolved negatively in item 1 above (that item is about people *returning to earth*; this is about the language of Revelation 22 still assuming an "outside" exists in what's presented as the final state). Held open, not resolved here.
    - **A related passage, not a resolution to the above but worth holding alongside it:** 2 Peter 3:9 — ESV: "The Lord is not slow to fulfill his promise as some count slowness, but is patient toward you, not wishing that any should perish, but that all should reach repentance." CJB: "it is not his purpose that anyone should be destroyed, but that everyone should turn from his sins." RVR1960: "no queriendo que ninguno perezca, sino que todos procedan al arrepentimiento." Peter frames apparent delay in judgment as patience/mercy, not as evidence against judgment happening. Doesn't answer the Revelation 22 question directly, but is the right-adjacent text to weigh it against — judgment and patience aren't presented as contradictory in Peter's own framing.
13. **The Solomon "search/seek all things" verse — CORRECTED (2026-08-08).** The first research pass checked only Proverbs 25:2 and Ecclesiastes 7:23 and wrongly concluded no match existed — it missed two much stronger candidates. **Ecclesiastes 1:13** (ESV: "And I applied my heart to seek and to search out by wisdom all that is done under heaven"; CJB: "I wisely applied myself to seek out and investigate everything done under heaven") and **Ecclesiastes 7:25** (ESV: "I turned my heart to know and to search out and to seek wisdom and the scheme of things"; CJB: "I turned myself and my thoughts to know, search out and seek wisdom and the reasons behind things") both match "search/seek all things" closely — Qoheleth (traditionally Solomon) applying wisdom to search out everything. Neither is "test all things" verbatim (that phrase is Paul's, 1 Thessalonians 5:21, already in [[01-convictions]] A.15) — the memory likely blends Solomon's "search/seek" language with Paul's "test" language, which are genuinely two different instructions from two different authors. Both Ecclesiastes verses are now in [[05-scripture-index]] under Seeking Wisdom & Testing. Worth deciding: promote to [[01-convictions]] as an explicit teaching, since these are now real, sourced citations.

14. **"And in this way all Israel will be saved" (Romans 11:26) — whom does Paul mean, and when? (raised 2026-08-09, see [[00e-olive-tree-and-covenant-identity]])** What's explicit in the text: Paul expects a "fullness of the Gentiles"; Israel's hardening is described as **partial**; "all Israel will be saved." What's genuinely contested: the referent of "all Israel." Major live readings — (a) ethnic/national Israel ultimately turning to Messiah, (b) the faithful remnant of Israel across history, (c) the complete covenant people composed of Israel plus incorporated Gentiles, (d) combinations. **Deliberately not forced**, and specifically not forced toward whichever reading would be most convenient for this vault's preferred terminology — that temptation is real here and worth naming. Romans 11:25's own stated purpose ("lest you be wise in your own sight") argues against confident resolution by anyone, including us.

## Bookmarked — Flagged for Later, Not Yet Investigated

Distinct from the numbered list above: those are things actually researched that remain unresolved. These are topics explicitly deferred — noted so nothing gets lost, but genuinely not yet examined. Move an item out of this section (into the numbered list above, or into [[01-convictions]]/[[03-hypotheses]]) once real investigation happens — don't let it sit here pretending to be settled just because it's written down.

- **Luke 1:3's "orderly" (Greek *kathexēs*) doesn't necessarily mean strict chronological order.** (Flagged 2026-08-09, see [[00b-biblical-epistemology]] "Luke's Own Method.") Relevant later when comparing the Gospels' sometimes-differing event sequences — don't assume "orderly account" makes Luke's sequence the chronological arbiter against Matthew's or Mark's without checking what *kathexēs* actually claims.
- **The trumpet comparison — one event or independent uses of shared imagery?** (Flagged 2026-08-11, see [[00k-the-gathering-of-the-elect]] §9.) Requires comparing **Isaiah 27:13** (great trumpet, regathering) · **Matthew 24:31** · **1 Corinthians 15:52** (last trumpet, dead raised) · **1 Thessalonians 4:16** (trumpet of God, resurrection and gathering) · **Revelation's trumpets**. Both shortcuts refused: cannot align Matthew with Isaiah while ignoring Paul, and cannot assume shared imagery means shared event (*compatibility ≠ identity*). **This is where a past-millennium reading faces its hardest test** — it must establish which of these events are identical and which aren't, and the elaborateness of any required exceptions is itself evidence.
- **Does Revelation inherit Isaiah 27's serpent-defeat/restoration pattern?** (Flagged 2026-08-11, see [[00k-the-gathering-of-the-elect]] §8.) Isaiah 27:1 has Leviathan/the dragon judged, then vineyard restored, Israel gathered, great trumpet. Revelation has dragon/ancient serpent → defeat → Messiah's authority → the people of Yahweh. Recorded as an intertextual observation, deliberately unresolved.
- **Who are "the elect" in Matthew 24:31?** (Flagged 2026-08-11.) Scattered Israel alone, or the renewed covenant people centered on Messiah including the nations? *Eklektos* has deep Israel roots (Isaiah 45:4, 65:9) but is applied to Messiah's people in Romans 8:33 and Colossians 3:12. Carried forward from [[00k-the-gathering-of-the-elect]] §6.
- **Yeshua's departure and Ezekiel's glory.** (Flagged 2026-08-10, see [[00j-cosmic-signs-and-intertextual-priority]] §8.) Ezekiel 11:23 has Yahweh's glory leaving the condemned city and stopping on "the mountain east of the city" — the Mount of Olives. Matthew 23:38 declares the house desolate, 24:1 has Yeshua leaving the Temple, 24:3 places Him on the Mount of Olives. **Evidentiary status: supports** (stronger than permits, well short of requires). Matthew never states the fulfillment; the pattern is striking and the authorial intent unstated.
- **Danielic Kingdom Parallel — Daniel 2, the stone, and when Messiah's reign begins.** (Flagged 2026-08-12.) **Deliberately NOT used as evidence in [[00j-cosmic-signs-and-intertextual-priority]]**, because Daniel 2 does not supply Matthew 24's cosmic-luminary vocabulary. Inserting it there merely because it also concerns kingdoms and judgment would have made that argument *less* precise. **This illustrates something the method should keep:** a passage can be true and enormously relevant to the larger theology while not being relevant evidence for the specific exegetical question currently under investigation. We are not collecting verses that broadly agree — we are asking each text a specific question.
    - **What Daniel 2 textually establishes:** four successive kingdoms (gold head = Babylon, explicitly, 2:37–38; then silver, bronze, iron) → the fourth "strong as iron" (2:40) → feet/toes "**a divided kingdom**, partly strong and partly brittle," which "will not hold together, just as iron does not mix with clay" (2:41–43) → a stone "**cut out by no human hand**" strikes the feet, the whole image is pulverized, and the stone "became a great mountain and **filled the whole earth**" (2:34–35) → "**in the days of those kings** the God of heaven will set up a kingdom that shall never be destroyed" (2:44).
    - **Traditional identification** (substantial pedigree, not uncontested): Babylon → Medo-Persia → Greece → Rome. Strengthened by **Daniel 8:20–21**, which explicitly names "the kings of Media and Persia" and "the king of Greece." **Competing scholarly reconstructions exist** — notably ones separating Media from Persia and making Greece the fourth kingdom — and should be documented rather than ignored.
    - **Corrections recorded (2026-08-12):** (1) The sequence has **four** kingdoms, not five — separating "Medes" and "Persians" would give five. (2) Daniel does **not** identify the iron/clay phase as modern governments descended from Rome; that belongs to later eschatological systems, not the Biblical Observation bucket. (3) Nor does Daniel identify it as Republic vs. Empire — and for the record, **the Roman Republic preceded the Empire** (conventionally 509–27 BC, then Augustus from 27 BC), so any such reconstruction would need its phases stated in the right order. The text says: divided, partly iron, partly brittle, alliances that don't adhere. Everything past that is interpretation.
    - **The genuinely productive intertextual question this raises:** Daniel 2 gives *four kingdoms → an everlasting divine kingdom* (2:44). Daniel 7 gives *four beasts → judgment → Son of Man → everlasting dominion* (7:13–14). Same endpoint, different symbolic register. And Daniel 2:44 places the kingdom's establishment "**in the days of those kings**" — not millennia after the fourth kingdom vanished. Yeshua then appears under Roman rule announcing **Mark 1:15**: ESV: "**The time is fulfilled**, and the kingdom of God is at hand." CJB: "The time has come, God's Kingdom is near!"
    - **And the stone imagery recurs in Matthew.** **Matthew 21:42–44** — quoting Psalm 118's rejected stone, then: ESV: "the one who falls on this stone will be broken to pieces; and when it falls on anyone, **it will crush him**." RVR1960: "sobre quien ella cayere, **le desmenuzará**" — the same crushing/pulverizing language Daniel 2 uses of the stone striking the image. **And Matthew 21 sits immediately before the Matthew 23–24 judgment material this framework has been working.** *(Textual note: some manuscripts omit Matthew 21:44 — CJB's rendering ends at v.43. Worth verifying before building on it.)*
    - **Open Intertextual Question:** what relationship exists among Daniel 2's stone kingdom, Daniel 7's Son of Man kingdom, Psalm 118's rejected stone, Matthew 21:42–44's crushing stone, and Yeshua's announcement that the kingdom had drawn near?
    - **The larger question it feeds:** before asking *when did the thousand years begin*, this framework must first establish **when Scripture says Messiah's reign began**. Those are not necessarily the same question, and collapsing them prematurely would be exactly the error the method exists to prevent.
- **Open hypothesis — Rupes Nigra and Daniel 2:35.** (Raised 2026-08-12.) Daniel 2:34–35's stone "cut out by no human hand" becoming "a great mountain" that "filled the whole earth" prompted a connection to later cartographic traditions concerning **Rupes Nigra**, depicted as a great magnetic black rock/mountain near the geographic north.
    - **No identification between Daniel's mountain and Rupes Nigra is established.** Compound hypothesis requiring several independent propositions to survive: (a) what Daniel's mountain means textually, (b) the provenance and development of the Rupes Nigra cartographic tradition, (c) any connection between them.
    - **Method requirement:** investigate Daniel 2 on its own, investigate Rupes Nigra on its own, **then** compare. Do not research Rupes Nigra *in order to* prove Daniel 2 — that inverts the epistemological loop.
    - **Lexical note:** Daniel 2 is **Aramaic** at this point, not Hebrew. 2:35's "whole earth" is **כָּל־אַרְעָא** (*kol-ʾarʿāʾ*). Conclusions reached about Greek *gē* or *oikoumenē* ([[00g-matthew-24-and-the-oikoumene]], [[00l-tribes-of-the-land]]) **cannot be imported** — *ʾarʿāʾ* needs its own study within Biblical Aramaic. Same principle, different language.
    - **Brake on the magnetism argument:** compass needles align with Earth's magnetic field; they are not attracted toward a magnetic mountain at a geographic center. "Compasses point north → therefore Rupes Nigra" is not usable evidence. *(Term note: the field is described as **toroidal**, not "Horus.")*
    - **Brake on the cartographic argument:** old European maps genuinely did depict a great Arctic magnetic rock, and the **tradition itself is a legitimate historical research subject** — how old are the surviving sources, where did the story originate, what did they actually claim, were cartographers reporting observation or reproducing inherited lore? **Critical condition:** independent maps only count as independent evidence if they are not all copying one earlier source. Twenty maps reproducing one tradition are one witness, not twenty. Belongs in [[02-historical-observations]] when earned.
- **The Cross as Cosmic Turning Point.** (Flagged 2026-08-10, see [[00i-stars-the-dragon-and-the-cross]] §7 for the assembled arc.) Job's accuser with heavenly access → John 12:31 "now will the ruler of this world be cast out" → the cross → the torn veil → Colossians 2:15's disarmed rulers → Hebrews 10:19–22's new access → Revelation 12:10–11's dragon cast down and conquered "by the blood of the Lamb." Deserves its own treatment; possibly also Ephesians 4. The *connection* is textually supported; the *chronology* of Revelation 12's heavenly war relative to the cross still needs testing.
- **Melchizedek: Messiah as King-Priest.** (Flagged 2026-08-10, sources captured in [[00i-stars-the-dragon-and-the-cross]] §8.) Genesis 14:18–20, Psalm 110:1+4, Hebrews 5–7, Zechariah 6:12–13. Includes the pre-Levitical priesthood question (Exodus 19:22, 24:5; Jethro) and the caution that Abraham's tenth predates Sinai without thereby establishing a universal tithe command.
- **"The powers of the heavens" (Matthew 24:29) — what are they?** (Flagged 2026-08-10, see [[00j-cosmic-signs-and-intertextual-priority]] §9.) Greek *hai dynameis tōn ouranōn*. Live possibilities: celestial bodies, heavenly/spiritual powers, symbolic ruling structures, cosmic order generally. Revelation 12's heavenly-beings material makes the spiritual reading live; Isaiah 13/34 and Ezekiel 32 equally support cosmic-judgment imagery. Needs the phrase examined elsewhere in Scripture before deciding. *(The broader falling-stars question is now largely worked in 00j — this specific phrase is what remains open from it.)*
- **New Testament use of the Septuagint where it differs from the Masoretic Text.** (Flagged 2026-08-09, see [[00c-commission-covenant-investigation]] Acts 15.) James's citation of Amos 9:11–12 in Acts 15:16–18 follows the Greek Septuagint tradition more closely than the later Masoretic Hebrew wording. **This directly complicates ADR-0003's "go to the source language" rule** — the apostles themselves sometimes quote the Greek Scriptures circulating in their world, so "go back to the Hebrew" is not always the simple corrective it sounds like. Needs real work: which text-form did NT authors use, when, and what follows for a study that treats original-language priority as a methodological principle. Not resolved; genuinely important for the translation policy.
- **Independent testimony and variation in detail.** (Flagged 2026-08-09.) General historical principle worth stating carefully rather than loosely: independent witnesses don't need identical wording to be credible, and perfectly harmonized accounts can suggest dependence rather than independence. But variation isn't automatically proof of authenticity either — each discrepancy still needs its own examination. Proper formulation to work from: *agreement in core events + independent variation in detail is consistent with genuine testimony, but every specific discrepancy still requires examination.* Belongs with the Luke-as-historian material ([[00b-biblical-epistemology]], "Luke's Own Method") and any future Gospel-comparison work — not developed yet.
- **Circumcision of the heart, and whether Gentile believers required physical circumcision.** (Flagged 2026-08-09.) This is precisely the question Acts 15 forces into the open — whether Gentile men entering Messiah had to be circumcised and take on Mosaic covenant markers. The Jerusalem council's ruling (Acts 15:19–20, already cited in [[00c-commission-covenant-investigation]]) addresses it, but the fuller "circumcision of the heart" thread (Deuteronomy 10:16, Deuteronomy 30:6, Jeremiah 4:4, Romans 2:28–29, Colossians 2:11) hasn't been traced. Next in sequence — Acts 13–15 is where this gets worked.
- **The Sinai/Horeb naming question.** (Noted 2026-08-09, minor.) The mountain of the Ten Commandments is **Sinai**; "Horeb" is the closely-related name used more often in Deuteronomy, with "Sinai" more common in Exodus. Not two different mountains in the biblical tradition. *(Separately, and unrelated to the naming: claims relocating Sinai outside the conventional region belong in [[03-hypotheses]] item 10, alternative geography — not here.)*
- **"The Way" (ἡ ὁδός, *hē hodos*) as the movement's earliest self-description.** (Flagged 2026-08-09, see [[00c-commission-covenant-investigation]] Acts 9.) Acts uses it repeatedly (9:2, and elsewhere) before "Christian" appears at Antioch (11:26). Worth tracing all occurrences and asking what the term itself claims — relevant to the naming/label discussion in [[01-convictions]] B.5, but not yet investigated.
- **Messiah identifying Himself with His people.** (Flagged 2026-08-09, see [[00c-commission-covenant-investigation]] Acts 9.) Acts 9:4 — Saul is arresting Yeshua's followers, and Yeshua asks "why are you persecuting **me**?" Relevant to covenant-identity and body-of-Messiah material; connects to Romans 11's olive tree and Ephesians 2's "one new man," neither developed yet.
- **Acts 11:20 textual variant — *Hellēnistas* vs. *Hellēnas*.** (Flagged 2026-08-09.) Manuscripts differ between "Hellenists" (Greek-speaking Jews) and "Greeks" (Gentiles). Real consequences for how large a step Acts 11:20 records — if Hellenists, the Antioch believers were still within Israel; if Greeks, this is a Gentile-mission datapoint independent of Cornelius. Needs manuscript-evidence work per ADR-0003's source-language priority; not resolved.
- **Romans 9–11 on Israel's ongoing place.** (Flagged 2026-08-09, see [[00c-commission-covenant-investigation]] Acts 9.) Acts 9:15 keeps "the children of Israel" inside Paul's commission alongside Gentiles and kings — the shape is not *Israel discarded, Gentiles substituted*. Romans 9–11 addresses this directly and will have to be worked through; deliberately not touched yet.
- **"Last days" as a first-century chronological marker.** (Flagged 2026-08-09, see [[00c-commission-covenant-investigation]] §1, Acts 2 section.) Peter applies Joel's "last days" prophecy (Acts 2:16–17) to his own first-century audience, not a future era. Needs comparison with Hebrews 1:1–2 ("in these last days he has spoken to us by his Son"), 1 Peter 1:20 ("manifest in the last times for the sake of you"), Hebrews 9:26 ("at the end of the ages"), and 1 Corinthians 10:11 ("on whom the end of the ages has come") — all apostolic-era authors placing "last days"/"end of the ages" language on their own generation. Directly relevant to the preterist timeline question this whole framework circles back to — not touched yet, deliberately deferred until the direct return to [[06-revelation-19-22-timeline]].
