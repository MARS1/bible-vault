# Open Questions

Unresolved. No conclusion drawn yet — that's what distinguishes this box from [[03-hypotheses]].

1. **The "respawn" verse.** Where in Scripture (Revelation, or possibly elsewhere) is there a basis for people returning/reappearing on earth after the millennium during the "little season"? Referenced in [[03-hypotheses]] item 9 — not yet located. Needs a real citation before it can move to Convictions or even harden as a Hypothesis.
2. **Where were believers during the millennial reign, on the preterist reading?** If the millennium already happened and saints weren't taken up to heaven for it, what does Scripture actually say about their location/state during that period? This needs to be nailed down biblically, not just asserted, before the "already-fulfilled millennium" framework can be called complete on this point.
3. **Historical identity of Gog and Magog.** Who/what does this refer to under a preterist, already-fulfilled reading of Revelation 20?
4. **Relationship, if any, between the unexplained-architecture observations and biblical chronology.** Is there an actual textual link, or is this purely a historical-hypothesis-side question that shouldn't be forced into the biblical timeline?
5. **Historical and theological value of Enoch, Jubilees, and Jasher.** How much weight should they carry — illuminating background material vs. something closer to authoritative? (Explicitly excludes Thomas and Judas, already judged inauthentic — not an open question.)
6. **Reconciling biblical chronology with secular chronology.** Where do they actually conflict, and which discrepancies matter for this framework vs. which are noise? Includes calendar-transition questions specifically (Julian → Gregorian shift, older calendar systems) and how they affect any date comparison between biblical and secular timelines.
7. **Which historical traditions preserve genuine information vs. which don't.** No general method yet for sorting these — case by case so far.
8. **Great Commission: fulfilled or ongoing?** Colossians 1:6, 1:23, Romans 10:18, and Romans 16:25–26 read as if the gospel had already reached global scope within the apostolic generation — the preterist reading leans on this. But Matthew 28:20 explicitly extends the command "to the very end of the age," which reads as ongoing rather than closed. Genuinely unresolved here, not just a matter of picking the preterist-friendly verses and ignoring the rest.
9. **The Solomon "search/test all things" verse.** Referenced from memory as something like "search all things, seek all things, test all things" — not yet confirmed. Candidates to check: Proverbs 25:2 ("It is the glory of God to conceal a matter, but the glory of kings is to search out a matter") or Ecclesiastes 7:23. Neither confirmed as the intended verse — don't cite either until verified.
