15. **If "all is accomplished" landed in the first century, what does Matthew 5:19 mean afterward? (raised 2026-08-12, see [[00n-torah-fulfilled-and-what-changed]] §15)** Matthew 5:18 says not one *yud* or stroke passes from Torah **until all is accomplished** — and Matthew 5:19 immediately adds that whoever relaxes the least of these commandments and teaches others to do so "will be called least in the kingdom of heaven." **This is the first point where a preterist reading creates a problem for the reader rather than solving one.** The easy exit — "verse 19 applied only to the transitional period before AD 70" — is precisely the kind of protective exception this framework refuses to grant its opponents, so it cannot grant itself one either. **Recorded as an unresolved cost of the position, not a footnote.** Simultaneously true and unreconciled: Paul tells Gentile believers physical circumcision is not their covenantal requirement; Hebrews says the priesthood changed; Messiah's sacrifice is presented as sufficient; Temple access changed. **Something changed. Something didn't. Which, and on what textual basis?** — that is the next stage's table, not a conclusion available here.

16. **The relationship between Matthew 5:18's two "untils" is undetermined by the text. (raised 2026-08-12, see [[00n-torah-fulfilled-and-what-changed]] §2)** "Until heaven and earth pass away" (*heōs an parelthē ho ouranos kai hē gē*) and "until all is accomplished" (*heōs an panta genētai*) are both present, and any interpretation must satisfy both. **Four possibilities, none selected:** (a) **coterminous** — both mark the same moment, stated twice; (b) **B defines A** — "until all is accomplished" explains what the passing of heaven and earth means; (c) **independent** — two separate conditions, both required; (d) **merism/emphatic** — a rhetorical way of saying "for as long as anything stands." Recording this as undetermined is deliberate: silently choosing one and building on it is exactly how eisegesis enters a careful argument.

17. **Was Hebrews written before the Temple's destruction? (raised 2026-08-12, see [[00n-torah-fulfilled-and-what-changed]] §8)** Hebrews 8:13 describes the first covenant as *becoming* obsolete, *growing* old, *ready to* vanish — a transition in progress. Hebrews 8:4 says "there **are** priests who offer gifts according to the law"; Hebrews 10:1–2 describes sacrifices "continually offered every year" and asks whether they would not "**have ceased** to be offered." If the letter was written while the Temple still stood, an institution it describes as near disappearance did in fact disappear — which would be relevant to AD 70 without AD 70 having been forced into it. **But the pre-70 dating is an inference, not a statement — Hebrews never dates itself.** Three counter-considerations, recorded because they cut against the convenient reading: (a) Greek present tense can describe a system generically or customarily, not only as currently operating; (b) the standard scholarly objection is that other writers describe Temple service in the present tense *after* AD 70 — **flagged explicitly as UNVERIFIED by this project; the specific primary-source citations have not been checked and must not be asserted until they are**; (c) Hebrews 8:4/10:1 may describe Levitical service as *defined by Torah* regardless of whether an altar is currently standing. **Status: permits, not establishes.** Building a chronology on this would repeat exactly the move this framework has refused since Stage 1.

18. **Luke 21:32's Greek has not been checked. (raised 2026-08-12, see [[00n-torah-fulfilled-and-what-changed]] §9)** Matthew 5:18 (ἕως ἂν **πάντα γένηται**) and Matthew 24:34 (ἕως ἂν **πάντα ταῦτα γένηται**) differ only by ταῦτα — a genuine, checkable intratextual datum. The Lukan parallel would either strengthen or complicate it. **Do not cite Luke 21:32 as corroboration until its Greek has actually been verified against a text** — the Solomon "test all things" incident (item 13 above) is the standing reminder of what happens when a plausible-sounding citation gets recorded before it's checked.

19. **Ezekiel 37:26–28's sanctuary "in their midst forevermore" — a real cost, not a footnote. (raised 2026-08-12, see [[00o-new-covenant-what-changed]] §7)** The everlasting-covenant passage this project *is* using for the one-king and Torah-keeping material **also promises a sanctuary, twice**: ESV "will set my sanctuary in their midst forevermore" (37:26), "when my sanctuary is in their midst forevermore" (37:28); RVR1960 "pondré mi santuario entre ellos **para siempre**." **A framework reading Temple language as passing away in AD 70 owes an account of this promise** — and so does any reading treating the New Covenant as fully arrived without a sanctuary. **Four live options, none selected:** (a) fulfilled in Messiah / the Spirit-indwelt people as sanctuary — **2 Corinthians 6:16 appears to cite Ezekiel 37:27, but that citation is FLAGGED AND NOT YET VERIFIED by this project**; (b) fulfilled partially/conditionally in the Second Temple period; (c) still future; (d) governed by Ezekiel 40–48's temple vision, which needs its own investigation. **Ezekiel 40–48 deliberately not opened at Stage 15** — same discipline as Daniel 2 at Stage 13; cashing it mid-stage would repeat the error that discipline exists to prevent.

20. **Jeremiah 31:34 — "they shall all know me" and "no longer shall each one teach his neighbor." (raised 2026-08-12, see [[00o-new-covenant-what-changed]] §4)** Quoted approvingly by Hebrews 8 as describing the covenant being inaugurated. **But it is not obviously descriptive of any observable community, then or now** — teaching plainly continued in the apostolic congregations, and Hebrews is itself a teaching document written to correct people's understanding. **Possible readings, none adjudicated:** an eschatological not-yet; a claim about covenant *membership* (all who are *in* it know Him, unlike Israel where membership was ethnic and included the unfaithful); hyperbolic covenant language. **This is a genuine cost for any reading that treats the New Covenant as fully consummated**, and it is recorded here rather than quietly skipped because Hebrews 8 quotes the clause without qualifying it.

21. **How can circumcision be commanded within Torah while Paul distinguishes physical circumcision from "keeping the commandments of God"? (raised 2026-08-12, see [[00o-new-covenant-what-changed]] §14)** **1 Corinthians 7:19** — ESV: "For neither circumcision counts for anything nor uncircumcision, **but keeping the commandments of God**"; CJB: "what does mean something is **keeping God's commandments**"; RVR1960: "**sino el guardar los mandamientos de Dios**." **All three translations agree — there is no translational escape hatch.** Paul does *not* say circumcision is irrelevant because commandments are irrelevant; he says circumcision does not determine covenant standing **and that commandment-keeping does mean something.** Complicating the picture further: circumcision is an **Abrahamic** covenant sign (Genesis 17:10–11), predating Sinai — so it is not straightforwardly "Levitical law" under any taxonomy. **This is not a contradiction to conceal but an investigation to run**, and it strongly suggests Paul holds a worked-out conception of *how commandments function through Messiah under the New Covenant* that this project has not yet recovered from the text.

22. **Leviticus 16's *Azazel* — and the 1 Enoch connection, with an unverified foundation. (raised 2026-08-12, see [[00p-sacrifice-priesthood-and-fulfillment]] §21)** **ESTABLISHED (Tier A):** *Azazel* occurs at Leviticus 16:8, 10, 26, and **all three of this project's translations transliterate it as a name** rather than rendering "scapegoat" (ESV "Azazel," CJB "'Az'azel," RVR1960 "Azazel") — the popular gloss comes from KJV and is the origin of the English word. That does **not** establish a personal referent (transliteration is also what translators do when meaning is uncertain), but it does establish that "it obviously just means scapegoat" is **not** the settled position it is commonly assumed to be. The live goat is sent **לַעֲזָאזֵל** *la-Azazel* ("for/to Azazel") into the wilderness. Candidate meanings remain: (a) the goat itself / "goat that departs"; (b) removal/sending away; (c) a wilderness location; (d) a personal or spiritual referent. **⛔ REPORTED BUT NOT VERIFIED:** claims about 1 Enoch — that **Semihazah/Semyaza** (not Azazel) leads the Watchers' mutual oath in ch. 6; that **Azazel** teaches metalworking, weapons, and ornamentation in ch. 8; that **Raphael binds Azazel and casts him into a desert place** in ch. 10. **An attempt to verify these against R.H. Charles's translation returned HTTP 403 (blocked).** They must not be cited as established. **Note this includes a correction to the original recollection** — that Azazel led the oath — which is itself unverified; both layers preserved. **HYPOTHESIS (Tier D):** the Enochic Azazel tradition may preserve or develop an ancient Jewish reading in which *la-Azazel* had a personal/spiritual referent. **NOT ESTABLISHED:** that Leviticus itself describes the Watcher figure of 1 Enoch. **GOVERNING BRAKE: do not let 1 Enoch define Leviticus before Leviticus is exegeted on its own terms** — that is Intertextual Priority run backwards, precisely the error the rule exists to prevent. **Inherits the constraint from item 5** (Jude quoting Enoch does not canonize Enoch; Paul quoting Greek poets is the control). **Would strengthen:** Second Temple / Dead Sea Scrolls material showing the personal reading in use near Leviticus's interpretive world. **Would weaken:** evidence the Enochic name derives *from* Leviticus, or a solid philological derivation from *ʿez* + *ʾazal* ("goat that departs"). **Verification step:** obtain 1 Enoch 6, 8, 10 from a retrievable public-domain edition and confirm chapters, content, and names directly.

23. **Matthew 26:28 — is "new" in the text? (raised 2026-08-12, see [[00p-sacrifice-priesthood-and-fulfillment]] §6)** ESV: "this is my blood **of the covenant**"; RVR1960: "mi sangre **del nuevo pacto**"; CJB: "my blood, **which ratifies the New Covenant**." The presence of *kainēs* ("new") at Matthew 26:28 is **text-critically contested** — present in the Byzantine/majority tradition RVR1960 follows, absent from the critical text ESV follows; CJB's rendering is interpretive. **Consequence, already applied:** the claim "Yeshua Himself called it the New Covenant" now rests on **Luke 22:20 and 1 Corinthians 11:25**, where the word is secure — **not** on Matthew 26:28. The *covenantal framing* of Matthew 26:28 is secure regardless (it cites Exodus 24:8's "blood of the covenant"); only the adjective is in question. **Full manuscript-apparatus work is beyond what this project has done** — flagged per ADR-0003 rather than resolved.

24. **Isaiah 53:10 — does Isaiah assert the servant becomes a guilt offering, or pose it conditionally? (raised 2026-08-12, see [[00p-sacrifice-priesthood-and-fulfillment]] §4)** ESV: "**when** his soul makes an offering for guilt, he shall see his offspring"; RVR1960: "**Cuando** haya puesto su vida **en expiación por el pecado**, verá linaje"; **CJB: "to see **if** he would present himself as a guilt offering. **If he does**, he will see his offspring."** CJB construes the Hebrew as a **conditional** where the other two read a temporal clause — a different construal, not a stylistic choice. **אָשָׁם *asham* (guilt offering) is present in all three;** what varies is the certainty of the assertion. **Recorded because this project would prefer the stronger reading and two of three translations supply it** — the third does not, and that gets printed rather than smoothed. Needs Hebrew-syntax work per ADR-0003 to go further.

25. **🔖 `OLAM` — what does Hebrew *olam* actually mean, and does "everlasting" survive it? (raised 2026-08-12, see [[00q-circumcision-covenant-sign-and-heart]] §21)** Genesis 17:13 calls circumcision **בְּרִית עוֹלָם — *berit olam***, yet its covenantal function appears transformed in Messiah. **The question cannot be answered from English "everlasting."** Two candidate senses: (a) **metaphysically endless, incapable of transformation**; (b) **perpetual, age-lasting, enduring throughout an appointed order**. **The translations already split before analysis begins** — ESV/CJB "everlasting" (metaphysical lean); **RVR1960 "perpetuo"** (durational lean). **Stakes far exceed circumcision:** *olam* appears regarding priestly statutes, festivals, covenant promises, the land, Yahweh Himself, and other institutions — referent and context clearly matter. **🚧 GOVERNING BRAKE, stated as a rule: do NOT resolve *olam* opportunistically because circumcision created a difficulty.** This is the single most tempting shortcut currently available to the framework — a convenient reading would relieve several pressures simultaneously, **including Ezekiel 37:26's "everlasting covenant" and its sanctuary (item 19)**. That convenience is exactly why it must be held. *A word study conducted in order to relieve a pressure is not a word study; it is advocacy with a lexicon.* Requires its own lexical study per ADR-0003.
    - **🔴 MAJOR DATUM ADDED 2026-08-12 (Stage 16.5, see [[00r-what-is-a-covenant]] §5) — and it strengthens the brake rather than relieving it.** *Berit olam* is attached to **three** institutions: the **rainbow** (Genesis 9:16), **circumcision** (Genesis 17:13), and **Shabbat** (Exodus 31:16). **Nobody claims the rainbow covenant ended. The apostles did not require circumcision of Gentiles. Shabbat remains contested.** Same Hebrew phrase, three institutions, **and no tradition applies one rule mechanically to all three.** **What this establishes:** the phrase alone cannot settle any of the three cases. **What it does NOT establish:** that *olam* means "temporary" — that would be the Anti-Replacement error, swapping one rigid reading for its opposite. **It means *olam* is not the load-bearing word it is widely assumed to be.** The lexical study is still required; this finding narrows what it can be expected to deliver, and removes the temptation to treat a single lexical verdict as decisive for circumcision, Shabbat, and Ezekiel 37's sanctuary all at once.
    - **🔍 Translation inconsistency recorded (ADR-0008):** **ESV renders the identical phrase differently** — "everlasting covenant" at Genesis 9:16 and 17:13, but "covenant forever" at Exodus 31:16. CJB reads "perpetual" at Exodus 31:16 and "everlasting" at Genesis 17:13. **RVR1960 is the most consistent of the three witnesses: *perpetuo* in both places.**

26. **Acts 15:21 — does "Moses is read every Sabbath" imply progressive Gentile Torah observance? (raised 2026-08-12, see [[00q-circumcision-covenant-sign-and-heart]] §12)** After the four requirements, James adds: "For from ancient generations **Moses has had in every city those who proclaim him, for he is read every Sabbath in the synagogues**." **A known interpretation reads this as:** the four rules were a starting point, and Gentiles would progressively learn the whole Mosaic Torah through synagogue attendance. **The verse genuinely deserves investigation.** **But Acts does not add** "and therefore the four requirements are temporary until Gentiles observe every Mosaic command." **That is an inference — possibly defensible, possibly not — and it must be labeled inference, not read as text.** The opposite over-reading is refused equally: "Acts 15 proves Gentiles have only four commandments" is also not what the text says — the letter states four requirements without claiming they exhaust Gentile obligation. **Anti-Replacement Principle: refuting one does not establish the other.**

27. **What does Paul mean by "the commandments of God" if circumcision was itself commanded by God? — CARRIED FORWARD, still open. (raised at Stage 15 as item 21; Stage 17 added data without resolving it, see [[00q-circumcision-covenant-sign-and-heart]] §17–18)** **New data from Stage 17:** Paul uses the same "neither circumcision nor uncircumcision… **but**" construction three times with three different completions — **"keeping the commandments of God"** (1 Cor 7:19), **"faith working through love"** (Gal 5:6), **"a new creation"** (Gal 6:15). These read as **converging** rather than competing (*new creation → faith → love → obedience*), echoing Ezekiel's *new heart → Spirit → obedience*. **Recorded at "supports," not "establishes"** — asserting Paul intends a single ordered sequence across three letters is more than the texts have shown. **Why it remains unresolved: Paul never defines "the commandments of God" in context.** Resolution requires gathering his usage across letters — its own investigation. **Stage 17 was expected to resolve this or explain why it cannot; the second obtains.**

28. **Did Genesis 9:3's "every moving thing that lives" include species already classified unclean? (raised 2026-08-12, see [[00s-clean-and-unclean-from-noah-to-messiah]] §3–4)** ESV: "**Every moving thing that lives shall be food for you.** And as I gave you the green plants, **I give you everything**" — followed immediately by the blood restriction (9:4). **This is a genuine difficulty for any reading that makes the pre-Sinai clean/unclean distinction universally *dietary*, and it is recorded rather than explained away.** **Established:** the categories predate Sinai (Gen 7:2); their first explicit narrative function is **sacrificial** (Gen 8:20); Genesis 9 broadly grants animals as food while prohibiting blood. **Neither "Noah ate unclean animals" nor "Noah followed Israel's later dietary restrictions" is explicitly stated — both are inferences.** Open: would Noah naturally have understood the existing distinction to constrain what counted as food, or does "everything" mean everything? **Note the jurisdiction datum:** the blood prohibition is given to **Noah** — humanity broadly — not to Israel, and it reappears in the one list the apostles hand to Gentiles (Acts 15:20, 29).

29. **Mark 7:19 — the Greek is a participle with no stated subject, and every English translation supplies something. (raised 2026-08-12, see [[00s-clean-and-unclean-from-noah-to-messiah]] §13)** **καθαρίζων πάντα τὰ βρώματα — *katharizōn panta ta brōmata*, "cleansing all the foods."** **ESV** "(Thus **he declared** all foods clean.)" · **NIV** "(In saying this, **Jesus declared** all foods clean.)" · **CJB** "(Thus he declared all foods **ritually** clean.)" — adding an adverb that materially narrows the scope · **RVR1960** "Esto decía, **haciendo limpios** todos los alimentos" — **keeps the participle and supplies no subject.** **Two distinct interpretive moves are visible: supplying "he declared," and (CJB alone) inserting "ritually."** The historical alternative reading — that the digestive process is what cleanses — is **grammatically available precisely because the participle has no stated subject.** **Interest noted in both directions and settling nothing:** CJB is a Messianic Jewish translation with a stake in the narrow reading; ESV and NIV are traditional-Christian translations with a stake in the broad one. **A SECOND question survives even if the declarative reading is correct: what does *brōmata* denote?** — *every organism is now food*, or *things already classified as food cannot morally defile via ritual hand contamination*? **Not identical claims.** Requires Greek-grammar work per ADR-0003. **Constrained by Parallel-Text Control:** Matthew 15:20's summary is far narrower and Matthew has **no parenthesis at all**.

30. **Romans 14:14 uses *koinos*, not *akathartos* — does the distinction carry weight? (raised 2026-08-12, see [[00s-clean-and-unclean-from-noah-to-messiah]] §24)** **κοινός *koinos*** = common / profane / defiled-by-association. **ἀκάθαρτος *akathartos*** = the standard Septuagint term for animals categorized as **inherently unclean**. **Peter distinguishes them in a single breath at Acts 10:14 — "common *or* unclean" (*koinon ē akatharton*).** **English flattens both into "unclean"; Greek does not.** **Recorded as a live lexical question, NOT as a conclusion in either direction** — *koinos* can also function broadly, and a single word choice does not by itself establish that Paul excludes Levitical categories. **Note also CJB collapses the distinction at Acts 10:15**, reading "unclean" where ESV keeps "common." Requires lexical work per ADR-0003.

31. **What was actually happening at the Roman table? (raised 2026-08-12, see [[00s-clean-and-unclean-from-noah-to-messiah]] §23, §25, §28)** **Romans 14:2 blocks the standard reading outright: "the weak person eats only vegetables" (λάχανα *lachana*).** **Torah never required Jews to eat only vegetables** — so whatever the Roman dispute is, at least one party's practice extends well beyond avoiding Levitically unclean species. And **Paul's instruction is symmetrical (14:3): neither party is told to change practice**, which is itself a datum about the kind of dispute it is. **Five candidate explanations, none selected:** (a) meat associated with **idolatry/pagan sacrifice**; (b) **unknown preparation or market provenance**; (c) **ritual contamination**; (d) **ascetic practice** — voluntary abstention as spiritual discipline; (e) disputes between communities with **different scruples**. The chapter also concerns **esteeming particular days**, which complicates any purely-dietary reading. **Historical context is required before Romans 14 can be used as a universal repeal of Leviticus 11 — or as evidence that it is not one. Investigate these; do not select whichever helps the current hypothesis. THIS IS THE NEXT INVESTIGATION**, deliberately placed before 1 Corinthians 8–10, because identifying the dispute makes Romans 14 much harder to weaponize in either direction.

32. **Why *these* four requirements in Acts 15 — Leviticus 17–18's *ger* laws, the Noahic blood prohibition, or both? (raised 2026-08-12, see [[00s-clean-and-unclean-from-noah-to-messiah]] §21)** The council requires abstention from things polluted by idols, sexual immorality, **what has been strangled**, and **blood**. **Two proposals commonly discussed, and they are not mutually exclusive:** a connection with **Leviticus 17–18**, where blood/sacrifice/sexual-practice laws apply not only to native Israelites but to the **ger**, the resident foreigner; and a connection with the **Noahic blood prohibition of Genesis 9:4** — notable because that one was given to humanity broadly rather than to Israel. **🚧 But we do not announce "Acts 15 is the Noahide laws." The text does not say that.** **And both silences must be preserved: Acts 15 does NOT say "do not eat pork," and it does NOT say "you may now eat everything Leviticus prohibited."** Acts 15 alone cannot settle the dietary question.

33. **Why would the king's provisions have defiled Daniel? (raised 2026-08-13, see [[00s-clean-and-unclean-from-noah-to-messiah]] §22.5)** **Daniel 1:8 says *that* the food and wine would defile him. It never says *why*.** Historically plausible: species prohibited by Torah · meat not prepared per Israelite blood requirements · association with Babylonian idolatrous practice or offerings · inability to verify preparation and provenance. **🔑 The decisive complication: Daniel also refuses the WINE, which Torah does not prohibit** — so "the king was serving pork" cannot by itself explain the decision; something broader is involved. **PRESERVE VERBATIM for the future objection:** *Possible association with Babylonian idolatry — plausible, but Daniel 1 does not explicitly identify idol sacrifice as the reason for Daniel's refusal.* If asked where Daniel says the meat was idol-sacrificed, **he doesn't, and the argument does not need him to.** **ESTABLISHED, narrower and therefore stronger:** Daniel believed the king's provisions would defile him and requested plant food and water instead. **Also corrected here:** the recollection that an angel or vision told him not to eat — **Daniel "resolved," acting on prior conviction; the visions come afterward as a gift (1:17)** — and the recollection that the others became weaker, where the text says Daniel's four were "better in appearance and fatter in flesh."

34. **Which day-dispute does Romans 14:5 have in view? (raised 2026-08-13, see [[00s-clean-and-unclean-from-noah-to-messiah]] §23)** "One person esteems one day as better than another, while another esteems all days alike." CJB: "considers some days **more holy** than others." Candidates: **Sabbath · feast days · voluntary fast days · special abstinence days · other calendrical observances.** **🚧 "Sabbath" is NOT to be inserted by default merely because later Christians commonly deploy Romans 14 in Sabbath debates** — that would be the Contextual Question Principle violated in the exact way it was formulated to prevent. **Note the datum that food and days appear in the SAME dispute**, which any explanation has to account for. Unresolved.

35. **Colossians 2 — two unresolved questions, and they are separable. (raised 2026-08-13, see [[00s-clean-and-unclean-from-noah-to-messiah]] §25d)** **(a) *sōma* at 2:17.** Greek **σῶμα** literally means **body**. **ESV renders "the substance belongs to Christ" (abstract, philosophical); CJB and RVR1960 keep "the body is of the Messiah" / "el cuerpo es de Cristo"** — which may point to **the body of Messiah, i.e. the assembly**, rather than to an abstract reality casting the shadow. **A live fork with real consequences for how the shadow→reality model applies here. Not adjudicated.** **(b) What is actually prohibited at 2:16?** The verse says "**let no one JUDGE you**" in these matters — **which is not identical to "never observe them."** Is Paul forbidding the observance, or forbidding others' judgment concerning it? **And 2:20–23 explicitly names "self-made religion and asceticism and severity to the body," attributing the rules to "human precepts and teachings"** — so Colossians may involve additional restrictive teachings beyond Torah observance, which **complicates using 2:16 as a simple "Sabbath and food laws abolished."** The whole argument is required, not the one verse.

36. **Which "word of God" sanctifies food at 1 Timothy 4:5? (raised 2026-08-13, see [[00s-clean-and-unclean-from-noah-to-messiah]] §27b)** Greek: **ἁγιάζεται** *hagiazetai* — **διὰ λόγου θεοῦ καὶ ἐντεύξεως** *dia logou theou kai enteuxeōs*, "through the word of God and prayer." RVR1960: "por la palabra de Dios y por la oración es santificado." **Four candidates, none selected:** (a) Yahweh's **creative declaration** that His creation is good; (b) **scriptural declarations identifying or providing food**; (c) the **gospel/message of God**; (d) a **combination** of scriptural authorization and thanksgiving. **🚧 We do NOT announce Paul means Leviticus 11 — he never says "as written in Leviticus," and asserting it would be eisegesis. ⚖️ But neither may the phrase be erased**, reducing Paul's argument to *prayer makes anything edible* — **he says word of God AND prayer, and both are in his explanation.** **Blocks the common shortcut** ("pray over it and you can eat anything") by the same test applied anywhere else: *"Yahweh prohibited X, but I thanked Him before doing X"* — **thanksgiving does not independently overturn a commandment.** **Genuinely relevant background:** Genesis 1:29, Genesis 9:3–4, and Leviticus 11:47 all contain prior scriptural language designating what may and may not be eaten — **which does not prove Paul invokes Leviticus, but does establish the continuing-food-law reading is not inventing the concept.**

37. **1 Timothy 4:4 — "created by God" cannot by itself mean "edible," so what bounds the category? (raised 2026-08-13, see [[00s-clean-and-unclean-from-noah-to-messiah]] §27b)** **The structural argument, which does not depend on lexicography:** Paul's proposition cannot be *everything Yahweh created must therefore be eaten* — **Yahweh created poisonous plants, toxic fungi, insects, predators, and human beings.** **Therefore verse 4 must operate inside the category verse 3 establishes: *foods* (βρωμάτων *brōmatōn*; RVR1960 "alimentos") that God created to be received.** Anyone applying v.4 without v.3's qualifier owes an account of why the same logic does not license eating anything Yahweh made. **BUT the pressure runs the other way too and is recorded:** if Paul meant *only Levitically clean animals are foods created to be received*, **why does he not say so, and why is his language this broad?** "Everything created by God is good" / "nothing is to be rejected" **is real textual evidence for broad dietary liberty, and it is not shrunk merely because a hypothesis needs it shrunk.** **NOT ESTABLISHED in either direction:** that *brōmata* necessarily excludes every creature Leviticus classified unclean, **or** that prayer transforms a biblically prohibited creature into permitted food.

38. **What exactly is Peter calling "the yoke" at Acts 15:10? (raised 2026-08-13, see [[00t-acts-15-and-the-four-requirements]] §3)** "Why are you putting God to the test by placing **a yoke on the neck of the disciples that neither our fathers nor we have been able to bear**?" **Commonly read as *the Torah itself was an unbearable burden* — but Scripture elsewhere describes Yahweh's commandments very differently:** **Deuteronomy 30:11** ("this commandment… is **not too hard for you**"; CJB "not beyond your reach"), **Psalm 19:7** ("**the law of the LORD is perfect, reviving the soul**"; CJB 19:8 "the *Torah* of *Adonai* is perfect, restoring the inner person"), **1 John 5:3** ("**his commandments are not burdensome**"). **Four candidates:** (a) the Torah as Yahweh gave it; (b) the entire covenantal administration; (c) **the demand that Gentiles become circumcised proselytes and assume the full Mosaic legal identity**; (d) the accumulated *manner* in which Torah had come to be administered. **Not settled by slogan.** **Context strongly ties the "yoke" to the proposition actually under debate at Acts 15:5** — Gentile circumcision plus commanded Torah-keeping *as a condition of inclusion* — **and the narrowest supported reading is the one this project takes when the text does not specify.** **📌 Versification recorded:** CJB numbers Psalm 19:7 as **19:8** (superscription counted as verse 1) — **third confirmed offset**, after Jeremiah 31 and Zechariah 2:6.

39. **Acts 15:21's *gar* — Reading A, Reading B, both, or neither? (raised 2026-08-13, supersedes and expands item 26; see [[00t-acts-15-and-the-four-requirements]] §5–8)** Greek **γάρ *gar*** normally introduces a reason or explanation, so **James is not changing subject — there is a logical relation between v.20's four requirements and v.21's "Moses is read every Sabbath."** **READING A:** the four are what makes **Jewish/Gentile table fellowship** possible, since Moses is proclaimed everywhere and these sensitivities are universally known — v.21 explains *why these particular issues were urgent*. **READING B:** the four are **initial** requirements, not the whole curriculum — converts will continue hearing Moses and learning. **🚧 BRAKE: v.21 does NOT say "Gentiles will attend synagogue every Sabbath and gradually learn the rest of Torah." READING B IS AN INFERENCE** — possibly a strong one, worth investigating, **but it cannot be upgraded to textual statement because it fits a hypothesis.** **THIRD POSSIBILITY:** A and B are **not mutually exclusive**. **FOURTH:** James may not be constructing our later theological categories at all — *"entry requirement or permanent list?"* may be a question the text was never answering. **Acts 21:25 may bear on this directly** and is the next control case.

40. **Did James draw the four requirements from Torah's *ger* legislation? (raised 2026-08-13, see [[00t-acts-15-and-the-four-requirements]] §10–12; expands item 32)** **The overlap is with precisely the laws Torah applied to BOTH Israelites and resident foreigners:** **Leviticus 17:10** ("any one of the house of Israel **or of the strangers who sojourn among them**" — blood), **17:12** (same, restated), **18:26** ("**either the native or the stranger who sojourns among you**" — sexual prohibitions), plus Leviticus 17's improper-slaughter and sacrifice material. **Compare Acts 15: idol pollution · sexual immorality · strangled meat · blood.** **Serious intertextual hypothesis — NOT announced as established.** **Supporting background:** **Numbers 15:15–16** — "**one statute for you and for the stranger who sojourns with you… One law and one rule**"; CJB "the same *Torah* and standard of judgment." **The Tanakh already contains categories for foreigners attaching themselves to Yahweh's people, so Gentile inclusion does not appear from nowhere in Acts** — converging with Exodus 12:48, Ezekiel 47:22–23, and Ruth. **🚧 But do NOT flatten *ger* into "Gentile Christian"** — ancient Israel under Sinai and a first-century Gentile follower of Yeshua are historically and covenantally different situations. **The legitimate question is whether James *drew upon* those requirements, not whether the categories are identical.**

41. **The Acts 15 textual asymmetry — blood retained, unclean species absent. (raised 2026-08-13, see [[00t-acts-15-and-the-four-requirements]] §12)** **Explicitly commanded for Gentiles:** abstain from blood · strangled meat · idol pollution · sexual immorality. **Explicitly absent:** any prohibition of **unclean animal species**. **If James is drawing from Leviticus 17–18, it is notable that Leviticus 11's animal classification is not reproduced.** **⚖️ This is real evidence AGAINST assuming the council imposed the entire Levitical dietary code on Gentile believers, and it is let to stand rather than explained away.** **But Acts 15:21 blocks the opposite conclusion** — "therefore Moses no longer matters" — since James invokes Moses being proclaimed every Sabbath in the very next sentence. **Both simplistic answers hit a wall, and that is the finding.** **Related independent datum:** at the council, **in the one setting where a dietary argument would have been maximally useful, Peter does not make one** — he says Yahweh cleansed their *hearts*. Third line pointing the same way as Acts 10:28 and 11:18.

42. **Could Jewish believers continue Torah-prescribed purification rites while regarding Messiah's sacrifice as the unique and completed basis of ultimate atonement? (raised 2026-08-14, see [[00u-acts-21-paul-torah-and-the-two-groups]] §5 — the canonical Researcher's Commentary example)** **The problem, not hidden:** if Hebrews presents Messiah's sacrifice as once-for-all, why does Paul participate in Temple rites involving offerings (Acts 21:26)? **Attempt to segregate the offering types FAILED: Numbers 6:14 shows the Nazirite completion includes a *sin offering*** (alongside burnt, peace, grain, drink, and wave offerings). **What the failed attempt exposed:** this project had been **flattening "sacrifice" into one category** — burnt, grain, peace/fellowship, sin/purification, guilt/reparation, thanksgiving, and vow-related offerings have different functions. **The narrowing that reopens it:** the Levitical **חַטָּאת *ḥaṭṭāʾt*** is often better understood as a **purification** offering — prescribed for ritual impurity, childbirth, bodily conditions, sanctuary purification — so the modern category *"sin offering = animal dies so eternal sins are forgiven"* is itself too crude. **Therefore participating in a rite containing a *ḥaṭṭāʾt* does NOT automatically establish that Paul sought an alternative to Messiah's redemption.** **THE SURGICAL QUESTION, left open: what function did the Numbers 6 purification offering perform?** **Future branch flagged: work Numbers 6 offering-by-offering BEFORE deciding whether Acts 21 conflicts with Hebrews — or whether the conflict is being manufactured by importing a later, flattened definition of "sacrifice."**

43. **ὑπὸ νόμον — *hypo nomon*, "under law": what does Paul actually mean? (raised 2026-08-14, see [[00u-acts-21-paul-torah-and-the-two-groups]] §12)** The phrase recurs in Paul and is often treated as having one obvious sense. **"Obeying any Torah command" creates immediate problems**, because Paul himself quotes Torah as authoritative, **calls the Law holy** (Romans 7:12), tells children to honor father and mother, condemns idolatry/adultery/theft — **and practices Jewish observance in Acts 21.** **Candidate senses:** jurisdiction under the Mosaic covenant · law as a **system of justification** · **condemnation** under law · particular rhetorical contexts. **And 1 Corinthians 9:20 makes it sharper rather than easier:** Paul says he became "as one under the law (**though not being myself under the law**)" — **while in Acts 21 he is described as living in observance of the law.** **Any definition must accommodate both.** Requires a lexical/contextual study of ***nomos*** and ***hypo nomon*** per ADR-0003, rather than English slogans.

44. **Does Luke portray the Torah-zealous Jewish believers of Acts 21:20 neutrally, positively, or as an immature transitional practice? (raised 2026-08-14, see [[00u-acts-21-paul-torah-and-the-two-groups]] §15)** Luke calls them **believers** *and* **"all zealous for the law"** (CJB: "zealots for the *Torah*") — **decades after the Jerusalem Council — and does not condemn them.** **Neither does he explicitly commend the zeal as such.** **Related unresolved tensions from the same passage:** (a) **Temple offerings** vs Messiah's once-for-all sacrifice — see item 42; (b) **how much Torah applies to Gentiles** beyond the Acts 15 decree and apostolic moral instruction; (c) **AD 70** — the Temple's destruction made Temple/priesthood/sacrifice-dependent observance impossible **in its biblical form**, but *how that event should be understood theologically* is a separate question that **reconnects directly to Matthew 24** ([[00g-matthew-24-and-the-oikoumene]]).

45. **Could the destruction of the Temple in AD 70 represent the historical termination of an already-surpassed sacrificial administration? (raised 2026-08-14, see [[00u-acts-21-paul-torah-and-the-two-groups]] §27)** **This project has approached AD 70 exclusively through prophecy** — Matthew 24, "this generation," cosmic judgment language, preterist chronology ([[00g-matthew-24-and-the-oikoumene]], [[00m-fig-tree-generation-heaven-and-earth]]). **The Torah/food investigation has now reached it from a completely different direction.** **The striking sequence:** Messiah's sacrifice → apostolic proclamation → **Jewish believers still using the Temple (Acts 21:26)** → Hebrews develops Messiah's superior priesthood and sacrifice → **Temple destroyed.** **🚧 BUT THIS IS A HYPOTHESIS AND MUST BE LABELED ONE. Hebrews does not say "keep doing this until AD 70." Acts does not say "these sacrifices remain legitimate until Rome destroys the Temple." NEITHER TEXT SUPPLIES THE TERMINUS.** **What is historically obvious:** Temple/priesthood/sacrifice-dependent observance became impossible **in its biblical form**. **What is not established:** what that event means theologically. **Recorded because the convergence of two independent investigative lines on the same event is itself a datum** — and because it gives the eventual Matthew 24 return a second line of approach that prophecy alone would not have produced.

46. **What is the actual semantic content of *ḥaṭṭāʾt*, given Numbers 6:9–12? (raised 2026-08-14, see [[00u-acts-21-paul-torah-and-the-two-groups]] §20; refines item 42)** **A Nazirite beside whom someone dies suddenly — through no fault, no deliberate act — must bring a *ḥaṭṭāʾt* (6:11) AND an *asham* (6:12), and the text states the priest makes atonement "because he sinned by reason of the dead body" (CJB: "inasmuch as he sinned because of the dead person").** **⚖️ BOTH HALVES MUST STAND.** **ESTABLISHED:** the biblical category is **broader than deliberate moral transgression**, since it plainly covers involuntary corpse-contact — which is why *ḥaṭṭāʾt* is frequently explained as a **purification offering** dealing with impurity and restoration within the sanctuary system. **NOT ESTABLISHED:** that *ḥaṭṭāʾt* carries **no** sin-content at all — **the text does use sin-language, and we are not entitled to relabel it "purification" and move on because that resolves a difficulty for us.** **Consequence for Acts 21 (item 42): the presence of a *ḥaṭṭāʾt* neither proves Paul sought an alternative to Messiah's atonement NOR is safely dismissed as merely ceremonial.** Requires Hebrew lexical work per ADR-0003. **Note also that a normally-completed vow (6:14) includes a *ḥaṭṭāʾt* as well — so this is not confined to the defilement case.**

47. **What is the *cheirographon* of Colossians 2:14 — and why it is NOT "the Torah." (raised 2026-08-14, see [[00v-colossians-2-shadow-and-body]] §2)** ESV: "by canceling **the record of debt** that stood against us with its legal demands. This he set aside, **nailing it to the cross**." CJB: "**He wiped away the bill of charges against us.**" **Greek: χειρόγραφον — *cheirographon*, a handwritten document, commonly a certificate of indebtedness.** **❗ THE COMMON CLAIM "the Torah was nailed to the cross" REPLACES PAUL'S NOUN WITH A DIFFERENT ONE. Paul does not write νόμος *nomos*.** **This is the same shape of error as reading "planet Earth" into *gē* (ADR-0003) or citing CJB's "legalism" as though the Greek said it (Galatians 5:4).** **Candidates for what the *cheirographon* is** — a debt certificate, a record of transgressions, the indictment standing against us — **but Colossians does not define it here, and this project does not pick one.** Requires lexical and contextual work per ADR-0003. **Recorded chiefly as a guard: whatever it turns out to mean, the substitution must not be made silently.**

48. **Colossians 2:17's τῶν μελλόντων — "things to come," written AFTER the cross. (raised 2026-08-14, see [[00v-colossians-2-shadow-and-body]] §8)** Paul writes after Yeshua's death and resurrection, yet describes the shadow in relation to **"the things to come."** **If everything represented by Sabbath, festivals, food, and sacred calendar had been completely exhausted at the cross, why the future-oriented language?** **🚧 DO NOT OVERSTATE:** Greek participles of *mellō* can function broadly, and the exact temporal force requires contextual examination. **This is not an argument yet.** **But it earns the question: did Paul understand some realities signified by Israel's sacred calendar as still awaiting fulfillment?** **Potentially significant for eschatology**, since Passover, Firstfruits, Pentecost, Trumpets, Atonement, and Tabernacles carry substantial symbolic associations — **and this project has deliberately not jumped there.** Requires Greek-grammar work per ADR-0003.

49. **Colossians 2 — who is doing the judging, and what are they criticizing? (raised 2026-08-14, see [[00v-colossians-2-shadow-and-body]] §9–12; refines item 35)** **READING A (discontinuity):** Torah-observant/Judaizing people criticize Gentile believers for **not** observing dietary and calendar requirements; Paul replies that these were shadows fulfilled in Messiah. **READING B (continuity):** ascetic or syncretistic teachers criticize believers **for how they observe**; Paul defends them from extra-biblical restriction. **THE OPPONENT PROFILE FROM 2:18–23 FAVORS SOMETHING STRANGER THAN ORDINARY TORAH OBSERVANCE:** **asceticism · worship of angels · going on in detail about visions · "Do not handle, Do not taste, Do not touch" · and explicitly "according to HUMAN precepts and teachings."** **Resembles Mark 7's commandment-of-God vs tradition-of-men, and 1 Timothy 4's forbid-marriage-and-require-abstinence** — a recurring Pauline concern with **religiously motivated deprivation elevated into spiritual righteousness.** **⚖️ BUT THE BRAKE RUNS BOTH WAYS AND NEITHER READING ABSORBS EVERYTHING:** continuity readers cannot say "nothing here concerns Torah" — **v.17 plainly calls food/drink + festival + new moon + Sabbath a shadow whose body is Messiah**; discontinuity readers cannot say "therefore Torah was nailed to the cross" — **Paul didn't use *nomos*, and the opponents include clearly extra-biblical practices.** **Unresolved.**

50. **σαββατισμός — *sabbatismos* (Hebrews 4:9): what does the word mean, and is the rest already entered, still future, or both? (raised 2026-08-14, see [[00w-sabbath-creation-covenant-sign-shadow-rest]] §12, §22 — THIS IS THE NEXT INVESTIGATION)** **Hebrews avoids the ordinary σάββατον *sabbaton* and uses an unusual word.** **🔴 THE TRANSLATIONS DIVERGE ALONG THE EXACT FAULT LINE UNDER INVESTIGATION:** **ESV "there remains a *Sabbath rest* for the people of God"** (a **state** entered) vs **CJB "there remains a *Shabbat-keeping* for God's people"** (an **observance** practiced). The *-ismos* ending is verbal in shape, which is why CJB reads it that way — **but morphology does not by itself settle the referent.** **⚖️ Interest noted in both directions and settling nothing:** CJB is a Messianic Jewish translation with a stake in the observance reading; ESV is traditional-Christian with a stake in the state reading *(same category as Mark 7:19's parenthesis, item 29, and Matthew 24:34's "this people")*. **Three determinations required:** (a) what *sabbatismos* meant in its linguistic and historical setting, and **why Hebrews chose it over *sabbaton***; (b) how Hebrews builds the argument — **Genesis 2 → wilderness Israel → Psalm 95 → Joshua → "Today" → Messiah**; (c) **🔑 is the rest ALREADY ENTERED, STILL FUTURE, or BOTH?** **That third question may reconnect Sabbath to the eschatological framework built since Stage 7, because *already / not yet / both* is the same structural question the millennium, the "little season," and Matthew 24 keep raising.** **Note the convergence: third line arriving at that junction from a non-prophetic direction**, after Acts 21's AD 70 question (item 45) and Colossians 2's *tōn mellontōn* (item 48). Requires Greek lexical and historical work per ADR-0003.

51. **Isaiah 66:23's Sabbath after the restoration horizon — and the Ezekiel consistency brake. (raised 2026-08-14, see [[00w-sabbath-creation-covenant-sign-shadow-rest]] §17–18)** ESV: "**From new moon to new moon, and from Sabbath to Sabbath, ALL FLESH shall come to worship before me**, declares the LORD" — placed after the **new heavens and new earth** of 66:22. **Read straightforwardly as eschatological, Sabbath appears AFTER the judgment/restoration horizon, and not merely for Israel.** **That makes "Sabbath belonged only to Sinai and disappears afterward" harder to sustain.** **🚧 BUT THIS PROJECT CANNOT USE ISAIAH 66 RESPONSIBLY YET:** where does it sit relative to the New Jerusalem, the covenant transition, AD 70, and Revelation 21–22? **Marked HIGH RELEVANCE, ESCHATOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION UNRESOLVED.** *(Note Isaiah 65:20's death-at-100 complication already recorded at [[00m-fig-tree-generation-heaven-and-earth]] — the same passage-complex is already flagged as difficult.)* **❗ AND THE CONSISTENCY BRAKE, WHICH CUTS BOTH WAYS: Ezekiel's temple vision includes Sabbaths, new moons, AND SACRIFICES, priesthood, and Temple measurements. If Isaiah's future Sabbath proves a literal future Sabbath, consistency demands asking what we are doing with Ezekiel's future sacrifices. WE MAY NOT LITERALIZE WHICHEVER PROPHETIC INSTITUTION SUPPORTS OUR POSITION AND SYMBOLIZE THE INCONVENIENT ONES.** **Isaiah 56 + Isaiah 66 + Ezekiel 40–48 eventually need their own prophetic-control study.**

52. **Sunday — a completely separate proposition requiring its own evidentiary bridge. (raised 2026-08-14, see [[00w-sabbath-creation-covenant-sign-shadow-rest]] §20)** **Even if this project eventually concluded that weekly seventh-day Sabbath is not covenantally obligatory for Gentile believers, that would NOT establish Sunday as the new Sabbath. 79. **Is Pentecost a deliberate Babel reversal? (raised 2026-08-16, see [[01d-had-the-gospel-already-gone-throughout-the-world]] §9)** **Genesis 11: languages divided → peoples dispersed. Acts 2: many languages → one message understood → peoples gathered, then sent out.** **Tier D — INTERTEXTUAL HYPOTHESIS, NOT ESTABLISHED.** The contrast is strong; **the text does not state the connection**, and this project does not supply connections texts leave unstated. **Not necessarily a reversal of all Babel consequences.** Relevant because Matthew 24:14 concerns proclamation to the nations and Acts 2 depicts that mission beginning from Jerusalem.

80. **Acts 18:10 — what does "I have many in this city who are my people" denote about people who have not yet believed? (raised 2026-08-16, see [[01e-did-yahweh-know-whom-the-mission-needed-to-reach]] §2)** **ESV verified.** Yeshua tells Paul to keep speaking *because* of these people. **The reasoning runs: Yeshua knows His people → knows where they are → keeps His messenger there → the message reaches them.** **This is the single closest text to the author's hypothesis and it is strong — and it is ONE statement, in a vision, about ONE city.** **A doctrine may not be built on it alone.**

81. **Acts 16:6–7 — on what basis were Asia and Bithynia forbidden AT THAT TIME? (raised 2026-08-16, see [[01e-did-yahweh-know-whom-the-mission-needed-to-reach]] §4)** **ESV verified: "having been forbidden by the Holy Spirit to speak the word in Asia" · "the Spirit of Jesus did not allow them" [into Bithynia].** **❗ THE PROHIBITIONS ARE THE POINT — they were TRYING to proclaim and were twice stopped.** **NOT ESTABLISHED** whether the restriction was about **timing**, sequence, resource allocation, or something else. **Note Asia IS later evangelised (Acts 19), which favours a timing reading — but favouring is not establishing.**

82. **John 10:16's "other sheep" — who? (raised 2026-08-16, see [[01e-did-yahweh-know-whom-the-mission-needed-to-reach]] §7)** "I have other sheep that are not of this fold. I must bring them also, and they will listen to my voice." **Gentiles is the obvious major reading — but it may not be stamped onto the text without doing the Johannine work.** Diaspora Israel is a live alternative. **⚠️ NOT RETRIEVED this pass (see item 84).** **The underlying shepherd theology is unmistakable regardless: Yeshua knows His sheep before the narrative depicts them gathered.**

83. **🔑 Acts 13:48's *tetagmenoi* — middle or passive? And should it be resolved together with Romans 9:22? (raised 2026-08-16, see [[01e-did-yahweh-know-whom-the-mission-needed-to-reach]] §6)** **Greek verified by interlinear: τεταγμένοι, lemma τάσσω (5021), PERFECT PARTICIPLE — MIDDLE *OR* PASSIVE — nom. masc. plural (V-RPM/P-NMP).** **❗ THE VOICE IS GRAMMATICALLY UNDETERMINED: the form does not decide between "having been appointed" and "having arranged/disposed themselves."** **🔑 AND THIS IS THE SECOND TIME THIS PROJECT HAS VERIFIED EXACTLY THIS SITUATION — Romans 9:22's κατηρτισμένα (item 59) is also a perfect participle, middle or passive, with no stated agent.** **The two most-contested election texts in the canon both rest on a perfect participle whose voice the grammar does not settle — verified independently, both times, without importing either side of the historic debate. NEITHER SIDE CAN CLAIM THE GRAMMAR.** **That convergence is itself a finding.** **The two may need to be resolved together or not at all.** **The later Calvinist/Arminian debate is NOT imported into Acts.** The textual observation is unavoidable: Luke connects those who believed with those described as *tetagmenoi*.

84. **⚠️ RETRIEVAL DEBT — Stage 25½. (raised 2026-08-16, see [[01e-did-yahweh-know-whom-the-mission-needed-to-reach]] §1, §7, §8, §9, §15)** **NOT retrieved this pass and therefore PROVISIONAL wherever used:** **Matthew 7:13–14** (narrow gate / "few") · **John 10:14–16, 27** · **Romans 8:29** (foreknew/predestined) · **Ephesians 1:4** (chosen before the foundation of the world) · **2 Peter 3:9** (not wishing any should perish) · **1 Timothy 2:4** (desires all to be saved). **They were carried from the tranche because the stage's central claim does NOT depend on them — the Acts texts, which WERE verified, carry it.** **❗ Note that 2 Peter 3:9 and 1 Timothy 2:4 are the COUNTERWEIGHT texts against the "they weren't meant to hear anyway" formulation — a brake resting on unverified citations is itself a weakness, though the conservative direction of the error means the brake should stand until closed.**

85. **🔑 Is Matthew 28:20's "to the end of the age" the SAME *synteleia tou aiōnos* as Matthew 24:3? (raised 2026-08-16, see [[01d-had-the-gospel-already-gone-throughout-the-world]] §16)** **VERIFY, DO NOT ASSUME.** **If they are the same phrase denoting the same age, then the Great Commission's own DURATION CLAUSE is tied to Matthew 24's framework — which would bear directly on whether Matthew 28 establishes a perpetual universal obligation or one bounded by the same horizon as Matthew 24:14.** **Potentially decisive, and the reason Stage 26 must be Matthew 28.** **Interacts with item 77 (which age?) — neither can be settled without the other.** **⚠️ AND THE BRAKE MUST RUN BOTH WAYS: identical phrasing does not by itself prove identical referent, any more than a shared lemma proved shared meaning at items 68 and 76.**

86. **🔑 REFERENTIAL half of q85 — do Matthew's four uses of συντέλεια τοῦ αἰῶνος denote ONE horizon? (raised 2026-08-16, see [[01f-matthew-28-the-great-commission-in-context]] §11)** **LEXICAL HALF NOW CLOSED BY RETRIEVAL: all four are the same phrase — συντέλεια (4930) + αἰών (165) — at Matthew 13:39, 13:49, 24:3, and 28:20.** **This is a MATTHEW-DISTINCTIVE expression and the Great Commission's own duration clause is one of the four.** **Invisible in English**, where older renderings give 13:39 "end of the world" against 24:3/28:20 "end of the age." **🚧 BUT IDENTICAL PHRASING DOES NOT PROVE IDENTICAL REFERENT — the same brake that killed the Stage 23 bridge (three shared lemmas collapsing to one) and that bounded Romans 15:19's shared *plēroō* (item 76).** **CONSEQUENCE: if the four DO denote one horizon, then Matthew 28:20's "until" runs to whatever Matthew 24 describes, and the Commission's DURATION and Matthew 24's TIMING become the same question — making item 77 the hinge of BOTH the preterist timeline AND the Great Commission.** **Contexts to examine: 13:39–43 (weeds — harvest, angels, furnace) · 13:47–50 (net — separation) · 24:3 against 24:34's "this generation" · 28:20.** **⚠️ STATED IN ADVANCE: if they do NOT point to one horizon, the preterist hypothesis must absorb that evidence.** **This is Stage 27.**

87. **Does the recursive-commission argument establish individual obligation or only communal continuation? (raised 2026-08-16, see [[01f-matthew-28-the-great-commission-in-context]] §8–§10)** **The argument at full strength:** Yeshua commanded the eleven to make disciples, then to teach those disciples *"all that I have commanded you"* — therefore disciple-making is itself taught, therefore reproduces indefinitely. **NOT DISMISSED — probably the strongest textual case for the Commission being structurally ongoing.** **❗ BUT IT CANNOT DISTRIBUTE EVERY COMMAND IDENTICALLY: Yeshua also commanded the apostles things specifically apostolic or situational — Matthew 10's mission instructions, themselves restricted to Israel, are the example. If "everything I commanded" distributed uniformly, every believer would have to be apostle, prophet, teacher AND evangelist, which 1 Corinthians 12:29 denies.** **ESTABLISHES MORE READILY: the community continues making disciples. LESS READILY: every individual member carries an identical personal commission to proclaim throughout the earth.** **Requires closing item 66's retrieval debt (Eph 4:11, 1 Cor 12:29) before the denial can be cited as verified.**

88. **⚠️ RETRIEVAL DEBT — Stage 26. (raised 2026-08-16, see [[01f-matthew-28-the-great-commission-in-context]] §19)** **NOT retrieved this pass:** **Matthew 10:5–6** (the restriction to the lost sheep of Israel — load-bearing for §6's development argument) · **Matthew 10:14** (shake off the dust — §13's "rejection was anticipated") · **Matthew 28:16–18** full text · **Matthew 13:39–43 and 13:47–50** in full narrative context · **Acts 1:8**. **The GREEK that carries the argument WAS retrieved** (*poreuthentes*, *mathēteusate*, *panta ta ethnē*, *tērein*, *heōs tēs synteleias tou aiōnos*, and all four συντέλεια occurrences); **the surrounding narrative was carried from the tranche.** **Matthew 13's full contexts must be closed as part of Stage 27, since the parabolic settings are exactly what item 86 turns on.**

## Bookmarked — Flagged for Later, Not Yet Investigated

Distinct from the numbered list above: those are things actually researched that remain unresolved. These are topics explicitly deferred — noted so nothing gets lost, but genuinely not yet examined. Move an item out of this section (into the numbered list above, or into [[01-convictions]]/[[03-hypotheses]]) once real investigation happens — don't let it sit here pretending to be settled just because it's written down.

- **Luke 1:3's "orderly" (Greek *kathexēs*) doesn't necessarily mean strict chronological order.** (Flagged 2026-08-09, see [[00b-biblical-epistemology]] "Luke's Own Method.") Relevant later when comparing the Gospels' sometimes-differing event sequences — don't assume "orderly account" makes Luke's sequence the chronological arbiter against Matthew's or Mark's without checking what *kathexēs* actually claims.
- **The trumpet comparison — one event or independent uses of shared imagery?** (Flagged 2026-08-11, see [[00k-the-gathering-of-the-elect]] §9.) Requires comparing **Isaiah 27:13** (great trumpet, regathering) · **Matthew 24:31** · **1 Corinthians 15:52** (last trumpet, dead raised) · **1 Thessalonians 4:16** (trumpet of God, resurrection and gathering) · **Revelation's trumpets**. Both shortcuts refused: cannot align Matthew with Isaiah while ignoring Paul, and cannot assume shared imagery means shared event (*compatibility ≠ identity*). **This is where a past-millennium reading faces its hardest test** — it must establish which of these events are identical and which aren't, and the elaborateness of any required exceptions is itself evidence.
- **Does Revelation inherit Isaiah 27's serpent-defeat/restoration pattern?** (Flagged 2026-08-11, see [[00k-the-gathering-of-the-elect]] §8.) Isaiah 27:1 has Leviathan/the dragon judged, then vineyard restored, Israel gathered, great trumpet. Revelation has dragon/ancient serpent → defeat → Messiah's authority → the people of Yahweh. Recorded as an intertextual observation, deliberately unresolved.
- **Who are "the elect" in Matthew 24:31?** (Flagged 2026-08-11.) Scattered Israel alone, or the renewed covenant people centered on Messiah including the nations? *Eklektos* has deep Israel roots (Isaiah 45:4, 65:9) but is applied to Messiah's people in Romans 8:33 and Colossians 3:12. Carried forward from [[00k-the-gathering-of-the-elect]] §6.
- **Yeshua's departure and Ezekiel's glory.** (Flagged 2026-08-10, see [[00j-cosmic-signs-and-intertextual-priority]] §8.) Ezekiel 11:23 has Yahweh's glory leaving the condemned city and stopping on "the mountain east of the city" — the Mount of Olives. Matthew 23:38 declares the house desolate, 24:1 has Yeshua leaving the Temple, 24:3 places Him on the Mount of Olives. **Evidentiary status: supports** (stronger than permits, well short of requires). Matthew never states the fulfillment; the pattern is striking and the authorial intent unstated.
- **Danielic Kingdom Parallel — Daniel 2, the stone, and when Messiah's reign begins.** (Flagged 2026-08-12.) **Deliberately NOT used as evidence in [[00j-cosmic-signs-and-intertextual-priority]]**, because Daniel 2 does not supply Matthew 24's cosmic-luminary vocabulary. Inserting it there merely because it also concerns kingdoms and judgment would have made that argument *less* precise. **This illustrates something the method should keep:** a passage can be true and enormously relevant to the larger theology while not being relevant evidence for the specific exegetical question currently under investigation. We are not collecting verses that broadly agree — we are asking each text a specific question.
    - **What Daniel 2 textually establishes:** four successive kingdoms (gold head = Babylon, explicitly, 2:37–38; then silver, bronze, iron) → the fourth "strong as iron" (2:40) → feet/toes "**a divided kingdom**, partly strong and partly brittle," which "will not hold together, just as iron does not mix with clay" (2:41–43) → a stone "**cut out by no human hand**" strikes the feet, the whole image is pulverized, and the stone "became a great mountain and **filled the whole earth**" (2:34–35) → "**in the days of those kings** the God of heaven will set up a kingdom that shall never be destroyed" (2:44).
    - **Traditional identification** (substantial pedigree, not uncontested): Babylon → Medo-Persia → Greece → Rome. Strengthened by **Daniel 8:20–21**, which explicitly names "the kings of Media and Persia" and "the king of Greece." **Competing scholarly reconstructions exist** — notably ones separating Media from Persia and making Greece the fourth kingdom — and should be documented rather than ignored.
    - **Corrections recorded (2026-08-12):** (1) The sequence has **four** kingdoms, not five — separating "Medes" and "Persians" would give five. (2) Daniel does **not** identify the iron/clay phase as modern governments descended from Rome; that belongs to later eschatological systems, not the Biblical Observation bucket. (3) Nor does Daniel identify it as Republic vs. Empire — and for the record, **the Roman Republic preceded the Empire** (conventionally 509–27 BC, then Augustus from 27 BC), so any such reconstruction would need its phases stated in the right order. The text says: divided, partly iron, partly brittle, alliances that don't adhere. Everything past that is interpretation.
    - **The genuinely productive intertextual question this raises:** Daniel 2 gives *four kingdoms → an everlasting divine kingdom* (2:44). Daniel 7 gives *four beasts → judgment → Son of Man → everlasting dominion* (7:13–14). Same endpoint, different symbolic register. And Daniel 2:44 places the kingdom's establishment "**in the days of those kings**" — not millennia after the fourth kingdom vanished. Yeshua then appears under Roman rule announcing **Mark 1:15**: ESV: "**The time is fulfilled**, and the kingdom of God is at hand." CJB: "The time has come, God's Kingdom is near!"
    - **And the stone imagery recurs in Matthew.** **Matthew 21:42–44** — quoting Psalm 118's rejected stone, then: ESV: "the one who falls on this stone will be broken to pieces; and when it falls on anyone, **it will crush him**." RVR1960: "sobre quien ella cayere, **le desmenuzará**" — the same crushing/pulverizing language Daniel 2 uses of the stone striking the image. **And Matthew 21 sits immediately before the Matthew 23–24 judgment material this framework has been working.** *(Textual note: some manuscripts omit Matthew 21:44 — CJB's rendering ends at v.43. Worth verifying before building on it.)*
    - **Open Intertextual Question:** what relationship exists among Daniel 2's stone kingdom, Daniel 7's Son of Man kingdom, Psalm 118's rejected stone, Matthew 21:42–44's crushing stone, and Yeshua's announcement that the kingdom had drawn near?
    - **The larger question it feeds:** before asking *when did the thousand years begin*, this framework must first establish **when Scripture says Messiah's reign began**. Those are not necessarily the same question, and collapsing them prematurely would be exactly the error the method exists to prevent.
- **Open hypothesis — Rupes Nigra and Daniel 2:35.** (Raised 2026-08-12.) Daniel 2:34–35's stone "cut out by no human hand" becoming "a great mountain" that "filled the whole earth" prompted a connection to later cartographic traditions concerning **Rupes Nigra**, depicted as a great magnetic black rock/mountain near the geographic north.
    - **No identification between Daniel's mountain and Rupes Nigra is established.** Compound hypothesis requiring several independent propositions to survive: (a) what Daniel's mountain means textually, (b) the provenance and development of the Rupes Nigra cartographic tradition, (c) any connection between them.
    - **Method requirement:** investigate Daniel 2 on its own, investigate Rupes Nigra on its own, **then** compare. Do not research Rupes Nigra *in order to* prove Daniel 2 — that inverts the epistemological loop.
    - **Lexical note:** Daniel 2 is **Aramaic** at this point, not Hebrew. 2:35's "whole earth" is **כָּל־אַרְעָא** (*kol-ʾarʿāʾ*). Conclusions reached about Greek *gē* or *oikoumenē* ([[00g-matthew-24-and-the-oikoumene]], [[00l-tribes-of-the-land]]) **cannot be imported** — *ʾarʿāʾ* needs its own study within Biblical Aramaic. Same principle, different language.
    - **Brake on the magnetism argument:** compass needles align with Earth's magnetic field; they are not attracted toward a magnetic mountain at a geographic center. "Compasses point north → therefore Rupes Nigra" is not usable evidence. *(Term note: the field is described as **toroidal**, not "Horus.")*
    - **Brake on the cartographic argument:** old European maps genuinely did depict a great Arctic magnetic rock, and the **tradition itself is a legitimate historical research subject** — how old are the surviving sources, where did the story originate, what did they actually claim, were cartographers reporting observation or reproducing inherited lore? **Critical condition:** independent maps only count as independent evidence if they are not all copying one earlier source. Twenty maps reproducing one tradition are one witness, not twenty. Belongs in [[02-historical-observations]] when earned.
- **The Cross as Cosmic Turning Point.** (Flagged 2026-08-10, see [[00i-stars-the-dragon-and-the-cross]] §7 for the assembled arc.) - **"The powers of the heavens" (Matthew 24:29) — what are they?** (Flagged 2026-08-10, see [[00j-cosmic-signs-and-intertextual-priority]] §9.) Greek *hai dynameis tōn ouranōn*. Live possibilities: celestial bodies, heavenly/spiritual powers, symbolic ruling structures, cosmic order generally. Revelation 12's heavenly-beings material makes the spiritual reading live; Isaiah 13/34 and Ezekiel 32 equally support cosmic-judgment imagery. - **New Testament use of the Septuagint where it differs from the Masoretic Text.** (Flagged 2026-08-09, see [[00c-commission-covenant-investigation]] Acts 15.) James's citation of Amos 9:11–12 in Acts 15:16–18 follows the Greek Septuagint tradition more closely than the later Masoretic Hebrew wording. **This directly complicates ADR-0003's "go to the source language" rule** — the apostles themselves sometimes quote the Greek Scriptures circulating in their world, so "go back to the Hebrew" is not always the simple corrective it sounds like. Needs real work: which text-form did NT authors use, when, and what follows for a study that treats original-language priority as a methodological principle. Not resolved; genuinely important for the translation policy.
- **Independent testimony and variation in detail.** (Flagged 2026-08-09.) General historical principle worth stating carefully rather than loosely: independent witnesses don't need identical wording to be credible, and perfectly harmonized accounts can suggest dependence rather than independence. But variation isn't automatically proof of authenticity either — each discrepancy still needs its own examination. Proper formulation to work from: *agreement in core events + independent variation in detail is consistent with genuine testimony, but every specific discrepancy still requires examination.* Belongs with the Luke-as-historian material ([[00b-biblical-epistemology]], "Luke's Own Method") and any future Gospel-comparison work — not developed yet.
- **Circumcision of the heart, and whether Gentile believers required physical circumcision.** (Flagged 2026-08-09.) The mountain of the Ten Commandments is **Sinai**; "Horeb" is the closely-related name used more often in Deuteronomy, with "Sinai" more common in Exodus. Not two different mountains in the biblical tradition. *(Separately, and unrelated to the naming: claims relocating Sinai outside the conventional region belong in [[03-hypotheses]] item 10, alternative geography — not here.)*
