# Provenance Correction Log

> ### **WHAT GOES IN HERE: any sentence in which the manuscript stated something about the author — what he believed, why he believed it, what he was avoiding, what he would or would not do — that the author had to correct because it was not true of him.**
>
> ### 🛑 **THE RULE THIS REGISTER EXISTS TO ENFORCE: a theological position does not disclose an interior motive.** Knowing which side of an argument someone takes tells you nothing about why they take it, what it costs them, or what they do in practice. The distance between "he argues X" and "he argues X because he wants Y" can only be closed by asking him. It may never be closed by inference, however well the inference fits the argument.

# Standing rules

## PR-READING-001 — Prior-reading provenance

> ### 🛑 **NEVER INFER THAT THE AUTHOR WAS ENCOUNTERING A BIBLICAL BOOK, CHAPTER, PASSAGE, FIGURE, DOCTRINE OR CONCEPT FOR THE FIRST TIME MERELY BECAUSE THE INVESTIGATION FIRST ANALYZED IT AT THAT POINT.**
>
> ### 📐 **THE MECHANICAL TEST: first appearance in the research chronology ≠ first exposure in the author's life.**

The author had read Revelation and the rest of Scripture extensively before this project began. What is new in this investigation is the **analytical frame** and the **connections**, not the text. Unless the research record explicitly establishes that something was genuinely new to him, describe novelty in terms of **the investigation**, never in terms of his prior biblical exposure.

| ✅ **Use** | ❌ **Avoid unless the record supports it** |
|---|---|
| a chapter we had not yet analyzed | a chapter I had never read |
| a passage I had not yet examined in this investigation | a passage I have not read yet |
| a connection I had not previously noticed | I encountered this passage for the first time |
| I had read the passage before, but I had not connected it to… | I had never seen this before |
| we had not yet placed this passage into the sequence | I didn't know Revelation said… |
| a familiar passage now read in a different context | |
| not yet **exegeted** / not yet **retrieved verbatim** | not yet **read** |

❗ **This is a provenance rule, not a style preference.** The book speaks in the first person. A sentence claiming the author had not read a chapter of Scripture is the book stating a false fact about the person whose name is on it — the same failure class as PR-001, reached by a different route. PR-001 inferred a *motive* from a *position*; this one infers a *reading history* from a *research chronology*. Both substitute an inference for something only the author can report.

✅ **An audit against this rule is required before any Part closes.** A blind search-and-replace is forbidden: *"I had never noticed the connection between X and Y"* is usually true and should stay, while *"a chapter I have not read yet"* is false. Every hit is checked against the research record individually.

## PR-001 — Part IV described the author's position as approaching "a permission slip for silence"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Status** | ✅ **CORRECTED in both editions, 2026-09-13, before Part IV was snapshotted.** |
| **Where** | `en/ch21-romans-10.md` and `en/ch24-matthew-28.md`, plus the Spanish siblings `es/ch21-romanos-10.md` and `es/ch24-mateo-28.md`. Spanish Part IV had already been drafted and pushed, so both editions carried the error and both were corrected in the same pass. |
| **What the manuscript said** | That the author's original position, examined honestly, was "closer to a permission slip [for silence] than I would have admitted" — and, in ch24, attributed to him the words *"preaching is not my calling, therefore the question does not reach me,"* framed as *"which is what I actually said."* |
| **What is actually true** | He has spoken about Messiah whenever the subject arose, for as long as he has been reading the Word, and holds that anyone who genuinely believes should have something to say in defense of it. His actual argument was narrower and entirely different: that his relative's position — *everyone must proclaim in that specific manner, or they are sinning* — frames the obligation wrongly. He was never avoiding the question. |
| **Why the sentence was wrong, precisely** | It inferred a *motive* from a *position*. Because he was arguing against a universal-proclamation obligation, the draft treated that as evidence of a personal reluctance the obligation would have exposed. Those are unrelated. Someone can argue that an obligation is misframed while fully discharging what he does believe is owed — which is what was actually happening. |
| **How it was found** | The author caught it on reading the reviewed Part IV manuscript: *"I never said that I would never talk about Messiah if brought up… I think you or Claude took too much liberty in assuming rather than actually asking whether that was true or not."* |
| **What the correction did** | The opening statement of the position in ch21 was expanded to include what he was **not** saying, since its incompleteness is what made the later sentence possible. The "permission slip" sentence was replaced with a statement that the second half of the formulation was never in dispute on his side. In ch24 the misattributed quotation was reframed as something he was reaching for imprecisely, and the retrospective was rewritten to the claim that actually survives: that a supposedly completed first-century proclamation can no longer be used as a cheap way of disposing of the continuing-mission question. |
| **Cost if it had shipped** | The book's core method is that a claim is only as strong as what was actually retrieved. A first-person sentence the author would not sign undercuts that method more than a weak argument would — a reader who knows him would stop trusting the retrieved material too. |

## PR-002 — Part VI states that the author had not read Revelation 20

| | |
|---|---|
| **Status** | ❗ **OPEN — logged 2026-09-20. Part VI is closed in both editions and is NOT being reopened for it.** ⚠️ **This is a SUBSTANTIVE correction, not a presentation repair — it does not belong in the same pass as LAYOUT-001/002 and must not be folded into a format-fix milestone.** |
| **Where** | `en/ch37-the-harvest-the-bowls-and-armageddon.md` — section heading, EN **p. 154**. Sibling: `es/ch37-la-siega-las-copas-y-armagedon.md`, same heading. **Both editions.** |
| **What the manuscript says** | EN: *"Three figures gathering kings — and a chapter I have not read yet."* ES: *"Tres figuras reuniendo reyes — y un capítulo 20 que todavía no he leído."* The Spanish names the chapter outright, so the referent is **Revelation 20**. |
| **What is actually true** | The author has read Revelation many times, Revelation 20 included. What had not happened at that point in the investigation was **exegeting Revelation 20 within this walkthrough** and placing it in the sequence being assembled. |
| **Why the sentence was wrong, precisely** | It converted a fact about the **research chronology** — chapter 20 had not yet been worked through in the investigation's sequential pass — into a fact about the author's **life**. See **PR-READING-001**. |
| ★ **The tell that makes this unambiguous** | **The same chapter already contains the correct formulation, a few lines away, in both editions:** EN *"deciding a chapter's meaning from a chapter I have not yet **exegeted**"* · ES *"desde un capítulo que todavía no he **exegetado**."* ❗ **The distinction was understood and correctly applied in the body; only the heading slipped.** That makes this a wording slip rather than a misunderstanding, and it makes the repair low-risk. |
| **How it was found** | The author caught it on reading the rendered Part VI PDF: *"you keep making it seem like this is the first time I've ever read Revelations… that's not true… I'm reading it with new eyes now because of all the connections."* |
| **Proposed correction, not yet applied** | EN: *"Three figures gathering kings — and a chapter I have not yet examined in this investigation."* ES: *"Tres figuras reuniendo reyes — y un capítulo 20 que todavía no he exegetado"* — which simply matches the wording already used in the body of the same chapter. ⚠️ **Wording is the author's call at repair time; both editions must land together.** |
| **Cost if it had shipped unremarked** | The book's authority rests on the author being a careful long-time reader of Scripture who is now re-reading with a new analytical frame. A heading announcing he had not read a chapter of Revelation undercuts the premise of the entire project in one line — and it is the kind of sentence a reader remembers. |

## A prior occurrence of this same class, not recorded at the time

A correction of this species happened once before, during Part I review, and **was not logged** — which is why this register begins at PR-001 rather than PR-002 and why it exists at all. The recurrence is the point: the first instance was treated as a one-off editing miss rather than as a pattern with a named cause, so nothing changed and the same inference produced the same kind of error four Parts later. Its specifics are not reconstructed here, because reconstructing them from memory would be the identical mistake this register is meant to stop.

---

> ### 📐 **How to avoid adding to this register.** When drafting a first-person sentence about motive, history or practice — as opposed to argument — check whether it was *stated by the author* or *inferred from the position he holds*. If inferred, it is a question to ask, not a sentence to write. Asking costs one line in a review round. A wrong answer costs the reader's trust in every retrieved claim in the book.
