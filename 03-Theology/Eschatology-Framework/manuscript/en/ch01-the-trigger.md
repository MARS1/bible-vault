# Chapter 1: the trigger

The question came from a relative, and it was not a friendly abstraction. It was aimed at me.

He had been reading about carrying a burden for people who are lost, the kind of burden that keeps a person awake, and he meant it. Then he put a verse in front of me.

> Go into all the world and proclaim the good news to every creature (Mark 16:15)

And then, plainly, without dressing it up: *with what you know, you could go out and teach the good news to people who are lost. You could preach on buses and in parks. Don't you have that burden?*

That is a real question from a real person who was not trying to score a point. I want to keep that in view, because it would be easy to turn him into a debating position, and he was not one.

I answered in four words.

**Predicar no es lo mío.** Preaching isn't my thing.

## What I meant, and what I had ready

I did not mean that I was refusing to obey God, and when I saw how it landed I said so at length.

What I meant was that I did not believe I had been given the calling of a preacher. I believed I should love people, live according to His word, and be ready to explain the hope in me when the opportunity came. And I had verses for that.

> Always be prepared to make a defense to anyone who asks you for a reason for the hope that is in you (1 Peter 3:15)

> Are all apostles? Are all prophets? Are all teachers? (1 Corinthians 12:29)

> And he gave the apostles, the prophets, the evangelists, the shepherds and teachers (Ephesians 4:11)

Read that group on its own and a real picture forms. Gifts are handed out unevenly. Evangelist appears as a specific function, which suggests not everyone holds it. And Peter's instruction is not go find people and start talking; it is be ready when someone asks.

He had verses too, and he kept producing them.

> Whoever knows the right thing to do and fails to do it, for him it is sin (James 4:17)

> Not everyone who says to me, Lord, Lord, will enter the kingdom of heaven, but the one who does the will of my Father (Matthew 7:21)

> Go therefore and make disciples of all nations (Matthew 28:19)

> Those who were scattered went about preaching the word (Acts 8:4)

Two stacks of verses. Both real. Both in the same book.

I want to be careful about that last one, because it comes back. Acts 8:4 was quoted at me in that conversation, by him, as an argument I was expected to answer. I answered around it. Chapter 4 is where I finally met it in its own context and it did something to me that it had not done sitting in a message on my phone.

## And I had a second argument, which is the more interesting one

There was another layer to my position, and it is the layer that eventually turned into this book.

I was reading the Great Commission as something that had already been carried out. Not cancelled. Fulfilled, in the first century, by the people it was given to. And I had texts for that too, which I brought out early:

> This good news of the kingdom will be proclaimed in all the world as a testimony to all nations, and then the end will come (Matthew 24:14)

> the good news that has come to you, as indeed in all the world (Colossians 1:6)

> the good news that you heard, which has been proclaimed in all creation under heaven (Colossians 1:23)

> Their voice has gone out to all the earth (Romans 10:18)

So my answer to *go into all the world* was not really *I am not gifted for it*. Underneath, it was: *that was accomplished.*

That is the position I actually walked in with. It is worth saying plainly because both halves of it are about to cost me something.

## What I was actually doing

I was not about to persuade him, and he was not about to persuade me.

If he came back with three more verses, I would go looking for four. The exchange had a shape, and the shape was that whoever runs out of ammunition first concedes, and whoever concedes is taken to have been wrong.

That is not an argument. It is an inventory contest.

The problem is not that the verses are fake. Every verse in both stacks is really there. The problem is that stacking them tells you nothing, because there is no step in the procedure where evidence decides anything. There is only a step where somebody gets tired.

And I had a second move available that was worse, though it did not feel worse at the time. When a text was pressed on me, I could reach for the framework instead of the text. *That was fulfilled in the first century.* Which may even be true, and this book spends a long time on whether it is. But used the way I was using it, it was not an answer. It was a way of not having to give one.

I had been calling all of this Bible study for a long time.

## The question that actually started the book

Then he asked me something else, and it is the reason you are holding this.

He wanted to know how I had arrived at the way I was reading prophecy. Which author. Which teacher. Which commentary.

And I could not answer him.

Not because there was no answer, but because there was no short one. It had come from years of reading, comparing passages, looking things up, changing my mind, praying about it, and holding pieces together that I had never once laid out in order. I told him honestly that any two-message reply would leave out the context and just generate more questions.

Then I said that I had started, that same day, trying to organize it, because I had never written it down.

That is the true first sentence of this project. Not a decision to write a book. Somebody asked me to show my work, and I discovered I could not.

## Three things I could not answer either

Once I stopped trying to hold my ground, some questions surfaced that I had never actually asked.

The first was about audience. Matthew 28 is spoken to somebody. Not to nobody, and not, in the first instance, to me. There are people in the room. I had been answering that verse for days without once checking who was standing in front of the person who said it.

The second was about meaning. All nations. All the world. I was leaning hard on Colossians saying the good news had gone out into all the world, and I knew what that phrase meant to me, standing in the twenty-first century with a map of the whole earth in my head. I had no idea what it meant to Paul. I had never asked. I had just loaned him my map.

The third was about categories. What kind of thing is a commission? An office given to particular people for a particular task, the way a king commissions an envoy? Or a standing order to a whole community forever? Those are different things, the difference decides the whole argument, and I had never separated them.

None of those is a question about the end of the world. That comes later, and it comes uninvited. They are all questions about reading.

## Why I stopped

I told him I would organize it properly.

That sounds smaller than it felt. What I meant was that I had noticed my method was broken, and that a method that could not settle this could not be trusted on anything else either, including the parts of my position I liked.

That is the trap in proof-texting. It can arrive at a true conclusion. It just cannot tell you whether it did, because the procedure has no way to distinguish "this is correct" from "I found more verses."

So there were two options. Keep defending and hope. Or go build a way of settling questions like this before returning to this one, so that whatever I built could not quietly be bent toward the answer I already liked.

I picked the second. It took much longer than I expected, and it is the next chapter.

::: openquestion
**Open question**

I have quoted "all nations" and "all the world" in this chapter without examining either, and so has he. That is deliberate. It is how both of us were using them at the time. Whether those phrases meant to their first hearers what they mean to a modern reader turns out to be one of the largest questions in this book, and it is not settled until Chapter 23.
:::
