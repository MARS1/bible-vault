# Chapter 24: Matthew 28 — the Great Commission in context

This is the verse my relative put in front of me, and it is the strongest remaining text against where I started. I am approaching it the way I approached Romans 10: directly, with the outcome unprotected, and with the framing stated before the evidence.

If Matthew 28 says what he took it to say, my original answer does not survive.

## Who is actually in the room

> Now the eleven disciples went to Galilee, to the mountain to which Jesus had directed them (Matthew 28:16)

The eleven. Matthew tells us who receives this, and the first interpretive step is to notice that he bothered to.

That does **not** mean the command has no continuing implications, and I want to block that inference immediately because it is the cheap version of my own position. It means the first thing established is that Yeshua is commissioning a specific apostolic group, and anything beyond that has to be argued rather than assumed.

## It flows from enthronement

> All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me (Matthew 28:18)

The commission does not begin with a task. It begins with a claim to authority, and the language is Daniel 7's — authority given to one like a son of man.

This is a **royal** commission, issued on the basis of an enthronement. It is not general encouragement that evangelism is a worthwhile activity. Whatever else follows, it follows from a throne.

## "Go" is not the main verb

Here is the first thing that surprised me, and it is verifiable in any interlinear.

::: wordstudy
**Original language**

<span class="gr">πορευθέντες οὖν μαθητεύσατε πάντα τὰ ἔθνη, βαπτίζοντες … διδάσκοντες</span>

<span class="gr">πορευθέντες</span> *(poreuthentes)* — **aorist participle**, "having gone" / "as you go." Not the imperative.

<span class="gr">μαθητεύσατε</span> *(mathēteusate)* — **the imperative.** *Make disciples.* This is the main verb and the only one.

<span class="gr">βαπτίζοντες</span>, <span class="gr">διδάσκοντες</span> — present participles: baptizing, teaching. These explain **how**.

**The centre of the commission is not travel. It is making disciples.** Going is attendant circumstance; baptizing and teaching are the method.
:::

I want to be careful not to over-collect on that. It does not mean *nobody goes anywhere* — the book of Acts is the counter-evidence, and the participle assumes movement rather than forbidding it. What it means is that the command's content is discipleship, which is a longer and different thing than announcing a message once.

And that distinction matters for the original argument, because **evangelism and discipleship are not the same activity**, and my relative and I had been using them interchangeably. So had I, when it suited me.

## "All nations," and Matthew's own development

> make disciples of **all nations** (Matthew 28:19)

That phrase has to be read against Matthew's own earlier material, and the contrast is stark.

In Matthew 10, Yeshua sends the twelve and tells them to go nowhere among the Gentiles and enter no town of the Samaritans, but rather to the lost sheep of the house of Israel. In Matthew 15 He says He was sent only to the lost sheep of the house of Israel.

Then Matthew 28 says all nations.

::: historical
**Historical context — the Jerusalem Council, and the category that already existed**

Within roughly two decades, the question Matthew 28 opens had to be settled in practice: on what terms do the nations come in?

Israel already had a category for the outsider who attached himself to the covenant community — the <span class="he">גֵּר</span> *(ger)*, the sojourner or resident alien. Torah legislates for him repeatedly: he keeps the Sabbath, he may eat the Passover if circumcised, he is to be loved as oneself, and one law is to apply to the native-born and to the *ger* alike. The category is not an afterthought; it is built in.

What the Jerusalem Council of Acts 15 decided was **not** that the nations could come in — Torah already knew that. It was whether they must enter through circumcision and the full Mosaic obligation in order to be saved, and the answer was no.

So the council is doing something narrower and more interesting than inventing Gentile inclusion. It is deciding which of the existing categories the incoming nations occupy, in a situation the earlier categories had not been written for.
:::

::: finding
**Finding — a genuine development, with the resurrection standing between the two**

Matthew contains a real development, and Messiah's death and resurrection stand between the two commissions.

The mission **begins Israel-centred** and **later expands to the nations**. Matthew 10 does not need to be distorted to defend Matthew 28, and Matthew 28 does not need to be suppressed to preserve Israel's priority. Both belong to the story, in that order, and the order is the point.

Flattening the earthly Israel-centred mission, the initial commission to the Twelve, the later Gentile mission, Paul's particular apostleship and the incorporation language of Part III into one undifferentiated "Great Commission" is how this argument goes wrong on both sides. It went wrong that way for me.
:::

## And the commission genuinely establishes a nations mission

Now the part that costs me, and I am stating it as plainly as I can.

::: evidence
**How strongly evidence bears**

Matthew 28 **ESTABLISHES** that Yeshua commanded the eleven to disciple all nations.

If someone claims the apostles were never commissioned beyond Israel, **Matthew 28 does not allow it.** The mission genuinely expands, by explicit command, from a risen Messiah claiming all authority.

Any reading — including any version of mine — that needs this text to be smaller than it is has a problem with the text and not with its opponents.
:::

My relative was right that Matthew 28 commands a mission to the nations. He was right that it is not a suggestion. Nothing in this chapter takes that back.

## The strongest form of the argument against me

The serious question is not whether the eleven were commissioned. It is whether the commission is **structurally ongoing**, and there is a real argument that it is. I am going to give it its strongest form rather than the version that is easy to answer.

The argument runs: the eleven are told to teach the nations *to observe all that I have commanded you*. But *make disciples* is among the things He commanded them. So the disciples they make are themselves taught to make disciples, and the commission reproduces itself down the generations by its own internal logic. The closing promise — *I am with you always, to the end of the age* — extends past the lifetime of anyone standing on that mountain, which suggests the command extends with it.

::: finding
**Finding — this argument is not dismissed**

That is a real argument, and it is probably the strongest textual case for the Great Commission being structurally ongoing rather than discharged by the apostolic generation.

I do not have a refutation of it and I am not going to manufacture one. My original position treated the commission as simply completed, and this argument is the reason that position was too flat.
:::

## But "everything I commanded" does not distribute every role

Here is the distinction that does survive, and it is not a dodge — it is a reductio that Paul supplies.

If *teach them to observe everything I commanded you* distributed every command identically to every disciple, then every believer would have to be an apostle, a prophet, a teacher and an evangelist.

Paul explicitly denies that. *Are all apostles? Are all prophets? Are all teachers?* The expected answer to each is no. Ephesians 4:11 lists evangelist as one function among several given to some, for the equipping of all.

So a reading in which Matthew 28 distributes every apostolic role to every believer collides with Paul. And it collides with itself, since nobody actually holds it consistently: the same reader who says every believer must evangelize does not say every believer must be an apostle, though both are in *everything I commanded*.

## Corporate mission, individual vocation

Which produces the cleanest formulation I have reached, and it holds both halves without softening either.

::: finding
**Finding — the resolution, and why it is not a compromise**

Matthew 28 strongly supports: **Messiah's people possess an ongoing disciple-making mission.**

Paul's body theology supports: **members participate differently according to calling and gift.**

**These do not contradict.** A body can possess a mission collectively without every cell performing the same function — and the body metaphor is **Paul's own**, not an analogy imported to rescue a conclusion. That distinction matters to me, because rescuing conclusions with borrowed analogies is precisely what I have spent four Parts trying not to do.

This is more compatible with **both** Matthew 28 and Paul than *every believer must personally become a travelling evangelist* — and more compatible with both than *preaching is not my calling, therefore the question does not reach me*, which is what I actually said.
:::

## What this does to the original argument

So let me put the two positions from that first conversation side by side, accurately, because Part I had to correct my own account of it once already.

My relative argued that believers are obligated to proclaim outwardly, from Mark 16, Matthew 28, James 4:17 and Acts 8:4. He did **not** argue that every believer holds an identical evangelistic office; that is a strawman and I am not going to build it here in order to knock it down.

I argued that gifts are distributed unevenly, that evangelist is a named and specific function, and that 1 Peter 3:15 asks readiness to answer rather than initiative to proclaim. Underneath that, unstated, I was treating the commission as already discharged in the first century.

Four Parts later: his concern survives, and mine survives in a narrowed form, and the thing underneath mine has been weakened rather than confirmed. The mission is real, ongoing, and corporate. Participation in it is differentiated. Neither of those was the position either of us was defending, and the second one costs me more than it costs him.

::: openquestion
**Open question — what the corporate mission requires of a member who is not an evangelist**

Saying the mission is corporate and participation differentiated does not tell anyone what to do on a Tuesday.

The formulation from Chapter 21 still stands as the closest I have: not everyone is an evangelist, and that does not relieve anyone of responsibility to speak truthfully about Messiah when faithfulness, opportunity or direct questioning calls for it.

That is a real answer and it is not a complete one. What a differentiated participation actually obliges remains underdetermined by everything I have assembled, and I would rather say so than issue a rule the texts have not given me.
:::

::: whereweare
**Where we are.** Matthew 28 is a royal commission flowing from enthronement, given to the eleven, whose main verb is *make disciples* rather than *go*. Matthew's own development runs Israel-centred first and nations later, with the resurrection between, and neither commission needs to be suppressed to hold the other. The text genuinely establishes a mission to the nations, and no reading of mine is permitted to shrink it. The recursive argument for an ongoing commission is strong and is not refuted here. What does not follow is that every command distributes to every believer identically — Paul denies that outright. Corporate mission, individual vocation, on Paul's own metaphor.
:::

::: earned
**What it earned.** The question that started this investigation now has an answer I can defend, and it is not the answer I was defending when I was asked. Which clears the way for the thing I have been deferring since Part II: the ages, the resurrection, and the door into Revelation.
:::
