# Chapter 37: the harvest, the bowls, and Armageddon

Revelation 13 ended with people bearing a mark on the hand or the forehead.

Revelation 14 opens with people bearing a name on the forehead.

> Then I looked, and behold, on Mount Zion stood the Lamb, and with him 144,000 who had **his name and his Father's name written on their foreheads**. (Revelation 14:1)

That answer arrives one verse into the chapter, and it settles something I had argued for on other grounds two chapters earlier. The mark is not first a technology. It is first a **mark of allegiance**, because Revelation's own counter-mark is a name — and a name is whose you are.

::: finding
**Finding — Revelation confirms the covenantal reading from inside itself**

I read the mark through Deuteronomy's hand-and-forehead covenant vocabulary before Revelation 14 was on the table. I could have been importing that.

I was not. Revelation answers the beast's mark with **the Lamb's name and the Father's name**, in the same bodily location, one chapter later.

The vocabulary was Torah's. The application is John's.
:::

And one thing I will not do with this verse. The Lamb stands **on Mount Zion**, and I do not know what that locates. Heavenly Zion, earthly Jerusalem, and a symbolic covenant-place are all live. Anyone building a millennial geography out of those two words is building on an unexamined assumption, and I would be doing it with a theory already in my pocket.

## The 144,000 return, and the chapter makes them harder, not easier

I declined, five chapters ago, to equate the sealed 144,000 with the innumerable multitude. Revelation 14 does not relieve that refusal. It complicates it.

> It is these who have not defiled themselves with women, for they are **virgins**… and in their mouth no lie was found, for they are blameless. (Revelation 14:4–5)

Three readings stay open, and I am not going to pretend one of them is obvious: literal celibacy; ritual purity in the sense of men consecrated for holy war, which has real Tanakh precedent; or spiritual fidelity, since Scripture routinely calls idolatry adultery and Revelation is about to describe a great prostitute.

What matters more is the other word in the same verse.

> …these have been redeemed from mankind as **firstfruits** for God and the Lamb. (Revelation 14:4)

::: finding
**Finding — firstfruits are definitionally not the whole harvest**

*Firstfruits* is not a synonym for *the saved*. In Torah it is the first portion of a crop, offered precisely because more is coming. The word carries the rest of the harvest inside it.

Paul uses it the same way, and supplies an order:

> Christ has been raised from the dead, the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep… But each in his own order: Christ the firstfruits, then at his coming those who belong to him. (1 Corinthians 15:20, 23)

**The control:** wherever Revelation calls a group firstfruits, that group is **not** the complete population of the saved, and no chronology may be built on treating it as complete.

This is the kind of rule that costs me something. A tidy reading would let the 144,000 stand for everyone and be done. Revelation used a word that forbids it.
:::

## An angel preaches the good news — and I had the preacher wrong

Here is a passage I have heard used as a chronological hinge for most of my life.

> Then I saw another angel flying directly overhead, with an eternal gospel to proclaim to those who dwell on earth, **to every nation and tribe and language and people**. (Revelation 14:6)

The nations-thread that started this entire investigation runs straight through it. But two details in the sentence dismantle the use it is usually put to.

::: finding
**Finding — the Proclamation-Agent Distinction**

**The proclaimer is an angel.** Not the assemblies, not missionaries, not a completed human enterprise. Revelation 14:6 does not describe the Great Commission being finished; it describes an angel in mid-air.

**And the timing runs the other direction.** Compare the two sentences:

| | |
|--------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| **Matthew 24:14** | proclaimed to all nations, **and then the end will come** |
| **Revelation 14:7** | *Fear God and give him glory, because the hour of his judgment **has come*** |

Matthew's proclamation precedes an end. Revelation's proclamation is made **inside** a judgment that has already arrived.

Correspondence is obvious. Identity is not established. And a passage where an angel announces that judgment *has come* cannot function as proof that a human missionary task has been *completed*.
:::

I want to state the chain I am refusing, in the exact form I have watched it run, because a refusal stated vaguely is not a refusal:

> *We want the Great Commission completed → therefore Revelation 14 proves it → therefore the millennium happened → therefore we are in the little season.*

Every arrow there is load-bearing and not one of them is earned. The first is a wish. The second misreads the agent and the tense. The third and fourth inherit the error and then cite each other. If my hypothesis is true, it will survive without this verse, and if it needs this verse, it is not true.

## Babylon falls, and Revelation does not tell me who she is

> Another angel, a second, followed, saying, **"Fallen, fallen is Babylon the great"**… (Revelation 14:8)

This is the first appearance of the name. She falls before she is described, and she is described three chapters later.

I am going to wait. I made the beast wait, I made the two witnesses wait, and I made the 144,000 wait. Identifying Babylon here — while the only data I have is a name and a fall — would be doing exactly what I have spent forty stages refusing.

What Revelation adds instead is a moral summary of the alternative, and it is worth noticing which two things it pairs:

> Here is a call for the endurance of the saints, those who **keep the commandments of God** and **keep their faith in Jesus**. (Revelation 14:12)

Not one or the other. Both, in a single clause, in a book written to assemblies under pressure.

## The harvest — and a verse that damaged my own reading

> Then I looked, and behold, a white cloud, and seated on the cloud **one like a son of man**, with a golden crown on his head, and a sharp sickle in his hand. (Revelation 14:14)

*One like a son of man* is Daniel 7:13, and my instinct — and probably yours — is to read this as Messiah coming to reap. Then comes the next verse.

> And **another angel came out of the temple, calling with a loud voice to him who sat on the cloud, "Put in your sickle, and reap, for the hour to reap has come."** (Revelation 14:15)

::: finding
**Finding — recorded against my own preference**

An angel emerges from the temple and **tells the figure on the cloud when to reap**.

That sits awkwardly with a straightforward identification of the figure as Messiah. Not impossibly — the Son says of that very hour that no one knows it but the Father, and a heralded command is not the same as a superior's order. But awkwardly, and I am not going to smooth it.

And Daniel's direction runs the same way. In Daniel 7:13 the son of man comes **to** the Ancient of Days and is presented before Him. He does not descend from Him. The verse most often used to make this figure a descending Messiah is, in its own context, an ascent.

**Status: the identification is weakened, not refuted, and it is left open.**
:::

Two harvests follow — grain, then grapes — and only the second is explicitly described in the language of wrath, trodden **outside the city** in a winepress whose blood runs for 1,600 stadia. *Outside the city* is a phrase with a great deal of resonance and no determinate referent yet. The number is one of Revelation's arithmetical symbols, and I am recording it rather than solving it. I have no interest in a solution I invented.

## The Song of Moses stands in front of the worst thing in the book

> Then I saw another sign in heaven, great and amazing, seven angels with seven plagues, **which are the last, for with them the wrath of God is finished**. (Revelation 15:1)

That is a strong chronological datum, and it constrains what may be placed after the bowls. If wrath is *finished* in them, another equivalent series of divine-wrath judgments cannot casually be appended unless Revelation itself requires it.

It also needs discipline. **Last what?** The text says last *plagues*, because wrath is completed in them. That is considerably stronger than "the next seven things John happened to see," and considerably narrower than "the final events of all history." I will hold it at its own size.

Then, before a single bowl is poured, Revelation shows me who is already standing.

> …those who had **conquered** the beast and its image and the number of its name, standing beside the sea of glass with harps of God. (Revelation 15:2)

Chapter 13: the beast demands allegiance. Chapter 14: do not worship it; here is the endurance of the saints. Chapter 15: the ones who refused are shown victorious. These are not disconnected visions.

And the word *conquered* is doing something remarkable, because the same book has already used it of the opposite party.

::: finding
**Finding — two definitions of victory operating at once**

> …it was allowed to make war on the saints **and to conquer them**. (Revelation 13:7)
>
> And they have **conquered him** by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony, for they loved not their lives even unto death. (Revelation 12:11)

The beast conquers the saints **by killing them**. The saints conquer the beast **by refusing to abandon Yahweh**.

Both statements are true in the same book about the same events. This is the same logic as the Lion who conquers as a slain Lamb — the reversal I was handed in chapter 5 and have been carrying ever since.
:::

Then the song itself, and its title is a whole argument in four words:

> And they sing **the song of Moses, the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb**. (Revelation 15:3)

Not Moses *or* the Lamb. Not Moses corrected by the Lamb. The Exodus story is being reused as the vocabulary of Messiah's victory, which is a very different thing from replacement.

And the lyric contains something I did not expect at this location in the book:

> Just and true are your ways, O King of the nations! Who will not fear, O Lord, and glorify your name? For you alone are holy. **All nations will come and worship you**, for your righteous acts have been revealed. (Revelation 15:3–4)

::: openquestion
**Open question — recorded, not resolved**

Immediately before the severest judgment sequence in the book, the conquerors sing that **all nations will come and worship**.

That is not a judgment-only horizon. It connects directly to the angel's proclamation to every nation nine verses earlier, and to the nations-thread this whole investigation began with.

Two temptations, refused in both directions. I will not build a universalism claim on one line of a song. And I will not quietly leave it out because it complicates a wrath-only reading of what follows.

It goes into the ledger of open questions at full strength.
:::

The plagues then come out of the opened heavenly sanctuary, which fills with smoke so that no one can enter until they are finished. Whatever historical events eventually correspond to the bowls, Revelation's theological claim about them is unambiguous: **Yahweh judges.** Rulers and armies may be instruments. They are not causes.

## The bowls, and where they land

| Bowl | What is poured | Trumpet parallel | Exodus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | sores on *those who bore the mark of the beast and worshiped its image* | earth struck | boils |
| **2** | the sea *became like the blood of a corpse*, **every** living thing died | ⅓ of the sea | water to blood |
| **3** | rivers and springs *became blood* | ⅓ of rivers and springs | water to blood |
| **4** | the sun *was allowed* to scorch people with fire | ⅓ of sun, moon, stars darkened | — |
| **5** | poured on **the throne of the beast**; its kingdom plunged into darkness | abyss, darkness | darkness over Egypt |

Two things in that table are not decoration.

**The bowls are targeted.** The first strikes those bearing the mark. The fifth strikes the beast's throne and kingdom. This is not indiscriminate catastrophe; it is judgment aimed at a specific allegiance and a specific power structure. And *its kingdom* and *the throne of the beast* strengthen what Daniel already suggested — the beast is inseparable from an identifiable kingdom structure, not merely an individual.

**The grant-language survives even here.** The fourth bowl says the sun *was allowed* to scorch. At the height of divine wrath Revelation is still marking authority as delegated.

And across the whole sequence, one refrain repeats:

> …they cursed the name of God… **and they did not repent** and give him glory. (16:9)
>
> …**they did not repent** of their deeds. (16:11)
>
> …they **cursed God** for the plague of the hail. (16:21)

The point of the bowls is not the casualty count. It is that judgment arrived, was recognized as judgment, and produced no turning.

## The third bowl names the crime

> Just are you, O Holy One, who is and who was, for you brought these judgments. **For they have shed the blood of saints and prophets**, and you have given them blood to drink. It is what they deserve! (Revelation 16:5–6)

That is one of the strongest internal clues in the book about **who is being judged**. The judged power is guilty of the blood of saints and prophets.

And I know another text that says that, in those words, about a named city.

> …some of whom you will kill and crucify… so that on you may come **all the righteous blood shed on earth**, from the blood of righteous Abel to the blood of Zechariah… Truly, I say to you, **all these things will come upon this generation**.
>
> **O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, the city that kills the prophets** and stones those who are sent to it!… See, your house is left to you desolate. (Matthew 23:34–38)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Matthew 23** | blood of the prophets → **Jerusalem, named explicitly** → *this generation* → judgment |
| **Revelation 16:6** | blood of **saints and prophets** → bowl judgment |
| **Revelation 18** | Babylon → blood of prophets and saints |

::: textualnote
**Declared debt.** The third line of that table is the one I have **not** verified. Revelation 18:24 was never retrieved verbatim at the stage where this chain was assembled, and it is the leg that would close it.

The first two legs are verified. The third is currently a claim about a verse I have not read in front of me, and it is marked as such here rather than allowed to pass as evidence.
:::

The chain is now formally open. It is **not** an identification. Revelation 17 and 18 are about to describe Babylon at length, with concrete identifying characteristics, and I made every other major figure in this book wait for its own chapter. Babylon does not get to be the exception because the correspondence is finally exciting.

## The Euphrates, and Tanakh gets there before I do

> The sixth angel poured out his bowl on **the great river Euphrates**, and its water was dried up, to prepare the way for **the kings from the east**. (Revelation 16:12)

I have a geography available to me. I have had it available for a long time. It gets no privileged entry here, and I am going to say what is established before I say anything else.

| Established | Not established |
|---|---|
| the Euphrates dries | what geographical referent the Euphrates has here |
| the drying **prepares a way** | whether it is literally the river ordinarily so called |
| **kings from the east** are involved | whether it functions as a boundary |
| | whether the kings are specific historical rulers |
| | **whether this has any connection to alternative geographical reconstructions discussed earlier in this project** |

That last row is deliberate and it is aimed at me.

Because Scripture already has drying-river imagery, and it is not atmospheric — it is specific.

> …who says to the deep, "Be dry; **I will dry up your rivers**"; who says of **Cyrus**, "He is my shepherd, and he shall fulfill all my purpose"… Thus says the LORD to his anointed, **to Cyrus**, whose right hand I have grasped, **to subdue nations before him** and to loose the belts of kings, **to open doors before him** that gates may not be closed. (Isaiah 44:27–45:1)

> Behold, I will plead your cause and take vengeance for you. **I will dry up her sea and make her fountain dry.** (Jeremiah 51:36)

Isaiah puts drying rivers and a king subduing nations and opening gates in consecutive breaths. Jeremiah puts drying directly inside **a Babylon oracle**. And Revelation has just announced Babylon falling, two chapters before the river dries to open a way for kings.

The Babylonian prophetic background has to be worked before any novel geography is assigned. It is going to matter enormously in two chapters.

## Frogs, and a rule about borrowed images

> …coming out of **the mouth** of the dragon and out of **the mouth** of the beast and out of **the mouth** of the false prophet, three unclean spirits like frogs. **For they are demonic spirits, performing signs**, who go abroad to the kings of the whole world, to assemble them for battle on the great day of God the Almighty. (Revelation 16:13–14)

Note where they come from: **mouths**. Mouths produce speech, proclamation, decrees, deception.

And note that Revelation solves this symbol itself, inside the sentence: *for they are demonic spirits.* Deferred internal interpretation, handed over without my help. These are not three amphibians emerging from a river, and anyone who reads them that way is fighting the verse.

*Performing signs* also triggers a rule I already have: a sign that works is not thereby authenticating. Deuteronomy 13 settled that — the sign comes to pass, and the prophet is still to be refused, because the test is allegiance, not spectacle.

Why frogs, then? Because of Egypt.

> …I will plague all your country with **frogs**. The Nile shall swarm with frogs… and the frogs came up and covered the land of Egypt. (Exodus 8:2–6)

But look at what the image is doing in each place:

| Exodus | Revelation |
|---|---|
| frogs are **a plague upon Egypt** | frog-like spirits are **agents of deception gathering rulers** |

::: finding
**New rule — Intertextual Function Control**

When Revelation reuses imagery from the Tanakh, preserve **both** the earlier source **and** Revelation's new contextual function. Shared imagery does **not** require an identical historical referent or an identical symbolic function.

**Intertextuality supplies vocabulary. Context supplies function.**

The frogs are the cleanest demonstration available: same image, opposite role, and Revelation names the role itself so I cannot get it wrong.
:::

## Three figures gathering kings — and a chapter I have not read yet

The dragon, the beast, and the false prophet act together, and their spirits gather the kings for battle.

| Revelation 16 | Revelation 20 |
|---|---|
| demonic deception | Satan released |
| → kings gathered | → **deceives the nations** |
| → battle | → **Gog and Magog gathered** → battle |

Deception → gathering of rulers → battle. Twice.

So: two separate battles, or two visions of one consummative conflict?

**I am not answering that here.** This is one of the strongest tests of recapitulation this investigation will ever face, and answering it before Revelation 20 has been read on its own terms would be the precise failure mode this entire project exists to avoid — deciding a chapter's meaning from a chapter I have not yet exegeted, because the answer I want lives there.

Correspondence: strong. Identity: unresolved.

## An interruption

Mid-gathering, in a bracket, the voice changes.

> (Behold, **I am coming like a thief!** Blessed is the one who **stays awake**, keeping his garments on, that he may not go about naked and be seen exposed!) (Revelation 16:15)

The battle gathering is explicitly connected with Yeshua's coming. And *thief* is a word with a trail behind it — Matthew 24, 1 Thessalonians 5, 2 Peter 3, and Revelation 3 all use it. That branch is owed, and it is owed carefully, because identical vocabulary does not establish an identical event.

But the pastoral force is a control in its own right, and it is the thing most commonly lost in books like this one. *Blessed is the one who stays awake.* Revelation does not present prophecy as fascinating information about people millennia later. It repeatedly turns and demands something of **its own readers** — stay awake, keep your garments, endure — and it does that here, inside the bowls, at the assembling of the kings.

## Armageddon, where I have to say I do not know

> And they assembled them at the place that in Hebrew is called **Armageddon**. (Revelation 16:16)

Usually rendered *Har Megiddo*, "Mount Megiddo."

And there is an immediate problem with that: **Megiddo is not a prominent mountain.** It is a tell overlooking a plain — a strategically decisive place, and famously so, but the name as usually parsed promises a mountain that the geography does not straightforwardly supply.

Candidates that need real investigation: Megiddo itself; the wider Carmel-Megiddo region; a connection to Zechariah's mourning *in the plain of Megiddo*; and alternative Hebrew constructions entirely — *har mo'ed*, "mountain of assembly," has been proposed, and so has a reading built on *ir*, "city," rather than *har*.

::: textualnote
**Source-provenance requirement, stated in place.** This branch cannot be closed by explanation. It requires actual lexical and historical sources — the Greek forms in the manuscripts, a lexicon, and archaeological evidence for the site — attached and cited.

I do not get to settle a Hebrew etymology by finding an account of it persuasive. **An explanation of a word is not evidence about the word.**

Status: open, and named as open rather than passed over with a familiar rendering.
:::

## "It is done" — and three items, not one

> …a loud voice came out of the temple, from the throne, saying, **"It is done!"** (Revelation 16:17)

Then lightning, rumblings, thunder, and an earthquake *such as there had never been since man was on the earth.*

That is a major completion marker, standing alongside 15:1's *the wrath of God is finished* — and it belongs in a small family: *there would be no more delay* in chapter 10, and *It is done!* again in chapter 21.

And then a verse I had previously read as one statement turns out to be three.

> **The great city** was split into three parts, and **the cities of the nations** fell, and God remembered **Babylon the great**, to make her drain the cup of the wine of the fury of his wrath. (Revelation 16:19)

::: openquestion
**Open question — is "the great city" Babylon, or distinguished from her?**

The verse names three items in sequence: *the great city* · *the cities of the nations* · *Babylon the great*.

This is not pedantry, and here is why it is not. **Revelation 11:8 already used "the great city" for the place "where their Lord was crucified."**

So if 16:19 distinguishes the great city from Babylon, that bears directly on Babylon's identity. And if it equates them, it bears on it far more strongly.

Either answer is consequential. I do not have the answer, and the chapters that might supply it are next.
:::

The last two verses take the de-creation language as far as it goes: every island flees, no mountains are to be found, hailstones of about a hundred pounds fall.

Insisting on ordinary physical description here generates enormous difficulties immediately. But I established the alternative long before I reached Revelation: Isaiah 13, Isaiah 34, Ezekiel 32 and Joel all use cosmic and geographical collapse language for judgments that fell on identifiable powers in identifiable centuries.

And the brake runs both directions, which is the only way a brake is honest. **Apocalyptic magnitude does not automatically establish literal physical mechanics — and that does not mean nothing actually happened.** Prophetic symbolism can describe very real historical judgment in cosmic language. Referent is determined from context, not from volume.

Hail, incidentally, is the seventh Egyptian plague.

## The Exodus frame stops being speculative

Look at the whole sequence in order:

> Song of Moses → the tent of witness → plagues → sores → water to blood → darkness → frogs → hail → judgment and deliverance.

::: finding
**Finding — John pointed to Moses before the plagues began**

I am not imposing Exodus on Revelation. **Revelation named Moses first**, in the song, before a single bowl was poured, and then produced Egypt's plagues in sequence.

The New Exodus pattern is at its strongest point in this investigation.
:::

Which forces the question the pattern implies: **if this is an Exodus, who is Pharaoh? Who is Egypt? Who is being delivered?**

Revelation already called the great city **"Sodom and Egypt, where their Lord was crucified."**

That correspondence is promoted. It is still not a final identification, and the guard I attached to it when I first wrote it travels with it here, unchanged: whatever this identifies, it identifies a **covenantal** position, not an ethnic people. Revelation judges a city for shedding the blood of prophets. It does not license anything said about a nation.

## Trumpets and bowls, side by side

| # | Trumpet | Bowl |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | earth struck, ⅓ burned | sores on those bearing the **mark** |
| **2** | ⅓ of sea → blood | **all** the sea → blood, **every** living thing dies |
| **3** | ⅓ of rivers and springs | rivers and springs → **blood** |
| **4** | ⅓ of sun, moon, stars darkened | the sun **scorches with fire** |
| **5** | abyss opened, darkness, locusts | **the beast's throne**; its kingdom darkened |
| **6** | **Euphrates** — four angels released | **Euphrates dried** — kings gathered |
| **7** | loud voices: the kingdom has come | **"It is done"** |

Not exact identity. Far too parallel to ignore. The movement is consistently **⅓ → all**, and the sixth in both series is the Euphrates.

Two possibilities remain live: successive escalation, or recapitulation with intensification. **I am not choosing.** Revelation 20 may force the choice — or may show that both operate at once, which is a real option and not a dodge.

What does constrain the question is the completion language. *The last plagues, for with them the wrath of God is finished.* *It is done.* Those prevent casually stacking another equivalent wrath-series after the bowls. They do not, by themselves, establish that nothing whatever follows.

::: finding
**New rule — the Correspondence–Identity Distinction**

Two passages sharing imagery, vocabulary, chronology or structure establishes **correspondence**. Identity of referent requires **additional evidence**.

| Pair | Correspondence | Identity |
|---|---|---|
| Revelation 6 martyrs ↔ Revelation 12 faithful unto death | strong | not established |
| Revelation 16 battle ↔ Revelation 20 Gog and Magog | strong | **unresolved** |
| Revelation's Babylon ↔ Matthew 23's Jerusalem | increasingly strong | **pending Revelation 17–18** |
| Trumpets ↔ Bowls | strong | not established |

This rule protects in both directions — against missing real connections, and against overstating them. This project has needed both halves, sometimes in the same paragraph.
:::

## What the plagues did to the question

Once the Song of Moses appeared in front of the bowls, I could no longer treat Revelation's plagues as isolated catastrophes. John had told me where to look. Sores, blood, darkness, frogs, hail — the Exodus vocabulary accumulated until the question stopped being *whether* Revelation was invoking Egypt and became *why*.

Then an earlier detail came back with new weight. Revelation had already called the city where the Lord was crucified "Egypt." The bowls judged a power guilty of the blood of saints and prophets. Yeshua had accused Jerusalem, by name, of the blood of the prophets. And Revelation was about to accuse Babylon of the same blood.

None of those observations identifies Babylon. Together they create a question the next chapters will have to answer.

The Euphrates taught the same restraint from the other side. I had theories available about geography, and Revelation belonged to Isaiah, to Jeremiah, to Exodus and to Babylon long before it belonged to any reconstruction I might bring to it.

If the theory was true, it could survive waiting.

::: whereweare
**Where we are.** Revelation answers the beast's mark with the Lamb's name on the same foreheads, which confirms the covenantal reading of the mark from inside the book. The 144,000 are called firstfruits, a word that forbids treating them as the whole harvest. The proclaimer of the eternal gospel is an angel, and Revelation's *the hour has come* runs the opposite direction from Matthew's *and then the end will come* — so Revelation 14 cannot certify a completed human mission. The figure on the cloud is told when to reap by an angel from the temple, which weakens rather than strengthens the identification I would have preferred. Babylon has fallen, been accused of the blood of saints and prophets, and still has not been identified, with one leg of that chain openly unverified. The Euphrates has been given to Isaiah and Jeremiah before any geography of mine. Armageddon's etymology is unsettled and the sources are owed. And the bowls parallel the trumpets too closely to ignore and not closely enough to settle, which leaves the recapitulation question exactly where Revelation 20 will find it.

::: earned
**What it earned.** Two rules that will outlive this chapter — that borrowed imagery keeps its old vocabulary and takes a new function, and that correspondence is not identity. A refusal chain written out in the exact words I have heard it argued in, so it cannot be smuggled back in later. A verse recorded at full strength against my own reading, because 14:15 is genuinely awkward and pretending otherwise would have cost more than admitting it. And Babylon, named four times now, still not identified — which is the only reason the identification, when it comes, will be worth reading.
