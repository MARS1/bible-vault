# Chapter 43: Daniel never said it twice the same way

I had carried *the abomination of desolation* around for years as though it were a title — a fixed thing with a fixed name, waiting to be matched against a fixed person.

Then I read the four verses in Hebrew, side by side.

## Four forms, two nouns, and one of them is not the word at all

| Passage | Hebrew | Noun |
|-------------|----------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| **8:13** | <span class="he">וְהַפֶּשַׁע שֹׁמֵם</span> | ★ **<span class="he">פֶּשַׁע</span> — *transgression*** |
| **9:27** | <span class="he">שִׁקּוּצִים מְשֹׁמֵם</span> | <span class="he">שִׁקּוּץ</span> — **plural** |
| **11:31** | <span class="he">הַשִּׁקּוּץ מְשֹׁמֵם</span> | singular, **with the article** |
| **12:11** | <span class="he">שִׁקּוּץ שֹׁמֵם</span> | singular, **without it** |

::: finding
**Finding — Daniel 8:13 does not use the word at all**

It uses <span class="he">פֶּשַׁע</span> — a **different noun**, meaning transgression or rebellion. And 8:12 carries the same root, so it runs through the whole unit. **Daniel 8 is lexically distinct from the other three.**

So *already we have a pattern* is true of the **structure** and false of the **wording**. There is a recurring complex — sanctuary, the regular offering disrupted, a desolating something, trampling. **There is no fixed phrase.**

That should have been deflating and instead it clarified everything. The thing recurring across those passages was never a phrase. **It was a shape**, and the words move while the shape holds.
:::

And a second finding sits inside the Hebrew grammar, which I would have missed entirely reading English.

::: finding
**Finding — abomination and desolation are not the same thing**

The desolation root functions as a **participle modifying** the noun, in every one of the four cases. **The desolation is the condition associated with or resulting from the abomination. It is not a synonym for it.**

❗ **That blocks three common shortcuts at once:**

❌ abomination = army · ❌ abomination = destruction · ❌ abomination = one particular human being.

**None of the three is supplied by the Hebrew.** Each would have to be established passage by passage, on its own evidence — and I have watched all three asserted as though the phrase itself supplied them.

⚠️ And one thing I cannot yet establish in the other direction: whether <span class="he">שִׁקּוּץ</span> carries a specifically **idolatrous, cultic** range. **No lexical authority was obtained.** It is reported, not verified — and it is not contradicted by anything I retrieved.
:::

Two more small disciplines. Daniel uses **two different sanctuary words** — one at 8:11, another at 9:26 — and they are not interchangeable. And the article **inverts** between Hebrew and Greek at 11:31 and 12:11, which I recorded as a puzzle and could not then explain. *(It gets explained later in this chapter, from a direction I did not expect.)*

## The control case, and the question it could not answer

Antiochus IV is the historical control case, and the correspondence with Daniel 11 is substantial: ships of Kittim, rage against the holy covenant, forces who profane the sanctuary, the regular offering removed, the desolating thing set up, and those who know their God standing firm.

And 1 Maccabees confirms the event-profile in detail: swine sacrifice named explicitly, Torah scrolls burned, circumcision forbidden on pain of death, Sabbaths and festivals profaned, burnt-offerings made to cease, **the desolating sacrilege erected on the altar of burnt-offering** — and 2 Maccabees adds that the sanctuary was to be called the temple of Olympian Zeus.

::: textualnote
**Source-type discipline, held throughout**

Every Maccabean statement above is *the text says X* — **a fact about a document.** It is **not** *an event occurred exactly so*, and it is **not** *Scripture means X*.

1 and 2 Maccabees sit in some Christian canons, are not in the Tanakh, and are classed as Apocrypha in Protestant traditions. **This chapter resolves no canonical question**, and does not need to: a witness can be evidentially useful without its canonical status being settled, provided nobody pretends the two questions are the same one.

⚠️ And they were retrieved in **English only**.
:::

Which brings the control case to the thing it was for, and could not deliver.

::: finding
**Finding — the decisive question is unanswered, and the failure is the finding**

**Does 1 Maccabees use Daniel's phrase — and if so, in which of Daniel's three Greek forms?**

❌ **Unknown.** The Greek failed after twelve sources. English *desolating sacrilege* preserves no Greek morphology whatever.

**That was the entire point of the control case** — to learn what the people who actually lived through the crisis meant by the phrase, and whether they used Daniel's exact wording.

📌 And note what this means structurally: **the best finding in this chapter and its biggest gap are the same question asked of two different centuries**, and only one of them got an answer.

★ **And a second thing the control case would not supply.** 1 Maccabees 1:54 **does not say what the abomination *was*.** Not an idol, not an altar, not a statue, not a sacrifice — the text names none of them there. It says only that it was erected **upon the altar of burnt-offering**, and moves on to the swine and the scrolls.

**Even the closest historical witness leaves the object underspecified.** That is itself a finding, and it cuts against any confident modern assertion about what an abomination of desolation must physically be.
:::

One thing I am deliberately leaving open: **whether Daniel 11:36 continues describing Antiochus or the horizon shifts.** That is a commentary-level dispute and no retrieval can decide it. Recorded open — ⚠️ **and it must not be settled later on the ground that one answer helps my chronology.**

## The Synoptics agree about the flight and differ about the sign

All three verified in Greek.

| Beat | Matthew 24 | Mark 13 | Luke 21 |
|------------------|------------------------|-----------------------|------------------------|
| *when you see* | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| **the sign** | **the abomination of desolation** | **the abomination of desolation** | ★ **Jerusalem surrounded by armies** |
| its state | *standing in a holy place* | ★ *standing where it ought not* | *her desolation has drawn near* |
| **Daniel cited** | ✅ **by name** | ❌ absent | ❌ absent |
| flight to the mountains | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| the pregnant and nursing | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ — **near-verbatim in all three** |
| Sabbath | ✅ **only Matthew** | ❌ | ❌ |
| sword, captivity, trampling, times of the nations | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ **only Luke** |

**The flight complex is verbally shared across all three.** But the **sign differs**: Matthew and Mark name an abomination; **Luke names armies.**

And two negatives, both confirmed by direct search: **Luke uses the word *abomination* nowhere in that passage, and never mentions Daniel.** **Only Matthew cites Daniel at all.**

📐 So: Matthew and Mark against Luke is a **very strong event correspondence**. ❌ *The abomination is the Roman army* remains **open and not established** — and Luke's own wording is the reason why.

::: finding
**Finding — Luke says the armies are the sign, not the thing**

*When you see Jerusalem surrounded by armies, then know that her desolation **has drawn near**.*

**The armies are the sign that desolation has approached. Luke does not say they *are* it.**

The distinction is textual rather than interpretive, and it is now verified in Greek.

⚠️ **And Mark's grammar is the sharpest single datum in this section, and I am not allowed to use it.** Mark writes *the abomination of desolation **standing** where it ought not* — and **the participle is masculine while the noun is neuter. They disagree.**

A masculine participle attached to a neuter noun points toward **someone standing** rather than **something placed**, which would bear directly on everything above.

🛑 **Recorded as an observation only.** A Greek grammar authority is owed before it carries any weight. I do not parse disputed syntax on my own authority, and this is exactly the kind of datum where doing so would be most tempting and least defensible.
:::

And one more of my own rules comes back to bite. Luke 21:24 has Jerusalem trampled by the nations **until** the times of the nations are fulfilled — the same *until* construction I have tracked at 1 Corinthians 15:25 and Revelation 20:3. **It guarantees the trampling continues *through* that point. It establishes nothing whatever about what follows.** Any claim about the far side needs independent evidence, and the rule was written long before this verse mattered to me.

Luke also says those are days of vengeance, *to fulfil all that is written* — which is a very large claim about this crisis, made by Luke. **Flagged, not yet worked.**

## The festival, half verified

John records Yeshua in Jerusalem at *the Dedication*, in winter — and 1 Maccabees describes an annual eight-day observance instituted after the sanctuary was cleansed.

⚠️ **The link is half verified and is recorded that way.** John's Greek word is verified. **1 Maccabees' Greek was never retrieved** — a lane supplied it from memory and graded the pair *verified*. **Rejected.** What stands is that the Gospel names a festival. What does not yet stand is that it is verbally the same word.

## A rule about recurrence, with both halves

::: finding
**New rule — prophetic recurrence**

**An earlier historical realization of prophetic imagery does not automatically exhaust its later scriptural application when a later inspired writer explicitly reapplies that prophecy.**

**And equally: later reapplication does not erase the earlier historical referent.**

The warrant is not mine. **Antiochus dies in the second century before Messiah. Yeshua nevertheless tells first-century disciples to watch for the abomination of desolation spoken of through Daniel, and to flee Judea when they see it.**

So Antiochus cannot exhaust every legitimate application of Daniel's language — **and I am not saying he was not a referent.** The evidence that he was is substantial. **Yeshua Himself creates the interpretive problem. It was not invented to rescue a theory.**

📌 And a structural observation worth keeping: this is *expansion is not erasure* transposed from **identity** into **prophecy**. The same shape has now appeared in three separate domains — covenant identity, textual expansion, and prophetic reapplication. **A rule that recurs independently across domains is better evidence for the rule than any single application of it.**

⚠️ **Its leash:** it may **not** be invoked to keep a reading alive that its worst datum has killed. It licenses the **question** of recurrence. It never supplies the **answer**, and each claimed recurrence must be demonstrated from the text.
:::

## A ledger of a motif, which is not a chain of identifications

| Figure | The act | The outcome |
|--------------------------|--------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| **Nebuchadnezzar** | boasts of his own power and glory | humbled — ★ **then acknowledges the Most High and is restored** |
| **Belshazzar** | ★ profanes **the vessels taken from the Jerusalem temple** | killed that night |
| **Antiochus IV** | sanctuary and cultic profanation | destroyed |
| **Herod Agrippa I** | accepts *the voice of a god* | struck down |
| **Paul's lawless one** | self-exaltation **in Yahweh's sanctuary** | destroyed at the coming |

🛑 **Motif is not identity.** These five are not one figure, and this is a conceptual category against which Paul's language can be **tested** — nothing more.

❗ **And the partial-parallel brake bites immediately: Nebuchadnezzar's arc resolves in repentance and restoration.** Similarity at departure cannot survive contradiction at resolution. That is precisely why he belongs in a motif register and not in a chain.

⚠️ **All five rows are reported rather than retrieved in this pass**, except Paul's. **Verify before use** — and I would rather print that sentence than let a tidy table pass as verified work.

# The debt that failed three times

Four separate findings had been blocked by one missing text: **Theodotion's Greek Daniel.** Three attempts, across three stages, in three different places. All three failed.

And the diagnosis matters more than the fix.

::: textualnote
**Textual note — the Theodotion recovery, and why persistence was not the answer**

Every prior attempt went looking for **a reading edition that displays Greek Daniel.**

**Two assumptions were wrong, and neither had ever been examined.**

**One:** that Theodotion would exist as **a reading edition**. It exists as an **academic plain-text corpus** — a morphologically tagged Septuagint, kept where specialists keep such things rather than where a reader would look.

**Two:** that *the Greek Daniel* is one text with Theodotion somewhere inside it. **It is not. The corpus ships the two recensions as two separate files, under two names.**

❗ **And the clue was already in my own archive, unread.** Two chapters ago I recorded that a particular source's Daniel gave a distinctive reading, and identified it as **Old Greek from the text's own tell**. The standard critical edition prints **Theodotion** as its main Daniel text. **Those two facts sitting together imply that a different edition tree would yield Theodotion — and three subsequent stages searched without ever combining them.**

🆕 **The rule this forces, third in a family:** *failing to find something is not evidence that it does not exist* · *where you look matters as much as what you look for* · and now — **ask what *kind* of artifact the text is.** Some texts are not web pages, and a text existing in two recensions may be shipped as two files.

**In both of the last two recoveries the fix was not persistence. It was asking a different question about the artifact.**
:::

And it did not merely fill a gap.

::: finding
**Finding — a claim I had let be destabilized was never in real doubt**

| Witness | Preposition | The second clause |
|------------------|-------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| **Aramaic** | *with* | *he came **to** the Ancient of Days and was **presented** before him* |
| **Theodotion** | *with* | ✅ *he **reached** the Ancient of Days… and was **brought before** him* |
| **Old Greek** | *on* | ⚠️ *and **as** an Ancient of Days **was present*** |

**Theodotion agrees with the Aramaic on both points. The Old Greek is the outlier on both.**

So the claim that Daniel 7:13's son of man is **presented before** the Ancient of Days rather than descending to earth is **confirmed by two of three witnesses**.

📌 **And my earlier handling is vindicated.** It had been asserted to me that the Old Greek said *he approaches the throne*; I rejected the assertion and recorded the divergence **unresolved** rather than smoothing it. **Had I accepted it, this finding would have been invisible** — the Old Greek's genuine oddity would have been erased into agreement, and the two-of-three result could never have appeared.
:::

## A fingerprint I was not looking for — and the brake that limits it

::: finding
**Finding — Matthew and John follow different Greek Daniels**

| | Preposition with the clouds | Matches |
|------------------------|------------------|--------------------------|
| **Matthew 24:30** | <span class="gr">ἐπί</span> | ★ **Old Greek** |
| **Revelation 1:7** | <span class="gr">μετά</span> | ★ **Theodotion / Aramaic** |

And the same split appears independently at the abomination phrase: **Matthew's articular form matches the Old Greek of 12:11**, and **Theodotion's is anarthrous — so Matthew does not match it.**

**Two independent data points, same direction.**

📌 And this supplies the **cause** of the article-inversion puzzle I could not explain earlier in this chapter. Matthew's article is not the Hebrew's, which is anarthrous, and not Theodotion's, which is also anarthrous. **It is the Old Greek's.** The inversion was never a mystery. It is a **recension fingerprint**.

🛑 **And the brake, printed as prominently as the finding, because the answer arrived the same day:**

| | Preposition | Matches |
|---------------------|--------------------------------|---------------------|
| Matthew 24:30 | <span class="gr">ἐπί</span> | Old Greek |
| **Mark 13:26** | ★ <span class="gr">ἐν</span> | ❗ **neither** |
| **Luke 21:27** | ★ <span class="gr">ἐν</span>, and **singular** | ❗ **neither** |
| Revelation 1:7 | <span class="gr">μετά</span> | Theodotion |

**Four witnesses, three prepositions, and only two of the four match either Greek Daniel.**

The Matthew and Revelation alignments are genuine. **But Mark and Luke show the cloud-coming tradition is not simply tracking recensions** — *in* is neither *on* nor *with*, and **Luke's noun is singular where every other witness is plural.**

📐 So the claim must be stated at its actual scope: **Matthew's two quotations align with the Old Greek, and Revelation 1:7's preposition aligns with Theodotion.** It may **not** become *the New Testament splits along recension lines*. **It does not.**

⚠️ And a conclusion overruled again: it was reported to me that Mark and Luke *align with the Theodotion tradition or employ* <span class="gr">ἐν</span> *independently*. **Theodotion has *with*, not *in*.** Only the second half of that disjunction survives.
:::

And the recovery closed one of my own open questions in the direction I did not want.

**Theodotion 11:36 uses the same exalting verb as the Old Greek**, plus another — and **neither is Paul's word.** So the chain is now fully mapped and Paul matches **no link in it**: Hebrew, Old Greek, Theodotion, Paul — four steps, four different words. *Paul is quoting Daniel 11:36* is refused against **both** recensions rather than one.

::: textualnote
**A near-miss, refused in writing so the refusal outlives the memory of why**

Theodotion 11:36 contains a word for **arrogant, swollen speech** sitting a few words from where Paul's verb would be. It shares Paul's **prefix**.

**And nothing else.** Different roots, different words, different parts of speech.

**A shared prefix is not a lexical link.** I am recording this as a refusal specifically because it is the kind of near-miss that becomes a *striking parallel* thirty stages later, when nobody remembers that it was checked and rejected.
:::

## And Theodotion varies too — which finishes the chapter's first finding

| Passage | Hebrew | Old Greek | **Theodotion** |
|-------------|-------------------|---------------------|----------------------------------|
| **8:13** | *transgression* | ❌ not verified | ★ **<span class="gr">ἁμαρτία</span> — *sin*, not *abomination*** |
| **11:31** | articular | *abomination of desolation* | ★ **a different desolating word entirely** |
| **12:11** | anarthrous | **articular** | **anarthrous** |

**Theodotion renders Daniel 8:13's *transgression* as *sin*, not *abomination*. The Hebrew's lexical distinction survives into Greek.** And Theodotion's own two renderings at 11:31 and 12:11 differ from each other.

📐 **So there is no fixed phrase in any of the three textual traditions.** *The abomination of desolation* is a recurring **shape**, not a title — and that is now established in Hebrew, in the Old Greek, and in Theodotion.

::: textualnote
**An edition mismatch this recovery introduces, declared rather than absorbed**

The Old Greek in every comparison above comes from one edition; the Theodotion comes from another. **These are two different editions**, and the Old Greek file sitting in the same corpus as the Theodotion one **was not downloaded.**

The findings are almost certainly robust to this — the preposition and article differences are the standard, well-attested distinctions between the recensions rather than edition quirks.

🛑 **But *almost certainly* is not a verification**, and I have rejected that exact phrasing from my own agents twice. A same-corpus comparison is owed before any of this is leaned on harder.
:::

# The test I wanted to fail

By this point I had collected so many resemblances between Matthew 24 and 2 Thessalonians 2 that I no longer trusted myself with them. Every new correspondence felt like confirmation, and that feeling is exactly what the method exists to distrust.

So I tried to break it instead. **One question, and it is unkind:**

> **If these describe the same crisis, can their sequences coexist without rearranging either text?**

**No historical candidates. No named emperor. No attempt to identify the restrainer.**

::: textualnote
**A debt paid before the argument leaned on it**

Matthew 24:12's *lawlessness* had been sitting in my files as **reported rather than independently verified.** **And it is the lexical spine of this entire test** — so I verified the Greek before allowing the argument to depend on it, rather than letting it inherit credibility from the verified claims standing next to it.

So it was verified before being used. It is now confirmed in Greek.

📌 Recorded because the alternative was available and tempting: the claim sat adjacent to a dozen verified ones and **would have inherited their credibility by proximity.** That adjacency is precisely how an unverified claim acquires borrowed weight, and nobody would have gone back to look.
:::

## Where the correspondence is not lexical — recorded first, deliberately

**Falling away.** Matthew has one word at 24:10; Paul has a different one at 2:3. **And a direct search settles it: Paul's word does not occur anywhere in Matthew 24.** Conceptual correspondence: moderate. **Lexical correspondence: none.**

**The sanctuary.** Matthew has *abomination of desolation… in a holy place*; Paul has *the sanctuary of God*. I established two chapters ago that those two verses share **zero vocabulary**. So this is **intertextual correspondence via a third text — Daniel — not lexical correspondence between the two authors.** A distinct and weaker evidence class, named as such.

## Where it is lexical — and there are two, plus a third held under caution

::: wordstudy
**Word study — <span class="gr">ἀνομία</span>**

<span class="gr">ἀνομία</span> is *lawlessness* — literally the condition of being without <span class="gr">νόμος</span>. Not merely crime; **the absence or repudiation of the law's rule.**

**Matthew 24:12** — *because **lawlessness** will be increased, the love of many will grow cold.*

**2 Thessalonians 2:7** — *the mystery of **lawlessness** is already at work.*

**2 Thessalonians 2:8** — *and then **the lawless one** will be revealed.*

**Same word family, and — this is the part that matters — the same position in both sequences.** Both place it **before** the climactic intervention, not after.

📌 And the word carries a Matthean history worth noting: it is the same root Matthew uses of *those who practise lawlessness* in the parable of the weeds, gathered out at the end of the age. **That is an observation, not an argument** — but it means the word is not incidental vocabulary in Matthew.
:::

**And the second lexical link is stronger still.** Matthew 24:24 has *great **signs and wonders**, so as to lead astray, if possible, even the elect.* 2 Thessalonians 2:9 has *all power and **signs and lying wonders***.

★★ **Identical lexemes, different cases — and in both, the function is deception immediately preceding Messiah's intervention.** That is the strongest single correspondence the two passages possess.

**The third is the gathering family**, carried from earlier — and still held under the finding that it is **not inherently eschatological.**

⚠️ And the correction that stays corrected: **Matthew 24:31 itself contains no *coming*.** Matthew supplies that word at four other points in the discourse. **2 Thessalonians 2:1 is where the pair actually stands in one clause. The pair is Paul's, not Matthew's.**

## The test, and what surviving it does and does not mean

One structural point first, and it decides whether the test is fair. **Paul loops backward at 2:6–8** to explain the mechanism behind what he introduced at 2:3–4. **2 Thessalonians 2 is not written as a straight timeline**, and narrative order must be distinguished from event order before any alignment is attempted. A test that ignored this would manufacture a contradiction that is not there.

| | Matthew | Paul |
|---|--------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| 1 | lawlessness increases | mystery of lawlessness already active |
| 2 | Danielic sanctuary crisis | lawless one, sanctuary, self-exaltation |
| 3 | **deceptive signs and wonders** | **deceptive signs and wonders** |
| 4 | Messiah's coming | Messiah's coming |
| 5 | gathering of the elect | *(the gathering is the stated subject at 2:1)* |

✅ **Both move in the same direction. Neither has to be rearranged.** I do not have Matthew running *coming → lawlessness* against Paul running *lawlessness → coming*.

::: evidence
**What surviving a falsification test is, and is not**

**The hypothesis was given a chance to die and did not.** That is a real result and it is the point of the exercise.

🛑 **It is not the same as being proven.** Compatible order is a **necessary** condition for identity, not a **sufficient** one — and **two different crises built on the same page of Daniel would also produce compatible order.**

A falsification test that passes tells you less than one that fails. I am recording the passing result with its limit attached, because a bare *the sequence survives* reads far stronger than it is.
:::

## Three asymmetries, and the third is the honest one

**One.** Matthew's *great tribulation* has no explicit counterpart in Paul.

**Two.** Matthew's cosmic-sign complex has none either.

**Three.** ❗ **Paul's explicit destruction of the lawless one has no individual counterpart in Matthew at all.**

The first two are **omissions**, and a shorter account need not contain every feature of a longer one. **Omission is not contradiction.**

**The third is different and sharper. Paul has a named figure who is destroyed. Matthew has no individual destroyed at all.**

🛑 **Candidates exist** — the judgment language, the city's destruction, the coming itself — **and the blank is deliberately left unfilled.** Filling it now would be building the bridge out of the gap it is supposed to cross, which is the specific failure I have spent fifty stages avoiding.

**And these are recorded as prominently as the correspondences. A table of only matches is not a test.**

## A rule I am adopting, and it is the most dangerous one yet

::: finding
**New rule — the burden of distinction, with its leash**

**When two passages independently share an unusually dense combination of vocabulary, source-text background, event sequence, audience horizon and theological function, maintaining that they describe *different* events requires positive grounds for distinguishing them — not merely the theoretical possibility that they could differ.**

**Why it is legitimate:** without it, scepticism becomes unfalsifiable. *They could still be different* can be said of any two passages forever, and a method that permits an unanswerable objection at every step is not a method.

🛑 **And its leash is part of the rule, because this is one inversion away from *similar therefore same*:**

**1.** It shifts a burden **at a density threshold**. It does not lower the standard of proof.

**2.** **The density must be independent.** Correspondences inherited from a shared source — both drawing on Daniel — are **one datum, not five**. And the sanctuary link above is exactly that kind.

**3.** **It may never retire an asymmetry.** The three gaps are positive grounds for distinction and remain live.

**4.** It does not apply to correspondences I have already downgraded. Paul's Danielic link is conceptual, not lexical, and **may not be counted at full weight here.**
:::

## The verdict, stated at its real strength

📐 **Matthew 24's crisis and 2 Thessalonians 2's crisis: very strong correspondence; *probable* event-complex identity. Not established. Not closed.**

**What earns *probable* rather than correspondence alone:** two genuine lexical links plus a third held under caution · shared Danielic background · **compatible event order under a real falsification test** · convergent audience-time pressure · and Paul explicitly coordinating the coming with the gathering.

**What prevents *established*:** the three asymmetries, **especially the destroyed individual** · the restrainer, unidentified · the sanctuary referent, **open** · the relation between the Day of the Lord, the coming and the gathering, unworked · and the plain fact that **a shared Danielic template could produce compatible order without identity.**

And one constraint that now presses directly. Matthew says *immediately after the tribulation of those days*. **If these are one complex, Paul's coming and gathering cannot be displaced millennia after Matthew's tribulation without an account of that word.**

## What I actually have

I have been asking what the abomination **is**. I am starting to think Daniel is answering a different question: **what it does.**

The Maccabean account was supposed to settle what the thing physically was. It refused too — it says only that they erected it on the altar, and moves on to the swine and the burned scrolls and the forbidden circumcision. **Even the people closest to it did not think the object needed naming.**

And the sequence test gave me less than it felt like it gave me. Three genuine shared words, one shared prophet standing behind both, an order that refused to break — and three holes. A tribulation Paul never mentions. A sky Paul never darkens. **And a man Paul says is destroyed whom Matthew never destroys.**

The last one is the honest one. **Paul kills somebody. Matthew does not.** I do not know what to do with that yet, and I would rather write that sentence down than fill the hole with something convenient.

::: whereweare
**Where we are.** Daniel has no fixed phrase — four forms, two nouns, and a recurring shape rather than a title, now confirmed in Hebrew and in both Greek recensions. Abomination and desolation are grammatically distinct, which blocks three shortcuts at once. The Antiochus control case confirmed the event-profile and failed to answer the one question it existed for, and even it never says what the abomination was. The Synoptics share the flight and differ on the sign, and Luke says the armies are the sign that desolation has drawn near rather than the thing itself. Theodotion, after three failures, confirmed that Daniel's son of man is presented before the Ancient of Days, refused Paul's supposed quotation against both recensions, and produced a recension fingerprint that Mark and Luke immediately limited. And Matthew and Paul survived a sequence test built to kill them — which establishes compatibility, not identity, with three asymmetries still standing and one of them unanswered.
