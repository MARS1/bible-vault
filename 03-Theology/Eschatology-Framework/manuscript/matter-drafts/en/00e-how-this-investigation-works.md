# How this investigation works

This page is meant to be returned to. It explains the labels you will see throughout the book.

## Two different questions about every claim

For any claim, the book asks two separate things:

1. **What kind of statement is it?** Something the text says, or something I worked out from it?
2. **How strongly does the evidence bear on it?**

They are different tools, and the book keeps them apart.

## Six kinds of statement

| Kind | What it means | An example from this book |
|---|---|---|
| **Explicit text** | The passage says it outright. | Revelation 20:7: when the thousand years are ended, Satan will be released. |
| **Strong cumulative inference** | Not said in one place, but several texts point the same way. | That the beast and false prophet are already in the lake when the devil is thrown in at 20:10. The clause has no verb; the inference comes from the sequence, since 19:20 puts them there first. |
| **Probable reconstruction** | My best account of how the pieces fit. Defensible, not proven. | That Satan's binding restrains one specific kind of deception: the kind that gathers the nations against Yah's people. |
| **Historical observation** | Something that happened in the world, known from history rather than from Scripture. | Rome destroyed Jerusalem and its temple in AD 70. |
| **Hypothesis** | An idea worth testing that has not earned more. | That we are living in the "little season" after the thousand years. |
| **Open question** | Not settled, and I say so. | Where the present day falls within Revelation 20. |

If I ever move a claim up a level because a sentence reads better that way, I have broken the method.

## Four words for evidence strength

Evidence can bear on a claim in four ways. These words are set apart in the text so you can spot them:

- <span class="grade">contradicts</span> — the evidence counts against the claim.
- <span class="grade">permits</span> — the claim can survive alongside the evidence. This is much weaker than it sounds, and most of what people call proof lives here.
- <span class="grade">supports</span> — the evidence makes the claim more likely.
- <span class="grade">requires</span> — the evidence leaves no reasonable alternative.

Where something only permits, I say so, even when I would rather it required.

## The boxes

Each chapter uses a few recurring boxes. Every type has its own color and its own icon, so you can tell them apart at a glance. If your copy is printed in black and white, the icon and the style of the border still tell you which is which.

- <span class="boxicon finding">Finding</span> states a result and how far it goes.
- <span class="boxicon evidence">Evidence</span> lays out what a passage or source can and cannot carry.
- <span class="boxicon wordstudy">Word study</span> follows one Greek or Hebrew word through its uses.
- <span class="boxicon textualnote">Textual note</span> flags a translation problem or a manuscript variant.
- <span class="boxicon historical">Historical</span> gives background a reader may not have.
- <span class="boxicon person">Person</span> introduces someone you may not know.
- <span class="boxicon commentary">Commentary</span> is me thinking aloud about the work, set apart from the findings.
- <span class="boxicon claimtypes">Claim labels</span> sum up the six kinds of statement.
- <span class="boxicon openquestion">Open question</span> marks what the chapter leaves unresolved.
- <span class="boxicon whereweare">Where this leaves us</span> and <span class="boxicon earned">The question this leaves</span> close most chapters.
- <span class="boxicon chain">The growing chain</span> shows, at intervals, the case built so far, link by link.

## Greek and Hebrew

You do not need either language. When the wording matters, the original is printed, then spelled out in English letters in italics, then explained. For example: <span class="gr">γενεά</span> (*genea*), *generation*.

## Failures are kept

Some ideas in this book are tested and fail. They stay in, with the reason they failed. A method that never costs its owner anything is not a method.
