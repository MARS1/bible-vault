# How to read apocalyptic literature

Daniel, parts of Ezekiel and Zechariah, and Revelation belong to a kind of writing called **apocalyptic**. The word comes from the Greek for *unveiling*. These books speak in visions: beasts, horns, seals, trumpets, cities, numbers. They are not written like a letter or a history, and reading them as if they were causes most of the confusion around them.

The investigation settled on six habits for reading them.

## 1. Ask where Scripture used the image first

Revelation invents very little of its imagery. It draws again and again on Daniel, Ezekiel, Isaiah, Exodus, Zechariah and the Psalms, and on the language of the temple, Babylon, and Gog and Magog. So the first question about any image is:

> **Where has Scripture used this image before?**

The answer usually narrows the meaning more than any modern guess can.

## 2. Let the text explain its own symbols

Sometimes the book explains itself. The seven lampstands *are* the seven assemblies (Revelation 1:20). The seven heads *are* seven mountains, and also seven kings (17:9–10). When the text gives its own interpretation, that interpretation comes first. When it waits, the reader should wait too.

## 3. A chapter number is not a timestamp

Revelation circles back. It shows related events more than once, from different angles, with rising intensity. This book calls that pattern a **spiral**. So chapter 12 does not have to happen after chapter 11.

## 4. But some sequences are real

Circling back does not erase every sequence. Some steps depend on what came before. Revelation 19–20 is the clearest case: the beast and false prophet are judged, then Satan is bound, then the thousand years, then Satan is released, then the final judgment. Reading the book as a spiral still means paying attention to these steps.

## 5. Start with the first readers

Revelation was written to seven real assemblies in what is now Turkey. It tells them the time is near. Before asking what a vision means today, ask what it meant to them.

## 6. Do not decode it from the newspaper

Matching John's images to modern objects that happen to look like them is not interpretation. It brings the meaning in from outside and then calls it a discovery. Present-day events can be noticed. They cannot be used to date a prophecy.

## And let it stay strange

Some images are not explained, and this book does not force them. The creatures of Revelation 9 are an example; they are listed with the other unresolved questions at the back of the book. Saying "I do not know what this is" is part of reading it honestly.
