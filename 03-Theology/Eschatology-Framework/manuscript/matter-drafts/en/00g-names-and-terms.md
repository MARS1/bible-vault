# Names and terms

This book uses some names that may be unfamiliar. Here is what they mean.

| You will see | Meaning | You may know it as |
|---|---|---|
| **Yahweh**, **Yah** | The personal name of God in the Hebrew Scriptures, written with four Hebrew letters, <span class="he">יהוה</span> (*YHWH*). *Yah* is its short form, as in *Hallelu-Yah*. | God; the LORD |
| **Yeshua** | Jesus' name in Hebrew and Aramaic, <span class="he">יֵשׁוּעַ</span> (*Yeshua*). | Jesus |
| **YahuShua HaMashiach** | A fuller Hebrew form of His name, with His title. The later chapters use it. | Jesus Christ |
| **Messiah**, **HaMashiach** | *Anointed one*, from Hebrew <span class="he">מָשִׁיחַ</span> (*mashiach*). The Greek equivalent is <span class="gr">Χριστός</span> (*Christos*), which gives English *Christ*. | Christ |
| **Ruach HaKodesh** | *The Holy Spirit*, in Hebrew <span class="he">רוּחַ הַקֹּדֶשׁ</span> (*ruach hakodesh*). | The Holy Spirit |
| **Torah** | *Instruction*: the five books of Moses, and the covenant teaching they contain. | The Law |
| **Gentile** | Anyone who is not Jewish. | — |
| **Assemblies** | The communities of believers that Revelation and the letters address. | Churches |

## Why these forms

Over the course of the investigation I came to prefer these forms because they keep the Hebrew covenant world in view, the world the whole story comes from. They are a preference, not a test. A reader who says *God*, *Jesus* and *Holy Spirit* is talking about the same God, and nothing in this book depends on which form you use. The last part of the book comes back to this.

## Quotations keep their own wording

When the book quotes a translation, it keeps that translation's wording. So you will also see *Jesus*, *Christ* and *the LORD* inside quotation marks. Many English Bibles print *LORD* in small capitals where the Hebrew has the divine name.

## Languages and translations used

- **Greek New Testament:** the SBL Greek New Testament.
- **Greek Old Testament:** the Septuagint, and for Daniel, also the version associated with Theodotion.
- **Hebrew Scriptures:** *Miqra according to the Masorah*, an edition of the traditional Hebrew text, read through Sefaria and checked verse by verse where the wording matters.
- **English translations quoted:** chiefly the English Standard Version (ESV) and the Complete Jewish Bible (CJB), compared where they differ.

Dates are given as BC and AD.
