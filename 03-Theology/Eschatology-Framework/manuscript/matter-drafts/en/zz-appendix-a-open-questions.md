# Appendix A — Open questions

This book stopped when its central question had been answered as far as the evidence allows. Many other questions were still open at that point. Chapter 53 promised that they would not be lost, and this appendix keeps that promise.

These are not hidden conclusions. Each entry says what is open, what the book did establish around it, and where to reread. None of them is a prerequisite for the conclusions the book reached. Some I intend to carry into a further investigation; others are here because a careful reader will ask them, and should know that I asked them too.

The full research list holds 441 numbered questions. Most were answered along the way and the answers are in the chapters. The ones below are still open.

## 1. Where are we in Revelation 20?

**1. What determines how long the thousand years last?**
The book established that the thousand years are a bounded period that ends: each time John refers back to them as a completed whole, the phrase carries the article and the same verb, *should be completed*. It did not establish that the number is a calendar count; the evidence for a roughly quantitative reading is no higher than *permits*. *Chapters 47, 49 and 53.*

**2. Where are we now?**
Late in the thousand years, early in the little season, or somewhere else. This is the most important unresolved conclusion of the investigation (PF-02). We can have substantial confidence about an earlier point in the sequence without knowing our present coordinate. *PF-02 is stated in Chapter 53 ("Present eschatological location"); the three possibilities are set out just before it. See also Chapter 54.*

**3. If some premise not yet identified is the one that gives way, which is it?**
Four statements cannot all be true together: a first-century beginning for the transition (PF-01); the thousand years are roughly a thousand calendar years; the little season is genuinely short; and we are in it now. At least one has to give, and the book names five ways that can happen:

- **(a)** the beginning was not in the first century;
- **(b)** the thousand years are not roughly a thousand calendar years;
- **(c)** we are not in the little season;
- **(d)** conventional chronology does not match the biblical interval (eliminated);
- **(e)** some other premise, not yet identified, is wrong.

Option (e) is the one with no advocate. It is easy to neglect because it is only humbling, and eliminating (d) made it more important, not less. *The options are set out in Chapter 47 ("The four-way constraint"), weighed again in Chapter 50 ("Where the five options stand now"), and summarised in a table in Chapter 53 ("But we cannot yet say where we are").*

**4. Who are "the rest of the dead" in Revelation 20:5?**
The two-stage structure of the passage is established: a first resurrection, the thousand years, then the rest of the dead. Who belongs to each group is not. *Chapters 39, 44 and 53.*

**5. Do the saints reign in heaven, on earth, or both?**
That the saints are with Messiah is established. That the reign takes place only in heaven is not; only on earth is not either. Revelation 5:10 and 20:9 put land imagery on both sides, and 20:4 names no location for the thrones. *Chapters 47 and 51.*

**6. What is "the beloved city" of Revelation 20:9?**
The camp and the beloved city identify Yah's covenant people corporately, with Jerusalem and Zion associations. Revelation 20 does not allow the exact geography to be established, and proposals for a hidden or alternative location have not produced evidence. *Chapters 52 and 53.*

**7. Does the gathering at Armageddon belong with Revelation 19, with Revelation 20, or with neither?**
16:14 and 20:8 share word-for-word wording; 16:13 has all three enemies active, as only chapter 19 does. The evidence points in two directions, and treating a favoured reading as settled would be the kind of upgrade this book refuses. *Chapter 51.*

## 2. Resurrection and "the last day"

**8. How do Paul's gathering passages, Matthew 24 and Revelation 20's first resurrection relate?**
The book connected the crisis Yeshua describes in Matthew 24 to the one Paul describes in 2 Thessalonians 2, and recorded that link as *probable*, not certain. It is the weakest joint in the chain, and if it breaks, the beginning of the thousand years moves later on its own. Paul and John also describe the raised saints differently: Paul has a meeting in the air, John has thrones and a reign. *Chapters 42, 44, 51 and 53.*

**9. If believers are raised "on the last day," where does a thousand-year interval fit?**
In John, the last day carries both the raising of believers and the judgment of those who reject Him. That maps more naturally onto the great white throne than onto a first resurrection a thousand years earlier. Recorded as an obstacle; no way through it was selected. *Chapters 40 and 41.*

**10. Daniel 12 says "many" awake; John 5 says "all who are in the tombs." One scene, or several?**
Do Daniel and John compress several moments into one prophetic scene? Does Revelation's first resurrection mean something qualitatively different? Or is the sequence being misread? Stated, not solved. *Chapter 27.*

## 3. Revelation's images

**11. What are the locusts and horsemen of Revelation 9?**
Literal beings, spiritual beings, symbolic powers, historical armies, visionary composites, or a combination. Historical proposals exist, including Parthian cavalry from beyond the Euphrates, and are legitimate hypotheses. None is established. The book did establish that their king, Abaddon, is not identified with Satan. *Chapters 33 and 53; Appendix C.*

**12. Who is the rider on the white horse of the first seal?**
What the text gives is a white horse, a bow, a crown *given*, and conquest. Revelation 19 puts Messiah on a white horse, but identifies Him in ways chapter 6 does not, and the same symbol need not carry the same referent. *Chapter 31.*

**13. Are the 144,000 and the innumerable multitude the same people, different people, or related?**
Each of the three answers is defensible. None was demonstrated. *Chapter 32.*

**14. Who are the two witnesses?**
Revelation never names them Moses and Elijah. They may be individuals resembling them, a corporate prophetic witness, symbolic representatives, or some combination. *Chapter 34.*

**15. What, physically, was the mark of the beast?**
The mark signifies allegiance to beastly authority and carries real social and economic consequences. Whether it was a literal mark, a certificate, a cultic token, a legal requirement, a commercial mechanism, or shorthand for taking part in an imperial-religious economy needs historical investigation. *Chapter 36.*

**16. Seven kings, and an eighth who "belongs to the seven": who are they?**
Any reading of Revelation 17 has to explain both the seven and the eighth. That datum should damage weak theories, including mine, and it is recorded unresolved. *Chapters 38 and 46.*

**17. Who or what is Babylon?**
The beast and Babylon are related but distinct: the beast and its horns burn the woman. Jerusalem fits the prophetic-blood and covenant-prostitute evidence, and "the great city" where the Lord was crucified. Rome fits the dominion, the hills, and the commerce. Whether "the great city" of 16:19 is Babylon or distinct from her bears directly on the answer. *Chapters 37, 38, 46 and 53.*

**18. When was the dragon cast down?**
The casting down is causally tied to Messiah's victory: His death, resurrection and exaltation, and the testimony of His followers. Whether it happens at that moment, or the vision combines that victory with a later outworking, the evidence does not say. *Chapter 35.*

**19. What does "the sea was no more" mean?**
The Greek really says *sea*; there is no translator's device to remove. Sea and abyss are different words. 20:13 uses *sea* in its ordinary sense one verse earlier, and 22:1 has a river of the water of life. A physical change, the removal of what the sea stood for, or both: the wording does not decide. *Chapter 39; Appendix C.*

## 4. The New Jerusalem and after

**20. Could the final judgment and the New Jerusalem already lie behind us?**
Set out and not adopted. It is logically open, but carries much less support than the two leading possibilities. Death is still part of human experience, and Revelation's post-judgment order says it will be no more. *Chapters 53 and 54.*

**21. What is the New Jerusalem?**
A literal city, the covenant people, heavenly Jerusalem, geographic Jerusalem, or how they relate. Revelation 21 identifies the city with no external geography at all. *Chapters 39 and 53.*

**22. Who is "outside" in Revelation 22:15?**
Two readings are well grounded: covenantal description rather than a post-judgment neighbourhood, and recapitulation of an earlier period. A third, some other post-judgment reality, would need far more evidence than the word *outside* sounding spatial. *Chapters 39 and 53.*

**23. Why do nations, kings and "the healing of the nations" remain in Revelation 21–22?**
Along with the gates that are not shut and the invitation still extended. These details deserve their own investigation. *Chapters 39, 53 and 54.*

## 5. Older prophecy

**24. What is Isaiah's "flying fiery serpent"?**
<span class="he">שָׂרָף מְעוֹפֵף</span> (*saraph meʿopheph*), in Isaiah 14:29 and 30:6. What it represents in its original contexts was raised during this investigation and not examined in it. *Chapter 53.*

**25. Was Isaiah 17's oracle against Damascus fulfilled in the Assyrian judgment, or does something remain?**
Raised during this investigation and not examined in it. *Chapter 53.*

**26. What are Daniel 12's 1,290 and 1,335 days, and the forty-five days between them?**
I do not know, and I did not invent a meaning. This is the kind of text where intervals get matched against history until something lines up, and the result gets called a discovery. *Chapter 29.*

**27. Ezekiel 37 promises a sanctuary among them forever. Fulfilled, partly fulfilled, or still future?**
Four live options, none selected: fulfilled in Messiah or in a Spirit-indwelt people; fulfilled partially in the Second Temple period; still future; or governed by the temple vision of Ezekiel 40–48, which this book deliberately did not open. *Chapter 14.*

## 6. Covenant, Torah and the people of God

**28. What does "all Israel will be saved" mean in Romans 11:26?**
Ethnic Israel, the elect within Israel, the whole covenant people including Gentiles, or a future restoration. Four readings, none selected; theological instinct, including mine, does not settle it. *Chapters 19 and 22.*

**29. If "all is accomplished" in the first century, how does Matthew 5:19 apply afterward?**
The easy exit, that the verse was only transitional, is exactly the kind of protective exception I refuse to grant other readings. So I do not grant it to mine. Recorded as a cost of my own position. *Chapter 12.*

**30. "They shall all know me": how does Jeremiah 31:34 fit a new covenant already here, when teaching still goes on?**
Possible readings: an eschatological not-yet; a statement about who is inside the covenant; recognised covenant hyperbole. Not adjudicated. It is a cost for any reading that treats the new covenant as fully consummated, including the one this book moves toward. *Chapter 14.*

## 7. Method, sources and the reader

**31. The apostles sometimes argue from the Greek Septuagint, not the Hebrew. What does that cost a method that says "go to the source languages"?**
At Romans 10:18 Paul quotes the Septuagint verbatim; at Romans 11 he matches neither text; at the Jerusalem Council, James argues for Gentile inclusion from Amos 9 in its Septuagint form. Those conclusions are in the canon. A real cost, not explained away. *Chapters 20 and 22.*

**32. John the Baptist says he is not Elijah; Yeshua says he is. How do both stand?**
John 1:21 and Matthew 11:14. Respectable reconciliations exist, such as John denying a literal returned Elijah while Yeshua affirms a role. I find some more plausible than others, and I do not know. *Chapter 3.*

**33. When, if ever, is it legitimate to connect ancient imagery with modern technology?**
The book rejected reassigning the mark to every new technology, and turning unusual imagery into machinery by visual resemblance. It also found that Revelation's silence about artificial intelligence proves nothing either way. The positive question, under what conditions such a connection could be legitimate, is still open. *Chapter 53.*

**34. What do 1 Enoch, Jubilees and the wider Ethiopian canon change?**
Additional sources to test. They may change, clarify or leave untouched what this book concluded. *Chapter 54.*

**35. Old polar maps and the Rupes Nigra.**
External hypotheses requiring evidence, not on the same footing as the biblical texts: claims drawn from old polar maps and the "black rock" some early modern maps placed at the pole. Naming them is not a forecast; a further investigation may validate none of them. *Chapter 54.*
