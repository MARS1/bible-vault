# Appendix B — Tested and rejected

Some of the most useful work in this book went into ideas that did not hold. They are listed here so you can see what was tried, what each would have needed, and why it was not adopted.

These are proposals, not my own mistakes. Those are in Appendix G. One of these proposals I would have liked to be true, because it would have solved a real problem for my position. It is first on the list.

A word on what *rejected* means here. For almost every item, the evidence examined in this investigation does not warrant the conclusion. That is not the same as proving it impossible. Where an idea was actually contradicted by evidence, the entry says so.

## Chronology

**1. A thousand years were secretly inserted into conventional history.**

- **What it would have solved:** a thousand-year reign beginning in the first century would have ended around the eleventh, which makes a short "little season" afterward hard to fit with today. Remove a thousand years from the calendar and the arithmetic works.
- **What it needed:** evidence, independent of European chronicles, that roughly a thousand counted years never happened.
- **Why it failed:** contradicted by the evidence. The Islamic calendar's continuous count, computed across fourteen centuries, lands within about a year of the actual Islamic year; the inserted millennium would need it to be wrong by about 1,032 years. Halley's comet records, tree-ring chronologies and other calendar traditions agreed. Chapters 47 (the proposal), 50 (the tests), 53.

**2. Heavenly time dilation: the saints experience a thousand years while a different amount of time passes on earth.**

- **What it would have solved:** the same arithmetic problem, without touching the calendar.
- **What it needed:** a conversion mechanism in the text.
- **Why it was not adopted:** Revelation gives none. A different *location* does not establish a different *duration*. Chapter 53.

## Identifications

**3. Gog and Magog are today's Russia, China, Iran or Turkey.**

- **What it needed:** two different things, and only the first was found. First, historical geography. Ezekiel's names belonged to real peoples and places of his own day, and some can be located on a map. Persia, which Ezekiel 38:5 names among Gog's allies, lies in today's Iran, much as Babylon lies in today's Iraq. That kind of correspondence is legitimate and worth investigating. Second, and much harder: evidence that the prophecy is fulfilled by those modern states acting together now.
- **Why it was not adopted:** the second step was never shown. Popular prophecy charts often go beyond geography and match names by their sound in English, such as *Rosh* with Russia or *Meshech* with Moscow. That is not evidence. Revelation 20 also widens Ezekiel's picture: John places Gog and Magog among the nations at the four corners of the earth, after the thousand years, and carries over Ezekiel's names and very little else. No prophet was asked to draw a modern map, and this book does not pretend one did. Where the historical correspondence is strong, it deserves investigation. That research is still open. Chapters 48, 53.

**4. The mark of the beast is a modern technology (chips, barcodes, digital currency, biometric ID and the like).**

- **What it needed:** the whole complex Revelation 13 describes: the beast, worship and allegiance, the beast's name and number, and economic coercion. Not just a technology that affects buying and selling.
- **Why it was not adopted:** resemblance alone does not establish identity, and in the book's leading sequence the beast belongs before the thousand years. Chapters 36, 53.

**5. The beast and Babylon are the same entity.**

- **What it needed:** a text that equates them.
- **Why it was not adopted:** Revelation keeps them distinct. The beast and its horns destroy the woman (17:16). Each has its own evidence. Chapters 38, 53.

**6. Abaddon, or Apollyon, the angel of the abyss, is Satan.**

- **What it needed:** a text that makes the identification.
- **Why it was not adopted:** Revelation never makes it, and when Revelation wants to identify someone, it does (12:9, 20:2). Chapters 33, 35, 53.

**7. Unusual biblical imagery describes modern technology because it looks like it.**

- **What it needed:** more than visual likeness: a reason from the text itself.
- **Why it was not adopted:** Revelation's images come from earlier Scripture. Revelation 9's locusts are read through Joel before they are compared with anything modern. Chapters 33, 53.

## Sequence

**8. Every persecution of believers is Revelation 20's final war.**

- **What it needed:** the full sequence Revelation 20 describes: Satan released, the nations deceived, broad mobilization, the camp of the saints surrounded, and Yah's direct intervention.
- **Why it was not adopted:** persecution is real, past and present. That complete sequence has not been established in history. Chapter 53.

**9. The war of Revelation 19 is the same war as Gog and Magog in Revelation 20.**

- **What it needed:** the two to sit at the same point in Revelation's sequence.
- **Why it was not adopted:** the beast and false prophet are judged in chapter 19, then Satan is bound, then come the thousand years, and only afterward does the Gog and Magog war happen. Chapters 39, 51, 53.

## Words

**10. The abyss is the ocean.**

- **What it needed:** Revelation using *abyssos* for the sea.
- **Why it was not adopted:** in Revelation the abyss is consistently a prison, with a key and a shaft. The sea has its own word. Chapter 39.

**11. Revelation's word *gē* (earth, land) proves a particular modern cosmological model.**

- **What it needed:** the word to carry a shape or model of the world.
- **Why it was not adopted:** *gē* means land, earth, ground or territory. Its eighty-two occurrences in Revelation describe places and people, not a cosmology. Chapters 46, 53.

## Israel and the nations

**12. Gentile believers must become Israel by ancestry or circumcision.**

- **What it needed:** Scripture requiring it as the condition of belonging.
- **Why it was not adopted:** the Jerusalem Council did not impose it (Acts 15), and Romans 11 pictures Gentiles as wild branches grafted in, never renamed Israelites. Chapters 5, 16, 17, 53.

**13. The church replaced Israel.**

- **What it needed:** a new tree in place of the old one.
- **Why it was not adopted:** Romans 11 grafts the wild branches into Israel's existing olive tree. It does not plant a new one. Chapters 22, 53.

## What rejecting these does not mean

None of this means every part of every idea above is impossible. It means the evidence this investigation examined does not warrant the conclusion. If new evidence appears, the same test applies to it.
