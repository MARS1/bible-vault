# Appendix C — The audits

An audit here means one thing: going through *every* occurrence of a word or theme, not a convenient handful. Where a chapter rests on a word's usage, the chapter gives the result. This appendix gives the complete list, so you can check the result yourself.

Every list below was taken directly from the Greek text this book uses (the SBL Greek New Testament), not from an English concordance. A count is given as occurrences, then verses, because a word can appear twice in one verse. *(×2)* marks a verse where it does.

## C1. The abyss: *abyssos* in Revelation

<span class="gr">ἄβυσσος</span> (*abyssos*), *abyss*, *the deep*: **7 occurrences in 7 verses.**

| Verse | What happens |
|---|---|
| 9:1 | the key to the shaft of the abyss *is given* to a fallen star |
| 9:2 | the abyss is opened; smoke rises |
| 9:11 | the locusts' king is *the angel of the abyss*, named Abaddon in Hebrew and Apollyon in Greek |
| 11:7 | the beast *rises from* the abyss and makes war on the two witnesses |
| 17:8 | the beast *is about to rise from* the abyss and go to destruction |
| 20:1 | an angel comes down *holding* the key of the abyss and a great chain |
| 20:3 | Satan is thrown into the abyss; it is shut and sealed over him |

What the thread shows:

- **The abyss is a prison, not a sea.** It has a key and a shaft. Things are shut in it and released from it. Revelation has a separate word for the sea (C3), and when the new creation comes, it is the *sea* that is no more, not the abyss. Elsewhere in the New Testament the word carries the same sense of confinement: in Luke 8:31 the demons beg not to be sent into the abyss.
- **Nothing opens or closes it without authority.** At 9:1 the key *is given*; at 20:1 the angel already *holds* it. Above both stands 1:18, where Yeshua holds the keys of Death and Hades.
- **The same place serves two opposite moments.** The beast rises from it; later the dragon who empowered the beast is shut into it. An image can return without the event being the same: *the abyss opened* is not *the abyss sealed*.
- **Abaddon is not identified as Satan.** Revelation names Satan explicitly twice, in nearly the same formula (12:9, 20:2). At 9:11 it calls this figure *the angel of the abyss* and stops there. When Revelation wants to identify someone, it does. (Chapters 33, 35, 39.)

## C2. Revelation 20:1–10, clause by clause

The chapter on Revelation 20 (Chapter 39) rests on the wording of these ten verses. Here is each verse in Greek, with what its wording establishes and what it does not.

**20:1**

> <span class="gr">Καὶ εἶδον ἄγγελον καταβαίνοντα ἐκ τοῦ οὐρανοῦ, ἔχοντα τὴν κλεῖν τῆς ἀβύσσου καὶ ἅλυσιν μεγάλην ἐπὶ τὴν χεῖρα αὐτοῦ.</span>

- *Coming down* (<span class="gr">καταβαίνοντα</span>, *katabainonta*), *holding the key of the abyss* and *a great chain*.
- **Does not establish** who the angel is. The text says *an angel*, nothing more.

**20:2**

> <span class="gr">καὶ ἐκράτησεν τὸν δράκοντα, ὁ ὄφις ὁ ἀρχαῖος, ὅς ἐστιν Διάβολος καὶ ὁ Σατανᾶς, καὶ ἔδησεν αὐτὸν χίλια ἔτη,</span>

- *Seized* (<span class="gr">ἐκράτησεν</span>, *ekratēsen*), then four names for one figure: the dragon, the ancient serpent, the Devil, Satan. One of the clearest identifications in the book.
- *Bound* (<span class="gr">ἔδησεν</span>, *edēsen*, from *deō*, to bind) is stated outright. The binding is not inferred from the abyss; the verb is there.
- After the accusative *the dragon*, *the ancient serpent* stands in the nominative. That is a known feature of Revelation's Greek. It is recorded, and nothing is built on it.

**20:3**

> <span class="gr">καὶ ἔβαλεν αὐτὸν εἰς τὴν ἄβυσσον, καὶ ἔκλεισεν καὶ ἐσφράγισεν ἐπάνω αὐτοῦ, ἵνα μὴ πλανήσῃ ἔτι τὰ ἔθνη, ἄχρι τελεσθῇ τὰ χίλια ἔτη· μετὰ ταῦτα δεῖ λυθῆναι αὐτὸν μικρὸν χρόνον.</span>

- **Five acts of restraint in a row:** seized, bound, thrown into the abyss, shut, sealed over him. Revelation wants the reader to see strong confinement, not reduced influence.
- **The purpose clause** <span class="gr">ἵνα μὴ πλανήσῃ ἔτι τὰ ἔθνη</span> (*hina mē planēsē eti ta ethnē*): *so that he no longer deceive the nations*. English Bibles often say *might not*; there is no separate Greek word for *might*. The construction is a plain purpose clause, cleaner than the English.
- *Any longer* (<span class="gr">ἔτι</span>, *eti*) implies he had been doing it before. The binding restrains an existing activity.
- Three markers of sequence in one verse: *until the thousand years are completed* (a bounded period that ends), *after these things*, and *he must be released* (<span class="gr">δεῖ</span>, *dei*: necessary within the sequence).
- **Does not establish** why. The text says the release *must* happen; it does not give Yah's reason, and none should be supplied.
- *A little while* (<span class="gr">μικρὸν χρόνον</span>, *mikron chronon*). Revelation 12:12's *short time* uses different words (*oligon kairon*), and 20:3 places its period after the thousand years. Nothing in the wording forces the two periods to be the same.

**20:4**

> <span class="gr">Καὶ εἶδον θρόνους, καὶ ἐκάθισαν ἐπ’ αὐτούς, καὶ κρίμα ἐδόθη αὐτοῖς, καὶ τὰς ψυχὰς τῶν πεπελεκισμένων διὰ τὴν μαρτυρίαν Ἰησοῦ καὶ διὰ τὸν λόγον τοῦ θεοῦ, καὶ οἵτινες οὐ προσεκύνησαν τὸ θηρίον οὐδὲ τὴν εἰκόνα αὐτοῦ καὶ οὐκ ἔλαβον τὸ χάραγμα ἐπὶ τὸ μέτωπον καὶ ἐπὶ τὴν χεῖρα αὐτῶν· καὶ ἔζησαν καὶ ἐβασίλευσαν μετὰ τοῦ χριστοῦ χίλια ἔτη.</span>

- *Those who had been beheaded* is a perfect participle: people who had been killed.
- Then <span class="gr">ἔζησαν</span> (*ezēsan*), *they lived* or *came to life*, and *they reigned* with the Messiah a thousand years. The sequence is killed, lived, reigned.
- **Not settled here:** whether the verse describes one group further described, or more than one. That needs grammatical expertise this investigation does not claim.

**20:5**

> <span class="gr">οἱ λοιποὶ τῶν νεκρῶν οὐκ ἔζησαν ἄχρι τελεσθῇ τὰ χίλια ἔτη. αὕτη ἡ ἀνάστασις ἡ πρώτη.</span>

- The same verb, negated: the rest of the dead *did not live* until the thousand years were completed. *This is the first resurrection.*
- Dead people, *lived*, *first resurrection*: three pieces that stand together. Language can use one verb in two senses close together, so a different sense is not impossible. But the burden falls on any reading that makes the first *lived* non-bodily and the second bodily. This is the strongest point in the Greek against a purely figurative first resurrection.
- *First*, with *the rest* living only later, gives an order: a first resurrection, the thousand years, then the rest of the dead.

**20:6**

> <span class="gr">μακάριος καὶ ἅγιος ὁ ἔχων μέρος ἐν τῇ ἀναστάσει τῇ πρώτῃ· ἐπὶ τούτων ὁ δεύτερος θάνατος οὐκ ἔχει ἐξουσίαν, ἀλλ’ ἔσονται ἱερεῖς τοῦ θεοῦ καὶ τοῦ χριστοῦ, καὶ βασιλεύσουσιν μετ’ αὐτοῦ χίλια ἔτη.</span>

- The second death has no <span class="gr">ἐξουσία</span> (*exousia*) over them: no *authority* or jurisdiction, the same word Revelation uses throughout for authority given or withheld.
- A tense shift English tends to smooth over: 20:4 *they reigned* (aorist), 20:6 *they will reign* (future). The vision reports what John saw, then speaks of the blessed status looking forward. Recorded, not resolved.

**20:7**

> <span class="gr">Καὶ ὅταν τελεσθῇ τὰ χίλια ἔτη, λυθήσεται ὁ Σατανᾶς ἐκ τῆς φυλακῆς αὐτοῦ,</span>

- *When the thousand years are completed*: the completion formula of 20:3 returns.
- *From his prison* (<span class="gr">φυλακή</span>, *phylakē*). Revelation itself calls the abyss his prison. That weakens any reading in which the bound Satan stays freely active everywhere.

**20:8**

> <span class="gr">καὶ ἐξελεύσεται πλανῆσαι τὰ ἔθνη τὰ ἐν ταῖς τέσσαρσι γωνίαις τῆς γῆς, τὸν Γὼγ καὶ Μαγώγ, συναγαγεῖν αὐτοὺς εἰς τὸν πόλεμον, ὧν ὁ ἀριθμὸς αὐτῶν ὡς ἡ ἄμμος τῆς θαλάσσης.</span>

- *To deceive the nations* answers 20:3's *no longer deceive the nations* almost word for word. The binding's scope is as clear as Revelation could make it.
- *The four corners of the earth*: <span class="gr">γῆ</span> (*gē*) is broad here, but the word itself does not decide between *earth* and *land* (C4).
- *Gog and Magog* stand beside *the nations* as a description of them. Revelation describes the gathered nations through Ezekiel's names; it does not hand the reader two modern place names (Appendix B, item 3).
- *For the war*, with the article, and a number *like the sand of the sea*: very many, not a remnant.

**20:9**

> <span class="gr">καὶ ἀνέβησαν ἐπὶ τὸ πλάτος τῆς γῆς, καὶ ἐκύκλευσαν τὴν παρεμβολὴν τῶν ἁγίων καὶ τὴν πόλιν τὴν ἠγαπημένην. καὶ κατέβη πῦρ ἐκ τοῦ οὐρανοῦ καὶ κατέφαγεν αὐτούς·</span>

- *They went up over the breadth of the land*: army movement, not a vertical ascent.
- *The camp of the saints and the beloved city.* The city is not named, and this book does not name it for the text.
- Fire *came down* and *consumed* them. Gathering, surrounding, fire, finished: judgment, not a contest.

**20:10**

> <span class="gr">καὶ ὁ διάβολος ὁ πλανῶν αὐτοὺς ἐβλήθη εἰς τὴν λίμνην τοῦ πυρὸς καὶ θείου, ὅπου καὶ τὸ θηρίον καὶ ὁ ψευδοπροφήτης, καὶ βασανισθήσονται ἡμέρας καὶ νυκτὸς εἰς τοὺς αἰῶνας τῶν αἰώνων.</span>

- *The one deceiving them*: deception is his defining act, closing the arc that opened at 20:3.
- The throwing verb (<span class="gr">βάλλω</span>, *ballō*) repeats from 20:3, but the destination changes: then the abyss, now the lake of fire. Temporary confinement, then final judgment. The shared verb must not blur the difference.
- *Where also the beast and the false prophet*: **the clause has no finite verb.** English supplies *are* or *were*. That they are *already* there is an inference from the sequence, not a word in the text: Revelation 19:20 throws them into the lake first, before the binding and the thousand years.

## C3. The sea: *thalassa* in Revelation

<span class="gr">θάλασσα</span> (*thalassa*), *sea*: **26 occurrences in 23 verses, across 13 chapters.**

4:6; 5:13; 7:1; 2; 3; 8:8 (×2); 9; 10:2; 5; 6; 8; 12:12; 18; 13:1; 14:7; 15:2 (×2); 16:3 (×2); 18:17; 19; 21; 20:8; 13; 21:1

What the thread shows:

- **The Greek really says *sea*.** At 21:1, <span class="gr">καὶ ἡ θάλασσα οὐκ ἔστιν ἔτι</span>: *and the sea is no more*. Unlike the *might* of 20:3, nothing here is a translator's device. So the Greek does not settle the question; it moves it one step deeper: what does John mean by *sea*?
- **Sea and abyss are different words, kept apart.** 21:1 removes the sea, not the abyss.
- **The beast rises *out of the sea*** (13:1), as Daniel's four beasts come up out of the sea. The second beast rises *out of the earth* (13:11). Revelation sets sea against land as well as heaven above the abyss.
- **But 20:13 forbids a one-word code.** *The sea gave up the dead who were in it*: here *sea* is the ordinary sea, one verse before 21:1. *Sea = chaos* or *sea = the nations* cannot be made a fixed rule.
- **"No more sea" is not "no more water."** The next chapter opens with *the river of the water of life* (22:1).
- **Left open:** whether 21:1 describes a physical change, the removal of what the sea stood for, or both. The wording does not decide it.

The lesson this audit taught the investigation: going to the Greek answers *what John wrote*. It does not by itself answer *what John's image means*. The Greek is where that work begins. (Chapter 39.)

## C4. Earth or land: *gē* in Revelation

<span class="gr">γῆ</span> (*gē*), *earth*, *land*, *ground*: **82 occurrences in 67 verses, across 18 of the book's 22 chapters.**

1:5; 7; 3:10; 5:3 (×2); 6; 10; 13 (×2); 6:4; 8 (×2); 10; 13; 15; 7:1 (×3); 2; 3; 8:5; 7 (×2); 13; 9:1; 3 (×2); 4; 10:2; 5; 6; 8; 11:4; 6; 10 (×2); 18; 12:4; 9; 12; 13; 16 (×2); 13:3; 8; 11; 12; 13; 14 (×2); 14:3; 6; 7; 15; 16 (×2); 18; 19 (×2); 16:1; 2; 18; 17:2 (×2); 5; 8; 18; 18:1; 3 (×2); 9; 11; 23; 24; 19:2; 19; 20:8; 9; 11; 21:1 (×2); 24

What it shows:

- The recurring patterns are *those who dwell on the earth* (ten times), *the kings of the earth* (eight), and *earth* standing beside *sea* (in eleven verses).
- The scope markers are mostly broad: heaven, earth and sea as the whole of creation (10:6, 14:7), *the whole earth* (13:3), *the four corners of the earth* (7:1, 20:8), *the breadth of the earth* (20:9), *a new earth* (21:1).
- **No occurrence restricts it to a named territory.** That is the dominant usage, not a seal: the word can still mean *land*, and Chapters 38 and 42 leave that reading open at 18:24 and 1:7. (Chapter 46.)

## C5. Earthquakes: *seismos* in Revelation

<span class="gr">σεισμός</span> (*seismos*), *earthquake*: **7 occurrences in 5 verses.**

| Verse | Great? | Scene | With it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6:12 | yes | sixth seal | the sun black as sackcloth, the whole moon like blood |
| 8:5 | no | before the trumpets | thunders, voices, lightnings |
| 11:13 (×2) | yes | after the two witnesses rise | a tenth of the city falls; seven thousand killed |
| 11:19 | — (the hail is great) | seventh trumpet; the temple opened | lightnings, voices, thunders, great hail |
| 16:18 (×2) | yes, and without precedent | seventh bowl | lightnings, voices, thunders, and at 16:21 great hail |

What it shows: a pattern that returns with more each time: thunders, voices and lightnings at the throne (4:5), then with an earthquake (8:5), with an earthquake and hail (11:19), and with the greatest earthquake and great hail (16:18–21). That is literary escalation, the spiral in the vocabulary itself. **It does not establish** whether 8:5, 11:19 and 16:18 describe one event or several. (Chapter 51.)

## C6. Horses: *hippos* in Revelation

<span class="gr">ἵππος</span> (*hippos*), *horse*: **16 occurrences in 15 verses.**

6:2; 4; 5; 8; 9:7; 9; 17 (×2); 19; 14:20; 18:13; 19:11; 14; 18; 19; 21

What it shows: in chapter 19 the horses are on both sides of the same scene: the rider called Faithful and True and the armies of heaven (19:11, 14), and the beast's forces (19:18–21). **Horses do not date the battle.** The imagery belongs to both heavenly and earthly participants and has to be read in context. (Chapter 51.)
