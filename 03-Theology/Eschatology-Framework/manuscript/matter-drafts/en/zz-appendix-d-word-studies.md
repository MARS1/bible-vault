# Appendix D — Word studies

This book keeps returning to single words in Hebrew, Aramaic and Greek, because an argument often turns on one. This appendix gathers them.

**D1** lists the words the chapters actually study. Each entry says what the word means, what it establishes, and — just as important — what it does not establish, with the chapter where the study is made. A word can make a reading possible without making it necessary, and most of the mistakes this investigation caught in itself were a word asked to carry more than its range allows.

**D2** lists every other Hebrew, Aramaic and Greek word the book cites, with the chapters where it appears.

Where a count is given, the complete list of occurrences is in Appendix C.

## D1. Word studies

### Hebrew and Aramaic

**<span class="he">כָּרַת בְּרִית</span> (*karat berit*)** — literally *to cut a covenant*.
Establishes: covenant and sacrifice are joined in the vocabulary itself; at Genesis 15:18 the verb sits beside animals cut in halves. *Chapter 13.*

**<span class="he">עוֹלָם</span> (*olam*)** — *everlasting*, or *perpetual, age-lasting*.
Not decided. Whether *olam* is always endless, or enduring throughout an appointed order, cannot be answered from the English *everlasting*; the RVR1960's *perpetuo* already leans the second way. Deliberately not resolved, because a convenient answer would relieve several pressures at once. *Chapter 16.*

**<span class="he">נָתַתִּי אֶת־תּוֹרָתִי בְּקִרְבָּם</span> (*natatti et-torati beqirbam*)**, Jeremiah 31:33 — *I will put my Torah within them*.
Establishes: the location of Torah changes, from stone to the heart. Does not establish: Torah discarded. *Chapter 14.*

**<span class="he">רַבִּים</span> (*rabbim*)**, Daniel 12:2 — *many*, not *all*.
Recorded, and deliberately not argued from. Beside John 5's *all who are in the tombs* it leaves a staging question open (Appendix A, 10). *Chapters 25 and 27.*

**<span class="he">יְשֹׁטְטוּ</span> (*yeshoteṭu*)** and **<span class="he">הַדָּעַת</span> (*ha-daʿat*)**, Daniel 12:4 — *range far and wide*; *the knowledge*.
The clause sits between the sealing command and Daniel's admission that he does not understand, so the ranging may be through the book and *the knowledge* may be of the prophecy. Does not establish: a prediction of modern travel or technology; that reading has not been earned. *Chapter 29.*

**<span class="he">שִׁקּוּץ</span> (*shiqquts*)**, Daniel — *abomination*.
Whether it carries a specifically idolatrous, cultic range: reported, not verified; no lexicon was consulted (Appendix F). *Chapter 43.*

### Greek

**<span class="gr">ἄβυσσος</span> (*abyssos*)** — *abyss*, literally *bottomless*.
Establishes: in Revelation, a place of confinement with a key and a shaft. Does not establish: the ocean; Revelation uses a different word for the sea. The angel of the abyss, Abaddon, is not identified with Satan. *Chapter 39; Appendix C.*

**<span class="gr">ἅγιοι</span> (*hagioi*)** — *saints*, literally *the holy ones*: those set apart to Yahweh.
Covenant vocabulary for a people, with a home in Daniel 7's *saints of the Most High*, to whom judgment is given. Does not mean the morally exceptional or a canonised class. *Chapter 38.*

**<span class="gr">αἰών</span> (*aiōn*)** and **<span class="gr">συντέλεια τοῦ αἰῶνος</span> (*synteleia tou aiōnos*)** — *age*; *the consummation of the age*.
Establishes: the phrase is about an age reaching its appointed end. Does not establish: the destruction of the earth; and it does not say which age. *Chapters 7 and 25.*

**<span class="gr">ἀμεταμέλητα</span> (*ametamelēta*)**, Romans 11:29 — *irrevocable*.
Placed first in its clause for emphasis; word order supports the force but should not carry the argument alone. *Chapter 22.*

**<span class="gr">ἀνάστασις</span> (*anastasis*)**, Hebrews 11:35 — *resurrection*.
The verse sets two resurrections side by side: the dead returned to ordinary life, and *a better resurrection*. The ESV's *a better life* moves the *better* off the resurrection. Establishes: early believers could speak of resurrection with differences of quality or status. Does not establish: any millennial timetable. *Chapter 40.*

**<span class="gr">ἀνομία</span> (*anomia*)** — *lawlessness*: the absence or repudiation of the law's rule.
Matthew 24:12 and 2 Thessalonians 2:7–8 share the word family, and in both sequences it comes before the climactic intervention. Matthew's earlier use of the root in the parable of the weeds is an observation, not an argument. *Chapter 43.*

**<span class="gr">ἀπάντησις</span> (*apantēsis*)** — *meeting*.
Three occurrences. In none does the noun itself say which way the parties go after the meeting. Does not establish: that the one met is escorted back. The "civic reception" reading is reported, not verified. *Chapter 42; Appendix F.*

**<span class="gr">ἀριθμὸς ἀνθρώπου</span> (*arithmos anthrōpou*)**, Revelation 13:18 — *a man's number*, or *a human number*.
The Greek has no article and permits both. Revelation 21:17 uses the same construction for ordinary human measure, which shows the "human reckoning" reading is John's own idiom. Does not settle 13:18. *Chapters 46 and 49.*

**<span class="gr">ἁρπάζω</span> (*harpazō*)** — *to seize, snatch*.
Fourteen occurrences; six state a destination, eight do not. Acts 8:39 seizes Philip to an ordinary town. Establishes: the destination always comes from context. Does not establish: *taken to heaven*. *Chapter 42; Appendix C.*

**<span class="gr">ἄχρι</span> (*achri*)** — *until*.
Something continuing *until* a point continues through that point. Does not establish: that it stops afterward (1 Corinthians 15:25; Luke 21:24; Revelation 20:3). *Chapters 41 and 43.*

**<span class="gr">βδέλυγμα</span> (*bdelygma*)**, with Mark's **<span class="gr">ἑστηκότα</span> (*hestēkota*)** — *abomination*; *standing*.
The noun is neuter and Mark's participle masculine. That could point to *someone standing* rather than *something set up*. Recorded as an observation only: it needs a grammarian, and the editions differ. *Chapter 43.*

**<span class="gr">γενεά</span> (*genea*)** — *generation*.
Thirteen occurrences in Matthew: eight a contemporary body of people, four the counting unit of the genealogy at 1:17, one disputed (24:34). With a demonstrative, every determinable case means a contemporary body of people. That a genealogical sense at 1:17 makes a *race* reading of 24:34 easier does not follow. *Chapter 41.*

**<span class="gr">γῆ</span> (*gē*)** — *earth*, *land*, *ground*.
Eighty-two occurrences in Revelation; the scope markers are mostly broad, and none restricts the word to a named territory. With *tribes* at Matthew 24:30 a land sense is supported, not required. Establishes: the dominant usage. Does not establish: that it can never mean *the land*. *Chapters 11, 38, 42 and 46; Appendix C.*

**<span class="gr">δεῖ</span> (*dei*)** — *must*.
At Revelation 20:3 the release happens inside an ordained sequence, not as an escape. Does not establish: the reason. Shared with 1 Corinthians 15:25, it is too common a word to count as a link between the passages. *Chapters 39 and 41.*

**<span class="gr">δέω</span> (*deō*)** — *to bind, tie*. An ordinary verb, not a technical term.
Matthew 12:29, Mark 3:27 and Revelation 20:2 share it: the binding of the strong man and the binding of the dragon use the same verb. Recorded at full strength, because it favours a reading the book had set aside. *Chapter 45.*

**<span class="gr">ἐγκεντρίζω</span> (*enkentrizō*)** — *to graft in*.
Six times in Romans 11 and nowhere else in the New Testament. A shoot inserted into an existing rooted tree: the tree and the root are not replaced. *Chapter 22.*

**<span class="gr">ἔζησαν</span> (*ezēsan*)** — *they lived, came to life*.
The same verb in Revelation 20:4 and 20:5, of two groups. The burden falls on any reading that makes the first non-bodily and the second bodily. The RVR1960 hides the repetition (*vivieron* / *no volvieron a vivir*). Neither *they lived* nor *they reigned* appears in 1 Thessalonians 4, 1 Corinthians 15 or 2 Thessalonians 2. *Chapters 39 and 44; Appendix C.*

**<span class="gr">ἐν τάχει</span> (*en tachei*)** and **<span class="gr">ὁ καιρὸς ἐγγύς</span> (*ho kairos engys*)**, Revelation 1:1, 1:3 — *soon*; *the appointed season is near*.
*En tachei* can also mean *rapidly once it begins*. *Kairos* is the appointed season, not a duration, so 1:3 is the stronger: it says the season itself is near. Does not by itself settle the twenty-two chapters. *Chapter 30.*

**<span class="gr">ἐξουσία</span> (*exousia*)** — *authority*, *jurisdiction*.
At Revelation 20:6 the second death has no *exousia* over them: no legal claim, not merely no force. *Chapter 39.*

**<span class="gr">ἐπισυνάγω</span> (*episynagō*)** / **<span class="gr">ἐπισυναγωγή</span> (*episynagōgē*)** — *to gather together* / *gathering*.
Ten occurrences in nine verses, including ordinary crowds (Mark 1:33, Luke 12:1) and a church meeting (Hebrews 10:25): not inherently end-time vocabulary. The noun pair *coming and gathering* is in 2 Thessalonians 2:1, not in Matthew 24:31. Matthew 13:41 uses a different verb, *syllegō*. *Chapters 10, 41 and 42; Appendix C.*

**<span class="gr">ἕως ἂν πάντα γένηται</span> (*heōs an panta genētai*)** — *until all is accomplished*.
Matthew 5:18 and 24:34 differ only by *tauta*, "these," and both use *pass away*. Recorded side by side; the comparison is an observation. *Chapter 12.*

**<span class="gr">θάλασσα</span> (*thalassa*)** — *sea*.
Twenty-six occurrences in Revelation. 21:1 really says *sea*, but 20:13 uses it in its ordinary sense one verse earlier, so it cannot be a fixed code. Does not establish what *no more sea* means. *Chapter 39; Appendix C.*

**<span class="gr">ἵνα μή</span> (*hina mē*)**, Revelation 20:3 — a purpose construction: *so that … not*.
There is no Greek word behind English *might*; translators supply it. Establishes the purpose of the confinement. Does not establish the scope of the deceiving. *Chapters 39 and 45.*

**<span class="gr">καινός</span> (*kainos*)** — *new*, *renewed*, *new in kind*.
The range is not simply *replaced by an unrelated second creation*, but lexical range alone cannot settle the theology of the new heavens and new earth. *Chapter 39.*

**<span class="gr">καιρός</span> (*kairos*)** / **<span class="gr">χρόνος</span> (*chronos*)** and Revelation's short times — *appointed season* / *duration*.
*A little while* at 20:3 (*mikron chronon*) uses the same two words as 6:11, in reverse order; they are Revelation's only uses of *mikros* for time. The *short time* of 12:12 (*oligon kairon*) and 17:10 use different words. Does not establish that the periods are the same. *Chapters 30, 47 and 51.*

**<span class="gr">καταλῦσαι</span> (*katalysai*)** / **<span class="gr">πληρῶσαι</span> (*plērōsai*)**, Matthew 5:17 — *to tear down* / *to fill up, bring to completion*.
Establishes: Yeshua denies tearing down and affirms bringing to completion. Does not establish either *fulfil means terminate* or *nothing may ever change*; both are imported. *Chapter 12.*

**<span class="gr">κηρύσσω</span> (*kēryssō*)** / **<span class="gr">ἀποστέλλω</span> (*apostellō*)**, Romans 10:14–15 — *to herald publicly* / *to send*.
Establishes that commission is in view. Does not establish a closed office of the Twelve, nor that every believer must preach. *Chapter 21.*

**<span class="gr">κοινός</span> (*koinos*)** / **<span class="gr">ἀκάθαρτος</span> (*akathartos*)**, Romans 14:14 — *common, defiled by association* / *unclean*, the Levitical category.
Paul says nothing is *koinon* of itself; he does not say nothing is *akatharton*. Whether he is speaking of species categories at all is left open. *Chapter 17.*

**<span class="gr">ναός</span> (*naos*)** / **<span class="gr">ἱερόν</span> (*hieron*)** — *sanctuary* / *temple complex*.
Paul uses both, so *naos* at 2 Thessalonians 2:4 is a choice. Establishes: sanctuary, not precinct. Does not establish whose sanctuary, since both candidates (the Jerusalem sanctuary and the community) are sanctuaries. *Chapter 42; Appendix C.*

**<span class="gr">οἰκουμένη</span> (*oikoumenē*)** — *the inhabited world*.
Luke uses it of Caesar's census and of a famine under Claudius: it can carry a bounded, first-century scope. Hebrews 2:5 uses it of a world to come. Where Paul speaks in his own words of the gospel's reach he writes *kosmos* and *ktisis*, not *oikoumenē*. Establishes: the referent must be determined at each occurrence. *Chapters 7, 23 and 26.*

**<span class="gr">παρεμβολή</span> (*parembolē*)**, Revelation 20:9 — *camp*.
A concrete camp, and in the Greek Old Testament the standard word for Israel's camp in the wilderness. Establishes: a camp of the covenant people on the earth at the end of the thousand years. Does not establish that the saints who reign in 20:4–6 were in it during them. *Chapters 47 and 48.*

**<span class="gr">παρουσία</span> (*parousia*)** — *presence*, *arrival*.
Paul uses it of the Lord's coming and, eight verses later, of the lawless one's. Establishes: an arrival of the Messiah in each passage. Does not establish the same arrival; that comes, where it comes, from the surrounding details. *Chapter 44.*

**<span class="gr">περὶ δέ</span> (*peri de*)**, Matthew 24:36 — *now concerning*.
Four times in Matthew: twice of a time of day, twice introducing a subject. Does not decide whether 24:36 marks off what precedes it. *Chapter 41.*

**<span class="gr">πλανάω</span> (*planaō*)** — *to deceive*.
Eight occurrences in Revelation, with several deceivers: Jezebel, the dragon, the second beast, Babylon, the false prophet. Establishes: 20:3 restrains an actor, not deception as such; *any longer* means he had been deceiving; every named nation-scale deceiver is removed before the thousand years. *Chapter 45; Appendix C.*

**<span class="gr">πληρόω</span> (*plēroō*)**, Romans 15:19 — *to fill up, complete*.
Paul regards something as standing completed. Does not establish which sense of completion; that comes from what he says next. *Chapter 7.*

**<span class="gr">τὸ πλήρωμα τῶν ἐθνῶν</span> (*to plērōma tōn ethnōn*)**, Romans 11:25 — *the fullness of the nations*.
Not established: whether it denotes a number, a covenantal fullness, a completed mission or something else. Left open. *Chapter 22.*

**<span class="gr">πόλεμος</span> (*polemos*)** — *war, battle*.
Nine occurrences in Revelation, in four uses: comparison, war against God's people, conflict in heaven, and the gathering for the final battle. Establishes: war against God's people need not involve two armies. *Chapter 52; Appendix C.*

**<span class="gr">πορευθέντες</span> (*poreuthentes*)** / **<span class="gr">μαθητεύσατε</span> (*mathēteusate*)**, Matthew 28:19 — *having gone* / *make disciples*.
The command is *make disciples*; going carries the force of the command with it and is not optional, and baptizing and teaching say how. *Chapter 24.*

**<span class="gr">σαββατισμός</span> (*sabbatismos*)**, Hebrews 4:9 — *a Sabbath rest*, or *a Sabbath-keeping*.
Once in the New Testament, and a deliberate shift from *katapausis*. Does not prove a command about which day to keep, nor may it be flattened into general spiritual rest. The ESV and CJB divide along exactly that line. *Chapter 18.*

**<span class="gr">σάλπιγξ</span> (*salpinx*)** — *trumpet*.
The CJB renders it *shofar* throughout: defensible, but it decides an intertextual question the Greek leaves open. Whether the trumpet passages describe one event or several is not settled (Appendix A, 12). *Chapters 10 and 41.*

**<span class="gr">τέλος</span> (*telos*)**, Romans 10:4 — *end*: termination, or goal and completion.
Romans 10:4 cannot be settled from the English word *end*. *Chapter 21.*

**<span class="gr">φυλαί</span> (*phylai*)** — *tribes*.
Overwhelmingly the vocabulary of Israel's tribes, but also used in Revelation's universal formulas (5:9, 7:9). Leans toward a covenantal-land sense at Matthew 24:30; cannot establish the referent alone. *Chapter 11.*

**<span class="gr">χίλια ἔτη</span> (*chilia etē*)** — *a thousand years*.
Establishes: a definite, bounded interval stated in the language of quantity; each time it is referred back to as completed, it carries the article. Does not establish: a calendar count. That reading is the weakest-graded claim in the chain. *Chapters 47 and 49; Appendix C.*

**<span class="gr">ψύχω</span> (*psychō*)**, Matthew 24:12 — *to grow cold*.
Only here in the New Testament; not in Revelation. *The love of the many*, with the article. *Chapter 51.*
