# Appendix F — Sources

This appendix lists every source outside the biblical text that this book relies on, and the texts of Scripture it reads from. Each entry says how far it was checked.

One rule governs the list: **a report of a source is not the source.** "Tacitus says…" has to point to the place in Tacitus, not to someone who said Tacitus says it. A source that was cited to me but that I did not open myself stays *reported, not verified* until it is opened.

The states used:

- **Verified** — opened and read directly; the wording relied on was checked.
- **Partly verified** — opened, but incomplete, or only in translation where the original matters.
- **Reported, not verified** — known only from someone else's report.
- **Not reached** — sought and not found or not accessible. This is not the same as *does not exist*.

*Verified* means the source says what this book says it says. It does not mean the source is right. Ancient historians had interests; later writers repeat earlier ones; reference works make judgments. The chapters weigh those things; this list records only what was checked.

## F1. Texts of Scripture

**The Greek New Testament — *SBL Greek New Testament* (SBLGNT).** The base text for every Greek New Testament quotation in the book. **Verified**, passage by passage, throughout Parts II–IX.

**MorphGNT** — the same SBLGNT text with the dictionary form of every word marked. Used for every complete word count in the book (Appendix C). **Verified.**

**SBLGNT apparatus** — records where the main printed editions differ: Westcott and Hort, Tregelles, Nestle-Aland (28th edition) and the Robinson–Pierpont Byzantine text. Used for Matthew 12:29, 24:36, Mark 13:14, Luke 17:37 and the tense at Revelation 5:10. **Verified at the level of the printed editions.** The manuscripts behind those editions were not examined (see F6).

**The Hebrew and Aramaic Scriptures — *Miqra according to the Masorah*** (a Masoretic edition, read through Sefaria). Used for Daniel 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 11 and 12; Isaiah 11, 13, 27 and 65; Ezekiel 38–39; and other Hebrew quotations. **Verified.**

**The Septuagint — Swete's edition.** Used for the Greek of Isaiah, Ezekiel, Zechariah, the Psalms, 3 and 4 Kingdoms and other books where the apostles' Greek is compared with it (Chapters 13, 20–23, 41–43, 48). **Verified.**

**The two Greek versions of Daniel — Old Greek and Theodotion** (the CCAT morphologically tagged Septuagint, University of Pennsylvania). Used for Daniel 3, 7, 8, 11 and 12 in both versions (Chapters 41, 43, 46). **Verified.**

**1 and 2 Maccabees.** The Greek of 1 Maccabees 1:21, 1:54, 4:36, 4:54–59 and 6:16 (same CCAT corpus): **verified.** The wider narrative of the Antiochus crisis was read in English only (NRSV): **partly verified.** Their canonical status is not decided in this book (Chapter 43).

**English translations compared:** the **ESV** and the **CJB** (Complete Jewish Bible), used side by side wherever a rendering affects an argument; the **NIV** (Mark 7:19, Chapter 17) and the **KJV** (Leviticus 16, Chapter 15) where a rendering is discussed. Quoted renderings **verified**. A translation is evidence about the translation, never about the original.

**The Revised JPS** translation is cited once, for a rendering of Daniel 12:4 (Chapter 29). It is not recorded separately in the research ledger: **reported, not verified.**

**Spanish translations:** the **Reina-Valera 1960**, the edition's reference Bible: **verified** for every passage where its wording carries an argument. The **Reina Valera Actualizada 2015**, used as a second witness where the RVR1960 hides a feature of the Greek: **partly verified** (one retrieval of Revelation 20:3 came back incomplete).

## F2. Early historical witnesses

**Tacitus, *Annals* 15.44** — Nero blames the fire of AD 64 on the Christians and persecutes them; *Christus* executed under Tiberius by Pontius Pilate. Roman, hostile, non-Christian, writing about fifty years later. **Partly verified:** English translation only; the Latin was not retrieved. Chapters 36 and 46.

**1 Clement, chapter 5** — a letter from the assembly at Rome near the end of the first century, remembering Peter and Paul as martyrs. **Verified in English translation.** Parts of the text are editors' reconstructions of gaps; the Greek was not retrieved. Chapter 36.

**Eusebius, *Church History* III.18 and III.20** — John exiled to Patmos under Domitian; his return to Ephesus after Nerva, which Eusebius himself labels "an ancient Christian tradition." Written in the fourth century. **Verified in English translation.** Chapters 36, 38 and 46. This is counter-evidence to the book's earlier date for Revelation and is kept at full strength.

**Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 5.30.3** — the vision "was seen no very long time since, but almost in our day, towards the end of Domitian's reign." The earliest external witness to Revelation's date. **Verified in English translation**: three independent copies of the standard translation agree word for word, and agree with the quotation in Eusebius. The Greek was not retrieved (F6). Chapters 36, 38 and 46.

**Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 5.30.1** — the number of the beast worked "by the Greek mode of calculation," and his knowledge of the variant 616. **Partly verified** (an excerpt only). Chapter 46.

**Josephus**, on the Jewish revolt and the fall of Jerusalem, including his report of armies seen in the clouds before the catastrophe. **Reported, not verified;** Chapter 28 says so where it mentions him.

## F3. Manuscripts, variants and calculations

**Revelation 13:18, the reading 616** — a papyrus of about the mid-third century (P115, damaged, with some ambiguity in the surviving space) and the fifth-century Codex Ephraemi Rescriptus. **Verified at the level of reference works;** a full critical apparatus was not consulted. Chapter 46.

**Revelation 13:7, the missing clause** — the witnesses that omit "it was given to it to make war on the saints" (P47, Alexandrinus, Ephraemi, Porphyrianus, part of the Byzantine tradition). From Bruce Terry, *A Student's Guide to New Testament Textual Variants*, with two older commentaries. **Verified at a secondary level;** the Nestle-Aland apparatus itself was not consulted. Chapter 52.

**The number of the beast, computed.** The letter values of *Nero Caesar* in Hebrew letters — <span class="he">נרון קסר</span> (*Nerōn Qesar*) totals 666, and <span class="he">נרו קסר</span> (*Nerō Qesar*) totals 616 — and of the other names tested in Chapter 46 were calculated in this book from the standard letter values. **Verified as arithmetic.** That <span class="he">נרון קסר</span> (*Nerōn Qesar*) is the spelling a first-century reader would have used is a separate question (F6).

## F4. The chronology tests of Chapter 50

These tests checked whether a thousand years could be missing from conventional history. The specific facts below were each checked against published reference material; the individual publications behind each check were not recorded in this book's ledger, and are not listed here as if they had been.

- **The Islamic calendar** counts continuously from AD 622, formally numbered from a few years later. The arithmetic in Chapter 50 (about 0.9 years' difference across fourteen centuries) was computed in this book. **Verified.**
- **The Hebrew calendar** keeps a continuous count of years. **Verified**, as a count; it corroborates rather than decides.
- **Illig's proposal** of about 297 missing years, AD 614–911, not a thousand. **Verified.**
- **The Gregorian reform** took the equinox at the Council of Nicaea (AD 325) as its reference point. **Verified**, with one discrepancy noted in Chapter 50.
- **Halley's comet** at roughly 76-year intervals, AD 530–1066. **Partly verified:** six of eight appearances located in independent records; 530 not located, 989 resting on a weak mention.
- **Tree rings:** J. R. Pilcher, M. G. L. Baillie, B. Schmidt and B. Becker, "A 7,272-year tree-ring chronology for western Europe," *Nature* 312 (1984): 150–52. **Citation verified, data not read.** A further claimed sequence (160 BC – AD 1129) was **not confirmed** at those dates.
- **Radiocarbon and stratigraphy:** **reported, not verified**, and not used.

## F5. Reference tools

**Blue Letter Bible concordance** (Greek and Septuagint word lists), used to cross-check counts in Part VIII. **Verified** against the SBLGNT text.

**The "civic reception" reading of** [*apantēsis*]{style="hyphens: none"} (the meeting in 1 Thessalonians 4:17 as citizens going out to welcome a dignitary), associated with Erik Peterson's 1930 study. **Reported, not verified;** no papyrus or inscription was consulted, and Chapter 42 does not rely on it in either direction.

## F6. Sought and not reached, or not yet consulted

These are the gaps that remain. None is a text that was simply not looked up; each was either sought and not reached, or lies beyond what this book could consult.

- **The Greek of Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 5.30.3.** The English is verified; the wording behind "what was seen" cannot be weighed from a translation.
- **The Latin of Tacitus, *Annals* 15.44**, and **the Greek of 1 Clement 5.**
- **Documentary evidence for the spelling** <span class="he">נרון קסר</span> (*Nerōn Qesar*) in a dated first- or second-century document. Sought through five routes; **not reached**, which is not the same as unattested.
- **The manuscripts behind Matthew 24:36** ("nor the Son"). The printed editions divide; the manuscripts were not examined.
- **A full critical apparatus** (Nestle-Aland, the UBS edition, or the Münster institute's data) for Revelation 13:7 and 13:18.
- **Whether the Septuagint ever uses** *naos* **(sanctuary) of a people**, across the whole Septuagint. Not searched exhaustively.
- **Standard lexicons and grammars.** No standard Greek or Hebrew lexicon or reference grammar was consulted. The book's word studies rest on usage: every occurrence of a word in the primary texts, counted and read in context (Appendix C). Where a question needs a grammarian's judgment — the syntax of Revelation 20:4, the participle in Mark 13:14, the article in Revelation 13:18 — the chapter says so and does not decide it.
- **Papias, as quoted in Eusebius III.39** (the "two Johns"), and **Eusebius Book II** on the deaths of Peter and Paul. Not reached; not used in the book.

The full research ledger, with every retrieval attempt, its date and the route that failed, is kept with the research files. It is a working record, not part of this book.
