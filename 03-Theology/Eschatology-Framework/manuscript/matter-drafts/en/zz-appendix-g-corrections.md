# Appendix G — Corrections made to my own work

This appendix is a feature, not an apology.

Every correction below is already in the book, at the point where it happened. You meet each one in its chapter, in the same typeface as the findings that went my way. This list only gathers them in one place, so you can see the whole pattern at once: where the investigation changed its mind because the evidence required it.

Some cost me a position I liked. Some cost me nothing. A few ran in my favour. They are all here, because a record that kept only the corrections that made me look careful would be a different kind of document.

Ideas that were tested and failed — proposals, not my own mistakes — are listed separately, in Appendix B. One of them cost me something I wanted: the proposal that roughly a thousand years were inserted into conventional chronology would have solved a real problem for my position, and it failed the external tests run in Chapter 50.

## Part I — The question that would not stay small

| What I had thought | What corrected it | Chapter |
|------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|---------|
| Only the apostles proclaimed the message in the early church. | Acts 8:4: the scattered ordinary believers went everywhere announcing the word, and the apostles were the ones who stayed. | 4 |
| Acts 8:4 showed ordinary believers taking the message to Gentiles. | Acts 11:19: they spoke the word to no one except Jews. The correction stood, but within Israel. | 4 |
| Because I am not an evangelist, proclamation has nothing to do with me. | Acts 8 and the distinction between gifts and the ordinary witness of every believer. | 6 |

## Part II — The Olivet Discourse, read in its own century

| What I had thought | What corrected it | Chapter |
|------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|---------|
| A first-century millennium has a problem: where do the nations at its end come from? | Revelation 20:7–8 simply says the nations are there. The difficulty was a premise I had brought, not something missing from the text. | 7 |
| "A third of the angels rebelled" is something Scripture states. | It is an inference from Revelation 12:4 plus assumptions. Moved from the text box to the inference box. | 9 |
| Matthew 13:41 uses the same gathering verb as 24:31. | It does not: 13:41 has *syllegō*, 24:31 has *episynagō*. The error is left standing in Chapter 10 and corrected in Chapter 41, where chasing the real verb found Matthew 23:37. | 10, 41 |

## Part III — What the new covenant actually changed

| What I had thought | What corrected it | Chapter |
|------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|---------|
| "One covenant story", with each covenant an addendum to a single contract. | Too flat. Scripture gives one covenantal story carried by distinct, accumulating covenants. | 13 |
| "Yeshua Himself called it the new covenant" can rest on Matthew 26:28. | *New* is text-critically contested there; the claim rests on Luke 22:20 and 1 Corinthians 11:25, where it is secure. | 15 |
| That the missing *he declared* in Mark 7:19 makes the older reading (that digestion is what cleanses) equally available. | I overstated it. Greek participles routinely carry an understood referent, so the gap alone does not establish the older reading; it stays a real possibility that only syntax could decide. | 17 |
| Daniel was told in a vision not to eat the king's food, and the other youths grew weaker. | Daniel *resolved*, with no command reported, and the visions came afterward. The others did not decline; the four simply looked better. | 17 |

## Part IV — Israel, the nations, and the commission

| What I had thought | What corrected it | Chapter |
|------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|---------|
| Paul's *the whole world* is Matthew's *oikoumenē*, so Matthew 24:14 was fulfilled. | One English word was standing in for three Greek ones. Paul's phrase is borrowed from a psalm; the bridge does not survive its own test. | 21, 23 |
| Acts' pattern of guided mission supports a broad conclusion about divine foreknowledge directing it. | The formulation went beyond the evidence; a narrower one survived, and the overreach is kept visible. | 23 |

## Part V — Ages, resurrection, and the door into Revelation

| What I had thought | What corrected it | Chapter |
|------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|---------|
| My first count of *the consummation of the age* in Matthew 13. | A first-pass miscount: it occurs three times in that chapter alone (13:39, 13:40, 13:49). The correction ran in my favour. | 25 |
| *Oikoumenē* always means the Roman inhabited world. | Hebrews 2:5 uses it of the inhabited world *to come*. | 26 |
| Hebrews 9:26 shows an age already completely ended while resurrection is still future. | *At* the consummation of the ages places the appearing at the culmination; it does not say an age lay finished behind the author. | 28 |
| *Taken* in Matthew 24 means gathered to Messiah. | Far less automatic than I had assumed, and possibly backwards. | 28 |

## Part VI — Revelation, read through earlier Scripture

| What I had thought | What corrected it | Chapter |
|------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|---------|
| Every *coming* in Scripture belongs to one category, the Second Coming. | Revelation 2–3: several comings are aimed at single congregations, as local judgments. | 30 |
| Revelation 12's *short time* and Revelation 20's *little season* are the same period. | I caught myself merging them. They may not be the same: chapter 12's follows the casting down, chapter 20's follows the thousand years, and the Greek uses different words. | 35 |
| Revelation 6 requires all the apostles to die before the millennium. | It says the martyrs wait until their number is complete. It gives no total and does not say they are all apostles. | 36 |
| The saints of the thousand years are hidden or invisible to ordinary humanity. | Revelation does not say that. | 39 |
| *It is done!* at 16:17 marks the final completion. | The same words return at 21:6. It is a completion marker, not a unique one; I had said more than I had earned. | 39 |

## Part VII — Testing the case against itself

| What I had thought | What corrected it | Chapter |
|------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|---------|
| I kept reopening whether Revelation 20 has two resurrections. | Revelation stages them itself; the live question is how other texts map onto them. | 40 |
| The first-resurrection saints are morally higher than those judged later. | The book of life is present at the later judgment; that ranking is not in the text. | 40 |
| My summary: Matthew 24:31 and Paul's gathering are *not* the same event. | My files said *identity not established*. The summary had turned "unverified" into "false". Status restored to *open*. | 41 |
| *The consummation of the age* is Yeshua's phrase in the Olivet Discourse. | At 24:3 it is in the disciples' question; Yeshua never repeats it there. | 41 |
| Paul supplies structure without chronology. | Too absolute: Paul supplies substantial relative chronology, but no absolute historical anchor. | 41, 42 |
| A *moderate-to-strong* case for a physical first-century sanctuary in 2 Thessalonians 2. | Withdrawn. Paul's own usage favours the community reading. | 42 |
| *In the twinkling of an eye* describes a disappearance; *we shall all be changed* is universal; *firstfruits* means the first person ever raised. | The twinkling modifies the change; *all* is bounded by *in Messiah*; *firstfruits* means the first raised never to die again. | 44 |
| The binding of Satan restrains *hostile mobilization*. | A sweep of all eight uses of *deceive* in Revelation: where a result is stated, it is mostly idolatrous allegiance and false worship. The sweep corrected the rule it was run to support. | 45 |
| A principle built on Daniel 7:17 saying *kings* while 7:23 says *kingdom*. | The tension exists only in the Aramaic; both Greek versions read *kingdoms* at 7:17. | 46 |

## The pattern

Read down the right-hand column and one thing stands out. Most of these corrections came from going back to the text itself: reading the Greek instead of the English, reading in order instead of from memory, counting instead of estimating. That is the method this book asks you to use on it.
